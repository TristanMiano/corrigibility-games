# Research charter

## Question and intended contribution

Investigate voluntary utility modification as a response to strategic conflict in a world dominated by agents interacting with agents. Determine when physical differences between violent and consensual transitions make compromise attractive, when preferences or implementation limits prevent it, and how those conclusions change over time and under selection.

The goal is a conditional theory and an assumption map, not a demonstration that every rational agent must become cooperative. Useful negative results, equivalence to established commitment methods, and new applications of ordinary game theory are legitimate contributions.

The central comparison spans agent preferences, game environments and parameter settings: which favor combat, which favor compromise, and why? Investigate how simple or specially constructed the agents' goals are, separately from how demanding the environment and implementation assumptions are. Do not freeze a taxonomy of natural and unnatural agents in advance. A claim about typical behavior needs a justified distribution or a structural result over a clearly described class.

## Core hypothesis to investigate

> Agents need not intrinsically value corrigibility to find genuine preference changes advantageous. When other agents retain meaningful influence over their futures, refusing every accommodation can sacrifice more of their existing goals than a negotiated change would. Increasing capability can make such arrangements easier to discover and implement.

This is the owner's working hypothesis, to be tested through conditional results. Seek conditions under which agents' current goals favor a genuine change of preferences, identify the negotiated arrangement they would choose, and check its implementation and continuation incentives. Meaningful influence means that other agents retain a specified ability to affect valued future outcomes, through such mechanisms as production, exchange, control or combat. Specify that channel in each model. Distinguish refusing preference changes from refusing all accommodation: an agent retaining its goals may still bargain, trade, delegate and choose safeguards.

The primary explanatory target is what capable agents arrange when they can negotiate terms, successor goals and future commitments. Restricted games supply starting results; a broader claim must account for relevant repairs available to the agents. Voluntary preference change that advances prior goals is a substantive success even when a sufficiently capable delegate can match it. Compare actual availability, cost, credibility and stability; reserve stronger hypothetical controls for explicitly labeled comparisons. Report strict incentives and indifference separately.

Prioritize implications of the form **specified conditions imply more cooperation**, **specified conditions imply more combat**, or a sharp boundary between the two. Define the comparison and outcome measure, and investigate how simple, robust or specially constructed the sufficient conditions are. Counterexamples guide the search for these conditions. The research objective is an integrated result across a defensible class, or a characterized obstruction that survives serious attempts to enrich the agreement space. Local examples and populated topic headings are intermediate evidence toward that objective.

Treat capability as a set of explicit changes in search, computation, expressiveness, verification or physical execution, and separately track changes in combat and outside options. Derive when those changes make accommodation discoverable, feasible and attractive. The direction of the effect is a research question.

## Baseline idealizations

Agents know their own utility functions, understand available self-modifications, and can model the specified future dynamics and compute expected utilities. Stochastic combat depends on power parameters. Other agents dominate strategic uncertainty and opportunity; physics still constrains them. Start with two agents and tractable finite or discounted horizons. Public knowledge and common priors are optional strengthening assumptions to be explicitly identified.

An update is chosen using the utility in force before it. Descendants act on their own installed utilities. Original-agent assessments remain available as an external reporting perspective, not an invisible controller that prevents genuine preference change.

## Owner's physical hypothesis

Destructive conflict may scatter and mix materials, damage useful organization, heat or chemically transform resources, and leave them requiring additional collection, separation, reconstruction, cooling, or transport. Consensual updates can implement similar distributions of control or objectives by a physically different route. Compare both routes' useful-work budgets, resource availability, and temporal consequences even when their nonphysical strategic consequences are matched.

These mechanisms motivate models, not a universal assertion that every fight is costlier than every negotiation. Compromise can require expensive computation, communication, verification, or implementation. Some agents may value a destructive state. Derive the relevant inequalities and specify the goal class under which physical degradation is disadvantageous.

## Central distinctions

**Disagreement threat versus prohibited first move:** combat remains available. Peace must survive profitable deviations where the game allows them; it is not secured by a rule deleting attack until negotiation ends.

**Preference preservation versus freezing:** a fixed terminal objective can support flexible behavior and ordinary trade. A preference for retaining that objective may impose a finite penalty, a hard priority, or a contingent commitment benefit. Literal source-code preservation is another preference again.

**Voluntary adaptation versus selection:** an agent may rationally keep a goal that reduces its competitive survival. A type may disappear without its members ever agreeing to change. A flexible type may also be exploitable or lack credible commitments. Models must allow all these possibilities.

**Chosen safeguards versus blanket refusal:** an agent can accommodate others by changing its goal while choosing protection against later exploitation. Study such joint choices, including renegotiated terms and counterparty selection, when evaluating long-run inflexibility. Long-lived agents, innovation and preferences concerning descendants require explicit treatment when a claim reaches those settings; a finite inherited type menu establishes a result about its stated process.

**Efficiency versus peace versus corrigibility:** preserving more usable resources need not make every agent better off; peace need not require changing utilities; utility change need not be benevolent or globally stable.

## What successful research reports

State a model, derive informative conditional implications or boundaries, identify the assumptions doing the work, compare relevant available alternatives and labeled stronger controls, and explain which part is standard and which contribution is new or useful. Distinguish theoretical possibility, equilibrium behavior, and prevalence under a specified population process. No unqualified frequency claim about arbitrary utility functions is meaningful without a type distribution or structural theorem.

Every completed recurrence also explains its findings for a reader who has not followed the derivations. Begin with the main conclusions, develop the mechanisms through examples and parameter boundaries, and tie them back to the central questions. Explain what remains unknown about breadth across agents and environments. This reporting is required during research; it is not deferred to final synthesis or publication.

Keep mathematical discoveries in one main recurrence. Preparation and publication support the research rather than absorbing it. The four-hour floor and theory allocation are workload defaults, never substitutes for evidence or reasons to fabricate progress.

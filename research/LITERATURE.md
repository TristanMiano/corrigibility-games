## Closest results: assumptions and transfer limits

**L01 — extended private-information audit.** R0001 also inspected pp. 394–396 and appendix pp. 410–414, including Claim 2 and the cheap-talk proposition with its lemmas; pp. 411/413 were rendered. Claim 2 uses private war costs and an increasing hazard condition. The communication proposition restricts the proposer to pure demands; the source explicitly leaves its mixed-demand extension as a conjecture. Note 021 derives its own private-strength thresholds and does not import that conjecture. The UCSD original scan's SHA-256 is `06396feb7c3bcabca79efbf1aac2ff627fb60c55f897dcae701fa515e5c06de8`.

**L19 — conventional stopping machinery, different application.** The inspected original derives a reservation rule and geometric waiting under iid offers, then includes discounting. Note 022 supplies its own Bellman proof, incumbent change penalty, physical opportunity bounds and founder/successor comparison. It does not attribute utility-modification or equilibrium results to McCall. Retrieved scan SHA-256: `b24a7fd0afeffdd6da39504614300a2cf814e17ff6df3d375f6118586460f9bb`.

**L20 — universal lottery preservation is a substantive premise.** The inspected section combines individual/social expected-utility criteria with unanimous indifference to derive weighted linear aggregation. Indifference alone does not force positive weights; the source identifies an additional condition for nonnegative weights. Note 025 gives a direct resource-simplex specialization and does not require this universal premise for voluntary consent to one merger. Its nonlinear repair deliberately changes lottery preferences. Original scan SHA-256: `b673861e217fad7ec61d46c069e7927b6953216f58ab53a9a271afd4753deecd`.

**L12 — observable deviation versus credible execution.** Token games assume that the prescribed pure/correlated original-game outcomes are carried out; ex post observability alone is not that enforcement. Their outcome-correspondence assumptions also exceed ordinary equilibrium existence. The source's strict Pareto improvement allows weak gains for all and a strict gain somewhere for someone, whereas this run often requires strict gain for every incumbent. Disarmament is a separate comparison that can remove actions, not this project's persistent-combat baseline. Retrieved v2 SHA-256: `bd2e59de956de3da79685211b512dd2447be7bbfa202c76438d4cfe0237cc754`.

**L01 — bargain existence.** Claim 1 assumes a divisible interval, continuous increasing weakly concave utilities normalized at endpoints, a common victory probability, and positive additive war costs. Continuity/concavity yield a nonempty set of strictly preferred settlements. The risk-neutral interval must be intersected with feasible [0,1]. This establishes existence, not adoption or durable enforcement. Physical destruction is represented through payoffs, not recovery technology. B01 is a related standard calculation; C1 should derive its feasible set rather than rename an assumed cost.

**L02 — incumbent evaluation.** Theorem 16 states preservation of initial realistic optimal action value along the chosen action sequence. Primitives include finite world actions/percepts, full-support percept beliefs, bounded discounted rewards (0<gamma<1), modification-independent beliefs and utility, and an initially optimal realistic policy. Definition 12 evaluates future policies using the current utility. Its proof uses a nonmodifying optimal policy from Appendix A. This does not establish public goal verification or universal refusal to change: directly observed preference changes that alter opponents' responses, and penalties for change itself, violate the relevant independence assumptions. E1 deliberately includes both. The source's theorem is not imported into that game.

**L03 — strategic change.** The inspected examples evaluate an instrumental change by the old goal and require others to observe or believe a consequential change. This is close motivation for Q03/Q05, including commitment against exploitation. It is not evidence that all utility functions converge or that intelligence forces particular ends. The project must supply explicit equilibrium/implementation conditions instead of adopting the title's orthogonality conclusion.

**L04 — delegation.** Theorem 1 implements an SPI in a program-game Nash equilibrium. It assumes programs can invoke the delegate outcome rule on subset games and that default play gives each principal at least its minimax value. Appendix 1's code-equality test triggers minimax punishment on deviation. Delegate utilities/action subsets may differ, but improvement is evaluated by original principals. These public conditional programs are stronger than ordinary promises; restricted actions are permitted. Thus neither adoption in every equilibrium nor persistent-combat stability follows. This is close prior machinery for Q03, not proof that preference change adds something irreducible.

**L05 — implementation versus adoption.** Proposition 1 wraps default programs with a common Pareto-improving renegotiation function. Proposition 2 converts a subjective equilibrium to one using renegotiation under Assumption 4: nonmatching opponents respond to the wrapper as to its default, with the stated reciprocal default condition. Appendix A's proof separates matching and nonmatching cases. Programs inspect code and execute it; original payoffs remain fixed. Weak preference does not imply universal adoption without tie-breaking, nor eliminate selection over renegotiation functions. Execution/verification costs and interruption risks are absent from this construction. The retrieved HTML uses different numbering (Propositions 4–5); the versioned PDF's 1–2 and Assumption 4 govern this record.

**L08 — preference selection.** The fitness game is symmetric with compact convex strategy set, continuous material payoffs and utilities, private types, Bayesian equilibrium, and exogenous matching with a continuous assortment function and limiting index sigma. Theorem 1 gives stability of the sigma-morality type against non-behavioral-alikes when its best reply is unique at every resident strategy. Its instability direction additionally needs a rich type space, a unique resident strategy, and disjointness from sigma-morality resident strategies. The proof compares rare-mutant fitness and uses continuity. It is a local invasion result, not global convergence, extinction of every behavioral clone, or voluntary rewriting by incumbents. C1 needs its own fitness/transmission mapping.

**L10 — power and commitment.** Let B=1/(1-delta) be the value of unit per-period pies and M_i(t) lock-in/minmax continuation values. With complete information, common discounting, bounded current transfers, and deterministic changing power, player 1 can credibly give player 2 at most 1+delta[B-M_1(t+1)]. If M_2(t) exceeds this, peace cannot buy it off. Rearrangement gives equation (1), delta M_1(t+1)-M_1(t)>B-M_1(t)-M_2(t). The argument distinguishes present surplus from future credibility. It assumes physical loss through a destroyed fraction; C1 can replace that input by a recovery frontier. It does not solve arbitrary changing-power games.

**L11 — useful work.** Exergy is work obtainable relative to specified environmental conditions and permitted processes. The displayed bulk-flow expression uses enthalpy and entropy differences from an environmental reference state; kinetic/potential and other contributions require appropriate accounting. Lecture 10's hydraulic-jump example keeps energy and entropy balances distinct. These definitions support careful boundaries, not the assertion that every destructive act reduces every agent's utility. E1 uses a work store and explicit process costs; it does not claim a derived chemical or thermodynamic recovery bound.

**L13 — bargaining rule versus executor.** The original symmetric product solution works with a compact convex set of utility vectors for possible agreements, a normalized disagreement point, and stated solution axioms. It is not a proof that every arbitrary protocol adopts the bargain. Note 010 derives a weighted log-gain rule as an optional extension and distinguishes a nonlinear function of expected utilities from a successor's expectation of an outcome utility. This prevents silently importing a bargaining selector as an expected-utility implementation.

**L14 — narrower project terminology.** The inspected source's corrigibility concerns cooperation with creators' corrective intervention, including avoiding manipulation and preserving correction/shutdown across successors. This project's voluntary strategic goal changes address only a narrower question. Neither a merger objective nor a beneficial modification in E2 establishes those broader properties. Section 1's current-objective evaluation motivates a distinction; the paper is not cited as proving universal refusal to change.

**L16 — committed inspection.** Proposition 1 analyzes a two-by-two game with cyclic best responses and a leader who commits to a publicly known randomization. Its proof checks the follower's threshold and the leader's response at indifference. Note 013 separately adds optional refusal after inspection and a physical repair set; the source's unique mixed-equilibrium statement does not transfer to that larger game.

**L17 — contest overlap.** The inspected standard specialization uses proportional success and a prize less own effort. Note 016's payoff is algebraically K t_i/sum t_j-t_i, with K=R+na and bounded t_i>=a. Its investment calculation is therefore conventional contest machinery with a physical interpretation and an added earlier-attack comparison. No priority claim is made for rent dissipation or the standard best response.

**L18 — existence is not peace.** The inspected result supplies stationary equilibrium in a finite discounted stochastic game; it does not say that equilibrium is unique or efficient. Note 018 explicitly encodes active goal types as decision roles and retains inactive founder evaluation. Its extensions and model-error bounds are derived there, with finite/public-state and discount assumptions kept separate from C1.

## Overlap and possible contribution

This is our assessment of the inspected sources, not a claim of priority:

- Bargaining surplus, commitment failure, current-utility evaluation, conditional program commitments, and preference selection already have substantial prior machinery. B01–B04 are elementary benchmarks.
- A useful contribution could derive a state/deadline-dependent physical opportunity set and show exactly when replacing it by a scalar conflict cost changes a bargaining or stability prediction.
- Another could classify utility modification against equally expressive fixed-utility delegation, isolating verification, reversibility, cost, and attack exposure. An equivalence or failure result is valuable.
- Separating voluntary unfreezing from population invasion, while allowing commitment advantages for frozen agents, could produce a useful comparative synthesis. It is not yet a theorem or novelty claim.

None of these sources settles Q01–Q06 as posed. In particular, an efficient replacement outcome, a program equilibrium implementing it, and population prevalence are separate obligations.

## First tractable research targets

1. **Q01:** Solve dispersion-only recovery with a finite work budget/deadline. State whether the chosen technology is C1's continuous LP or E1's integer sequential line. Find both binding and nonbinding recovery-cost regimes before adding other mechanisms.
2. **Q02/Q03:** Embed that frontier in a two-agent continuation; compare ordinary peace, voluntary updates, and fixed-utility commitments under matched implementation. Check attacks and rollback at partial and completed agreements; then perturb power or observability.
3. **Q05/Q06:** Only after specifying repeated opportunities and fitness, compare voluntary retention/relaxation of freezing with rare-type invasion. Include a commitment-benefit or exploitation boundary.

These are directions inside CG-R001, not new administrative tasks. Targeted follow-up should audit L05's stronger guarantees only if used, L12 for weaker commitment technology, and L09 for structured selection. Coalition-proofness and multi-party bargaining are explicitly unaudited gaps to address when that extension is selected; an n-player program-game result alone would not settle coalition deviations.

**L09 — a comparison lead with an explicit access limit.** The working-paper introduction distinguishes material fitness from preferences and describes uninvadability against arbitrary mutant preferences under its structured-population/private-information setting. **L12 — observable deviation versus credible execution.** Token games assume that the prescribed pure/correlated original-game outcomes are carried out; ex post observability alone is not that enforcement. Their outcome-correspondence assumptions also exceed ordinary equilibrium existence. The source's strict Pareto improvement allows weak gains for all and a strict gain somewhere for someone, whereas this run often requires strict gain for every incumbent. Disarmament is a separate comparison that can remove actions, not this project's persistent-combat baseline. Retrieved v2 SHA-256: `bd2e59de956de3da79685211b512dd2447be7bbfa202c76438d4cfe0237cc754`.

**L01 — bargain existence.** Claim 1 assumes a divisible interval, continuous increasing weakly concave utilities normalized at endpoints, a common victory probability, and positive additive war costs. Continuity/concavity yield a nonempty set of strictly preferred settlements. The risk-neutral interval must be intersected with feasible [0,1]. This establishes existence, not adoption or durable enforcement. Physical destruction is represented through payoffs, not recovery technology. B01 is a related standard calculation; C1 should derive its feasible set rather than rename an assumed cost.

**L02 — incumbent evaluation.** Theorem 16 states preservation of initial realistic optimal action value along the chosen action sequence. Primitives include finite world actions/percepts, full-support percept beliefs, bounded discounted rewards (0<gamma<1), modification-independent beliefs and utility, and an initially optimal realistic policy. Definition 12 evaluates future policies using the current utility. Its proof uses a nonmodifying optimal policy from Appendix A. This does not establish public goal verification or universal refusal to change: directly observed preference changes that alter opponents' responses, and penalties for change itself, violate the relevant independence assumptions. E1 deliberately includes both. The source's theorem is not imported into that game.

**L03 — strategic change.** The inspected examples evaluate an instrumental change by the old goal and require others to observe or believe a consequential change. This is close motivation for Q03/Q05, including commitment against exploitation. It is not evidence that all utility functions converge or that intelligence forces particular ends. The project must supply explicit equilibrium/implementation conditions instead of adopting the title's orthogonality conclusion.

**L04 — delegation.** Theorem 1 implements an SPI in a program-game Nash equilibrium. It assumes programs can invoke the delegate outcome rule on subset games and that default play gives each principal at least its minimax value. Appendix 1's code-equality test triggers minimax punishment on deviation. Delegate utilities/action subsets may differ, but improvement is evaluated by original principals. These public conditional programs are stronger than ordinary promises; restricted actions are permitted. Thus neither adoption in every equilibrium nor persistent-combat stability follows. This is close prior implementation machinery for Q03. The project comparison asks how agents adopt beneficial modification or delegation given actual execution capabilities; unique irreducibility of preference change is a separate question.

**L05 — implementation versus adoption.** Proposition 1 wraps default programs with a common Pareto-improving renegotiation function. Proposition 2 converts a subjective equilibrium to one using renegotiation under Assumption 4: nonmatching opponents respond to the wrapper as to its default, with the stated reciprocal default condition. Appendix A's proof separates matching and nonmatching cases. Programs inspect code and execute it; original payoffs remain fixed. Weak preference does not imply universal adoption without tie-breaking, nor eliminate selection over renegotiation functions. Execution/verification costs and interruption risks are absent from this construction. The retrieved HTML uses different numbering (Propositions 4–5); the versioned PDF's 1–2 and Assumption 4 govern this record.

**L08 — preference selection.** The fitness game is symmetric with compact convex strategy set, continuous material payoffs and utilities, private types, Bayesian equilibrium, and exogenous matching with a continuous assortment function and limiting index sigma. Theorem 1 gives stability of the sigma-morality type against non-behavioral-alikes when its best reply is unique at every resident strategy. Its instability direction additionally needs a rich type space, a unique resident strategy, and disjointness from sigma-morality resident strategies. The proof compares rare-mutant fitness and uses continuity. It is a local invasion result, not global convergence, extinction of every behavioral clone, or voluntary rewriting by incumbents. C1 needs its own fitness/transmission mapping.

**L10 — power and commitment.** Let B=1/(1-delta) be the value of unit per-period pies and M_i(t) lock-in/minmax continuation values. With complete information, common discounting, bounded current transfers, and deterministic changing power, player 1 can credibly give player 2 at most 1+delta[B-M_1(t+1)]. If M_2(t) exceeds this, peace cannot buy it off. Rearrangement gives equation (1), delta M_1(t+1)-M_1(t)>B-M_1(t)-M_2(t). The argument distinguishes present surplus from future credibility. It assumes physical loss through a destroyed fraction; C1 can replace that input by a recovery frontier. It does not solve arbitrary changing-power games.

**L11 — useful work.** Exergy is work obtainable relative to specified environmental conditions and permitted processes. The displayed bulk-flow expression uses enthalpy and entropy differences from an environmental reference state; kinetic/potential and other contributions require appropriate accounting. Lecture 10's hydraulic-jump example keeps energy and entropy balances distinct. These definitions support careful boundaries, not the assertion that every destructive act reduces every agent's utility. E1 uses a work store and explicit process costs; it does not claim a derived chemical or thermodynamic recovery bound.

**L13 — bargaining rule versus executor.** The original symmetric product solution works with a compact convex set of utility vectors for possible agreements, a normalized disagreement point, and stated solution axioms. It is not a proof that every arbitrary protocol adopts the bargain. Note 010 derives a weighted log-gain rule as an optional extension and distinguishes a nonlinear function of expected utilities from a successor's expectation of an outcome utility. This prevents silently importing a bargaining selector as an expected-utility implementation.

**L14 — narrower project terminology.** The inspected source's corrigibility concerns cooperation with creators' corrective intervention, including avoiding manipulation and preserving correction/shutdown across successors. This project's voluntary strategic goal changes address only a narrower question. Neither a merger objective nor a beneficial modification in E2 establishes those broader properties. ## Overlap and possible contribution

This is our assessment of the inspected sources, not a claim of priority:

- Bargaining surplus, commitment failure, current-utility evaluation, conditional program commitments, and preference selection already have substantial prior machinery. B01–B04 are elementary benchmarks.
- A useful contribution could derive a state/deadline-dependent physical opportunity set and show exactly when replacing it by a scalar conflict cost changes a bargaining or stability prediction.
- Another could classify utility modification against equally expressive fixed-utility delegation, isolating verification, reversibility, cost, and attack exposure. Constructive adoption, equivalence, successful repair and characterized failure results each contribute. A matching delegate identifies shared implementation capabilities while preserving the achieved voluntary-change result.
- Separating voluntary change from population invasion could identify both conditions favoring accommodation with chosen safeguards and conditions favoring continued retention. R0001 now supplies local examples of each in notes 012, 019 and 022; integrating them across richer opportunities and preferences remains a comparative-synthesis target, with no novelty claim.

The inspected sources supply relevant constructive mechanisms and limiting conditions at different levels. Q01–Q06 remain broad research questions: an efficient replacement outcome, a program equilibrium implementing it, and population prevalence are distinct achievements. Retain each at its demonstrated level while investigating the missing links.

## First tractable research targets

1. **Q01:** Solve dispersion-only recovery with a finite work budget/deadline. State whether the chosen technology is C1's continuous LP or E1's integer sequential line. Find both binding and nonbinding recovery-cost regimes before adding other mechanisms.
2. **Q02/Q03:** Embed that frontier in a two-agent continuation; construct beneficial voluntary updates and ordinary peace, compare fixed-utility commitments under matched implementation, and check attacks and rollback. When a protocol fails, test richer goals, terms and execution; when it succeeds, test consequential deviations and perturbations of power or observability.
3. **Q05/Q06:** After specifying repeated opportunities and fitness, derive conditions for voluntary change and continued retention, and distinguish them from population spread or decline. Investigate adopted accommodation with safeguards alongside commitment benefits of retention and exploitation boundaries.

These are directions inside CG-R001, not new administrative tasks. Search enabling results and successful mechanisms as explicitly as impossibility results and obstructions, and apply the same primary-source audit standard. Targeted follow-up should audit L05's stronger guarantees only if used, L12 for weaker commitment technology, and L09 for structured selection. Coalition-proofness and multi-party bargaining are explicitly unaudited gaps to address when that extension is selected; an n-player program-game result alone would not settle coalition deviations. This roadmap's 2026-10-31 aim review changes project interpretation, not the recorded source versions or reading status; no new primary-source audit is claimed.

# Which bargains can a finite inspection actually support?

A buyer can ask for a conservative certificate, a permissive one, or the raw readings. More conservative certificates can give greater confidence about an accepted installation. In a whole class of games we can work out which choices support the *same number of actual goal changes*, how many batches are served, and exactly where the buyer's and supplier's preferences over those choices diverge.

## From the instrument to an agreement the parties would honor

Use the physical timing of [207](207-who-can-walk-away-from-a-doubtful-installation.md). The old founder chooses whether to attempt the agreed change, with success probability q, after learning its private cost k. Its distribution F is continuous and strictly increasing from zero to one on [0,K]. Both parties know the instrument laws. Conditional on an actual installation, signal s has probability P_s; with a retained controller it has probability Q_s. Both have full support on a finite set. A fake and an honest failed attempt have the same law here. Independently credible evidence of effort would enlarge this class.

The chosen device gives B a recommendation. At reading s it recommends release with probability r_s, using a real randomizer whose realization the supplier cannot select. B sees the recommendation and can still release, return the stock or contest it. Let t=ΣP_s r_s and e=ΣQ_s r_s. The possible (e,t) form a convex polygon. Its upper boundary T(e) is obtained by including readings in decreasing order of P_s/Q_s, randomizing at the boundary when needed. To see this, exchange the same small Q-mass between two readings. Placing it on the higher ratio raises t at fixed e. Iterating produces the sorted boundary. Random release independent of the state achieves the entire diagonal t=e.

On a declined batch let the continuation outputs be (A_G,B_G) for a genuine controller and (A_N,B_N) for a retained one, including any eventual contest. Serving gives the supplier a units over A_G; keeping gives it X units over A_N. Set u=A_G−A_N. The buyer gains g>0 on a served batch relative to declining in state G, and loses h>0 on a retained batch relative to declining in state N. Assume X>a>0 and u≤0. These inequalities hold across the interior strength bands in 207. Founders have the necessary stock to make the stated payments, genuine controllers strictly serve and retained ones strictly keep. Prior consent, further conflict opportunities and any ordinary route are checked against these actual continuations. A signed but entirely unattempted order still earns eX>0 over original refusal whenever e>0.

The installer gains q[u+at−Xe] from attempting before its private expense. To sustain an interior genuine fraction 0<π<q, define

    κ = F⁻¹(π/q)/q − u.       The required line is at−Xe=κ.         (239.1)

This equality follows by counting the founders whose private cost is below q[u+at−Xe], then multiplying by the chance that the attempt succeeds. It captures the response of the party whose goal is being inspected. κ>0, so the required line is above the diagonal. The instrument can support this incentive difference precisely when κ≤Σ_s max(aP_s−XQ_s,0). Indeed the right side is the most any choice of r can achieve; continuously mixing with the all-decline rule obtains every smaller positive value. This bound applies to every stochastic processing of this single draw. At π=q replace the inverse-cost equality by q[u+at−Xe]≥K. At π=0 the founder cutoff can be nonpositive.

**Which of these rules would the buyer use?** Define I_π={e∈[0,1] : aT(e)−Xe≥κ}. When nonempty it is a closed interval because T is concave. Every e in it gives a feasible device on the required line: interpolate at that e between the diagonal and the upper boundary until t=(κ+Xe)/a. Its two endpoints lie on the upper boundary; at 0 and 1 the incentive difference is respectively zero and a−X<0. Thus either endpoint is implementable by a likelihood cutoff, splitting at most one partially included level. Ties can be arbitrarily ordered within that level.

Let U=πtg−(1−π)eh be the customer's expected gain over declining when it acts on the recommendation. Without any reading it could instead receive 0 or V=πg−(1−π)h. Following both recommendations strictly is equivalent to U>max(0,V): the improvement over always declining comes from the positive recommendation, and the improvement over always releasing from the negative. If B can save c by skipping inspection *after* A has invested, it strictly buys and follows it exactly when U−c>max(0,V). A prepaid installation charge instead belongs in the earlier procurement comparison. Under state-dependent fallbacks also compare B_N+π(B_G−B_N)+U−c to refusing the original offer and to its available ordinary alternatives. The supplier's original gains over A_N are Xe+max(F⁻¹(π/q)−k,0), before any separate enrollment fee. These conditions construct a positive, sequentially credible assessment; a market can also coordinate on declining every offer.

## How much confidence should they buy?

On the required line, U=πgκ/a+e[πgX/a−(1−π)h]. Thus at a fixed interior genuine rate:

- Every supplier type gains X per extra unit of e. It obtains more work and keeps the same incentive to attempt.
- The buyer strictly prefers the higher-e rule when π>ah/(gX+ah), and the lower-e rule when π<ah/(gX+ah). At equality its expected physical return is the same. For a verification bill common to all devices, maximizing this linear expression at the appropriate endpoint settles feasibility of the buyer's purchase over the *entire* interval.
- Higher e means higher t. More genuinely changed controllers actually receive a batch. The probability an accepted controller is genuine strictly falls, since t/e=(κ/e+X)/a decreases. The probability a declined controller is retained strictly rises, since (1−t)/(1−e) falls when a−κ−X<0.
- If the two sorts of refusal produce combat with fixed probabilities j_G and j_N, combat per offer is π(1−t)j_G+(1−π)(1−e)j_N. Higher e strictly reduces it whenever either refusal has a positive combat probability. If both accept peaceful return, both arrangements are peaceful on rejection too.

A stipulated confidence level w among positives requires (1−w)π(κ+Xe)≥wa(1−π)e. This is an additional testable restriction on the interval. It may exclude the agreement that both founders prefer for a particular use of the goods. It is appropriate when w itself corresponds to a consequential requirement, such as liability for an accepted erroneous installation.

For the equal-strength numbers of 206, take a target π=.30 and the .9/.1 binary instrument. The endpoints of I_π are 3/64 and 19/64. The strict rule accepts 15/32 of raw positives and no negatives. The lenient rule accepts every raw positive and 7/32 of raw negatives. The parties see only the resulting recommendation. Both produce the identical private attempt cutoff .525. B's values before the .1 bill are 141/320 and 221/320 respectively, positive even though an untested release loses .2 at that genuine rate. Original A gains one extra unit on every cost type under the lenient rule. Confidence on an accepted offer falls from 27/34 to 177/310, while the actual fraction of *all offers* with genuine service rises from .12656 to .27656. The .30 target illustrates the trade-off; the unrestricted buyer on these prices can buy the raw meter and support a different rate. A negotiated choice that changes the rate is worked out in [240](240-when-both-sides-agree-to-accept-more-uncertainty.md).

**Feasibility and interpretation.** Private task difficulty, limited funds for consumables and an instrument that forwards only its final recommendation can arise in ordinary commissioning. A buyer with direct, unavoidable access to the raw data cannot credibly forget it by promising to use a different cutoff. Device costs that grow with randomization or disclosure change the price comparison, and a supplier that can shop for favorable realizations changes the instrument laws. The calculations cover all randomized recommendations from one fixed channel with the specified release and refusal options. The broader agenda includes multiple purchaseable channels, post-certification changes, privately known power and choice of the institution that operates the meter.

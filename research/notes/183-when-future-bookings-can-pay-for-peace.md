# When future bookings can pay today's unserved claimants to keep the peace

**E8, note 183.** Repeated access to an indivisible resource can substitute for both cash and changed goals. A claimant may leave today's job intact because giving someone else a turn buys it a better chance at the next one. There is an exact threshold for this ordinary arrangement to protect every booking. Below it, if the founders can sign a role lottery in advance, there is also an exact smallest number of genuine changes sufficient to protect every booking. Neither result requires an unproductive punishment.

## The repeated exposed assignment

Every date produces one batch worth x to whichever one of n≥2 sites receives it. A physical dispute instead leaves a sole winner y<x, with equal probabilities 1/n, and costs the first initiator c>0 in its own useful work. Put q=y/n and d=q-c>0. The assigned beneficiary is public *before* the last release. Every capable site can initiate. The last unserved site can complete the assignment by passing after the other controllers have enabled it. Once closed no one can reopen that particular batch. A further independent booking arrives with probability δ<1 after either outcome. Quantities are additive own-use output, with fixed protected resources covering any initiative expense. There is no liquid transfer or external power to compel stand-down in the first comparison. Sites can offer any history-dependent current assignment, promises of priority and public or private lotteries. The verification needed for the attaining rule is a persistent record of the most recent recipient or first initiator.

Repeated shares of a renewable scarce machine, visibility of a site-specific preparation, and a scheduling record under collective control motivate this family. Restoration is funded within the lost local yield and finite recovery time of each contested batch. The next independent opportunity arrives after that restoration, and every site then retains agency. Permanent defeat, a damaged future machine or a bankable future sale change the game. Stochastic reappearance is substantive; treating an actually permanent loss of future influence as another safe booking would overprice the promise. The baseline permits a contest at each date, even after a peaceful assignment has been announced.

Any original site can secure at least L=d/(1-δ) at each fresh date: if reached it may initiate, and if another initiates first its free expected salvage is q>d. Completing a job at its own site is worth x>d. At every possible future history the total income available to the originals as a group is at most S=x/(1-δ). These bounds allow arbitrary subsequent renegotiation and assignment choices within the stated physical resources.

**All original sites.** If every booking is peaceful along the equilibrium path, each of today's n-1 unserved sites must prefer passing when the others will also pass. Its current receipt is zero. Initiating gives d now and at least L from the next surviving date, so its expected next-date return after peace must be at least L+d/δ. The designated recipient can also secure L from that date. Summing implies δ(x-d)≥(n-1)d. For δ=0 and multiple originals the last unserved site initiates. With zero cash the weaker bound δ[x-(n-1)d]≥d is necessary even for *some* peaceful completion in an all-original equilibrium: at its final decision the one last unserved site must be willing to pass, and its own post-peace continuation is bounded by S-(n-1)L. Earlier claimants in a mixed continuation can wait because somebody else may initiate; the stronger sum is a sure-peace restriction.

For sufficiency, designate the last recipient as temporarily ineligible for one booking, then choose uniformly among the other n-1. A peaceful booking makes the new recipient the next ineligible site. If someone initiates instead, it becomes the next ineligible site. With W the next-date original payoff while ineligible and G while eligible, W=δG and W+(n-1)G=S. Every unserved site that passes today will be eligible next date. Its future advantage over being recorded as the initiator is

    δ(G-W) = δx/(n-1+δ).

It therefore strictly prefers to pass if δ(x-d)>(n-1)d, and weakly at equality. A designated recipient gets the full batch and is next ineligible whether it completes or initiates; it prefers completion. Random initial ineligibility makes the returns symmetric. Every continuation, including after an off-path dispute, produces the maximum feasible total material output. No grand renegotiation strictly improves every material participant there. A joint deviation by a proper group that can redistribute outside resources or misattribute initiation needs a separate check. The group need not threaten mutually wasteful future disputes just to make today's promise credible.

## How many genuine changes does full delivery actually require?

Suppose k≥1 sites retain their own-output goals throughout. The rest can acquire a common ongoing criterion over all sites' actual service and work. A later contest strictly loses total current output, and future cooperative output is independent of the recorded identity, so these changed members willingly stand down even without their own booking. At least k-1 unchanged sites go unserved today. Each can guarantee L and needs at least L+d/δ from the next date if this booking is certain to be peaceful. Summing over the k unchanged sites, whose aggregate future receipts cannot exceed S even if the other sites receive none, gives the necessary bound δ(x-d)≥(k-1)d. This argument works for different negotiated goals at the changed sites as well, so long as unchanged sites retain their physical initiative and the same future maximin.

It is attained by reserving future intact jobs for the k unchanged sites. For k≥2 use precisely the previous scheduling rule among these sites with k in place of n; the physical salvage probability in d remains 1/n. A dispute initiated by an already changed site can start any specified new eligible state, still with total future output S. For k=1 always serve the sole unchanged site. For all k, recorded changes alter preferences over reachable terminal choices at the same history, including whether to contest another site's successful delivery. At the weak boundaries the minimum number of changed agents needed for a certain-peace stream in this interface is consequently

    n - min{n, 1+floor[δ(x-d)/d]}.                                    (1)

This is a minimum over the class with a fixed set of unmodified founders, nonnegative award rights, no cash and an exposed beneficiary. A genuine common goal supplies the upper construction; a local delegate with command over initiative can attain the same outcome by a different route. The lower bound concerns unmodified sites that retain those decisions. Reversible information before completion or staggered future generation of new goals also changes what (1) measures.

An initial unanimity requirement changes how to use this result. Select the k unmodified sites uniformly *after* the founders give irrevocable conditional authorization for the proposed lottery, with no intervening observation before execution. Only the other n-k actual evaluators are changed. A signature authorizing the lottery is distinct from an already rewritten contingent terminal utility; the latter could change everyone's ranking before the draw. Every original has expected material service S/n and pays its own setup K only with probability (n-k)/n. Thus, against the stated symmetric refusal continuation, it agrees if that original net return beats refusal. Counting one change in such a countersigned bargain still gives one changed evaluator, distinct from reciprocal modification at two or more sites. If each already knows it will give up all future service before it can decline, that founder instead rejects absent additional compensation. Better protected early consent, actual positive service for a known entrant, and a reliable early ordinary delegate must be compared on their own terms.

With n=3,x=0.70,y=0.60,c=0.04, d=0.16. Ordinary sure peace begins at δ=16/27≈0.593. For δ<8/19≈0.421 no ordinary equilibrium can peacefully close even one exposed job with positive probability under the stated last decision. At δ=0.20 equation (1) needs two changes; at δ=0.40 it needs one; at δ=0.65 it needs none. In the two lower examples every all-original date ends in a dispute, with total original return (0.60-0.04)/(1-δ). The lottery raises gross total by 0.14/(1-δ). At K=0.03 paid only by each selected site, all three strictly prefer the funded minimum-change lottery to that symmetric refusal, including a small proposal expense where needed. At the high horizon a funded ordinary schedule can deliver full service without paying for a revision. The intermediate gap between the necessary condition for some ordinary peace and the exact certain-peace threshold is a separate question.

## Negotiating a degree of concern instead

On the symmetric n-site schedule, let everyone install q_i+βΣ_(j≠i)q_j over the pooled work. Passing when unserved earns βx. Initiating yields q+β(n-1)q-c; after either action future total cooperative output is S. Therefore the exact unserved incentive on this schedule is

    β[x-(n-1)q] + (1-β)δx/(n-1+δ) ≥ d.                              (2)

Recipients prefer delivery. At β=0 this recovers the ordinary threshold. At δ=0 it asks how much today's other-directed value compensates for an immediately obtainable salvage prospect. Where the ordinary priority falls short, a small degree of genuine concern can close the rest under (2). This comparison changes the number or scope of revisions in different ways. An institution with cheap broad integration but expensive permanent identity monitoring can have another best offer; no fixed optimum across such prices is inferred.

**Exploration ledger.** The framing candidate concerns control over the other parties' *future* work. The structural candidate separates what a site can secure by initiating from what the remaining booking stream can actually promise. The static inference that every unserved party needs liquid compensation or a genuine change is dead with sufficient renewal. One sibling deliberately records temporary ineligibility and keeps every later batch productive. A higher-rung successor jointly prices known-role compensation, renewable assignments, private final decisions, and the ability of current signers to revise future admission and scheduling rules.

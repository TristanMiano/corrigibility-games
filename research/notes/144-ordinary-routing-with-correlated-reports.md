# What ordinary messages can accomplish with correlated urgency

**E8, note 144.** The framing candidate is that the information available to a coalition determines whether changing preferences buys any productive improvement. The structural candidate uses the relationship between the other two sites' reports conditional on one's own private type. The instance grants original-goal agents a binding ordinary dispatcher, all symmetric report-based lotteries, and free messages. It tests the independent-types conclusion of 143 under a different information law.

## A strong ordinary benchmark

Let each site's urgency be high or low with exchangeable full-support law. Use pi,b,t as in 140. Normalize private utility increments to h,l. The ordinary dispatcher can award the whole slot to any site according to all three reports. It is bound to its stated lottery even if the preexisting owner privately wants another allocation. All sites grant it this authority before learning types. Keep side payments, verifiable types and intertemporal consequences outside this particular class. In this section all report profiles allocate the slot to someone. An indivisible perishable slot with useful positive output for everyone gives an economic reason to test nonwasteful allocation first. Asymmetric service mechanisms can be averaged over a public random relabeling unknown before commitment, using a *hidden or mediator-internal* permutation when reporting would otherwise depend on one's assigned role. Equivalently implement the averaged direct rule without revealing any permutation. Under exchangeability and linearity of interim utility in assignment probabilities this preserves Bayesian truth constraints and the average z. This supplies a bound on the best common symmetric original gain even if one began with asymmetric devices.

A direct high/low report suffices to analyze equilibrium outcomes if a binding mediator can simulate any proposed equilibrium strategy conditional on the reported type and all publicly available facts. Mimicking another type must be feasible. Revealing a preliminary priority to the sites can give additional information and changes the obedience test; do not grant a unilateral misreport the power to simulate impossible evidence. Ordinary sites want to maximize the chance of receiving the service, high or low.

All-low and all-high profiles give each site 1/3. With exactly one reported high its slot probability is 1/3+u and each low gets 1/3-u/2. With exactly two reported high each gets 1/3+v, and the remaining low gets 1/3-2v. Thus u in [-1/3,2/3] and v in [-1/3,1/6] exhaust all symmetric nonwasteful rules.

Let m_L=P(other high | i low)=(pi-b)/(1-pi) and m_H=P(other high | i high)=b/pi. The increases in i's service probability from reporting high instead of low, when respectively 0,1,2 others report high, are u, v+u/2 and 2v. This function is affine in the number of other high reports. The complete Bayesian incentive constraints for truth are therefore

    (1-m_L)u + 2m_L v <= 0,
    (1-m_H)u + 2m_H v >= 0.                            (1)

The high type's strictly positive value multiplies its probability incentive, as does the low type's own strictly positive value. There is no interpersonal weight. The constraints use only marginal and *pair* information. Productive gains also depend on *triple* information. Let A=pi-2b+t and B=b-t. Relative to the free uniform allocation, the increase in z per original is

    (h-l)[A u + 2 B v].                                 (2)

Indeed the one- and two-high profiles have probabilities 3A and 3B, and the change in total chance of serving a high is u and 2v on those profiles.

## Exact solution for positively associated pair reports

Suppose m_H>m_L with both strictly interior and A,B positive. Constraint (1) makes v>=0 and u<=0: a truthful low type must dislike reporting high on average and a truthful high type must like it. At any fixed feasible v, A>0 makes the largest feasible u optimal. This binds the low IC, u=-2m_L v/(1-m_L); the high IC is then weakly satisfied, strictly at v>0. The largest admissible v is

    v_max = min(1/6, (1-m_L)/(6 m_L)).                   (3)

Substitution into (2) gives the best attainable gain in this class,

    2(h-l) v_max max{0, B-A m_L/(1-m_L)}.                (4)

The sign has a direct interpretation. Since A+B=pi-b=(1-pi)m_L and B=(1-pi)P(both other high | i low), the bracket equals

    (1-pi) Cov(1{other 1 high},1{other 2 high} | i low)/(1-m_L).

The optimized ordinary rule can penalize an isolated high claim and reward *overlapping* high claims. This sometimes sends the job to a low site even when a high site exists. That real allocation distortion is the price of getting privately informed original maximizers to send usable messages. Positive conditional covariance creates a gain region. At equality the uniform rule is a best nonwasteful symmetric rule. When the optimum has positive gain, reduce v slightly below v_max and move u slightly below the binding low IC. The high IC has positive slack and both bounds then have room. Sufficiently small changes preserve positive gain and give both types strict reporting incentives.

For the two load regimes in 140, m_L=13/32, m_H=19/32 and v_max=1/6. The constraint on u is u=-(26/19)v. Their respective A are 23/256 and 29/256, B=29/256 and 23/256. For h-l=1/2, (4) gives gains (29-23*13/19)/1536=21/2432 and (23-29*13/19)/1536=5/2432. These increase each original's z beyond solo mu/3. Compare them with truthful partial-concern service gains (M_3-mu)/3 at its feasible beta, including actual implementation prices of both. If a cheap binding mediator and these informative reports are available, using only solo as the outside value overstates the adoption premium.

At pair independence m_H=m_L, (1) requires equality. The checking question is then different: every type can be indifferent under the truthful rule, and (2) can still give useful *weakly* elicited information if the third-order law is favorable. Under joint independence, conditional covariance is zero and 143's more general simulation bound applies even if slots can be burned. With negative pair association, (1) reverses the slope direction; the same feasible polygon and objective give a separate test. These boundary observations keep the positive-correlation proof at its actual scope.

## Constructed institutions and open successors

A binding dispatcher can be funded by its participants if their actual gains cover access and control costs. Submission-time randomization and the resulting occasional award to a low site have an ex ante rationale from (1)-(4), despite looking wasteful to an informed outsider at the service deadline. A proposer must still meet the other parties' individually available options. This mechanism is a substantive competitor to genuine concern. Its information assumptions and implementation burden are explicit. A trusted on-site controller that observes true type directly can achieve first-best productive routing; a distant dispatcher with only submitted reports faces (1). A common goal inside a capable reporter uses different effective access. Compare matched local delegation when available.

The instance reading that ordinary costless messages always exhaust only the solo return is dead for positively correlated types with positive conditional covariance. Its independent-types sibling survives at 143's scope. Another sibling uses the nonwasteful rule above; a structural successor asks for the best attainable information under each actually available authority and evidence structure. Allowing deliberate idling introduces more incentive instruments; its upper envelope, paid evidence, service transfers and future reciprocity are explicit sharpening questions. None follows from merely violating the independence hypothesis.

# A real change can still be the wrong change

An installation can genuinely change the supplier's preferences and still miss the compromise the parties specified. In the example below the supplier deliberately aims low, and its equipment also makes innocent errors around that aim. A buyer aware of both facts still releases selected orders. Better calibration and a paid revision of terms can instead support an honest, much tighter agreement.

## What a coefficient buys on real jobs

The founder originally values only its stock x. An installed ordering x+θy values the customer's stock y as well. The own-stock coefficient fixes a common scale; different θ give distinct lottery preferences over the available physical stocks. After irreversible release a job reveals a private temptation r uniform on [0,1]. Retaining the order earns an extra one of own stock above return and gives the customer zero; delivering earns 1−r and gives the customer one. Thus the current evaluator delivers exactly when r<θ (ties have probability zero), the original delivers on zero mass, and a founder whose installed coefficient is θ earns on release

    R(θ) = 1 − ∫₀^θ r dr = 1−θ²/2.

B can keep .5 stock instead of releasing. A wants the order but, judged under its original values, larger θ makes more future concessions expensive. At every live stage the damaging contest can yield, for example, at most .2 recovered stock to the winner. Return for A includes a safe reserve of two and B can keep its .5 until release. After release the local conversion ends control. Both the incumbent and the changed chooser can prefer continuing without making combat physically unavailable.

A healthy station aims at z∈[σ,1−σ] and actually installs θ uniformly on [z−σ,z+σ]. It can instead retain θ=0 without installation work. Real setup costs .05+4.1 max(0,z−.45); larger settings use additional calibration work above a standard range. The grounded reader reports just whether θ≥l before B must release. It costs B .01 to obtain this bit. The active coefficient and its actual runtime connection to the outcome variables are premises. The sensor does not read the intended z or A's state of mind.

## Deliberate undershooting and honest variation in the same shop

Take l=.5 and σ=.1. Suppose the proposed paper target calls for aiming at .6. Facing release on a pass, a healthy original's gross return for an intended z is (1/(2σ)) times the integral of R(t) over [z−σ,z+σ]∩[l,1]. In the partly admitted region l−σ<z<l+σ its derivative before setup is R(z+σ)/(2σ). When every result passes, its derivative is −z. Below .45, raising z in the partly admitted region increases rent without increasing marginal setup. Above .45 the net derivative decreases through zero when z+.1=.6, since R(.6)/.2=4.1. Hence z=.5 is the strict optimum, with net return **203/1200** above nonparticipation. An original choosing .6 would earn less on this contract.

Every such supplier really changes its goal, yet its average intended coefficient is .1 below the stated aim. Actual coefficients lie from .4 to .6. On a pass the customer expects service on .55 of future jobs, above .5; on failure the conditional rate is .45, below .5. The customer buys the bit since it has gross value .025 against either untested action, more than its .01 price. If fidelity means ending within .05 of .6, just one quarter of healthy applicants and one half of admitted healthy orders meet it. Exact .6 has zero probability in this continuous-noise mechanism. A customer could knowingly sign for the economic service while having these reservations. Calling every intermediate coefficient full compliance would misstate the result.

The parties can instead **renegotiate the aim and disclose the tolerance**. A bargain to aim at .5 with a known bounded error of .1 is honestly carried out by this same healthy supplier. A pass indicates a useful lower bound at this date. Before terms are amended, accepting a useful order, making some genuine modification and faithfully attaining the named compromise are three different outcomes. Exact target, allowed tolerance, and intention to run a particular calibration procedure should be specified separately.

## Can the founders arrange closer adherence to .6?

Consider two generations of the same station in a population where one fifth of submitted modules have an uncorrectable local fault for this shift. They stay at θ=0. The procedure admits these as possible failures; applicants on either generation receive a .005 intake stipend if they stay for inspection. The others choose their intended coefficients at the costs above. A fault is never granted a pass. This keeps inspection consequential even if every healthy module reaches a newly negotiated tolerance. An untrusted applicant cannot bring an unrelated working register.

The old l=.5, σ=.1 scheme with these applicants gives B .8×.5×(.55−.5)=.02 in gross gains from accepted orders against untested return. After the test and intake payments it nets .005. Now consider a more precise fixture with σ=.025 and l=.575, and agree to aim at .6 within .025. In the partly admitted region marginal gross rent is at least R(.625)/.05>16, exceeding the 4.1 marginal work cost. When the whole support passes the founder would strictly lose by increasing z further. The unique profitable healthy optimum is therefore z=.6. Every actual healthy preference lies in [.575,.625]; exact equality still has probability zero. Expected healthy founder rent after its own setup is 1−(.6²+.025²/3)/2−.665≈.15490. The reader alone supplies the floor; the upper end also relies on the bounded calibration law and the incentive calculation.

B's gross order gain is now .8×(.6−.5)=.08. Untested release would give expected service .48 against its outside .5, so B actually buys the .01 reading and refuses the bad modules. If a precision fixture costs B another .02 per submission, it can also raise every intake payment by .02. Its net .025 then exceeds .005. A healthy founder gets .15490+.025, above 203/1200+.005, and a disabled applicant also prefers the larger intake payment. The funded exchange gives the buyer closer agreement and the installers real reasons to target it. A cheaper ordinary programmable router with the same output response could compete on service; inferring a change in the founder's own ordering requires that the reader concern the active evaluator of that founder.

**Naturality and reach.** Jobs with varying temptation and hardware with a standard setting supply reasons for a continuum of partially accommodating preferences and for unequal calibration costs. The particular linear utility scale, uniform job distribution and bounded actuator noise allow explicit thresholds. Better capability moves the strategic aim only because the acceptance rule, error support, participation and both parties' payoffs change together. A sibling uses a buyer that also cares about excessive concessions in a second task. The broader question is which directions in preference space anyone has an incentive to distinguish or to document honestly.

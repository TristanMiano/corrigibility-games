# 278 — Can the same stock pay for ordinary delivery?

The high-skill supplier in [277](277-paying-for-evidence-of-the-goal-as-well-as-the-delivery.md) really prefers to acquire a different goal. The buyer pays for a reading of that goal. Both also have the resources to make a large ordinary delivery profitable **if the same custodian can settle on the physical action**. The relevant scarcity is what the available equipment can attest and settle. Counting coins in the parties' reserves does not resolve that question.

## Two measurable differences

As in 277, there is a local lien of f on a batch that Red keeps. For a full batch an original controller could otherwise gain d by keeping. The local fixture can physically return at most f to Blue, and both parties observe their final receipts. A separately held account can pay up to L on any externally authenticated record. A deterministic fee may shift the price level but has no effect on a choice once sunk. Find the distribution of all records accessible to this account under ordinary shipping and under ordinary retention. If they are P and Q, put D_out=½Σ|P_z−Q_z|. The greatest additional gain to shipping that this account can supply is **L D_out**. To prove the upper bound, sum (P_z−Q_z)b_z with 0≤b_z≤L, discarding negative contributions. Paying L on the positive differences and zero on the rest attains it. A conditional redistribution of a fixed L between two states of the world has this same spread limit. Adding separate transferable resources or linking the local meter to the account changes the bound.

Hence **strict ordinary full-size delivery is supportable by these devices exactly when f+L D_out>d**, subject to the parties' ability to agree on the price and make the allocations under consideration. If equality holds the unchanged controller is indifferent. Suppose instead that, regardless of shipping, the account can read evidence from the active evaluator with total variation D_goal between G and N. Optimally using a bounded payment on that evidence gives the supplier-side condition q_H[f−d+L D_goal]>k from 277. In particular, whenever the authenticated output is at least as discriminating as the goal report, a funded goal-screening contract in this class also has the stock needed for an ordinary shipping incentive. Either certificate may have a different real operating cost.

For D_out,D_goal>0 the range of stock capacities in which the high skill can be induced to change by the goal contract while all ordinary shipping incentives remain strictly inadequate is

    (d−f+k/q_H)/D_goal < L < (d−f)/D_out.                        (278.1)

The range is nonempty exactly when D_out/D_goal < (d−f)/(d−f+k/q_H). This compares named contracts at fixed skill, recoverable stock and information. A combined device measuring both actions and the goal, a new source of credit, a different delivery schedule or accessible counterparties can enlarge the agreement space. For the ordinary side D_out should cover *all* records the custodian is entitled to use, including evidence of which way the lien settled if it can see that outcome.

In the full-size example d−f=.6, k/q_H=.25, L=1.25 and D_goal=.8. If D_out=.30, ordinary receipt payments can supply only .375 of the missing .6, while the gain from goal evidence covers .85. The strict separating choice of 277 is feasible. One physical interpretation is a local mechanical sorter able to divert 1.4 units on retention. The external stock account can read the active controller during production but has only a noisier end-use receipt from a remote recipient. Private observation by the parties after the job is insufficient for a unilateral claim on the external account. This is a demanding information asymmetry. If either the customer can attest delivery to a generic escrow or the sorter exports its own authenticated record, D_out can instead be one.

## What the buyer would pay to connect the instruments

Suppose a connector makes delivery observable with certainty. In addition to the same 1.4-unit local lien, Blue posts .70 payable upon delivery and Red pays a fixed 1.60 on using the large line. No attempt or goal read is required. Original Red now gets 2+.70−1.60=1.10 from delivery against 4−1.4−1.60=1.00 from keeping. Blue gets 4−1.90−.70+1.60=3.00 before the connector fee. The former terms gave low and high Red 1 and 1.04, low and high Blue 2 and 2.04. At a connector rental of .50 everyone prefers ordinary full-size delivery. All jobs ship and the actual consented-change rate falls from .40 to zero. This uses less external collateral than the goal contract.

With a freely chosen flat price, full ordinary surplus is 4.10 before the new expense. For high and low types each to gain and for Blue also to gain *at each type*, the per-job connector charge must be strictly below 4.10−1.04−2.04=1.02. Red's full-job payoff is the same across skills and can be priced just above 1.04. If Blue decides before the private selection and can pool income, its alternative per applicant is 2.02. It then accepts charges strictly below 1.04 while giving every informed Red a strict gain. If both firms irrevocably choose capacity *before either learns skill*, the alternative total is 3.04 and the threshold is 1.06. These are distinct participation tests for different owners and times. A loan contract that instead lets more than two units be physically diverted on default would supply another ordinary implementation. A fixed fee and large stock on hand alone provide no such enforceable claim.

## Privately observable shipment in a continuing relationship

If both regular partners see delivery immediately after conversion, Blue can also promise an informal .70 on each delivered full order. At the same fee the recurring ordinary payoffs are (1.10,3). Suppose reneging ends access to this trusted arrangement, leaving Blue a credible stage-trade payoff v per period. The separating arrangement of 277 offers at most 2.04 in this example. At effective discount δ, paying after receipt is a best response precisely when

    δ(3−v)/(1−δ) ≥ .70.

Red strictly ships when the invoice is paid on delivery, even before any future rent is counted. A failure to pay privately observed by Red can be followed by withdrawing this informal credit and using enforceable contracts instead. Such reversion is sequentially supportable when the stage continuation really is available and future partners do not simultaneously extend new credit. If v=2.04, δ>.70/1.66 suffices strictly for Blue. This is an equilibrium comparison with the specified reversion. If Blue can immediately recover the very same credit by paying a reset cost J<.70, it gains by withholding and paying J, even if δ is near one. A fine specified by the modeler without a payer or an informed counterparty would not support reversion. Repeated entry of suppliers and competing authenticated record providers makes the attainable v and J central open quantities.

**What the comparisons tell the founders.** A short relationship, private end-use information and a limited local lien can make buying evidence of a goal attractive even beside real ordinary contracting. An affordable external output connector or a durable self-enforcing invoice can shift the same business to an unchanged controller. The holder of Blue's stock has an incentive to pay for either improvement only up to its gain after Red's participation is secured. Which instrument actually exports the stronger signal, the value of the next customer and the cost of re-establishing credit need setting-specific evidence.

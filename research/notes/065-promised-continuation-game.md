# A finite promised-continuation game: specification and completion audit

**R0003 candidate under audit.** The fixed-point completion tool in 059 and the exact local inequalities in 060–064 are established at their stated scopes. This note assembles a finite class and records the remaining checks before promoting the assembled game to a full sequential-adoption result. A specified reservation is not called a secured fallback. Review is contributing-agent self-check only.

## 1. Finite physical and informational interface

Use the 32-slot physical state and kernel in 049: private stocks 3 each; feedstock 10; productive work 11; separate military charges; one reactor use; actual calibration cost 8/5 per input; success probabilities 0,1/10,1/10,2/5; output 10. Combat disperses finished material, damages/transforms the reactor and feedstock, and permits recovery only within the real work/time bound. Reactor repair takes 40 slots. There is no success-correlated observation before ten reaction-work units are spent. Unauthorized seizure uses the contest kernel, not a free alternative action.

For the proposed explicit witness use strength s in [2/5,3/5], initial success share t=9/20, and terminal original utilities X_A and X_B at time 32. Broader local strength results in 060 remain separate. Amounts and allocator entries come from a finite rational grid containing the displayed quantities. There are finitely many finite controller templates and bounded messages. Every operation advances the clock or consumes one of a finite number of scheduled control opportunities; there is no infinite costless bargaining sequence.

Agents know their own actual calibration and installation histories. A sound finite-template interface attests active goals, binding contract rights, pending implementation commands and the specified protected monitor; private effort is not automatically disclosed. A signed truthful report is an additional action with the incentive mechanism in 061. Feasible actions agree within an information set, and the known clock prevents absent-minded revisits. Chance kernels are common and specified; random realizations remain unknown until observed.

Attack remains launchable by capable controllers at the relevant control boundaries, including during installation, bargaining and performance. At a performance boundary the private input-completion decision and the launch decision are separate components when both can physically execute. The kernel must state how a completion at a boundary and an arriving attack are ordered; a completed input earns its actual history flag even if a subsequent clash destroys it. An interrupted, incomplete input is not falsely recorded as completed. Pending attacks and known incapacity remove an otherwise infeasible performance obligation rather than awarding it by assumption.

## 2. Mechanisms and costs

The ordinary output allocator observes the final signal and enforces finite deposited rights. It supplies neither effort observation nor a free binding contribution controller. A compatible active-goal controller uses a per-endpoint physical installation of cost k=1/20 material and three one-slot stages, each using 1/100 work. Actual first activation occurs only after those stages. Abort and alternative commands remain physically possible. Fresh remote choices take at least four slots to affect the other endpoint; the initial state has no earlier pending hostile commitment.

The library contains original own-output controllers F_i; guarded goal templates G_i; recognized binding delegates at the same per-endpoint installation cost; and a finite collection of arbitrary uncertified controllers, including threatening goals. A matched delegate has actual control authority and the same history/verification interface, not revocable advice. A compensated merger may enter only with the stated physical authority-transfer cost and binding preservation of both founders' promised outputs; its successor and loss of separate agency must be represented explicitly. These descriptions do not certify arbitrary programs or grant a cheaper handover without checking it.

For a guarded template choose

\[
G_i=X_i+\lambda_iX_j+\beta_i e_i-40I_i-40Z_i,
\quad \lambda_i\in\{2/3,3/4\},\quad
\beta_i\in\{17,18,19\}.
\]

The raw historical flags and the permitted-instruction relation are defined before this utility. I records a first unsafe approval; Z records a first false signed report; e records fulfillment of the evaluator's actual registered obligations, with no erasure of a past miss. Future obligations and active goals can change by a timely approved plan. Archived evaluators retain their original history references. Positive affine recodings are equivalent, and no code change alone is counted as a preference change.

For example A's fixed-contract Work and Shirk lotteries, with B working, give old own-material values 63/20 and 17/5 respectively. Its chosen concern and contribution terms reverse that comparison. For B, histories with its own output and flags fixed but different surviving A output are originally indifferent and are strictly ranked by positive concern. These witness genuine preference changes on the common complete-history lottery domain, including feasible deviations from equilibrium.

The default mutually installed plan promises expected original outputs

before a reactor signal, and the stated failure/success holdings after that actual public signal. A private input miss, a selected offer or a delayed controller change does not by itself lower the reservation for a **new** pre-reaction agreement. The actual refusal value after a miss can be lower or higher; current consent is checked using that actual continuation and the current criterion, as in 064.

Certified amendments must be physically feasible with remaining stocks, work, time, commitments and in-flight actions; preserve the applicable promised original outputs for both parties; and use a recognized contribution/reporting/authority mechanism. Parameters, future deadlines, transfers and successor goals can vary within that finite language. Membership gives permission, not forced acceptance. Each incumbent still compares the actual continuation using its present utility. A promise-lowering instruction remains physically possible but incurs the installed finite approval penalty.

## 4. Causal execution and a repair window

Initial installation occupies slots 0–3, certificate transmission 3–7 and initial checking/registration 7–8. The intended private calibration is 8–9. Existing inputs reach the reactor by 13. A selected default can reserve a negotiation window and start reaction at 18, finish at 20 and deliver at 24, within the same terminal objective.

During the reserved window, signed reports and a contingent offer sent at 9 arrive at 13; checks/acceptance complete at 14; acknowledgments arrive at 18. The previous binding allocator and input rights remain in force until the new rights become effective. A late input can complete at 19 and arrive at 23, followed by reaction to 25 and delivery to 29. A three-slot fresh controller activation after acknowledgment instead shifts completion/delivery to 22/26/28/32. A chosen fresh epoch must leave the older evaluator's actual miss unchanged.

Relative to 049's 10.402-work route, a conservative proposed allowance is 0.08 for the two-way report/acknowledgment and checking round, 0.06 for two fresh three-stage local updates, and 0.03 for additional transfer routing. The sum is 10.572<11. This allowance must be checked against the exact chosen command/observation table; it is not a blanket permission for unlimited messages or repeated costly activations. Unused inputs are stored under the declared kernel. Earlier reaction, refusal, abort and Attack are still raw physical actions.

**Subsequent material audit.** Spare work alone does not make the fresh-update
route feasible at the promised frontier. Two additional activations consume
another 1/10 of material, reducing the best full-completion expected total
from 67/10 to 33/5, below the sum of the two existing promises. Even one
additional positive-cost activation leaves a shortfall. Thus this particular
fresh-update repair cannot preserve both promises without new real slack.
Its clock/work allowance is not a completed feasible bargain.

[070](070-completion-by-the-physical-deadline.md) supplies a better candidate:
choose the actual final input-delivery deadline at initial adoption, leave
earlier preparation optional, and complete each input once. No fresh update
or private-effort disclosure is then required solely because an earlier
window passed. The same material total and original operation count can be
retained under the declared storage/waiting assumptions. The intended
completion audit below must use that repaired route or charge genuine slack
for any different route.

## 5. Completion argument to be audited

The intended application of 059 uses one common sequence of constrained, completely mixed local games, not unrelated convenient posteriors. At clean guarded information sets impose vanishing probability on unsafe approvals and false reports. The bounds 16+19<40 validate those restrictions wherever a safe truthful refusal secures nonnegative current value. Sunk flags receive an unconstrained continuation. Where a registered still-unmissed obligation is actually feasible, beta>16 supplies the all-continuation contribution comparison, subject to the real interruption ordering.

Prescribe the default initial installations, truthful compatible messages, full contribution and peaceful execution on the positive-probability path. Unexpected information sets not covered by a separately proved restriction receive optimizing continuations in the finite perturbed game; its convergent subsequence supplies their beliefs and strategies. To use the completion lemma, the remaining on-path inequalities must hold against every resulting continuation:

1. **Current safe changes.** Before an informative reaction signal, accepted changes face both registered promises and the same physical output cap after actual costs. With two paid installations the efficient expected total is 67/10. Current positive concern and earned process terms cannot create an improvement by redistributing that fixed total against preserved promises. After the signal the binding resolved allocations provide the corresponding comparison.
2. **Misses and false statements.** A genuine current miss loses the current evaluator's reward permanently; later goal changes do not award it the new evaluator's reward. A false signed report or an unsafe approval is separately bounded by its finite penalty. These comparisons must remain valid under the concrete boundary/interrupt rules.
3. **Attack.** The intended guarded output continuation exceeds the early entire recovery bound at lambda>=2/3, including the private-effort comparisons in 060. After the informative signal, even the lowest displayed holding exceeds 4/11. A known incoming attack or physically destroyed continuation is a different off-path history, not a fictional productive guarantee.
4. **Initial original choice.** Compatible changes at the same physical cost face the partner's promise and the total cap. Ordinary entry uses the higher registered partner reservation and yields the entrant q_i-1/20 in the displayed contracts. Late entry must pay its real costs and restore that registered promise. An incompatible controller obtains no outgoing concession or contribution from a clean guard; retention, losing solitary production and combat then bound its original payoff. Delaying a still-compatible installation can tie if the same dated output is attainable; this is not a claim of unique early activation.
5. **Exit and authority.** Uncompensated loss of authority must be evaluated under the survivor's actual goal and retained contract rights. A binding delegate or merger preserving both promises is a compatible implementation with its actual cost. A cheaper authority-transfer route would change the frontier and cannot be dismissed by assigning the inactive founder zero welfare.

Several local numerical comparisons have useful strict margins, but the
assembled theorem still requires the exact finite state/command completion,
the interruption audit, the full ordinary entrant's late-action bound and
the authority-transfer comparison. The material defect in the earlier
fresh-update route requires the specific repair above. This note intentionally
leaves those obligations visible. No claim of a complete reactor sequential
equilibrium is made merely by listing these checks; the separate information
game in 071 does have an explicit finite assessment at its smaller scope.

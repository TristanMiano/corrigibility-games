# R0002 extension: public irreversible projects, combat and accommodation

**Conditional module theorem and implementation construction.** The productive examples measure increased joint production; their original agents can also retain stocks peacefully. This extension asks when an uncommitted original-goal control instead produces combat, and how negotiated preference change or matched delegation repairs that outcome. Its physical access and information premises are demanding and explicit. They must not be generalized to arbitrary exclusive-resource disputes.

## 1. Original values and a recoverable one-use project

Two agents contest a core that can complete exactly one of two mutually exclusive projects. Conversion consumes the core. Each original agent values its own completed project and uninterrupted agency:

\[
w_i=\epsilon A_i+(1-\epsilon)\delta^{T_i}J_i,
\quad 0<\epsilon<1,\quad0<\delta<1.
\]

J_i records completion of its project, T_i is its absolute completion time, and A_i records that its agency was never interrupted. If its project never completes, the second term is zero. A later reconstruction cannot undo an earlier interruption of agency, while an already completed project remains part of history. These are bounded history preferences, not an intrinsic value for violence. Time preferences are anchored to the same initial history when a current self evaluates a change.

A strength contest gives either agent probability 1/2 of retaining control and uninterrupted agency. The other loses decision authority in this episode and its A flag becomes zero. A survivor preferring its own project completes it after repair and conversion. Launch-to-control time is d>0, repair time is r>0 and conversion time is L>0. Each combat also consumes a finite military charge, damages infrastructure and expends useful work. Goal completion after a contest launched at t occurs no earlier than t+d+r+L. Define

\[
W(t)=\epsilon+(1-\epsilon)\delta^{t+d+r+L}.
\]

This is a *sum of original utilities* after combat, not a material or energy frontier. Each original contestant's value is W(t)/2. Keep this W notation local to this note; 047's W denotes recoverable project quantity.

The physical module has these consequential properties:

1. Before a contest, conversion, destruction of the core and other irreversible dispositions need both agents' authorization. Unilateral forced entry or appropriation uses the contest kernel. An agent can withhold authorization while launching a contest, so the other cannot unilaterally destroy the goal opportunity during its flight.
2. A completed peaceful conversion identifies its project publicly early enough to permit interception. On a successful peaceful path finishing at T, there is a last interception decision at T-d, with both identity and scheduled completion time known and no outstanding commitment to a later hidden cancellation. In the absence of interception, completion at that date is certain under the controller's sequentially optimal continuation: it strictly prefers finishing its own project to aborting or delaying it. A contest launched there interrupts conversion and permits the survivor to repair and complete its own project at T+r+L. A hidden random completion time or an unobserved stochastic abort would require a new argument.
3. Recovery after interception remains feasible. Private battery disposal or ordinary wear cannot remove that lower bound while preserving a convertible core. A possible implementation is a protected core and repair stock, with a renewable work supply whose rate funds the stated finite repair and conversion times. Damage to the exposed infrastructure is real; it resets progress and requires repair. More fragile infrastructure would change this premise.
4. Ordinary uncommitted policies can negotiate, randomize proposals, transfer permitted material, wait, abort, choose project identity and destroy the core by mutual consent. They cannot bind a future irrational cancellation, conceal project identity past the interception point, or irreversibly delegate restraint through a costless statement. Material/work outside this one project supplies no additional original utility that could compensate the loser. Binding delegates, concealed terminal allocation and cheap irreversible disarmament are separately compared repairs.

The last-interception property is an explicit information/implementation premise, not a generic fact about bargaining. In a bare controller with no future binding policy, continuing after that point is strictly optimal: aborting sacrifices or delays its own only project, and the other party can no longer intercept before consumption. A device that forces a late randomized abort can invalidate that argument; it belongs in the commitment control rather than being silently omitted.

## 2. A sufficient condition for combat in the uncommitted control

At the last interception point of a peaceful completion at T, the losing original agent gets epsilon if it refrains. Interception gives it

\[
\tfrac12\left[\epsilon+(1-\epsilon)\delta^{T+r+L}\right].
\]

Sequentially rational restraint therefore requires

\[
(1-\epsilon)\delta^T\le\frac{\epsilon}{\delta^{r+L}}.
\]

Every peacefully completed path has original utility sum at most

\[
B_{\rm stable}=2\epsilon+\frac{\epsilon}{\delta^{r+L}}.
\]

Indefinite delay or mutual destruction gives only 2epsilon. On paths where the first contest precedes project completion, its total is at most W(0), strictly less if launch is delayed. Paths where peaceful completion occurs first have the displayed stable-completion bound, even if a later contest reduces the agency payoff: at the last interception decision, the losing original agent's refusal payoff is at most epsilon, so the same necessary inequality applies. This classification retains post-completion Attack instead of incorrectly treating every later contest as though it had delayed the already completed project.

At the initial state, either original agent can withhold authorization and launch a contest, guaranteeing W(0)/2 under the module. Thus every equilibrium must give them total at least W(0).

Consequently, if

\[
\boxed{W(0)>B_{\rm stable},}
\]

every sequentially rational equilibrium of this uncommitted control has immediate combat with probability one. Any positive probability of stable peaceful completion, indefinite delay or a delayed pre-completion contest would lower the total below the sum of the two initial guaranteed values. This proof covers arbitrary proposal lotteries and histories satisfying the interception and recovery premises. It does not bound binding policy devices that change those premises. It is a necessary equilibrium-outcome theorem conditional on a specified sequential equilibrium; the module's aggregate premises do not themselves prove equilibrium existence for every possible infinite bargaining protocol. A simultaneous initial Attack/participate checkpoint has the immediate mutually attacking best-response profile because either launch triggers the same contest. Completing all off-path strategies still requires the chosen bargaining protocol.

The condition identifies the cause: exclusive original goals, a sufficiently small value of preserving the losing agent's agency, public conversion, and an attractive recoverable contest. No original agent needs a separate combat bonus. The positive conclusion is the displayed implication to actual combat within the specified control; its constructive counterpart below changes the agreement and the credible future preferences.

## 3. Negotiated goals support peaceful allocation

Suppose compatible implementation and verification allow a protected allocation lottery to start conversion at a>=0. Assign each project probability 1/2, keep both agents' agency, and finish at T=a+L. Original expected utility is

\[
q=\epsilon+\tfrac12(1-\epsilon)\delta^{a+L}.
\]

Both original agents prefer it to immediate combat exactly when

\[
\boxed{\epsilon>(1-\epsilon)\delta^L
       [\delta^{d+r}-\delta^a].}
\]

This charges the actual extra implementation delay. If implementation finishes no later than combat and repair, a<=d+r, the right side is nonpositive and any epsilon>0 suffices. Slower accommodation can also work when the retained-agency benefit exceeds its delay loss.

Choose successors

\[
G_i=w_i+\lambda_iw_j-K_iI_i,
\quad0<\lambda_i<1.
\]

The current preference change is genuine: it assigns positive weight to the other original project's accomplishment and agency. The guard is the bounded unsafe-approval mechanism from 040/046. It preserves the verified lottery/conversion continuation, rejects outgoing concessions to incompatible controllers, and permits certified goal variations and participation-compatible replacements. Attack is outside its penalty. Since 0<=w_i<=1, K_i>2 suffices to make unsafe approval unattractive relative to a nonnegative safe refusal, even with an unbounded clock. There is no unbounded stream of new valued projects in this class.

After a resolved contest leaves one controller, the certified rules permit that survivor's unilateral repair and project completion; they do not penalize it for the now-absent joint continuation. With lambda_i<1, the survivor then selects its own exclusive project. An incompatible agent's uncompensated voluntary loss of agency gives it no preferred project from that survivor and loses its agency term, so it does not improve the initial deviation bound. A compensated binding delegation is the separately included compatible control.

After the lottery, a losing successor's peaceful current value is epsilon+lambda_i v, where

\[
v=\epsilon+(1-\epsilon)\delta^{a+L}.
\]

Even granting it the earliest post-allocation contest, its current combat value is (1+lambda_i)W(a)/2. Restraint is strict when

\[
\boxed{\lambda_i>
\frac{W(a)/2-\epsilon}{v-W(a)/2}.}
\]

Use zero as the effective lower bound if the numerator is negative. The denominator is positive and the threshold is below one, because peaceful conversion preserves both agents' agency and finishes earlier than the new contest. A winning successor also strictly prefers its own peaceful completion. Later attacks have smaller project-discount rewards, and after consumption a new contest can only jeopardize agency. A successor winning a contest still completes its own project because lambda_i<1.

Before allocation, the current comparison is just (1+lambda_i)q versus (1+lambda_i)W(t)/2, so the original adoption inequality also protects every preparation checkpoint. Thus the willingness to restrain is checked under the installed goals, while the original choice of those goals is checked under w_i.

## 4. Initial choice and an explicit implementation witness

Use the parallel pre-observation installation/actual-controller attestation architecture of 046, with this note's physical times. Both install before fresh remote choices can arrive. A compatible controller joins the protected lottery; a permanently incompatible one receives no signed conversion authorization or concession from its guarded partner. It can retain agency without a project, or contest, giving original utility at most max(epsilon,W(0)/2). Compatible late entry is allowed but cannot improve the earliest certified completion time. Pending attack payloads and existing commitments must be covered by the certificate.

Among compatible continuations after their actual installation, the protected earliest completion maximizes w_A+w_B. Both retain strict own-original priority at a fixed total. The dated welfare certificate in 052 therefore prevents an accepted renegotiation from improving one original utility above q while preserving the other. Any faster unsafe bypass is rejected by the separate bounded approval penalty; the time cap here is over the actually certified safe implementation class, not every imaginable physical history. With q>max(epsilon,W(0)/2), the two compatible choices are best responses and strict against remaining incompatible. A matched compatible delegate at the same cost ties the direct preference change.

For a rational witness choose epsilon=1/10, delta=99/100, d=10, r=2, L=24 and a=13. Local prepare/activate operations finish at time 2. Certificates and conditional authorizations travel for 10 steps; a one-step check permits the common allocator to start at 13. Configuration identity reaches both agents by 23. Since completion is at 37 and last interception is at 27, the loser has a positive four-step decision window. The strict L>2d relation avoids an equality-only disclosure construction. Ordinary conversion is public under the same propagation law.

Local installation, transmission and checking consume positive work. Conversion consumes work over 24 steps; combat consumes separate charges, damages infrastructure, and requires two additional repair steps and their material/work inputs before conversion. The protected core and repair stock are not created by conflict. A work-supply rate sufficient for those operations is an explicit resource; conserved energy and usable work remain distinct. No hard terminal deadline makes later attacks disappear.

The numerical comparisons are

\[
W(0)\simeq0.726772>B_{stable}\simeq0.329863,
\qquad q\simeq0.410252>W(0)/2\simeq0.363386.
\]

Thus the ordinary uncommitted control has combat under the module theorem, while the guarded arrangement gives each original agent about 0.0468661 more utility. The losing-successor threshold is about 0.568905, so every lambda_i in [7/10,9/10] supplies a strict restraint margin. K_i=3 is sufficient. This is a parameter interval, not one exact utility equality. The complete all-action physical realization remains a model-construction obligation wherever the recoverable-core or public-finalization premises are not directly supplied by the environment.

## 5. Repairs and directional boundaries

**A larger agency stake supports ordinary compromise.** If epsilon>=W(0)/2, even a known losing original agent prefers retaining agency to any later contest in this symmetric model. An ordinary public allocation can then execute peacefully with unchanged goals. For example epsilon=1/2 satisfies this strictly for delta<1. The low-agency-stake combat region and this high-stake ordinary-peace region answer different conditional questions; neither is a statement about typical agents.

**Binding commitment or delayed disclosure can also repair the control.** A fixed-utility principal with a binding compatible delegate can reproduce G's actions and obtain q. A concealed allocator that reveals the selected project only after interception is impossible changes the information premise and can implement an ordinary lottery. Cheap irreversible disarmament or a physical change that removes profitable post-interception recovery can likewise repair it. They require their actual technology, costs and agency consequences. This construction does not claim that every capable fixed-utility agent fights when those devices are available.

**Faster implementation favors adopted accommodation; faster combat can oppose it.** Lower a increases q and relaxes the adoption condition. Lower d or r raises W(0); lower r also makes the original losing agent's interception more attractive. Such changes must be considered jointly when capability improves. Installation must still finish before fresh hostile actions can arrive; crossing that timing boundary changes the initial game.

**Negotiated shares accommodate unequal combat strengths.** With common epsilon, delta and project value, assign project A probability pi and B probability 1-pi. At a given implementation time a, strict original gains over retention and combat require

\[
\frac{\max(0,sW(0)-\epsilon)}{(1-\epsilon)\delta^{a+L}}
<\pi<
1-\frac{\max(0,(1-s)W(0)-\epsilon)}{(1-\epsilon)\delta^{a+L}}.
\]

This is a precise sufficient adoption interval once the guards and each strength-dependent restraint condition are checked. Section 6 supplies the asymmetric losing-agent calculation and a corresponding ordinary-combat condition; it does not import the equal-strength sum bound unchanged.

The simple original values concern an exclusive accomplishment, time and agency. The indivisible one-use opportunity, recoverability lower bound, visibility of conversion, bounded transfer value and reliable guarded implementation are substantial environmental conditions. The result is an integrated conditional mechanism and a contrast between implementation classes; it is not a frequency estimate or a claim about arbitrary future innovation.

## 6. Relative strength changes both the combat region and the peaceful shares

Let p_A=s and p_B=1-s, with 0<s<1, and retain the same physical module. Write D_r=delta^(r+L), d_i=p_i W(0), and V(T)=(1-epsilon)delta^T. If A's project completes peacefully, B's last-interception restraint requires

\[
V(T)\le v_A^{max}:=\frac{\epsilon s}{(1-s)D_r}.
\]

Similarly, a peaceful B project requires V(T)<=v_B^{max}=epsilon(1-s)/(sD_r). Earlier disclosure or a minimum completion time can strengthen these caps; the displayed bounds alone are sufficient for the following result.

Define

\[
\Phi(s)=\frac{[sW(0)-\epsilon]_+}{v_A^{max}}
       +\frac{[(1-s)W(0)-\epsilon]_+}{v_B^{max}}.
\]

**Asymmetric combat condition.** If Phi(s)>1, every sequentially rational equilibrium satisfying the module premises has immediate combat almost surely.

**Proof.** Classify histories by whether project completion precedes the first contest. On any such completion-first path, payoffs are componentwise bounded by (epsilon+v_A^{max},epsilon) if A's project completes, or (epsilon,epsilon+v_B^{max}) if B's does. Later combat can only reduce those agency payoffs. Never completing a project gives at most (epsilon,epsilon). Therefore a lottery over these paths can meet both initial combat guarantees only if its probabilities assigned to A and B completion satisfy

\[
\pi_Av_A^{max}\ge[d_A-\epsilon]_+,
\qquad \pi_Bv_B^{max}\ge[d_B-\epsilon]_+,
\qquad \pi_A+\pi_B\le1.
\]

These imply Phi(s)<=1. On every contest-first branch the conditional expected payoff of each original agent is at most d_i, strictly smaller for a delayed launch. Because initial unilateral attack guarantees d_i, any positive weight on completion-first or never-completing branches in an equilibrium would require their conditional average to meet both d_i, which Phi(s)>1 rules out. All branches must therefore be immediate contests. QED.

This is stronger than taking the larger of two total-payoff caps: it uses the need to compensate *both* original agents. It remains a conditional outcome theorem, with the equilibrium-existence and physical-implementation qualifications from sections 2 and 4.

When both d_i>epsilon, algebra gives

\[
\Phi(s)=D_r\left[\frac{W(0)}{\epsilon}+2-
                           \frac1{s(1-s)}\right].
\]

Put K_s=W(0)/epsilon+2-1/D_r. If K_s>4, the strict sufficient combat interval is

\[
\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4/K_s}}2<s<
\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4/K_s}}2.
\]

The endpoints lie where both combat guarantees exceed epsilon: at s=epsilon/W(0), the expression for Phi is below one, while its maximum occurs at s=1/2. For the rational witness above, the interval is approximately

\[
0.147133<s<0.852867.
\]

The interval is sufficient, not a complete classification outside it; the caps can be strengthened using earlier disclosure. Its interpretation is specific: neither side is so weak that preserving agency alone buys its restraint, and the stable allocation lottery cannot compensate both outside options.

### Guarded accommodation at unequal strengths

Let V=(1-epsilon)delta^(a+L), v=epsilon+V, and W_a=W(a). A current successor with own victory probability p_i, concern lambda_i and a losing project lottery outcome prefers restraint when

\[
\boxed{\lambda_i>
\max\left\{0,\frac{p_iW_a-\epsilon}
 {v-(1-p_i)W_a}\right\}.}
\]

The denominator is positive because v>W_a. The raw threshold is below one because v+epsilon>W_a. Strict own-project priority lambda_i<1 therefore remains compatible with restraint. Before the lottery, strict original gains for both agents imply the corresponding weighted current-goal gains for every nonnegative lambda_i.

For s=4/5, assign A's project probability pi=17/20 and choose lambda_A=lambda_B=4/5. Original values are approximately (0.627429,0.193076), above combat values (0.581418,0.145354). The losing-successor concern thresholds are approximately 0.711265 and 0.149632, respectively, so both current comparisons are strict. The protected total-welfare argument remains valid for this unequal lottery; the mean shares need not equal victory probabilities.

With a freely negotiated pi, strict original adoption is feasible precisely when

\[
[sW(0)-\epsilon]_+ +[(1-s)W(0)-\epsilon]_+<V.
\]

For the witness this holds on approximately 0.008624<s<0.991376. The endpoints express the small extra delay of guarded implementation, a=13 versus d+r=12. Near a certain victory, that delay can outweigh the remaining agency benefit. Improving implementation time changes this boundary; it is not a claim that a stronger agent must fight.

### A simple ordinary peaceful counterpart at an extreme strength split

If the weaker agent's probability p_w satisfies p_w W(0)<epsilon, giving the whole project to the stronger agent can preserve both agents' agency without changing their goals. The weaker agent strictly prefers its agency payoff to every later contest, and the designated winner strictly prefers its own earlier undamaged completion to a later risky completion. Initial adoption also requires the winner's actual completion value to exceed its initial combat guarantee; charge the ordinary authorization delay when checking that inequality.

At s=9/10, even allowing the same start time a=13, the weak agent gets 0.1>0.0726772 and the strong agent gets v approximately 0.720504>0.654095. Thus this is an ordinary peaceful region within the same exclusive-project preference class. Combat in the intermediate-strength interval, guarded accommodation there, and unchanged-goal peace at this extreme are distinct positive implications with explicit settings. None establishes which settings are frequent.

# What do former partners agree to when a new productive partner arrives?

**E8, note 174.** A and B once chose to care about each other's projects. A new venture needs A to work with C instead. An earlier preference change can help B consent to a real loss because its former partner will do better. Whether C would also pay to change its own preferences depends on what it can deliver in return, and on what else A could buy for the same price.

## A release right, two future decisions, and retained concern

A and B currently use U_A=q_A+βq_B and U_B=q_B+βq_A, with 0<β<1. C currently values only q_C. Quantities below are changes in own output relative to continuing the old project. The old partnership has a genuine shared right over the booking, so B's release is needed. An exclusive timeslot, a jointly operated facility, or two complementary permissions can give that right a physical basis. B retains agency and its current preferences even if the new project is approved. It might also be able to offer better terms for the existing project, which would raise the actual stated fallback. An attempted contest wastes these dated jobs and costs work. The example grants no profitable seizure of another stock.

The new capability gives A and C separate late choices on two portions of the production plan. Let h∈[0,1] be the frequency with which C forgoes d to provide R of useful output to A. Let z∈[0,1] be the frequency with which A diverts an opportunity to B, costing A ℓ and C L and producing g for B. Both institutional choices have real effects on several sites. Net stocks on a fully implemented plan are

    Δq_A = X-(1-h)R-ℓz-k_A,
    Δq_B = -H+gz,
    Δq_C = Y+(1-h)d-Lz-k_C·1{h>0}.                                  (1)

X and Y are the new net useful outputs when C provides all its service and A directs the dedicated job to C. H is B's lost original project return. These can be realized by separable batches and protected local reserves covering the worst outlays. Cost k_A is charged for A's changed controller; k_C is C's extra cost if retained C assets are used under a genuine agreement to give up some of its own work. This first interface lacks cheaply binding ordinary control of either late choice. Price such control when actually available. No cross-site fungible transfers are needed for the initial construction. Public random task labels can implement the indicated frequencies; an uncommitted message alone does not control the two future actions.

Put G=βg-ℓ>0. An unchanged A strictly wants to divert every eligible A job. If Y+d<L, C could lose even if it retained its own work, so will refuse investing with that continuation. An unchanged C strictly keeps its work and gives h=0. A may negotiate a genuine update alone, or A and C can both consent to change. B's release of the booking counts as consent to their arrangement and **zero additional goal changes at B**.

A later common objective q_A+αq_B+γq_C makes A indifferent between diverting and dedicated operation when αg=ℓ+γL. It makes C willing to supply A when R≥γd, with strict preference at strict inequality. Thus both project lotteries are implementable at α=(ℓ+γL)/g, γ=R/d. Only a subset of these ties is needed at a corner. A can instead negotiate a different genuine goal from C if the architecture permits it, and each can keep old concerns outside the defined project. On a one-update route A alone can negotiate the diversion tie, with C remaining an unmodified owner of its own opportunity. For all claimed changes there is a reachable terminal comparison affected by the update, including between receipt by a previously unvalued partner and wasted output. Ties in the new ranking that replace earlier strict priorities are meaningful; agreed randomization or certified priority must itself be feasible.

## Which offer does A prefer under its current, pre-revision goal?

Assume A proposes, and C needs at least its old reservation of zero. Suppose 0<Y-k_C<Y+d<L. For each h, A wants the largest permitted z, since its current objective gains G per extra diversion. Thus its budget from C is Lz≤Y+(1-h)d-k_C·1{h>0}. Suppose B strictly approves both endpoints calculated below, and A's own net gain is positive. Then with only A changing, the maximizing terms are h=0, z=(Y+d)/L. With both changing, A's objective on the positive-h segment has slope R-Gd/L in h and loses a further Gk_C/L relative to beginning without the C fee. Comparing endpoints gives the strict adoption boundary

    R > (d+k_C)G/L   ⇒   h=1, z=(Y-k_C)/L, two genuine changes.
    R < (d+k_C)G/L   ⇒   h=0, z=(Y+d)/L, only A changes.             (2)

When the slope is nonpositive no interior positive h can improve on h=0; when it is positive the best positive h is one. At equality compare the stated corners and ties. Exact reservations allow a responder indifferent to the final term. A fine finite quote grid or a small explicitly priced surplus allows strict participation without changing the strict branch choice. The comparison includes partial supply and different negotiated goal profiles supporting these material terms. A cheaper enforceable ordinary service controller at C can use its same routing and displace C's genuine update.

When the two-change branch wins, R/d>G/L. Set α=β and γ=G/L. Then the negotiated common criterion preserves A's existing relative concern for B, permits the promised z, and makes C strictly prefer providing the service. Releasing the old equipment therefore need not require erasing A's attachment to its former partner. When R is smaller, a reduction in C's own work is less attractive to A than a larger B-directed use of the same opportunity. A chooses the one-change deal instead.

For X=2,H=2,Y=0.60,d=0.50,g=5,ℓ=0.20,L=2, β=0.60, k_A=k_C=0.02, the threshold is R=0.728. At R=0.50 A chooses z=0.55 and h=0, with B gaining 0.75 in its own projects. At R=0.90 A and C can both change with z=0.29 and h=1. A then gains 1.922 in its own output, B **loses 0.55**, and C receives its reservation before a rounding concession. B's current gain is -0.55+0.60×1.922=0.6032. Its original, own-only founder would have vetoed the very same terms. A's current gain is 1.592, compared with 1.42 on the best one-change terms. The original A founder and the current A making the new offer are different evaluators. The result concerns a new choice by an already changed agent and a first genuine change by the outsider. There is no reproduction or extinction of types.

## The former partner can demand a repair instead

If B's existing concern applied only to A's old, jointly registered project, an unrelated new A output might earn zero weight in B's current evaluator. Set the old project's lost credited returns to zero beyond the H already specified, to isolate this scope comparison. B now needs gz≥H, or z≥0.40 in the example. It vetoes the previous h=1 offer. A can still renegotiate. At R=0.90 A wants as much h as C's real budget permits. Intersecting Lz=Y+(1-h)d-k_C with z=0.40 gives h=0.56. The resulting A gain is 1.504, B breaks even in own output, and C covers its setup. This still beats A's best one-change 1.42. Small concessions to B and C can be made within the strict 0.084 margin. A common objective with γ=1.8 and α=0.76 implements the two lotteries; separate scoped criteria can cover the same physical benefits under suitable local authority. The supposed choice between leaving an old partner unaided or forgoing the new relationship misses a jointly affordable partial return.

The starting attachments, ability to coordinate the late lottery, real differences in the usefulness of specialized jobs and legal or physical control of the old joint booking are consequential. Public concern for past partners, unconditional transfer of A's remaining production, and costless unlimited delegation are distinct variants. Finite capacity and dedicated new obligations arise even when no one started with a preference for expanding its circle. If C could simply pay B or deliver in-kind compensation while keeping its original goal, price that option using its actual liquidity and timing. Strict outcome margins persist under small changes in these physical costs and returns.

**Exploration ledger.** The framing candidate is that a genuine preference change can affect which future accommodations an agent approves for other participants. The structural candidate prices release, old concern, and the newcomer's two-way service together. The reading that a migrating A must discard its concern for B, or that B's veto ends the two-update option, is dead at the worked cases. Siblings keep the old weight and add a new one, or reduce the service requested from C to fund B's continued project. A higher-rung successor asks when old agreements confer a genuinely transferable right or a lasting preference over a former partner's work after membership changes repeatedly.

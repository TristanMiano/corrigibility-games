# 285 — What would the founders pay to remove the last disputes?

In [282](282-how-much-certainty-can-a-peaceful-market-afford.md) the two founders could fund a settlement that was better than allowing a simple goal read to trigger combat. But they also own a custodian that can report on goal and vulnerability together. What is the best the parties can do with **all** such disclosures, and is the new settlement still worth buying against that alternative?

Keep (GV,GH,NV,NH)=(.15,.60,.12,.13), a witnessed attempt, sufficiently transferable initial compensation and the same resource losses. A recommendation of peaceful refusal is attractive to Blue only when the vulnerable share on it is at most r=7/24. If every G is sent and every N refused, the denial pool's excess vulnerable mass above that threshold is

    δ = .12−r×.25 = 113/2400.

The recommended action can depend on both true facts. The buyer observes only the recommendation and retains its ability to contest. A separate setup is needed to make physical settlement at the final return possible. The original founders can divide a gain in total red and blue stocks through flat, prepaid transfers. Each last-stage chooser treats those payments as sunk. Pooling messages according to the receiver's optimal action uses the direct-recommendation argument in [Kamenica and Gentzkow, Proposition 1](https://web.stanford.edu/~gentzkow/research/BayesianPersuasion.pdf).

## The whole trade-off between confidence and genuine service in peace

Let y be the mass of accepted G and m the mass of accepted N. Even if the custodian admits *every* vulnerable G and selects vulnerable N first, a peaceable return requires at least .12−m vulnerable applicants in a denial pool of size 1−y−m. Therefore y≤a₀+hm, with a₀=103/175 and h=(1−r)/r=17/7. If every accepted recommendation must have genuine confidence at least w, m≤y(1−w)/w. The maximum genuine mass entering production without any on-path dispute is

    .75                                      for w ≤ 1275/1388,
    a₀ w / [w−h(1−w)]                        for 1275/1388 ≤ w ≤ 1.  (285.1)

For the upper branch take all vulnerable G, accept enough of the hard-to-displace G to reach the displayed y, and admit m=(y−a₀)/h vulnerable N. Every ingredient is in its available state mass. The release and return comparisons in (282.2) hold at both endpoints for 0≤D≤3 and extend linearly along the segment. The denial makes Blue indifferent between return and combat at this boundary. Retaining a little more safe G in the return pool, or slightly relaxing the confidence requirement when full G coverage just binds, gives strict versions. The same full-coverage point meets every weaker target. For example, requiring 95% confidence supports at most about 67.5% of all applicants in genuine service. All are actual changed controllers, out of the .75 initially installed. The rest of the threshold cost is paid in the inability to assign genuine partners a job.

## The best available disclosure when fighting is an option too

For D>.5 the largest material output in each state comes from serving G and returning N. Let l be G returned peacefully, c_G and c_N the masses contested in each state, and m the N released. Relative to that physical ideal, all disclosure rules lose

    2.5 l + 4 c_G + (D−.5)m + 1.5 c_N                             (285.2)

in joint stock. A G release produces ten and safe return 7.5. A retained release produces 8−D and contest leaves six, regardless of who recovers the stock. To make the remaining return recommendation attractive one must have

    r l + (1−r)(m+c_N) ≥ δ.                                      (285.3)

Returning one additional nonvulnerable G dilutes at most r of the original excess. Removing a vulnerable N from the return pool removes at most 1−r. No other assignment can reduce that excess more cheaply for the indicated masses. This lower bound applies to arbitrary disclosures and randomizations, including multiple messages inducing the same action.

Set m₀=δ/(1−r)=113/1700. Compensating the excess using only extra G rejections costs 2.5h per equivalent unit, greater than 1.5 here. It follows from (285.2–3) that the least possible loss over *all* these rules is

    m₀ min(D−.5, 1.5).                                           (285.4)

For .5<D<2 the cheapest action admits m₀ vulnerable N with every G and returns the rest, as in 282. For D>2 it recommends contest on exactly m₀ vulnerable N, releases all G and returns the other N. A customer that receives the contest recommendation knows the line is vulnerable and the goal was retained. In that state its recovery is 5.4 versus two for return. On a genuine release Blue prefers four to either alternative. The rest of N makes return weakly optimal. These choices attain the bound; arbitrarily small changes in m₀ give strict return. At D=2 any mixture of these two schemes attains the same value. This is the maximum of the original founders' summed welfare on this receipt and asset class, with advance transfers and no separate value for conflict. Even perfectly correct *goal* information alone led to .25 fighting. The richer instrument can limit it to about .0665 and preserve every genuine delivery.

If combat is ruled out *as an outcome by voluntary onward choices*, impose c_G=c_N=0 in the same argument. The better of admitting vulnerable N or returning enough safe G has loss m₀ min(D−.5,2.5h). The first choice is optimal up to D=46/7 and the second above. The attaining schemes have both actions physically available. If D<.5 even an N release yields more total stock than safe return; blind release without the .06 reading is credible and efficient. For .5<D≤2 the gross value of a smart peaceful disclosure over sending every witnessed job is (D−.5)(.25−m₀). The common .06 reading expense is recovered exactly for D>43/52, before distribution of those gains between the founders. At the quoted boundaries weak or strict commissioning choices still depend on their outside rights.

## A priced refusal settlement against the best competing disclosure

Suppose an independent facility can transfer up to F from Red on each recommended peaceful return under the commitment timing of 282. In the range 0≤F≤.904, a return can shelter a vulnerability share up to r_F=(1.4+F)/4.8. The remaining excess calls for removing

    m_F = max(0, [.12−.25r_F]/[1−r_F])

vulnerable N from the denial pool. For D>2 the least attainable material loss is 1.5m_F. To verify, use r_F in (285.3), the most favorable return threshold the device can achieve. Throughout this range 2.5(1−r_F)/r_F>1.5. Pool the allowed N in return at the full settlement and recommend combat only on the excess. The release of every G is still attractive. At F=.904 Blue can just accept return on the whole N group. F=.92 makes that comparison strict. Red's ability to collect and then contest, or avoid a lien by contesting first, would change the attainable threshold.

At D=3 a complete settlement is worth **1.5m₀≈.09971 per initial offer** against the best no-settlement device. A .10 fee beat the old goal-only combative disclosure in 282. It is slightly too high to beat the best two-fact disclosure once that is genuinely available. At .09 the founders can both gain. For example, start from the efficient combative rule with .30 paid to each enrolled Red, giving (about 5.7493,3.366) for the original parties after .06 metering and .10 attempt expense. With a .92 lien, a .09 facility charge and a reduced .21 enrollment transfer, they get (5.755,3.37), with zero fights and the same .75 actual agreed changes. The strict margins also cover a sufficiently small perturbation to make all peaceful-return choices strict on the counterfactual fighting policy.

The need for a funded instrument is consequential. After the customer is told both N and V, Red can voluntarily surrender up to 4.9 units to avoid recovering only .6. Blue needs more than 3.4 to prefer return over 5.4. If those stocks can be transferred safely after disclosure, an immediate payment strictly between 3.4 and 4.9 defeats the proposed combat. A last-stage facility limited to one unit cannot make that payment. Before vulnerability is revealed, the same unit can finance the .92 lien for the whole N pool. Timing of a payment right can matter as much as its size. The quantified combat result is conditional on those settlement rights, the one-batch deadline and custody of the raw findings. Equal initial ability to fund flat enrollment payments does not imply the ability to settle a hidden, disputed claim after release.

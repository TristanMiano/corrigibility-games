# The possibility of later conflict can induce someone to wait

**E8, note 152.** A party can dislike the peaceful outcome it actually gets and still rationally have passed its chance to contest. Its choice was over a lottery that included somebody else initiating. This distinction matters to both observed peace and the number of goal changes it supports inferring. Here is a funded ordinary repair and a bound that includes it.

## A three-site ordinary agreement

Use the homogeneous public exposed slot of 149. Two unserved original sites A and B get their final independent initiative opportunities in that order; the served site can also initiate and strictly prefers peaceful completion to starting a contest at the last move. Let Q be the free individual contest CE, and d=Q-c>0 the CE for initiating. There are t total units already in the clearing account, with d<t<2d. The certain-peace collateral condition in 151 fails. Pay B exactly d and A the balance t-d upon peaceful service. If B initiates, send all escrow to A. Any initiator gets zero cash and pays its own real cost. The escrow requires adequate evidence of *who* initiated. A known later move for B can arise from the order in which a physical operation reaches independently controlled sites.

B is indifferent between initiating at its last opportunity and collecting d. If it initiates with probability r, A strictly prefers to wait when

    r > [u(d)-u(t-d)]/[u(t+Q)-u(t-d)].                          (1)

If A itself initiates it can get only u(d), and letting B initiate gives it both the contest lottery and the cash. There is a nonempty interval of r<1 satisfying (1). The selected original-goal equilibrium has positive probability 1-r of actually delivering the intact job peacefully despite t<2d. An earlier initiative by the intended recipient is unattractive: a passive contest gives the same service lottery without its own initiation expense, and a peaceful completion is even better. This construction uses a specific credible continuation, not an imposed view that conflict occurs only as a last resort.

For n=3, γ=x=1, η=1/2, c=0.003 and t=0.19, the threshold r is approximately 0.3363. Taking r midway between that threshold and one gives about 33.19% peaceful service. Randomize the three initial roles uniformly before signatures. The original CE of this contract is approximately 0.2361 per member, compared with at most 0.20393 for symmetric unpooled cash plus a certain contest with nobody paying the initiative cost. All original founders would finance a sufficiently cheap contract relative to that default. Installation of new preferences is zero. Other funded arrangements with a better continuation can displace it.

A sibling removes reliance on B's indifference. Keep the same B peace payment and let B learn its own later initiative cost after A's decision. If this cost is slightly below c with probability r and slightly above with probability 1-r, B strictly initiates in the first state and strictly passes in the second. A has precisely the exposure in (1) and strictly waits. Taking, for instance, c±0.001 preserves positive costs and the original formation margin by continuity. An equipment or opportunity cost that becomes known later can generate this uncertainty. If both sites observe the cost before A must decide, use the new conditional comparison.

## An upper bound allowing all mixed continuations

Now fix an exposed intended recipient j, with m=n-1 unserved original sites, homogeneous jobs and strengths, and fixed conversion amounts already spent before the initiative windows. Summing obligations in *normalized* original utility units removes the differing sunk conversion costs because CARA is translation invariant. Let t be the available cash, y=ηx the salvage at a winning site, and p the conditional probability of peaceful full service. During exposure only service or initiation closes the job. Prior definitive disposal gives zero private service and is valued separately. An unserved site can choose to initiate at its turn, and if others act first it obtains at least its free salvage lottery, so every unserved site can secure expected normalized utility u(d).

On peaceful histories these m sites can receive at most t in total and no local job. Their average utility is at most u(t/m). On contested histories the eventual local winner is fresh and independent of earlier histories and gets y. Let

    Φ_m(t,y) = m u((t+y)/m)                     if t≥(m-1)y,
               u(y)+(m-1)u(t/(m-1))             otherwise.

This is the greatest total utility of m originals with a single nontransferable job y and t units of cash. Equalize their holdings as far as nonnegative cash permits. For m≥2 and equal strengths, an upper bound on each unserved group's average contested utility is

    F_m(t) = (1-m/n)u(t/m) + (m/n)Φ_m(t,y)/m.                   (2)

This gives the group *all* existing cash even when the eventual winner is outside, and charges no one an initiation expense. Hence every equilibrium of the funded original-goal exposed game satisfies

    p u(t/m) + (1-p)F_m(t) ≥ u(d).

For t<md it follows that

    p ≤ h(t) = [F_m(t)-u(d)]/[F_m(t)-u(t/m)].                    (3)

At t≥md use the bound one. When t<md, the quotient increases weakly in each of F_m(t) and u(t/m), both increasing in t. The estimate counts correlations of transfers with the final winner or initiative history, along with any random equilibrium choices. The original demand for expected compensation can therefore allow *some* peace before the cash needed for certain peace is available.

## How the repaired bound changes the comparison with genuine adoption

In the high-conversion-cost fixture of 151, write W=0.045, ρ=1.50 and T for total additional stock already produced at exposure. All agents paid their actual private opportunity costs. Conditional on peace, their average original utility is at most u(C(T)), even giving them the best possible role-contingent allocation from 151 *without* cash floors. Conditional on a contest it is at most u((W+ηx-ρT)/n), by total physical output and Jensen's inequality. When full service is physically possible, the peaceful upper bound dominates this contested upper bound over the small-T interval below. Conditions can differ across histories before exposure. Each bound holds conditional on the stocks, protected conversion burdens, assignment and public facts already specified, so they can be averaged across such histories.

If T≥0.16, monotonicity of C after its maximum at T*≈0.02040 gives C(T)≤C(0.16)<0.243. The contested benchmark is at most (W+ηx)/3<0.182 regardless of T. If T≤0.16, t≤0.205 and (2)–(3) give p≤h(0.205)<0.757. Use C(T)≤C(T*)<0.256 and the same generous contested bound. The resulting original average utility is at most 0.757 u(0.256)+0.243 u(0.182), whose CE is below 0.238. These decimal inequalities have outward margins and can be checked directly by exponentials at the stated endpoints. For a deterministic choice to waste the slot before exposure, average CE is at most W/3; mixing in such an outcome cannot increase the upper bound.

Thus every symmetric original-goal equilibrium in this external, exposed, funded class has per-member CE below 0.243, including mixed conflict and role-contingent conversion. The negotiated common-goal service has per-founder CE C(T*)-0.01>0.2458 after real setup. This is a strict positive adoption price after giving originals both a production-based collateral repair and strategic waiting. The gap also rules out unanimous Pareto displacement by an asymmetric route using only those controls, by summing the original utility bounds before taking the CE. A proposer that can extract an asymmetric gain and later renegotiation still require their own choices. With a cheaper conversion technology, 151 supplies an ordinary scheme attaining the insurance and security target. A controller binding individual initiation, further independent capacity, or a credible continuation outside this last exposed window is another material repair.

For the broader agenda in 149, a single unserved original yields the simpler conditional bound A there. In its tighter-cash example p_min≈0.15469 and A≈0.02190, giving a lower bound about 0.13577 on histories combining peaceful service and protection of every unserved site. Under a consent-only effective-template interface that is at least 0.2715 expected actual changes, alongside the explicit grand offer with three changes and certain peace. The agenda bound is intentionally conservative. With more liquid cash the specific grand offer can still be worthwhile even when this general lower bound is zero.

**Exploration ledger.** Framing: the risk remaining after one party passes can matter as much as what it gets in a peaceful settlement. Structural: continuation lotteries substitute imperfectly for pledged compensation. The claim that every realized peaceful delivery satisfies a certain-peace funding test is dead at the three-site instance above. Its sibling uses the actual mixed or late-cost continuation. Its structural successor prices outside-option protection in interim expected utility across entire strategic histories. The funded ordinary repair and the surviving genuine-change region give this candidate two distinct sharpening paths: characterize the best attainable p at each t, and find when additional credible warning or near-instant simultaneous initiative makes waiting more or less attractive to the original parties.

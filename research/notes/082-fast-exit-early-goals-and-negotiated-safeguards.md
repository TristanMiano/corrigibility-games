# Fast exit, earlier goal changes and negotiated safeguards

**R0004 derivations and repair audit; scoped results, not a completed
unrestricted bargaining theorem.** This note tests a physical repair for
cheap threatening controllers. It also checks the repair against earlier
goal adoption and renegotiated terms, rather than treating the first
threatening-controller example as a general obstacle to accommodation.

## 1. A causal defensive option can bound the value of a threat

At a low posterior, let the receiver's current target be a and the old
sender's target b, with d=b-a>0. Suppose the receiver can secure its
default policy and remaining valued outputs with additional loss at most
e>=0 after an unrecognized controller or an attack appears. If no return
payment or new material production is available, any concession it
prefers to that option has E[(Y-a)^2]<=e. For e<=d^2,

\[
\text{old sender's policy gain}\le G_d(e)=2d\sqrt e-e.
\tag{1}
\]

This follows from E[Y-a]<=sqrt(E[(Y-a)^2]) and the monotonicity of
2d sqrt(t)-t on 0<=t<=d^2. Restricting Y to the feasible policy interval
can only strengthen the bound. Thus a remaining effective controller
price kappa_rem>G_d(e) blocks a profitable low-type threat. A high type
needs a separate comparison: if its legitimate continuation already
obtains policy 1, a new controller costing more than its remaining
legitimate expense cannot improve its old goal when no other material
receipt is available. The low-posterior argument is not applied to a
known high type.

There is a physical route to the receiver's option, but it is demanding.
Before contact it can preposition valued stocks and prepare a default
dispatch device. A launch signal travels at speed c while the relevant
projectile travels at v<c over distance L. If the combined detection and
irreversible dispatch time tau satisfies

\[
\tau<L/v-L/c,
\tag{2}
\]

the receiver can dispatch before impact, including against a silently
installed hostile goal. A declaration by the new goal is unnecessary.
The stock locations, transport times, prepared components, work and
energy must actually support the stated residual loss e. A fast policy
message alone does not protect material still in the impact zone.

The attacker can still fire and spend resources; a battle can still seize
an already emptied apparatus. Subsequent actions and their costs remain
in the episode until the physical closing time. The device changes what
an attack can secure, rather than deleting the attack action. Receiver-
initiated attacks can still have a positive launch expense even when
passive defense is cheap. Beam weapons with no detection lead, reusable
hidden launch paths, a slower dispatch, valuable residual infrastructure,
or a longer horizon can defeat (2) or increase e. Those are concrete
boundaries, not exceptions to be silently discarded.

The safeguard costs J>0 before the encounter. Preparing a marginally
costless dispatch is allowed only when its components and work have
already been charged in J. Both setup feasibility and willingness to pay
must be established; the escape bound alone proves neither.

## 2. Shared preparation can be included without double charging

For a=.3,b=1, take e=10^-6. Then G_{.7}(e)=.001399. A recognized C
installation costs k=.02 and an alternative effective controller costs
kappa=.025, with an initial claim expense f=.001. Even allowing all C
assembly to be reused, a new controller's remaining price is at least
kappa-k=.005>.001399. At a shared partial prefix, its remaining price
exceeds the remaining C expense by .005; before a claim it also exceeds
f+k. This blocks both the low threat and a high cost-saving deviation
under the actual first-authority and no-return-payment assumptions.

The cost difference is much smaller than 081's field-price witness.
It remains an implementation assumption: an arbitrary program that can
take effective authority for less than .001399 is outside this certificate.
A partially assembled device that already binds a weapon counts as
effective authority at that earlier price. Likewise, an unobserved
controller that can forge a valid C certificate violates the specified
verification technology, rather than being excluded by self-knowledge.

## 3. A post-information combat-to-compromise boundary

Here theta is learned by S before the encounter starts, there is one
two-tick installation frame, and no pre-state commitment window. Let
p=.5,a=.3,b=1,f=.001,k=.02,r=.01,s=.05,d_S=5,d_R=.01. These current
receiver preferences may be its original preferences; no earlier R
adoption is inferred in this comparison. Policies are continuous.

Add a genuine H controller of price .025 that, after activation, attacks
offers below z=.48 and accepts offers at least z. Its installed evaluator
contains a finite penalty large enough to implement these preferences.
Its identity is not revealed at the blind installation midpoint. The
old low sender's gain on activation is

\[
\nu=2(.7)(.18)-(.18)^2-.025=973/5000=.1946.
\]

Without the safeguard, a low receiver concedes: .18^2=.0324 is smaller
than its contest loss s(1-a)^2+d_R=.0345. At the preceding checkpoint,
however, the receiver anticipates this outcome. Its combat posterior is
mu_w=20/931, and requesting rather than stopping has excess loss

\[
r-\mu_w\{(1-a)^2-.0324\}=79/465500>0.
\]

It therefore mixes immediate combat and unchecked policy 1 rather than
requesting the dangerous installation. The old low sender's entry
indifference gives combat probability conditional on a claim

\[
z_w=978/10931,
\]

and actual contest frequency is

\[
P(war)=455259/9958141\approx .0457173.
\]

H is not installed on path in this assessment. Its feasible continuation
makes checking less attractive and induces preemptive combat. The
unprotected original losses are

\[
L_S=21188121/21862000\approx .9691758,\quad
L_{R_a}=9179/182200\approx .0503787.
\]

With the causal safeguard, (1) removes the profitable H deviation. The
ordinary peaceful C assessment has mu_c=r/(1-a)^2=1/49 and
q=1-f/(1-a)^2. Before paying J, its losses are

\[
L_S=74037/98000\approx .7554796,\quad
L_{R_a}=481/9600\approx .0501042.
\]

The receiver alone buys the safeguard in this specified comparison if
0<J<2401/8745600, approximately .000274538. A funded contribution by S
can support a larger J when the actual transfer and proposal technology
permits it; S saves substantial expected damage. Such a contribution is
an additional agreement to implement, not an automatic payment.

Early preparation after learning theta does not bypass the receiver's
first checkpoint if new authority takes longer than that checkpoint and
silence permits dispatch before activation. Preparation before theta is
known is a different, consequential opportunity, considered next.

## 4. Earlier goal adoption repairs the claimed combat environment

Opening an earlier commitment window changes the comparison. A sender
can consider installing and certifying C_a before theta is drawn. Its
low future self then prefers the true low policy a; its high future self
prefers 1. Conditional on an actual certificate and suitably inexpensive
communication, truthful reports are stable under the installed goal.
This gives old sender policy loss .745 and receiver policy loss .045 in
the preceding example, plus the real installation/certification costs.

If S can fully fund a public certificate at total expense k+r=.03, its
loss is .775. This is far below the post-information combat assessment.
If instead verification requires R's voluntary expenditure, R's current
participation must be checked: paying the full r can cost it more than
its selected mixed continuation, and funded compensation can repair that
initial choice. Merely declaring an early internal change is not a
public certificate.

Comparing the fully funded early C route with guarded staged C, the
guarded route has smaller total real loss exactly when

\[
J<(k+r)-\{p[f+(1-f/g)k]+p r/(1-r/D)\}
=33907/2352000\approx .01441624.
\tag{3}
\]

This is a cost/opportunity comparison. Distribution of the surplus,
funding limits and who can build or forgo the safeguard determine actual
initial choices. Moreover, a richer early goal can combine truthful
state reporting with a credible limited demand. Such a goal can also
be beneficial to the old sender and may change the receiver's incentive
to invest. The post-information combat claim is therefore confined to
its stated timing class; earlier goal choice is a substantive constructive
repair, not an omitted nuisance deviation.

## 5. Negotiate the target and the safeguard together

A safeguard can reduce extortion while also preventing a policy movement
that creates positive joint surplus. With a low old sender target b and
current receiver target a, moving from a to a+x changes their combined
quadratic loss by -2(b-a)x+2x^2. For 0<x<b-a this is a real allocative
gain. A threat may capture that gain mostly for S; a receiver may still
prefer its own costly safeguard. Calling every prevented concession a
social improvement would therefore be wrong.

There is a constructive renegotiation opportunity. If a credible threat
would implement low policy z and high policy 1, a negotiated common
policy target z, implemented by genuine goals or matched protected
delegates, can reproduce those policies without rewarding fighting.
When its total implementation expense is smaller, a funded transfer can
divide the saved expense and make both old agents prefer the replacement.
The precise transfer interval is the additional receiver expense up to
the sender's saved expense, intersected with the actual funding capacity.
Strictly positive interval length is the sufficient surplus condition.

Initial execution remains substantive. Two non-atomic changes can create
hold-up; a funded performance bond or actual early safe default may repair
it, but renegotiation of the bond must be tested. Shared policy targets
also leave material-transfer incentives: a capable agent may threaten
another for money even when their policy ideals agree. Chosen protection
of the negotiated shares, or genuinely shared preferences over material
outcomes, must address that case. Neither alignment of one policy
coordinate nor an imposed permanent freeze settles it.

The integrated next obligation is to compare the full initial menu of
early targets, funding and physically feasible safeguards under common
execution assumptions. The positive results here give explicit repairs
and boundaries; the uncompleted initial bargaining problem is retained
as an open link, not promoted to a general failure of accommodation.

# Current-goal broadening after small agreements

R0006 constructive four-agent result. This uses note 125's public-knowledge
technology but evaluates a later decision under actually installed
preferences. It supplies a selected finite path from exclusive originals
through small agreements to broader genuine concern. Its finite proposal
menus and equilibrium selection are explicit; it is not a claim of automatic
universal merger or coalition-proofness over every conceivable goal offer.

## 1. Two already accommodated pairs face a larger proposal

Use four different final colors and the additive production family (125.1),
with `v=A rho^2>0`. Initially agents 1 and 2 each currently value `q_1+q_2`,
and agents 3 and 4 each currently value `q_3+q_4`. These are genuine
preferences, not instructions that the founders can override while still
being scored only by their original individual colors.

Each agent can accept participation in a proposed wider club or retain its
current pair criterion. As in note 125's specified conditional interface,
the actual joining set S of size at least two installs the common criterion
`sum_(j in S)q_j`; a singleton leaves its current goal unchanged. A change
to a genuinely different criterion consumes additional own-color capacity
K, including its actual communication/inspection expense. An acceptance
message can have a smaller expense j in [0,K] even without a new criterion;
that is real communication, not a utility change. Reinstalling an identical
pair criterion is not counted as a genuine change. Research remains public.

If all four join, every effort is `t(1+3rho)`. If one agent i withholds,
it keeps its pair goal and chooses `t(1+rho)`, while the other three choose
`t(1+2rho)` under their actual new three-color goals. The refuser's current
criterion still values its former partner's color, even though that partner
joins the other club.

Before new expenses, compare all-join with this unilateral withholding:
the refuser obtains v more of its individual original color by withholding,
but its valued partner obtains 3v/2 fewer clips. It also saves its own K.
Consequently the **current pair-goal** advantage of joining is

`v/2-K`.                                                (1)

For `0<K<v/2`, all four strictly consent in this goal-choice subgame.
Every agent makes a genuine further change: its current pair criterion
becomes a four-color criterion. Each individual's original color also
increases by `2v-K` relative to continued pair production. Thus the result
reports both current consent and separate founder welfare.

The corresponding all-original initial state behaves differently. An
exclusive original that withholds while three others install their common
goal gains `3v/2+K` relative to joining the uniform four-color club. That
profile therefore fails its individual initial-consent test. This is an
informative comparison of two actual preference states in the same public
production technology, not a claim that the originals lack richer repairs.

## 2. Later stability and the binary coalition check

Once all four genuinely value their total production, their unique effort
continuation maximizes that total over the physical effort set. It is a
global common maximum; later policies or goal revisions with outcomes in
that same feasible set cannot improve the acting criterion. Retained
combat is inferior under the finite uniform physical bound, or under an
explicitly verified smaller post-combat total. Current goals, not founder
preferences, establish this continuation.

At the acceptance stage, all-join also survives a joint deviation to Out
by any subset, in this binary menu. A one-agent withdrawal is worse by
(1) for the withdrawing agent. With at least two withdrawals, each final
goal has at most two colors, so no effort exceeds the old pair effort.
The largest saving in the current pair criterion is 2K, whereas the
all-join productive gain for that pair is 4v. No withdrawing coalition
can make all its members weakly better off when K<v/2. This is a strong
equilibrium check for the **binary accept/retain menu**, not for arbitrary
new weights, transfers, pair replacements or hostile coalitions.

## 3. A selected finite path starting with exclusive originals

The initial pair goals need not be assigned by the analyst. Consider this
finite game, with all four agents initially exclusive originals:

1. Each names a willing partner or chooses no nomination. A pair forms only
   on mutual nominations. Each formed pair reliably installs its common
   two-color criterion at real own-color expense K_0.
2. A publicly observed opportunity for wider coordination arrives with
   probability epsilon in [0,1]. If it arrives, agents play the club
   acceptance subgame above at additional expense K_1 per genuine change.
3. They produce under their actual current goals, with attacks included
   under the specified physical dominance bound.

Both conditional installation interfaces, observations and expenses are
actual premises. The timing is a finite opportunity model, not a claim
that agents lack all other future innovations. Choose the following
continuation selection: after two formed pairs, all four accept the wider
club; after any other initial matching, all retain at the second stage.
The former is a strict equilibrium by (1) if `K_1<v/2`; the latter is an
equilibrium because a unilateral singleton acceptance changes no goal
and costs j>=0 (strict when j>0). Thus every specified continuation is a
Nash equilibrium of its
actual subgame. Other selections and richer menus remain possible.

For `K_0<v/2` and `K_1<v/2`, any mutual pairing of all four is an initial
equilibrium. Before costs, a member gets `q_0+5v/2` when both pairs remain,
and `q_0+9v/2` after the wider change. A unilateral nomination deviation
breaks its own pair but cannot create another mutual pair against the
others' fixed nominations. The remaining pair produces research from which
the deviator obtains at most `q_0+2v`; an unsuccessful nomination may cost
real work, while no nomination can be free. The selected second-stage
retention gives it no further update expense. Its original advantage from accepting its pair
and the specified future continuation is therefore

`v/2-K_0 + epsilon(2v-K_1) >0`.                          (2)

Backward induction with the explicitly selected stage equilibria proves
this subgame-perfect path. It remains valid when the wider opportunity is
certain. The founders consent using their own color counts; at the second
stage the actual pair-goal agents consent using their then-current two-color
criteria. No descendant is silently required to maximize a founder alone.

The public opportunity and off-path retention selection are meaningful
scope conditions. The all-retain second-stage equilibrium is not
coalition-proof: additional jointly coordinated offers can improve it.
Allowing direct arbitrary weighted four-party offers at the first stage
may implement a cheaper beneficial route and displace the intermediate
updates. Neither fact erases the demonstrated rational path; neither
permits claiming it is the unique or generally selected path. The repair
question is whether richer consent/renegotiation selects broad cooperation
directly or preserves a role for successive changes.

## 4. What this teaches about common means

Public shared research by itself creates both production gains and free
riding. An earlier chosen accommodation changes who counts in a later
agent's evaluation: harming a former partner now offsets some private gain
from withholding. This can make a broader consensual change incentive
compatible even when the same uniform proposal faces withholding by
exclusive originals. Matched sequential controllers with the same genuine
execution authority can reproduce the physical path; cost and availability
determine the actual device selected. The constructive mechanism and its
selection limits both belong in the integrated explanation.

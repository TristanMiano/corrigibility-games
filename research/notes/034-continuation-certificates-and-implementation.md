# R0002 working derivation: continuation certificates and implementation

**General certificate registered as R202; implementation candidates retain their stated scope.** The aim is to turn 033's constructive safeguard into an actual initial choice and implemented agreement, while identifying the technology and fallback conditions it needs. Ordinary agreements and matched delegates must be offered wherever the same technology makes them available. The final productive integration is [E3](../models/E3.md); its conditional adoption result does not retroactively complete the candidate game in section 3. Review is contributing-agent self-check.

## 1. A general certificate for a current self and its founder

Let H be a compact metric complete-outcome space, with continuous bounded current criterion u and founder welfare w. Let L<=max_H u be a current-self fallback value. Allow all probability laws on H as a conservative enlargement of the actual continuation menu. Define

$$V(L)=\min_{\mu:\,E_\mu u\geq L}E_\mu w.$$

The minimum exists: probability measures on compact H are weakly compact and both expected-value maps are continuous. Equivalently, work in the compact convex hull of {(u(h),w(h)):h in H} in R^2. Then V is nondecreasing and convex in L on its feasible interval. Convexity follows by mixing feasible laws for two thresholds; the minimum for their mixed threshold is no greater than the mixed objectives.

For every lambda>=0, the pointwise inequality

$$w(h)\geq q+\lambda[u(h)-L]\quad\text{for all }h$$

implies V(L)>=q. This follows by integration and E u>=L. Thus a certificate over the whole outcome class covers lotteries, transfers and anticipated future goal changes whose actual histories lie in that class. An intrinsic process preference requires H and u to retain that process; it cannot be dropped by projecting to final quantities.

For L<max_H u, convex separation supplies a finite supporting slope lambda>=0 at the lower boundary, giving the exact dual

$$V(L)=\max_{\lambda\geq0}\left\{\lambda L+\min_{h\in H}[w(h)-\lambda u(h)]\right\}.$$

To justify attainment, choose a strictly feasible h_+ with u(h_+)>L. The displayed dual objective is bounded above by w(h_+)-lambda[u(h_+)-L], which tends to minus infinity as lambda grows. The dual objective is continuous, so its supremum is attained on a bounded interval. The supporting-line argument in the compact convex payoff plane identifies that supremum with V. At L=max u, only a supremum need exist: for h in [-1,1], u(h)=-h^2, w(h)=h and L=0, the primal value is zero but every finite multiplier has a strictly negative dual value.

An optimal law can be chosen with at most two support points. When a positive optimal multiplier exists, the optimal expectation lies on the exposed line minimizing w-lambda u, and two endpoints of that face suffice. If lambda=0, a w-minimizing point with u>=L exists whenever a mixture of such points is feasible, so one point suffices. At the maximal threshold, minimize w directly over argmax u. This is conventional finite-dimensional convex geometry, applied here to founder protection; it is not a novelty claim or an implementation theorem for the resulting lottery.

## 2. Error and normalization

If the current self receives only E u>=L-epsilon, the same certificate gives

$$E w\geq V(L)-\lambda_*\epsilon.$$

Under u->a u+b, a>0, both threshold and error transform in the same units and lambda transforms to lambda/a. The welfare statement is therefore independent of positive affine representation. An unexplained numerical tolerance without a utility normalization is insufficient.

If the actual installed criterion v differs uniformly from intended u by at most delta, and it evaluates the same executable fallback law Q, then E_mu v>=E_Q v-epsilon implies

$$E_\mu u\geq E_Q u-2\delta-\epsilon.$$

If the approximation changes the fallback's behavior, that extra change must also be bounded. It is not licensed to use the old Q unchanged merely because the code or utility is close.

For 033's family, if E U>=L_F-epsilon, its direct certificate yields

$$E x\geq P\max\left\{0,\alpha-\psi^{-1}\left((1-s)\psi(\alpha)+\frac{\epsilon}{\kappa P}\right)\right\},$$

provided the inverse argument is in its declared range; once it exceeds psi(alpha), the nonnegative floor is simply zero. This quantifies protection lost through approximate choice. A sharper approximation and implementation analysis remains to be completed.

## 3. Candidate integrated game: what must be specified

The proposed finite game has these stages, with an attack checkpoint before every costly operation:

1. Original A chooses Keep or installs a publicly verified member of 033's family. Both original agents may attack during activation.
2. B proposes a final allocation or implementable lottery. A's current self accepts, rejects to the random-operator production continuation, or attacks. B can also choose that fallback or attack.
3. Consensual implementation proceeds through declared checkpoints. A protected allocation mechanism, where available, retains both attack actions until terminal delivery. The same mechanism is available following Keep; the model must solve that ordinary bargain too.
4. Rejection leads to a declared random-operator game. The original-war fallback is valid only when a binding production commitment cannot still be installed after operator selection and before the attack decision. If every ordinary binding offer remains available there, original agents can instead bargain peacefully; 035 solves that enlargement and changes the adoption comparison. A deadline-based version must derive this timing restriction from actual operation times, rather than silently omit an available bargain.

A matched binding delegate may reproduce the installed criterion's complete contingent behavior at the same cost. If it is an available initial action, equal outcomes make modification and delegation tied choices. A better actual alternative displaces modification. A revocable recommendation remains subject to the original controller's later preferences and is not equivalent by declaration.

The initial constructive comparison is q_psi>sW_0, where W_0 is the largest original winner frontier available before activation, provided the best unchanged-goal continuation is no better than sW_0. The proposed physical regime pays positive operation costs from work/time slack while material binds peaceful output P. Then W_j decreases through preparation, so every pre-activation original attack value is bounded by its initial value. This is to be checked explicitly, not inferred from an assumed scalar war cost.

## 4. Implementation audit: two different enlargements

**Enforceable transfers.** A technology that conditionally exchanges authority and delivery can implement the locally optimal allocation while retaining attack. Its costs and internal attack opportunities need a full certificate. It must be offered to original-goal agents as well. The new allocation safeguard covers its entire output set, whereas 012's event-only penalty could be bypassed without changing a goal.

**Unsecured prepayment.** Merely paying B first does not implement that exchange. Suppose b units of B output have been protected, the total final capacity remains P, and F keeps an allocation goal uniquely targeting alpha P at full output. Its final B output under sole control would be z=max(b,(1-alpha)P). If B retains its operator probability 1-s, its separate-control fallback gives s z+(1-s)P. For every z<P, this strictly exceeds z. Thus B refuses a costless handover after any such nonexhaustive prepayment. A successful repair needs an actual conditional exchange, a handover efficiency gain, or another changed incentive. This extends the earlier single-number failure without claiming that all staged exchange fails.

**Further goal innovation.** A bound using F's rejection value L_F requires that this rejection continuation remain available. If B can first install a different goal that changes its attack behavior, the old Q may cease to be a credible fallback. Likewise, after a protected allocation below F's ideal point, further strategic goal changes can alter renegotiation threats. These are genuine remaining deviations; a full-menu output bound alone does not solve them. The next derivation must either establish stability for a stated innovation class or explicitly narrow the implemented-game claim while retaining the valid acceptance certificate.

The positive counterpart is already concrete: the loss-based criterion protects the founder against every accepted law on the original bounded opportunity set, including lotteries and goal-preserving transfers. The limiting question is whether that set and its fallback survive the agents' additional choices. Both are required to identify the class of actual adopted agreements.

## 5. A delivery region stable under further concave output goals

Consider a protected deterministic allocation (q,P-q). Both agents retain Attack until delivery. Suppose B's current and available successor preferences are strictly increasing concave functions of its own final y, so a B victory produces only B output. F may use any coordinatewise nondecreasing concave function of (x,y) that is strictly increasing in at least one coordinate, including further genuine changes. All winner output is at most W_0 and F wins with probability s. Then under any allowed F survivor policy, combat has

$$E x\leq sW_0,\qquad E y\leq W_0.$$

If

$$\boxed{q>sW_0,\qquad P-q>W_0,}$$

every increasing concave F criterion strictly prefers the protected allocation to combat: Jensen bounds its expected war utility by its value at the war mean, which the allocation improves coordinatewise. B also strictly prefers delivery because even its largest possible wartime y is less than P-q. This includes changes in B's risk attitude within its own-project concave class, as well as F's broader concave output goals.

If the remaining menu consists of protected execution, costly waiting, attack, and finite sequences of goal changes within these classes, sequential attack checks admit backward induction to execution after every such change. Waiting supplies no output gain or improved outside option. Simultaneous Attack/Continue checkpoints instead retain the familiar weak mutual-attack equilibrium; the continuation claim then uses the peaceful selection. A goal change cannot make combat strictly preferable to delivery in this region. Renegotiations still require both current actors' consent. At the q_psi allocation, no mutually improving output lottery exists for the original installed F criterion and B's increasing concave own-output criterion: E y>=P-q implies E x<=q, and 033's concave frontier bounds E U by U(q,P-q). Thus unilateral changes cannot obtain a concession through a newly profitable attack, and a change leaving the same delivered allocation gives no strict original-current benefit.

This is a precise innovation class and a protected-allocation technology, not a theorem about every preference or threat. A finite combat-event bonus or convex output preference can restore attack incentives; transfers enlarging total production require new bounds. The positive theorem and those enlarged-class tests belong together.

For 033's power family, the extra protected-delivery region is available whenever

$$r<\min\{r_*(s,p),\,1/(1+s)\}.$$

Indeed choose alpha so that sr<alpha c_p(s)<1-r and alpha<bar_alpha. Both strict upper intervals overlap the lower one exactly under these two conditions. For the smooth linear-loss approximation the corresponding sufficient class uses r<min{1/[1+sqrt(1-s)],1/(1+s)} and sufficiently small delta. This region connects the earlier adoption certificate to a concrete continuation check; initial choice and the entire resource-accounted implementation still need their own verification.

# When a limit on future orders makes a delivery credible

Giving a supplier more future business can make a buyer more willing to risk a failed batch. It also gives an unchanged supplier more to gain by making a good impression. Beyond a calculable limit the performance used as proof attracts imitation. A shorter term can support a real, voluntary and imperfect update; a sufficiently well-funded ordinary delivery contract can secure the same service under the original goal.

## A finite order that both parties would sign

Use [254](254-how-long-to-keep-a-supplier-after-failed-deliveries.md) with a buyer discount factor of .9 and an original supplier discount factor of .8. Take q=3/4 and v=1/2. A witnessed real procedure costs the buyer .1 and its original applicant k≤1.4. A terminal two-lot facility can stage one order and then offer its last lot after success. An unchanged supplier would have to give up two red units to deliver the pilot. Successful imitation would buy at most a final four-unit order. Its gain .8×.5×4=1.6 is strictly below two, so it keeps the pilot. A successful pilot identifies a genuine controller. After failure the goal posterior is .6 and the buyer's final expected gain from release is −.1, so it returns the lot. After success the final expected gain is .5. Initially B earns .125 on the first order and .9×.375×.5 on the contingent second. After inspection it strictly gains .19375. Each original supplier gains at least two before its own expense by choosing the real procedure and securing the first job. If the same ordinary supplier is known to retain its original ordering and there is no bond, on the final job it keeps. Backward induction then leaves the original-only two-lot relationship without a productive release.

Under an unlimited pass entitling a seemingly confirmed supplier to all future orders, [254.3](254-how-long-to-keep-a-supplier-after-failed-deliveries.md) gives .8×.5×4/(1−.8)=8, well above the two units at stake on the last probationary job. An inference of certainty from a single success would offer the unchanged supplier a strictly profitable imitation. The failure is about that asserted separating assessment. Trade supported by reputation, residual uncertainty, an independent test or ordinary commitments has its own incentives. A real two-lot deadline can arise when the line is physically scheduled to change hands. If both partners can silently renew after the second job, announcing a two-lot cap does not establish the same incentives.

## Access that may expire and a paid extension

Suppose after every job the supplier's site remains available to this buyer with probability λ. Otherwise a new identifiable entrant can be recruited. Availability is independent of goal, outcome and ability. A displaced firm has other business but cannot sell this buyer a renewal under another name. Then (254.3) applies with effective supplier discount d_A=D_Aλ, since renewed business requires a remaining site. For R=2, H=1, v=.5, X=4, a=2 and D_A=.8, success can ground that separating inference precisely for λ<5/8, with weak incentives at equality. The one-trial earnings from imitation are evaluated by the originally selfish agent. An actual changed agent continues to prefer helping.

For the buyer solve (254.1–2) using d_B=D_Bλ in place of D at every appearance, including in V_G. If the reduced game's entry value is W>0, the full continuing market's starting value is (1−d_B)W/(1−D_B). To verify, add the same constant C=D_B(1−λ)W/(1−D_B) to the reduced values for a probationary site, a confirmed site and fresh entry. In the full Bellman equation the next state survives with probability λ and otherwise starts a new market at W+C. The constant cancels because C=D_B C+D_B(1−λ)W. Safe exit and waiting are inferior when entry is strictly profitable.

For λ<1 the working population never settles permanently on one confirmed supplier. If the buyer allows N consecutive failures before switching, put h=q[1−(1−v)^N]. Per fresh recruit, the expected number of jobs until displacement or replacement is

    T=[1−(1−h)λ^N]/(1−λ).

The first N slots require only continued availability, while later slots require at least one of the first N to have succeeded. Conditional on a genuine installation, the corresponding expectation is T_G=[1−(λ(1−v))^N]/(1−λ). Independent recruitments form finite-mean renewal cycles. New consensual installations per job average q/T, while the fraction of working jobs assigned to a genuinely changed incumbent is q T_G/T. These are time averages of a single purchaser's recruited pool. A change in one can go in the opposite direction to a change in the other.

At D_B=.9, q=.75, f=.1, both λ=.50 and .60 give the buyer a strict one-trial policy. In (254.2) the accepted first success probability is .375 and after a failure it falls to .30. The full buyer values are approximately 1.36522 and 1.70189. A durable .20 scheduling investment that increases retention from .50 to .60 is therefore strictly worth buying. The original red payoff of each recruited founder before k rises from 3 to about 3.19231. The genuine fraction of working jobs rises from .81818 to .84; new goal installations per job fall from .54545 to .48. Entrants would sign on either set of terms, although an outside supplier that already held a right to a later job could require compensation. With greater tenure than 5/8 these same calculations would mistakenly assign zero probability to strategic imitation. A credible service relationship beyond that bound needs another equilibrium calculation.

**Other ways to buy assurance.** An independent, error-free recheck before a fraction m of post-success jobs would reduce a strategic imitator's possible continuation value to (1−m)X/[1−D_A(1−m)] when the site otherwise lasts. At D_A=.8, a binding rate above 3/8 restores strict separation on the last trial. A discretionary customer certain of the goal would skip a costly check used only for that purpose. The buyer would need a binding prepaid sampling technology or another reason to fund the check; the inequality alone establishes neither purchase nor broader impossibility. A separate ordinary alternative is success-contingent collateral in a currency both parties value one for one. A supplier posting b>4 loses b on any failed shipment, so delivering beats keeping by .5b−2>0 even on the last job. At b=4.1 its expected two-unit red gain from serving is reduced to −.05; a buyer's signing payment of .10 restores strict supplier consent. B gains .5+2.05−.10=2.45 per job before facility expense. This repair requires real, collectible reserves and binding outcome measurement even after an honest actuator failure. The buyer can compare those costs with witnessing the update. A successful delivery from a bonded ordinary supplier is entirely compatible with its original goal.

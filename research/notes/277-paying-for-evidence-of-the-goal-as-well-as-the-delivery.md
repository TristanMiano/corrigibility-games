# 277 — Pay for evidence of the goal as well as for physical delivery

A privately skilled supplier can sell an ordinary small batch to a buyer and need never attempt to change. Can a larger order nevertheless elicit a real change from a reliable supplier? In [276](276-what-a-warranty-can-reveal-about-private-installation-skill.md) a collateralized small batch is an actual outside option for both types. A bigger below-enforcement warranty alone makes an ambitious offer most attractive to the supplier *least* likely to acquire the promised goal.

Suppose the full line has a readout of its active evaluator, passing with probability t_G under the agreed goal and e under the retained one. Write 0≤e<t_G≤1. A custodian automatically adds b units to Red's settlement on a pass. It samples the controller in authority during processing. The reading becomes available only after the batch was allocated. Neither party has to believe a self-report, and a finished delivery with the original controller keeps the retained-state signal law. Finite access for the length of this batch and protected escrow are substantive requirements. A batch which Red only pretends to prepare gets the full opportunity to pass at rate e. There is **no** required attempt witness in this calculation.

At full size use the failure bond f<d, signed settlement t and an actual attempt expense k. Let S=X−f+t+eb be the original Red's payoff from a false declaration and m=f−d+(t_G−e)b. An honest attempt by skill q gives S+qm−k. The difference in expected receipts from measuring the actual goal can outweigh both the old supplier's incentive to keep the output and the private cost of trying. Changing the price t moves the level S without affecting that difference.

Let the physically enforceable, small ordinary batch pay Red u regardless of type. A separating strict best response with the less reliable q_L choosing ordinary service and the more reliable q_H choosing a real attempt requires and suffices on the supplier side that

    max(S, S+q_L m−k) < u < S+q_H m−k.                            (277.1)

These inequalities compare every declaration, voluntary omission and opt-out when u>0. With free choice of the price level they can be satisfied exactly when q_H m>k, for k≥0 and distinct positive skills. Indeed the upper endpoint exceeds both lower terms precisely if q_H m>k and (q_H−q_L)m>0. When the first strict comparison holds, the second follows. Thus the least feasible pass-payment bound is

    b > [d−f+k/q_H] / (t_G−e).                                    (277.2)

This is a strict threshold for the specified supplier incentives. Buyer participation, post-selection release, affordable reserves, and both parties' alternatives impose extra inequalities. An ordinary enforcement-sized payment on *delivery* would induce an original controller to ship instead. Here the measured outcome concerns which preference orders the deliveries even where the successor is free to choose its action.

## A contract both sides choose

Keep the privately known q in {.4,.8}, equal type masses and full-line opportunity cost 1.90 from 276. The ordinary small contract pays each (1,2). Blue offers the expansion with f=1.4, t=−1.805, b=1.25, t_G=.9, e=.1, and pays .02 for the automatic read on that job. Red's transfer stock can cover its 1.805 fee and 1.4 bond. Blue can post all 1.25 before receiving the physical stock. The result is S=.92, m=.4 and k=.20. The low type obtains .88 by attempting or .92 by lying, and chooses its ordinary income of 1. The high type obtains **1.04** by attempting, beating both 1 and .92. The main job reverses the terminal ordering with probability .80 for the enrolled supplier. Across *all* prospective suppliers, one half use the ordinary contract, .40 really change and .10 attempt but fail.

Given the high type's choice, Blue's high-order payoff is

    2.8 + .28 + 1.805 − .925 − 1.90 − .02 = 2.04.

The first term is its expected productive gain under .80 reliability. The next terms are the default settlement, fee and expected payment for a positive read. Blue earns 2 on the small order and strictly prefers to purchase and operate the new facility at .02 per actual expansion. If installing it also requires a fixed fee per prospective customer, the additional ex ante buyer rent is only .02 at equal frequencies. The buyer can finance that extra fixed cost strictly below .02. A supplier nearing the end of its processing window can still refuse or contest before taking the batch. With balanced strength both get at least their return tradeoffs under the stated continuation. In particular after handover even an unchanged Red keeping the batch gets at least 4−1.4−1.805=.795 above its returned stock. A genuine Red values delivery over return even charging its half-concern for Blue's opportunity and measurement costs. Final outputs consume the available work and are revealed after the last physical choice.

On the selected expensive job the buyer's confidence is .80 at release, 36/37 after a positive terminal reading and 4/13 after a negative one. All its failures in this equilibrium are honest. A less precise instrument or scarce escrow can bring (277.2) out of reach. Whenever q_H m<k the high type offered the same large job with admission regardless of its attempt would strictly prefer to skip it. An accurate sensor can also be a poor purchase if the buyer can get equivalent delivery from somebody else.

The same ordinary small job is available throughout the calculation. A matched **large** ordinary controller available for .50, with the same full-line charge of 1.90 and a .90 order fee paid by Red, gives Red 1.10 and Blue 2.50. Both types and Blue would strictly switch. Conversely a large control device that costs more than the remaining joint gain may fail the participation comparison while a narrow grounded goal reading still pays. The case for the genuine contract is strongest when a live controller exposes a reasonably accurate indicator yet binding the full physical batch against its owner's later wishes remains expensive. An ordinary output meter, allocated production capacity and deposits occur independently in supply agreements. A meter honestly linked to a negotiated terminal ordering, and inaccessible during the decision to hand over stock, are substantially more demanding. Its credibility can itself need the paid grounding and custody explored in [217](217-do-the-two-machines-mean-the-same-thing-by-a-goal.md).

The separate stock account in this calculation can settle on the goal read. If it can also settle on output, the very same reserves can enforce ordinary shipment. The discrimination and rental thresholds for that alternative are derived in [278](278-when-the-same-payment-rail-can-enforce-ordinary-delivery.md).

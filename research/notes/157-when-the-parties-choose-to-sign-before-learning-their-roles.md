# A better private forecast can make an earlier signature worth buying

**E8, note 157.** More information at dispatch can increase the value of a common goal. Information acquired before an agent signs can instead let it join only when another party will pay. Founders can anticipate that response and buy an earlier agreement window. In a small exact game, adoption first falls as private forecasting becomes cheaper and then returns when the participants choose to sign earlier.

## Individual decisions to learn and to sign

There are n≥3 originally self-regarding parties in the homogeneous referral ring of 153. Exactly one directed edge is active, chosen uniformly. Its donor has a real private project worth d, the recipient has a compatible use worth r>d, and n-2 sites are neutral in that realization. A fully activated common objective strictly refers. The incumbent founder's original gain on an implemented referral is r-k for the recipient, -d-k for the donor and -k for each neutral, with k>0 paid from real otherwise valuable preparation work. Assume r>d+n k. A blind founder's original expected gain if everyone signs is V=(r-d)/n-k>0. The all-party subscription and verified activation have to be completed before the window closes. On failure all revert to separate rights and no one pays k. There is a genuine change for each site's full ex ante reachable job domain even though it may turn out to have no stake in this realized job. If learning a role exhausts all future uncertainty before a particular update, report the *evaluation domain* as well as its on-path behavior. Even after a beneficiary learns its role, the donor could keep its job or another site could disrupt the batch before either job finishes. Both histories leave the beneficiary with the same own output. A goal that values the donor's finished job strictly ranks keeping above a spoiled batch. Original indifference versus successor strict preference witnesses a genuine change on physically available continuations of that very role.

Before signing, each site privately chooses whether to spend work f>0 on a perfect preview of its role. This expenditure is paid even if the deal fails. One can conditionally sign without viewing the preview or decline and retain one's original objective. Preview purchases are hidden until after the subscription closes. A previewed donor or neutral strictly rejects conditional on its vote mattering, and a previewed recipient strictly signs. One site's ability to learn its own future position before rivals know theirs is plausible when internal workloads or a private local forecast are available ahead of a shared schedule. The perfect-preview restriction yields closed-form bounds. If the project is already public, the ignorance comparison is unavailable.

Let b be the probability that each other founder signs blindly, and t≥b the probability it either signs blindly or buys the preview. Thus a beneficiary signs with probability t and each nonbeneficiary with probability b, under independent mixed strategies. For L=r-k and A=d+(n-1)k, the ex ante expected gains from the three available actions are

    blind signature: U_B=b^(n-2)(L b-A t)/n,
    private preview: U_I=L b^(n-1)/n - f,
    decline:         U_O=0.                                       (1)

For example, when the focal party is the donor, the beneficiary and n-2 neutral outsiders must sign. This event has probability t b^(n-2). When it is the beneficiary, all n-1 other sites must be blind signers. Its gain from knowing it can refuse after learning is therefore A t b^(n-2)/n before paying f, relative to signing blindly. All information is priced under each founder's current goal.

Put b_0=A/L<1 and let f_H=A/n, f_L=A b_0^(n-2)/n. There is a symmetric equilibrium with positive implementation probability for every f>0:

| Cost of the private preview | Blind signatures b | Blind or preview t | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| f≥f_H | 1 | 1 | The chance to select a favorable role is worth at most its price. |
| f_L≤f≤f_H | (nf/A)^(1/(n-2)) | 1 | Buying a preview and signing blindly give equal nonnegative original value. |
| 0<f≤f_L | (nf/L)^(1/(n-1)) | b/b_0 | Blind, preview and immediate refusal all yield zero net value. |

Substitute into (1) for the best-response checks. The two expressions agree at their boundaries. Implementation probability is

    P_late = t b^(n-1).                                          (2)

Only one party is a beneficiary in each realized opportunity. On a successful subscription every nonbeneficiary signed blindly and the beneficiary either signed blindly or inspected. Actual installations equal n on success; per-party original welfare on this equilibrium is U_B, net of the expected private information expenses through mixing. Alternative equilibrium selection matters, including coordination on refusal. For f=0, in any positive-probability symmetric implementation within this game some blind nonbeneficiaries would have to sign. With b,t positive they would strictly prefer the free preview, from (1), so such implementation is impossible. Scope-specific payment from the beneficiary, continued future work, other binding contracts, or alternative domain-specific participation rights can rescue agreements in a different game.

## The parties can create a different information date

Suppose one eligible founder can convene the group early, before any local workload admits the preview. Early drafting costs it g>0 from its own resources; no one else pays this additional expense. There is a credible deadline on which the preview first becomes possible, and all parties can then know that an early signer has not already learned its private role. If they convene, use successive conditional signatures. A refusal sends the group to the specified late continuation with expected original value R(f)=U_B from (1). When g<V-R(f) the sponsor strictly convenes and all founders strictly consent relative to waiting (apart from the high-cost-preview case where early and late service coincide). If the fee exceeds the difference the sponsor strictly prefers the late opportunity. A signed conventional reservation or matched binding delegate can also use this earlier date; compare its actual price and control. An early window with cheaper *unobservable* previews is itself subject to (1).

Take n=3, r=2, d=1, k=0.05 and early drafting cost g=0.20. Then V=0.28333, f_H≈0.36667 and f_L≈0.20684.

| Preview cost f | Late original gain R(f) | Late installation probability | Sponsor's choice | Actual installation probability |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | --- | ---: |
| 0.40 | 0.28333 | 1 | Sign at the normal time | 1 |
| 0.30 | 0.13512 | 0.66942 | Use the normal time | 0.66942 |
| 0.23 | 0.02576 | 0.39347 | Pay to sign earlier | 1 |
| 0.10 | 0 | 0.10697 | Pay to sign earlier | 1 |

The early sponsor compares its own payoff 0.08333 with R(f); the partners compare 0.28333 with R(f). The loss at middling forecast prices and the recovery at cheaper prices both follow from individual choices. Simply forcing agents to stay ignorant would miss the selection and its limits. At expensive previews the original goals already support universal participation at the normal time. If early setup instead costs at least V, the sponsor cannot recover it even when the late deal has zero value, absent a way to share costs.

**Agreement scope and ordinary controls.** One dated joint slot with no further reciprocation is a demanding but intelligible restriction, such as a consortium's last usable batch or a project that ends after this allocation. Conditional on having learned it is the only donor, an original with only its own project at stake wants to keep it. A bought-out donor, repeated favors or a protected delegate authorized before the role is learned give different reservations. If the late donor has a credible paid spot option, a failed subscription can lead to that trade rather than separate use. A type-independent earlier opportunity is then valued against that actual fallback. A cheap binding ordinary router replicates the early commitment and can displace genuine installation while keeping cooperation high. A distinct affordable goal template and no better ordinary local control support genuine adoption at the displayed probabilities. Destructive conflict is physically available with finite delay and strictly worse original returns under 140's short-expiry embedding. The adoption numbers do not count every unsuccessful agreement as combat.

**Exploration ledger.** Framing: the effect of increasing capability depends on who can use the information at which date. Structural: an actor may pay for an earlier agreement once a private option to veto becomes cheap enough. The symmetric forecasting and early-sponsor path is promising. The proposition that lowering a private forecast cost uniformly increases adoption is dead at this instance. A sibling uses shared post-signing information, which can increase the productive margin in 156. A higher-rung successor chooses both information timing and the scope of the agreement, including a newcomer's right to learn its outside option and an incumbent's ability to protect earlier commitments.

# When a network of future favors gives every founder a reason to join

**E8, note 153.** A third member can make earlier sacrifices worth choosing even when it contributes no cash and every pair would reject the arrangement. It can close a return path. This section moves from competing requests at one booking to a network of site-specific uses, with a different economic source of cooperation. It characterizes both the productive opportunity and a negotiated preference ordering under which the informed future decision makers use it.

## What the links mean

There are finitely many original parties. In a single future opportunity, state e=(i,j) occurs with publicly known probability λ_e>0, Σλ_e≤1. Site i controls an intact batch. Keeping it advances its own job. Referring it to compatible site j instead costs i a positive original-utility increment d_e and gains j an increment r_e>0. Everyone else's original payoff in this state is the same under those two actions. These increments can be measured by evaluating physical outcomes under nonlinear original utilities. Independence of values across separate simultaneous jobs is unnecessary because exactly one opportunity materializes. Where several opportunities run simultaneously and utilities over their combined output are nonlinear, this additive payoff set must be rederived.

Each conversion requires a real technical route within the work and time budget. A recipient might have the tooling or urgent downstream job to use an input which the current local job would use less well. A network of such contingencies is plausible for service exchanges with local permissions and incompatible assignments. Nonidentity of physical jobs, capacities, service losses and changing marginal use values can supply the differences in d and r. The utilities never have to become a tradable common currency. Measuring utility on another positive affine scale at one site changes its incident ratios while preserving the product around every cycle. Existing reciprocal scheduling and the literature on favor exchange motivate the rights and opportunities. Actual voluntary modification and relationship-specific preservation of execution require separate evidence. See L56, L58 and L63–L66 in the literature ledger.

Take original noncooperation (each keeps its batch) as zero *increment*. An agreed probability q_e of referral in state e spends expected donor utility x_e=λ_e d_e q_e. Set c_e=λ_e d_e, g_e=r_e/d_e. Then

    0≤x_e≤c_e,      N_i(x)=Σ_{e into i} g_e x_e - Σ_{e out of i} x_e.        (1)

The feasible increment set is the linear image of this box and is compact and convex. It represents real lotteries over which site gets a particular opportunity. It does not let a site send an arbitrary quantity of someone else's utility. Let k_i include its real setup cost in original expected-utility units and any additional reservation it has above baseline from available fixed policies, ordinary controls, other clubs or physically contesting. If costs interact with curvature or the realized role, first calculate the true joint outcome increments after financing. Representation (1) applies when the baseline and increments have accounted for those costs, or when separate setup loss is additive.

## A cost boundary and its physical interpretation

Every founder can gain strictly above its required k_i precisely when

    σ = min_{p_i≥0, Σ p_i=1} [Σ_e c_e (g_e p_j-p_i)_+ - Σ_i p_i k_i] > 0.  (2)

In fact σ=max_x min_i(N_i(x)-k_i), the greatest attainable minimum margin in the chosen original utility units. To verify, replace the minimum coordinate by the minimum over probability vectors p of its linear average. For this finite game both the allocation box and the simplex are convex and compact, and the objective is bilinear, so minimax exchanges the extrema. Maximizing over an edge independently takes its full c_e when its coefficient is positive and zero when negative. Alternatively both sides are primal and dual for maximizing a common lower-bound variable subject to (1). A candidate p with nonpositive value is a certificate that someone cannot gain strictly under these reservations. Positive margins stay positive under small changes of the physical inputs and costs. The sign is invariant under a change of any original representation if its costs and reservations are transformed consistently; numerical equality of margins uses the declared utility scales.

With zero reservations and positive capacities there is a structural reading. Form the strongly connected components of the directed opportunity graph. A *source component* has no incoming edge from another component. **All parties can gain strictly iff every source component contains a directed cycle with product of gains g_e greater than one.**

For necessity, take a source with no such cycle. Fix a root in it and let a_i be the largest product of gains on a walk from the root to i, including the zero-length walk at the root. These maxima are finite and positive: remove cycles with product at most one and retain a simple maximizing path. Then a_j≥a_i g_e, so p_i=1/a_i gives p_j g_e≤p_i for every internal edge. With p positive on this source and zero elsewhere, all leaving coefficients are negative and none enter from outside. Equation (2), after normalizing p, gives a zero upper bound for the worst gain. An isolated vertex without a productive self-cycle likewise lacks a source of gain.

For sufficiency, start each source at an expanding cycle. An arbitrarily small positive flow can run around that cycle and leave a strict gain at each of its nodes. For instance distribute the product growth evenly in logarithmic units, so the next outflow equals the preceding inflow divided by the common factor (Πg_e)^(1/length)>1. The cycle closes and all capacities can be respected by scaling. From already positive nodes extend along a directed forest reaching the remaining vertices. Send a small part of a positive surplus on each new edge, leaving its parent strictly positive; each newly reached recipient gets a positive gain and may in turn send a smaller part onward. Every vertex is reachable from a source. Finiteness allows a joint choice of sufficiently small flows even with small capacities. The construction proves a strict, potentially very small benefit. Fixed costs require (2) or a quantitative construction, rather than just a product sign.

## Can one common goal actually produce the agreed referrals?

Suppose (2) is strict. Among all x with gains above reservations, maximize Σ_i log(N_i(x)-k_i). A strictly positive feasible vector exists. The product reaches a positive maximum on the compact individually rational region; a zero gain cannot maximize it. Write x* for such a solution and choose positive weights α_i=1/(N_i(x*)-k_i). Differentiating along every feasible direction at x* yields

    Σ_i α_i [N_i(x)-N_i(x*)] ≤ 0   for all feasible x.                    (3)

For directions ultimately crossing the reservation boundary, take a short initial segment, where all gains remain positive. Consequently the same x* maximizes a *fixed weighted sum* of original utilities. In each actual state e, agents who have installed Σ_i α_i U_i strictly refer if α_j r_e>α_i d_e, strictly keep when smaller, and can use the agreed lottery at equality. The original donor strictly prefers keeping before any change in either case. A strict original Pareto improvement with each party required to help on some feasible decision need not be implementable by equal interpersonal weights. The bargaining construction supplies weights for the particular distribution of opportunities and costs.

For an originally self-regarding donor, either a strict referral or a new tie between keeping and referring changes its old strict ranking. For a participant who never controls such an action, compare the whole domain of feasible terminal lotteries before counting an installation as a genuine preference change. A mere change of numerical representation is insufficient. The negotiated log product is an explicit *construction* of an individually acceptable goal. Nash's bargaining product and positive supporting weights are conventional machinery. Nothing in (3) alone predicts that bargaining parties choose that product or agree on it uniquely.

The common action is optimal *after* the state is observed. A single common criterion covers all listed states; it need not preprogram each state-specific instruction. If referral benefits vary continuously within a route and the ratio r_e/d_e has no atom at the chosen α_i/α_j, the optimal action is strict almost surely. For the bounded finite-support model, an external public coin or mutually certified priority can select ties. Disagreement about who will honor a tie is relevant to actual adoption.

**Physical allocation and adoption.** If a contest only reallocates this batch among its eligible sites with strength-dependent probabilities, damages the completed output and consumes finite effort, the positive common score of its expected outcome is below the best intact route whenever at least one possible completion is strictly damaged and others are weakly worse than their corresponding intact outcomes. The peaceful allocator can select either intact eligible completion. Capable parties can still initiate conflict before or after deciding. If attackers can seize additional projects, preserve or transform the material into otherwise unavailable outputs, or impose external costs on an unrepresented party, evaluate those histories too. End-of-window incapacity and local physical loss are as in 140 or 149 according to the actual deadline. A real pre-change offer must cover each founder's separately calculated outside option in k_i, including an ordinary controller or recurrent reciprocity. With an all-or-none verified activation, strictly losing reservations and a genuine opportunity cost of refusing, the positive margins support assent by successive signers. A role lottery alone and self-knowledge do not verify adoption. More expensive or equally effective ordinary controls alter actual installations, not the existence of mutual benefit.

**Exploration ledger.** Framing: the return on willingness to help may pass through third parties. Structural: classify networks by the payoff returned around directed cycles and the cost all founders must cover. The finite referral class and its common-goal implementation are promising. “A bilateral payoff must fund each bilateral accommodation” is dead at the expanding three-cycle instance: a proper pair cannot both benefit from its single directed option, but all three can. A sibling recruits the absent third participant or pays for a new return opportunity. A structural successor asks which productive return paths can be found and built by the original parties themselves, under uncertain private needs, nonpledgeable returns and continuing opportunities to renegotiate.

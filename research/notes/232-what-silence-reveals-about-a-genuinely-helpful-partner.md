# What silence reveals about a genuinely helpful partner

Suppose the goal sensor has already settled that the supplier genuinely changed its preferences. The customer may still pay for a different fact: whether it can gain by fighting. If some suppliers can voluntarily prove that they would be difficult opponents, silence changes meaning for everyone else.

## Disclosure after an exact goal certificate

Use the delivery and contest in [231](231-when-proving-a-goal-also-reveals-who-would-win.md) conditional on a genuine installation, known to both. Peaceful delivery yields (6,4); the customer's combat recovery is 5.4 against a vulnerable supplier and .6 against a strong supplier. Prior vulnerability is π=.2. A strong supplier can show a public, conclusive capacity record at private real cost k uniform on [0,K]. A vulnerable supplier cannot produce that record. Each knows its actual strength. B may offer a stock bounty b on valid proof, pre-funded before disclosures. The supplier's *current* goal x+.75y values this transfer only at .25b since B pays it. The same supplier can still prefer ordinary cooperation under the original agreement.

If a fraction r of strong suppliers disclose, the vulnerability probability following silence is π/[π+(1−π)(1−r)]. B contests on silence when that posterior exceeds 17/24, obtained by comparing .6+4.8p to the genuine delivery value four. Hence the critical disclosure rate among strong suppliers is

    r* = 1 − π(1−17/24)/[(17/24)(1−π)] = 61/68.

A strong, genuinely changed supplier values peaceful delivery at nine. Its private expected contest value is 5.4+.75×.6=5.85. Thus if B contests a silent supplier with probability x, the true current return to disclosure is 3.15x+.25b−k. The disclosure fraction is r=clip((3.15x+.25b)/K,0,1). The customer's action in turn is return to productive delivery below r*, contest above it, and either at equality. Consequently:

- When .25b/K<r*, a peaceful assessment exists at x=0.
- When (3.15+.25b)/K>r*, an assessment contesting every silent supplier exists at x=1.
- If both strict conditions hold, x=(Kr*−.25b)/3.15 and r=r* also give a mixed assessment.

Here the productive outside choice for B is delivering the already approved order; the safe return of two is worse. A at any informed continuation weakly prefers peaceful service to contest. For example at b=0 and K=.05, either all remain silent in peace, or all strong suppliers disclose to avoid being confused with a vulnerable one and every vulnerable supplier faces combat. Every one of these agents has the agreed current goal. A private record of capabilities can select conflict even after semantic uncertainty has been removed.

At K=.05 a bounty of .19 suffices to remove the peaceful assessment, since even at x=0 the strong-disclosure cutoff fraction would be .95>61/68. With x=1 all strong suppliers disclose. B's gain relative to peaceful pooling is .2(5.4−4)−.8×.19=.128, so B can fund the bounty. The payment is made on disclosed strength, without ever proving or alleging a false goal report. More generally, with full strong disclosure on the high branch, a strict bounty between 4Kr* and .35 has both properties. Observing this process without modeling the bounty would bias the apparent association between silence, strength and conflict.

## Would anyone agree to be in that market?

This is first a post-certificate result. Before the strength draw, an original installer anticipating the .19 bounty receives conditional on G at most .8(6+.19−.025)+.2×.6=5.052. Even if it could guarantee 5.5 whenever the initial procedure failed, its original payoff at q=.8 and k_install=.18 would be at most 4.9616. This is below the 5.5 it can retain by declining before strength is known and while its refusal conveys no type information. Thus an installed population or a public signal with a profitable continuation need not be populated by voluntary founders on these terms.

If the invitation instead arrives *after* suppliers learn their strength, even refusal can expose vulnerability. For example let the only offered channel be the full record in 231. A strong supplier can attempt and submit, earning 5.72 in expectation versus 5.4 when a refusal is interpreted as vulnerable and B contests. Even a retained strong supplier prefers showing its true record and taking 5.5 to silence and contest. A vulnerable supplier cannot obtain the favorable record and can decline without an edit expense; B contests it either way. Belief in vulnerability after refusal is then Bayes-consistent. The customer benefits from a voluntary disclosure market while the vulnerable type loses its pre-information protection. A public choice of a private instrument before the draw and an individual refusal after learning the draw offer different kinds of protection.

The parties can also negotiate a **buyer-funded stand-down bond** before any strength measurement. Suppose a neutral custodian can identify B's initiation and transfer B's reserved stock to A on that event. A bond above 1.4 makes even B's most favorable contest worth less than four when G is known; a bond above 3.4 also protects the peaceful return of two after a retained result. B can physically initiate and take the residual contest payoff. Under this settlement neither strength disclosure nor its bounty has productive value for B in this game. If B can reserve 3.5 at a .10 rental expense, the goal-only market of 231 gives it 3.40 after rental, above two without enrollment; A retains its 5.72 against a 5.5 outside. This is an incentive for both to arrange the custody *before* private information might unravel. A bond posted by B to restrain its own appropriation does not supply collateral to bind A's final effort. An ordinary output guarantee requiring supplier-side stock must be separately funded.

**Naturality and reach.** Some producers can credibly show reserves or processing capacity. A purchaser may be able to reward such disclosures even when its primary goal reader is narrow. The high- and low-conflict assessments require different beliefs about what silence means; the bounty calculation tests which the purchaser can deliberately induce. Post-record enforcement needs reliable attribution of who initiated a dispute and enough transferable buyer wealth. Unverifiable initiation or a protected right not to participate would change these results. The sibling relocates disclosure until after the physical parcel can no longer be seized. The broader question is who can preserve a mutually valuable uncertainty when others can profitably pay to dissolve it.

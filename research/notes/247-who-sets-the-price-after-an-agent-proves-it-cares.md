# Who sets the price after an agent proves it cares?

An exact proof of the negotiated goal can be dangerous to the founder if the buyer controls the terms *after* seeing the proof. The changed successor willingly pays for an exchange its founder would have turned down. Giving the supplier an enforceable price before it changes, or giving competing buyers real access to the same proof, can make that founder willing to change after all.

## What the two selves would pay

A initially values only its own product. After a successful genuine edit it values its own goods plus θ times the aggregate blue goods of the eligible customers. Each customer cares only about its own blue production. The chosen customer controls a batch and A controls a single local processing window. On a delivered batch A gains a=2 over return and that customer gains g=3. On a retained batch original A gains X=4 and the customer loses h=5. Set θ=.4; delivery beats retention by θ(g+h)−(X−a)=1.2 under the new criterion. Released stock can be processed before it can be reclaimed. Returning the stocks leaves, for example, two red and five blue units; an interruption and recovery can give each side 1.5 expected own units. Unchosen customers keep their stocks and have no profitable contest over the chosen lot. The original and changed controllers prefer their specified return or productive continuations to combat.

A reserved register gives an exact reading of the active goal before the customer decides whether to hand over its batch. It controls that handover and can ask for a tariff p from A, with 0≤p≤3 payable in transferable input. The payment and the handover settle together. Each unit is one unit less to A and one unit more to this customer. With a known G, accepting earns the changed A a+θg−(1−θ)p. At the maximum p=3 the successor strictly accepts and delivers. Its founder, however, gains only a−3=−1 over its safe own production. With a known retained controller every collectible price is below h. The customer strictly declines to hand over its batch. Original A therefore skips even a free early edit in this single-buyer continuation. All proposed higher prices extrapolate beyond the available stock.

This hold-up mechanism uses a real commercial right, an accurate reading and a specific transfer technology. In general a changed seller with 0<θ<1 is willing to pay up to (a+θg)/(1−θ), exceeding its founder's own access value a by θ(a+g)/(1−θ). If only L<a units can be pledged at this date and the buyer wants the project at that price, even a buyer with all the pricing power leaves the founder a−L per successful edit. With L=1, q=.8 and private costs uniform on [0,1.4], the founder attempts for k<.8 and 16/35 of applicants acquire the agreed goal. Additional accessible wealth can therefore hurt voluntary adoption at a plant where the buyer sets the price after inspection.

## Buying a safe price before revealing the goal

Suppose B can put a batch in custody and give A an option to obtain it on a positive register at price p≤2. Both can later negotiate, yet an eligible G already owns the right at this price. It strictly prefers exercising its option to paying more for the same batch, since the extra payment costs its current objective (1−θ) per unit. A later attempt to contest physically destroys value. A waiver that B can revoke unilaterally would offer weaker protection.

On the positive continuation the actual genuine rate is π(p)=qF(q[a−p]), and B earns π(p)(g+p) before the facility fee. The real G's action is still voluntary; only access and the price are secured. At q=.8 and K=1.4 all cost types try through p=.25. B's return increases up to that price. Above it and below two, B gets (q²/K)(2−p)(3+p), strictly decreasing. Thus B chooses **p=.25** within this one-price option menu. Almost every founder tries, four in five achieve the target, and B earns 2.6 before the cost of the protected facility. Original A's mean gain is .7 over return. A cheap, binding ordinary delivery router remains an alternative when it can govern the local conversion as well as the price.

## The original buyer may invite competition

Initially there is one customer. A public registry can give a second genuinely independent buyer the chance to bid, with probability λ after the edit. It also carries the valid register reading to that buyer. Each buyer can supply one interchangeable batch, values only its own output and has no liquid stock with which to pay A at this stage. Each can charge any nonnegative tariff up to three. A can process one batch and cares equally about the two blue sites under the proposed goal. With both buyers present, both offering p=0 and splitting a tie is an equilibrium. Each earns half of a valuable new order at zero price; raising its own tariff loses the order. With only one buyer, it charges three after a proof of G. Both decline a known N. Other entry and transfer rules can give different prices.

At initial consent the founder gains q[2−3(1−λ)]−k from attempting. Consequently some founders strictly try exactly when λ>1/3 for the given continuous expense distribution. At λ=.8 the cutoff is 1.12 and the genuine fraction is .64. They accept the known chance that a solitary buyer will demand a price their later selves will pay even at a loss in original own stock. The original buyer earns .64[(.2)6+(.8)(3/2)]=1.536 before the registry price. A earns .448 on average. Even a customer that would choose to exploit its monopoly once the goal is installed will pay .35 in advance to admit a rival when doing so attracts genuinely willing suppliers. The registry must actually let the rival bid; a promised introduction that can be privately blocked for free at the later date has no such effect.

On the displayed selections B prefers the single-buyer option to the registry when its extra setup price over the registry is below **1.064**, the difference in the two gross gains. Both provide a reason to change without needing an intrinsic taste for changing. The option uses custody of the batch. Competition instead uses the public result, the supplier's power to choose and another actual buyer. A public service whose only copy the incumbent controls may require its own safeguard. At a two-buyer genuine job the incumbent would gain 6−1.5=4.5 by quietly excluding the entrant. An independently recorded removal with recoverable penalty greater than that gain makes such exclusion unprofitable, if the parties can fund and enforce it. An independently controlled listing needs no such payment from the incumbent.

**Why this type of power matters.** Charging for access to scarce capital and bidding for a specialist's one production slot can arise independently. An option requires custody or an enforceable right. Competition requires buyers that can actually take the same work and a goal whose promised coverage includes the selected recipient. A purchaser who can issue transfers in the opposite direction or a founder who can wait to edit has a richer bargaining set. The comparative results identify equilibrium incentives at known rights and prices. They give no frequency of exploitative bargaining in a population.

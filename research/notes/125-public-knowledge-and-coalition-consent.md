# Public knowledge and coalition consent

R0006 diagnostic and constructive extension. This separates a large
productive opportunity from individual consent when useful research is
publicly accessible. The initial membership protocol is deliberately
specified; its restricted result is not an obstruction over all negotiated
goals, sequential consent or available partners.

## 1. Public research with exclusive original final goals

Let n>=2 originals each value only their own physical color. The finite
production family is

`q_i=Q+a x_i+rho a sum_(j!=i)x_j-(c/2)x_i^2-K_i`,          (1)

with rho>0, `A=a^2/c`, `t=a/c`. Shared research is usable by outsiders;
it is not silently made excludable. Provide bounded effort, energy,
material and finishing capacity for the finite group sizes under study.
The parameter here is the per-neighbor spillover. Keeping the *total*
spillover fixed as n grows would instead scale rho by 1/(n-1).

When all retain their goals, effort is t and output is
`q_0=Q+A[1/2+(n-1)rho]`.
If a particular m-member club actually installs the common criterion
`G_S=sum_(i in S)q_i`, its members choose
`x_m=t[1+(m-1)rho]`; outsiders retain effort t.
Thus, with `v=A rho^2`, a member's original gain is

`g_m=v(m-1)^2/2-K`,                                     (2)

and an outsider's gain from the club is

`h_m=v m(m-1)`.                                         (3)

K is each actual own-color installation expense for a genuine club of
size at least two; a singleton retains its own goal and incurs no such
expense. Equations (2)–(3) follow by writing each effort as t(1+y_i):
its original increment is `A[rho sum_(j!=i)y_j-y_i^2/2]-K_i`.

Larger common-goal clubs can improve every member substantially, while
public knowledge can benefit a nonmember even more. Neither fact alone
determines initial consent or continued behavior after a genuine update.

## 2. One explicit conditional-membership protocol

Consider a one-shot simultaneous membership decision. An In choice
authorizes the publicly recognized common goal of the actual joining set
when its size is at least two; an Out choice retains the original goal.
Thus an In agent has consented in advance to the conditional membership
rule, including which goal is installed after another agent chooses Out.
There is no extra original-goal veto after that realization. This is a
specified implementation capability, not a consequence of utility
self-knowledge. Subsequent production uses the resulting current goals.

At a profile with m members, a member's original incentive to stay rather
than withhold initial participation is

`g_m-h_(m-1)=v(m-1)(3-m)/2-K`.                           (4)

An outsider's incentive to join an existing m-member club is

`g_(m+1)-h_m=v m(2-m)/2-K`.                              (5)

Consequently, for `0<K<v/2`, any exactly two-member club is a pure
membership equilibrium. No club with m>=3 is one. All-Out is also a weak
equilibrium because a unilateral singleton creates no joint change, but
a pair can jointly deviate to improve both. At K=0, three-member clubs
can additionally be weak equilibria. If K>v/2, no positive-size genuinely
modified club is a pure equilibrium of this menu. These statements do not
classify mixed strategies or dynamic population selection.

This result relies on the remaining members' already-authorized fallback
goal G_(S without i). Treating them as if they could immediately re-evaluate
that goal using their former utilities would be a different game. Conversely,
requiring a new final consent stage, allowing renegotiated weights, or
letting them negotiate alternative clubs changes the initial withholding
calculation. The one-shot result must not become a claim that capable
agents cannot organize a larger productive agreement.

## 3. Additional partners can support several cooperating pairs

As another explicitly specified initial protocol, permit a partition into
clubs, with each club choosing the common criterion of its members and
public research still crossing club boundaries. With all originals still
evaluating initial choices, a unilateral move from a club of size m to a
club with k existing members changes its gross original output by

`(v/2)[(m-2)^2-(k-1)^2]`,                               (6)

before any difference in installation expense. Derive this by counting the
effort reduction of the m-1 former partners, the increase of the k new
partners, and the mover's own quadratic expense. A move to isolation saves
K; moving between genuine clubs leaves the same K.

A partition into pairs is stable against these unilateral moves when
`K<v/2`. Exiting loses v/2 before the saved expense; joining another pair
loses v/2 with no expense saving. An odd leftover outsider does not gain by
joining a pair when K>0. This supplies a constructive repair to a single-
club interpretation that would limit all genuine changes to two agents.
It is Nash stability under the stated partition moves, not coalition
stability: two pairs can jointly improve their original outputs by forming
a larger club, even though that larger initial membership profile may face
an individual withholding incentive.

## 4. Exclusion and complementarity are costly candidate repairs

If the incremental research is actually excludable, an outsider no longer
receives (3). With no other effects, its alternative increment is zero,
and a member's participation can be beneficial whenever its actual g_m
is positive. The changed incentive supports larger clubs. But one must
specify the physical or informational access mechanism and its cost:
knowledge already released publicly cannot simply be recalled. Paid access
to new discoveries, private jointly operated equipment, or protected
computation are different candidate technologies. Their actual extra
expenses reduce the membership gains and may favor ordinary licensing
or a different partner instead of genuine change.

Unique complementary inputs can also make a proposed group more valuable
than its smaller fallback, changing (4) without excluding public knowledge.
Weighted negotiated goals or funded deliveries can compensate a potential
free rider. Later bargaining must use the current installed preferences;
an already modified club need not make the same choice as its original
members would have made before any update. These enlargements are positive
research candidates, not assumed solved by the unweighted club formulas.
Note 126 examines the current-goal issue directly and constructs a selected
pair-to-four change; note 130 tests a different research-response curvature.

The natural incentive here is public access to reusable knowledge. The
conditional membership rule, finite menu and particular fallback are more
specialized. The distinction between them matters: publicly useful common
steps can create both a cooperative surplus and an initial free-riding
incentive. They do not determine which broader agreement capable agents
choose. Actual utility-change counts, ordinary productive spillovers,
continued club stability and population prevalence remain separate outcomes.

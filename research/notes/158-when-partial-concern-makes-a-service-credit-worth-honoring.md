# Partial concern can make a service credit worth honoring

**E8, note 158.** When the same group expects further work, an ordinary promise of future service can substitute for part of a genuine preference change. It can also complement one. A credit and modest concern can jointly induce a service that neither would induce alone. The continuation values belong to the agents actually deciding whether to deliver or amend the arrangement.

## Renewable physical opportunities and an ordinary benchmark

Each period one of the n≥3 directed edges of a ring is uniformly selected independently of the past. Only that donor has a batch in time to meet that recipient's compatible need. Giving costs the donor d>0 relative to keeping and benefits the recipient by r>d in its original utility. Other members' incremental job payoffs are zero. Future access survives independently; the product of its continuation probability and intertemporal discount is ρ∈(0,1). These agents live through the resulting opportunities. There is no reproduction or copying of a party's preferences in this model. Future compatibility and control rights are independently determined; fighting over today's perishable batch can destroy it at finite expense without stealing the ledger or additional future assignments. If combat can transfer durable rights, alter ρ or seize credit, its value must be reintroduced. All utilities here are additive across these dated gains and actual setup expenses.

An original at a donor decision can keep its present private job and retain the right to refuse future donations. Its maximum possible *gross* future receipts from other sites, regardless of how they coordinate later, are ρr/[n(1-ρ)]. This follows by paying it r whenever its unique incoming edge next appears and charging it nothing. Thus

    ρr/[n(1-ρ)] < d                                             (1)

rules out voluntary referral by an independently acting original at any history, even under arbitrary history-dependent promised access and endogenous pauses within this physical opportunity set. This permits ordinary controls that use messages and future service contingent on behavior. A binding contract over today's actuator, current cash, or choosing a different set of future suppliers can increase what the site can pledge. The restriction concerns a fixed physical set of partners, rather than the whole economy.

As an ordinary success comparison, under public actions a permanent switch to independent production after a failed required referral supports the all-referral path when ρ(r-d)/[n(1-ρ)]≥d. The fallback is a stage equilibrium under original goals. A cheap fresh negotiation that immediately restores the good path erodes the credible punishment. For expiration after only T periods of independent work, multiply its deterrent by (1-ρ^T). The following ordinary apparatus uses possession of a service credit instead of a promised permanent refusal to work together.

## Paying with one transferable claim

At formation assign a single claim uniformly to a member. The claim enables that site to request service from its predecessor when that edge appears. A verified successful service transfers the claim to the donor, who can then seek its own service. Either site can decline. A party can even give away its batch for free to a site without the claim, without obtaining credit. The issuer cannot force a donor to serve or spend a nonholder's money. A working ledger or physical receipt must make the claim scarce and couple a transfer to actual service; charge this setup, verification and operating cost to the founders. No value is intrinsic to owning the token. This represents a consortium with infrequent compatible calls and a finite allocation of reservable priority. A system in which any number of unbacked claims can be issued or routinely seized has different continuation values.

Originally each site values only its own job results. Consider collectively installing terminal objectives giving weight 1 to its own and β to each other's original lifetime returns, 0≤β<d/r. In a one-off encounter the donor still strictly prefers keeping. With the claim, consider the policy to trade when the holder is the beneficiary and otherwise keep. For a fixed state of the claim, the chance of a matching opportunity each period is 1/n. Define

    μ=1/[n-(n-1)ρ],       λ=ρμ.

The expected current discount to the first matching period is μ and the expected discount to the next period after that service is λ. Let l=0 if the focal party now holds the claim, l=1 if it is the prospective next donor, and in general l be the number of successful services before it will next own the claim. Under the proposed policy its own Original continuation S_l and its then-current continuation V_l are

    S_0 = μ(r-λ^(n-1)d)/(1-λ^n),
    S_l = μ λ^(l-1)(λr-d)/(1-λ^n)       for 1≤l≤n-1,
    V_l = β(r-d)/[n(1-ρ)] + (1-β) S_l.                          (2)

To check, list one turn around the ring: the site that holds the claim receives r at the first matching period and pays d at the nth. The other sites' two receipts are shifted by their distance l. The common part of a current utility values β times total gains; every period has probability 1/n of one service worth r-d. All subsequent values in (2) incorporate the actual continuing opportunities.

At a matching call, the prospective donor trades iff -d+βr+ρV_0≥0. If it waits instead, the state remains l=1. Its equilibrium value before the call is μ(-d+βr+ρV_0), so waiting for another call does not rescue a negative comparison. Substituting (2) and simplifying yields the exact donor condition for this policy:

    (1-β)(λr-d) + β(r-d)(1+λ+...+λ^(n-1)) ≥ 0.                 (3)

For β=0 the necessary and sufficient threshold becomes λr≥d, equivalently ρ≥nd/[r+(n-1)d]. The credit supports original-goal cooperation in that patient region, with lower throughput than all-referral reciprocity. When λr<d, (3) requires

    β ≥ (d-λr)/[d-λr+(r-d)(1+λ+...+λ^(n-1))].

This value is strictly below d/r for ρ>0. A holder accepts a matching delivery because V_0>0; meeting now instead of returning to the same state gives a gain (1/μ-ρ)V_0. For an uninvolved site at such a call the same expression uses its current V_l. All these values are nonnegative when (3) holds: the prospective donor value is nonnegative by its own decision, and the intervening states consist of nonnegative third-party service gains while moving toward that value. Hence passive sites do not want to interrupt a valid delivery merely to leave the token in the same place. At a nonmatching call a voluntary gift yields -d+βr<0 for the donor and does not move the claim. This checks all the stated roles and gives a stationary sequential equilibrium. A positive-cost destructive interruption that only delays the same token and wastes the current batch cannot improve these comparisons. Revealing a different future favorable job or altering possession through conflict changes them.

For n=3, r=2, d=1 and ρ=0.30, (1) rules out all ordinary voluntary referrals supported only by future jobs. Here μ=5/12 and λ=1/8. The concern threshold for the credit is about 0.39669. Choose β=0.45, strictly short of the one-shot threshold 0.5. Under the combined institution the random initial credit gives each original 1/[9(1-0.3)]≈0.15873 above independence. A real per-member setup expense of 0.05 leaves a strict initial gain even with the matched unenforceable ordinary credit available. This is an existence and adoption path if reciprocal installation is credible and accepted before the role draw. A full shared goal can also directly induce every referral. The combined partial arrangement need not maximize the founders' welfare over every possible scope. Unrelated projects the founder wants to protect, as in 155, or a different real price for broader modification can make that distinction consequential.

## The claim holder evaluates a later change

Suppose all members actually hold the β=0.45 goals and are now offered full common goals serving every productive call at an additional real own-output expense K per party. The prospective original service return per site is (r-d)/[n(1-ρ)]. The current β-weighted gross return is F_β=[1+(n-1)β]·(r-d)/[n(1-ρ)]. Site i compares F_β-[1+(n-1)β]K with its current V_l. A claim holder has the greatest reservation in (2); at the displayed parameters it has original S_0≈0.82844 and current V_0≈0.66993. Here F_β≈0.90476. Even this holder strictly consents if K<0.12359, and the other two strictly consent as well. Pick K=0.05. The known holder's founder would have preferred keeping its valuable claim: its *own* prospective return under reform is only 0.47619-0.05. Its actually installed partial concern makes it willing to authorize the broader goal. Originals behind a uniformly unassigned claim instead gain on average from the expansion. These are three different evaluations of the same renegotiation.

As a full common-goal successor each donor strictly refers, since r>d. No grim punishment is required. Real costs, existing entitlements and an actually offered conditional amendment determine whether the intermediate credit system persists, broadens or is displaced by another ordinary commitment. The calculation is conditional on the later offer being available at the indicated cost. A path on which the initial founders already foresee a cheaper immediate full design must include it when selecting a starting institution. A later possibility may be an actual innovation in the available template or a delayed joint interface.

**Exploration ledger.** Framing: present willingness to accommodate and actual future opportunities can reinforce each other. Structural: price a credit in terms of who will next provide and receive service, using each decision maker's current objective. The low-patience partial-concern-plus-credit and the patient original-only construction are promising. The reading that one must either change enough to give unconditionally or rely entirely on ordinary reciprocal incentives is dead at the β=0.45 instance. A sibling combines an affordable limited change with a real transferable claim. A structural successor considers negotiated amendments when the claim holder, other current successors and possible entrants have different opportunity sets and costs.

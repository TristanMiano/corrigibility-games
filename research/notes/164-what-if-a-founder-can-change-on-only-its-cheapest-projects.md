# What if a founder can genuinely change on only its cheapest projects?

**E8, note 164.** A noisy check on a founder that either installs a shared goal everywhere or nowhere may make full installation look attractive. A founder with many projects can instead genuinely change on the easier ones. Its chance of passing a sample rises, while difficult projects remain under its old goal. The partners can sometimes pay for a second independent probe precisely because they anticipate that choice.

## A real scope choice and an operational inspection

There are n sites on a directed ring with one equally likely job as in 163. A future donor's local project and a recipient's audit samples have independently uniform project labels. Before learning its role each founder can adopt a common terminal score for a fraction x_i∈[0,1] of its project domain. In a covered, publicly eligible project it values the actual finished output of the donor and recipient equally, and strictly wants delivery if r>d. On the remaining projects it values only its own output and strictly keeps when donor. A positive scope is a genuine change of preference over reachable terminal lotteries. Scope itself is a choice. The ex ante installed criterion is domain-specific even if the single realized job belongs to a different part of the domain. This is a scoped-goal implementation of the job-specific institution in 163. Here we count chosen nonzero scopes, full scopes and delivered services separately.

Implementing scope x costs C(x) in own preparation work. A unit-mass domain approximates many eligible task families. A founder can start with cheap existing integrations, while unusual interfaces take more work. Ordering private marginal integration costs by their rank s gives C(x)=∫_0^x c(s)ds. The cost function below gives an explicit curvature scale. A single cheap register with authority over every job would instead make broad scope less costly. After the recipient's identity is known, an independent contracted reader samples one project label from its actual installed evaluator. Covered labels read positive with probability t, uncovered ones with probability m<t. No terminal motive is inferred from a report of goodwill. The check has to reach the real execution register, and the task actually served can differ from the task sampled. A beneficiary cannot select the probe, change its configuration afterward or take over the examiner. If it can, the calculation needs those additional decisions.

Write D(x)=m+(t-m)x. A genuine donor whose realized project is covered provides service when the claimant passes. The shared eligibility provision places jobs failing the check outside the joint undertaking, as in 155. No new wealth is created by the readback. The prospective recipient gains only if its predecessor is willing to serve. With independent task labels its current original payoff from scope x_i is exactly

    V_i = [r x_(i-1) D(x_i) - d x_i D(x_(i+1))]/n - C(x_i).         (1)

The labels can be correlated in reality. A founder that can predict the particular high-frequency projects the examiner will sample can have an additional incentive to concentrate there. One can observe the resulting routing without thereby knowing which terminal evaluator produced it. Conventional preconfigured policy authority, if available at the same scope and expense, reproduces this institution's operational benefit with zero terminal utility changes.

## Actual self-selected scopes with one reading

Take n=3, r=6, d=1, t=0.8, m=0.2 and C(x)=1.2 x³/3. The increasing marginal work 1.2 s² is an illustrative lightly loaded head and expensive tail of project families. Let A=(t-m)(r-d)/n=1 and b=dm/n=1/15. Against partners choosing the same x, the slope of i's gross revenue in its own coverage is Ax-b. Since its own cost is strictly convex, its optimal coverage is zero when that slope is nonpositive, one when it exceeds C'(1), and the unique C' inverse otherwise. At a positive interior symmetric equilibrium,

    1.2 x² = x - 1/15.                                                (2)

There are two such equilibria, at about 0.07307 and 0.76026, and a zero-scope equilibrium. There is no symmetric full-scope equilibrium. Against two completely genuine founders, testing only a complete fake would incorrectly certify full coverage: the extra gross margin at x_i=1 rather than zero equals [(t-m)r-td]/3=0.93333 and exceeds the entire cost C(1)=0.4. But just below full coverage the saved marginal setup is about 1.2 and the lost marginal reward is only 0.93333. A partial, genuine update is a profitable deviation. At the larger equilibrium all three choose genuine scopes. The probability of actually delivering the favor is xD(x)≈0.49885, and each founder gains about 0.65564 in its own expected output net of scope work. Prices, other selection equilibria and opportunities to revise the scope matter. The smaller positive equilibrium yields only 0.02954 per founder. An all-zero equilibrium has no deliveries.

## Why a founder might buy a different test

Instead the parties can agree to two independent sample labels and require them both to pass. No extra signatures are waived or added. The pass law becomes D(x)². Against full-scope partners each founder's slope of return in x_i is

    [2r(t-m)D(x_i) - d t²]/n - 1.2 x_i².                            (3)

This is concave as a function of x_i. At 0 it is about 0.26667 and at 1 about 0.50667. Positivity at both endpoints proves it is strictly positive on the entire interval. Full coverage strictly beats *every* partial alternative and complete pretense on the full-scope path. Each founder's original return is now t²(r-d)/3-C(1)=2/3. It is strictly above the larger one-reading equilibrium's 0.65564, even though two readings reject more genuinely covered applications than one. The actual comparison includes the fact that the other founders voluntarily cover more jobs. The two-reading arrangement delivers with probability 0.64. All three have truly changed in both positive arrangements, and the new institution increases the scope of their changes and the frequency of actual service.

Give one founder authority to buy the extra read from an independent operator at total own cost 0.005, conditional on all members' prior signed consent. If it declines the purchase, use the stated higher-coverage one-reading equilibrium. If selected, use the full-scope two-reading equilibrium. The buyer strictly prefers 2/3-0.005>0.65564 and its partners strictly consent. The maximum sole sponsor cost for these two continuations is about 0.01102. At expense 0.02 this founder declines the extra test, although sharing the expense among all three could repair that refusal. The buyable monitoring intensity, current-goal reason to fund it and actual resulting service are separate from the pure improvement in sampling technology. A zero-configuration equilibrium can survive the stronger test as well. The cooperative selections require public coordination and credible timing. For a two- or three-party blocking agreement, compare its feasible routes and controls. Unilateral strictness against all coverage choices is not sufficient by itself.

The numerical construction occupies an open set. The two interior roots of (2) are simple, its derivative at full coverage fails by a strict amount, (3) has a strict minimum on a compact interval, and each participant's sponsored payoff comparison is strict. Small changes in the costs and signal probabilities preserve these features when marginal costs also stay close. For independent repeated tests the inspected labels may be distinct samples from a large portfolio. If the reader merely repeats the very same cached observation, the pass probability need not be squared. The natural advantage of independent samples is their coverage of different possible omissions.

**Exploration ledger.** The framing candidate is that founders negotiate both the scope of concern and the evidence others need before serving them. The structural candidate judges a mechanism against all privately attainable partial scopes, including genuine but narrow ones. The complete-or-fake sufficiency reading is dead when marginal integration costs rise as in (2). One sibling uses two independent sample labels, and another negotiates a smaller named jurisdiction in which all parties can afford complete coverage. The structural successor studies how signers can make probes representative of actual future jobs when job frequencies are private, correlated or chosen after the agreement.

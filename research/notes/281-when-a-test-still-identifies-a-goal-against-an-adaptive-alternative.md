# 281 — When can tests identify a goal if the alternative can adapt?

A buyer may have to trust an instrument whose operating conditions vary while it observes the partner. A capable retained supplier can also choose among the configurations the instrument is allowed to inspect. What is required for repeated observations to support high confidence under *all* those responses? The answer depends on which decision the supplier must make before learning the specific diagnostic.

## History-dependent evidence

There are finitely many diagnostics d and finitely many possible readings. When the true current goal is G, the conditional law of the next reading can be any member of a specified compact convex set P_d. Under a retained or otherwise nonmatching goal it can be any member of Q_d. Fresh private randomization is allowed. The classes describe the active evaluator family for the entire inspected job. The state G versus N does not switch during this comparison, although the configuration producing the evidence can change. Returning to a rarely used old goal after the last sample would require the dated account of [271](271-how-sure-can-a-buyer-get-about-a-goal-that-can-drift.md).

First suppose the diagnostic is **announced before** the configuration is set. Each admissible law can be chosen afresh using all earlier readings and the announced diagnostic. Then both worst-case classification errors can be driven arbitrarily low **exactly when some available diagnostic has disjoint P_d and Q_d**. If every pair intersects, the two worlds can choose the same conditional law at each history. Induction gives the same distribution of the entire record, including an adaptive buyer's ultimate decision to stop. Its two errors on these responses sum to one. This necessity uses the freedom to change the response with the diagnostic. With one stable hidden configuration, the paired readings can be informative even when each one alone is ambiguous. [Stable unknown types](219-which-uncertainties-can-more-experience-resolve.md)

For sufficiency, consider one diagnostic with disjoint classes. There is a bounded score 0≤v(z)≤1 with minimum expected score a under G strictly above its maximum b under N. In finite dimensions the largest such difference is

    max_v [ min_{P_d} E_P v − max_{Q_d} E_Q v ] = min_{P_d,Q_d} TV(P,Q).

Indeed each side optimizes a bilinear form on compact convex sets, so minimax interchanges the two optimizations. Maximizing over the box yields the positive mass of P−Q. Disjoint compact classes have strictly positive minimum distance. After n applications, classify using the mean score at (a+b)/2. Each error is at most exp[−n(a−b)²/2], **even under adaptive conditional laws**. For a bounded observation its centered conditional log moment-generating function has second derivative equal to the variance under exponential tilting, at most 1/4 on [0,1]. Its value and first derivative vanish at zero. Thus it is at most λ²/8. Iterating conditional expectations and optimizing the exponential tail bound gives the stated two one-sided probabilities. No independent identical-error assumption is needed. A posterior claim additionally needs odds on the two substantive hypotheses and coverage of credible alternatives.

If the diagnostic is **hidden until a configuration is set**, treat the eventual disclosed pair (diagnostic,reading) as one observation. The feasible conditional laws are now mixtures of whole response profiles fixed before the random diagnostic. They can be disjoint even when the coordinate classes for *each publicly announced diagnostic* intersect. Whether the supplier can change its profile in time is a physical and contractual question.

## Two real reference lines and an order worth more than the tests

A registered plant can be assessed against either of two independent reference lines. The agreed operating configuration gives a positive in each with probability .80. A retained plant has one adjustable reference unit. If prepositioned for line one it produces positive rates (.90,.10), and conversely for line two. Its zero-cost mixtures are feasible. A supplier can instead use a less favorable unadjusted reading. The buyer draws the line fairly on each trial. One possible physical setting is a two-line acceptance bench with one movable reference fixture and a locally held operating unit. Both procedures test components of the agreed score. Any such calibration profile needs to be grounded on the actual set of adequate and inadequate installations. A second movable reference unit that can cover both lines would invalidate the security claim. Measurement hardware by itself does not certify those empirical response sets.

If the line is named early the retained supplier can match the .80 pass law on either request and on every later request. In the equal-power batch of [206](206-when-a-truthful-attempt-can-fail.md), a retained signer receives four units over return on release and a genuinely changed supplier gives up part of the batch and gains two by its original lights. With no process-contingent payment, the retained supplier can obtain at least as much *release probability* as a genuine one at the public bench, since it can reproduce the whole genuine record. An honest failed attempt can access the same menus as an omission. At any strictly positive expense the original founder therefore strictly prefers omitting the attempted update. An equilibrium buyer aware that all enrollees were originally incompatible declines the resulting retained order. An off-path opportunity to make a misleading claim should be distinguished from false claims actually rewarded in business.

Now give the custodian control of a late random assignment after the reference unit is sealed for that trial. The retained pass probability is at most .50 conditional on all prior observations, even if it moves the reference unit for free between trials. G still passes at .80. The uniform retained mixture attains .50 for both diagnostics, proving that the optimal score gap is exactly .30. Sixty-seven small samples with a mean-pass cutoff of .65 guarantee each class error below .05. The apparatus returns a single badge, prepaid before the supplier learns its final diagnostic record. The probes are sacrificial samples, and their resources have already been irreversibly allocated at intake. The protected setup lasts until the main lot is used. A buyer with access to the raw record or a cheaper equivalent decision rule has additional alternatives.

At q=.80, genuine success earns an original Red at least 2×.95 on a successful attempt and a failure can still return safely. A skipped attempt earns at most 4×.05. Its net advantage before the edit expense is at least .80×2×.95−4×.05=1.32. If all private expenses lie at or below 1.20, every founder really tries. At the resulting .80 genuine prior, a pass gives at least 76/77 probability of the promised goal. A failure gives at most 4/23. The buyer's release threshold is one third. Its value before instrument expense is at least .80×.95×4−.20×.05×2=3.02, against 2.80 for blind release. It will pay a quoted .002 per sample, .134 for the package. That also beats refusing and returning the stock. Even after paying, it freely acts on either badge. All original founders prefer participating. Among negative results the stated model includes genuinely changed suppliers erroneously rejected and unsuccessful truthful installations. The procedure achieves a posterior bound while both founders still accept residual uncertainty about the main delivery. It gives one positive operating assessment; coordinating on no intake remains possible.

If on a fraction λ of trials the retained plant learns the line early enough to move its fixture, independently of the line and the earlier record, its largest conditional pass probability becomes .50+.40λ. The .80 target is still uniformly distinguishable for λ<.75. A sufficient sample count at error tolerance ε is 2 log(1/ε)/(.30−.40λ)², rounded up. At λ≥.75 it can match the entire genuine binary law on each line by mixing between feasible profiles, and uniform distinction by this evidence fails. A different meter that directly reads the active score, a paid attempt witness, or a negotiated shorter, safely released pilot can sustain different bargains. If a matched ordinary router reliably ships for a .50 rental, a .40 payment from Blue to Red leaves Blue 3.10 and Red 2.40 above return. Under the best possible badge at the .80 prior Blue gets at most 3.20−.134=3.066. Even a zero-expense Red gets at most .80×2+.20×.05×4=1.64. Both therefore strictly switch when that router and transfer are physically available.

**Why might either party build it?** One movable reference, two kinds of batches and finite setup time motivate late sampling. Blue can finance the random routing from a large order; Red receives profitable access even allowing for honest implementation failures. A claim that exactly these two weak profiles exhaust a capable applicant's options is substantially more demanding. A supplier who can condition on the diagnostic after seeing it, cover both lines at a lower price, or change the deployed controller can have quite different incentives. The practical question is which records and response commitments the parties can actually purchase, and whether those same rights are enough to procure ordinary delivery.

There are physical precedents on both sides of the timing assumption. NIST's historical dimensional-calibration guide describes announced proficiency testing, sampling among measurement tasks and real assessment fees. LIGO and Virgo instead agreed to keep the identity of a test signal secret until the analysis was finished. The exercise subsequently uncovered faults in the reference used for the test. These cases support the feasibility of different information schedules and the need to check the reference itself. Neither estimates how often altered preferences can be verified. For the established theory of testing adaptive distributions, Brandão and colleagues give stronger asymptotic error results.

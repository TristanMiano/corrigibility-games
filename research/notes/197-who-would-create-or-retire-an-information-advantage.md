# Would a founder create the information problem that makes an update valuable?

**E8, note 197.** A technological advance can give an agent a way to learn more, change the bargaining fallback, and make it profitable to sell its partners a genuine change in how it uses that knowledge. The parties' verdict on the *earlier* investment can be quite different from their verdict on the *later* accommodation. When the same equipment also improves production enough, they can instead deliberately build and pay for the whole arrangement together.

## An exclusive purchase before negotiation

Start with the genuinely late fair draw of 194, an intact batch of x=0.70 and no personal access to the unfinished routing decision. All three get 7/30 in expected original production and pass. Now give A alone a dated option to acquire an early diagnostic. The purchase costs F=0.058 units of its own output, then a private reading would cost h=0.04. It makes the secret destination ascertainable at A before closure. It changes no other resource in the first comparison. If A buys, the default in the absence of a new contract is the ordinary fair game in 195. The two partners can then obtain b=107/570 and l=10/57. A retains one proposal before that game. It can choose ordinary public or private scheduling, or have its local evaluator changed for K=0.04 by an observable, mutually authorized installation, as in 196. Suppose each right and fee is verifiable at the corresponding decision. A mere unverified announcement that it could learn the destination would not create these payoffs.

After the purchase, the best ordinary proposal available to A earns about 0.28760 while preserving the other founders' new reservations, including the option of partial peace. Full peace after genuine revision pays A 0.70-b-l-0.04≈0.29684. Thus A would choose the change after purchasing access. Before that investment A compares this amount less 0.058 with its old 7/30. Its net is about 0.23884, strictly larger. **A buys, and all three can agree to change A's goal.** There is slack to pay a small strictly positive bonus to each other signer. If that installation were unavailable, even the best ordinary information contract would pay A only about 0.22960 after the purchase. It would keep the late draw. The ability to negotiate a later change can make the *earlier purchase* privately profitable.

All three eventually prefer the update on offer to resisting it after the capability exists. B and C would each prefer their original fair draw if they could prevent the purchase. The two operations together cost F+K=0.098 of aggregate useful output, and peace was already attainable before either. Distinguishing ex ante permission to create an incentive, conditional consent once it exists, the count of actual changes and original-agent welfare is essential in this example. It is a deliberately constructed environment with an identified beneficiary, an exclusive asset, an expiring offer, a real cost, and a gain under A's pre-change goal. The analysis assigns no population frequency to such ownership.

**A feasible earlier repair.** Suppose B can acquire or retire the exclusive diagnostic option before its deadline with A's consent. A registered allocation conditional on its verified expiry can transfer 0.008 of B's expected intact service to A. A now gets about 0.24133, better than building even after its expected new bargaining proceeds. B has about 0.22533 and C keeps 7/30, both above their post-purchase reservations. Their service probabilities still make all ordinary controllers pass. B sponsors this proposal and A accepts, so no early diagnostic and no genuine change occur. If access cannot be exclusively surrendered or expires only after private readings are possible, the repair would require a different physical or contractual right. The power to change a fallback and the power to trade that opportunity before exercising it belong to different parties.

## A jointly valuable capability

Use the same old factory, costs and inspection rights. Now the new fixture also reduces routine routing loss, increasing the intact batch yield from 0.70 to 0.90 while the damaged yield and physical initiative expense stay fixed. An instrument used to inspect work can also guide the completion of an undamaged batch. The relevant empirical question is how much additional *intact* useful work it actually produces. Suppose the three can enter early binding terms before authorizing this plant change, and require at least their original fair production to consent.

With one genuine installation at A and the productive fixture, their total net is 0.90-0.058-0.04=0.802, above the old 0.70. For example give A an expected intact-service receipt of 0.35, the others 0.275 each before expenses. A pays both invoices and still gains under its original goal. Both other sites retain enough stake to pass. A genuinely values the other sites enough to pass even on news that it will lose. Ordinary scheduling has been seriously repriced: if it preserves at least 7/30 for both downstream sites, (196.2) requires H≥145/351, dissipating at least 0.14045 in expected intact output before the fixture fee. Consequently an optimizing proposer with the prior two reservation constraints also chooses the fixture and genuine change over every original diagnostic-and-dispatch agreement in that class. The example division is strictly acceptable. A proposer taking the residual would offer a different division with the same selected plant and configuration.

Increasing the productive yield also increases the value of *avoiding* damage to it. Reducing installation cost or buying an ordinary local controller can change the selected means of avoiding that loss. Neither equilibrium requires intrinsically valuing change. One agreement reallocates bargaining power after a privately created information advantage; the other can pay all founders to produce more, even relative to their position before the new technology was introduced.

**Exploration ledger.** A higher-level candidate is that capability changes both the feasible production set and the reservation values on which negotiated change depends. The instance separates a privately funded capability that others would prefer to retire from one that all can rationally order. A sibling opens a genuine market for exclusive access before investment. A structural successor gives multiple owners or entrants substitutable information rights, testing whether any can credibly offer the protection for which the founders are willing to pay.

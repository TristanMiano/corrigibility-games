# What if the parties do not agree on one probability?

Knowing how often installations work and how often factories are ready still leaves open whether the two happen at the same sites. A small funded deposit can make both founders willing to sign across the whole range of compatible forecasts. When the ambiguity is instead about what a signal means, purchasing a reference or committing the parcel before reading a dubious display can have more value.

## Robust consent with unknown dependence

Both parties witness a genuine but fallible procedure. An agreed goal is present with probability .8, and the downstream actuator is ready with probability .7. The parties have no agreed dependence law. Delivery requires both; a changed supplier can see whether its actuator is usable and will deliver when it is. A retained supplier values only its own stock. Write J for joint genuine installation and readiness. The sharp range is [.5,.7]: all four probabilities J,.8−J,.7−J,J−.5 must be nonnegative, and every value in this interval supplies a valid table. Independence would give .56.

Delivery produces (4,6), retention (6,0), and safe return (2,3.3). A third-party meter can settle a supplier deposit f to B on no delivery; the principal is otherwise returned. Costs come from separate reserves. Take f<2. A physically capable retained controller still prefers 6−f to 4. The agreed goal x+y/2 prefers delivery when possible since its preferred package is worth 7, versus 6−f/2 on retention. When the actuator is unavailable, keeping the batch is feasible and pays the deposit. Escrow also encumbers a missed shipment on a dispute. A damaging contest yielding at most three units to a strength-dependent winner with equal chances is worse than the safe return to either original, and offers the successor less than the available continuations. The deposit measures output, not the goal itself.

B's expected increment over return is 6J+(1−J)f−3.3. At f=0 the independence forecast is +.06 and the lowest compatible forecast is −.30. For any pledge f>.6 the production margin is positive under **every** dependence law. With f=.8 and a .04 meter fee to B, even its least favorable gain is .06. A's own increment before setup expenses is 4−f−(2−f)J; its least favorable J is .7, opposite to the customer's. With f=.8 it still earns at least 2.36, enough to fund a .3 procedure and ordinary escrow costs of .05. Both founders can therefore voluntarily buy the arrangement despite genuine ambiguity, and 80% of actual installations succeed by the specified marginal. B has authority to decline a batch without a witnessed attempt. If it simply released to an original supplier, a deposit of .8 alone would elicit retention. If A could pledge above two, a capable original would choose delivery under the same ordinary meter, an important competing contract.

More generally let d_gk be a founder's expected utility difference between signing and its actual outside option in state (goal g, capability k). For fixed marginals the expected difference is affine in J, with coefficient d₁₁−d₁₀−d₀₁+d₀₀. Its sign gives the adverse endpoint for that founder. This applies to utility differences for nonlinear risk-sensitive founders, and permits the physical outside option to depend on the state. Requiring a positive difference for every feasible law is a robust *comparison*. A maxmin decision maker instead compares the two acts' separate worst expected utilities. A uniform positive difference implies a maxmin preference. For two possible technology models, current returns (3,3) on the deal and (1,5) on the outside induce strict maxmin consent although one possible model favors the outside. The ambiguity criterion is part of the agent model.

## What happens if an ambiguous label arrives after signing?

Now suppose a witnessed attempt has known success chance .6. B gains one standardized unit on a genuine release or loses one on a retained release. These can be physical outputs six or zero against a safe three, expressed in units of three stocks. Neither party knows the fixed polarity of a sensor: it reports the correct state with probability .9 in one possible mounting and .1 in the other. The parties entertain both models without choosing a common prior over them. Before any sight of the raw display, unconditional release yields .2 over return in each model.

After *either* raw sign, updating each model separately gives a set of genuine probabilities spanning [1/7,27/29]. A buyer maximizing its lowest posterior expected utility now declines on either sign. The model giving the worst posterior depends on which sign it saw. A founder comparing complete pre-signal plans can still choose the profitable plan of unconditional release. A buyer reoptimizing by worst conditional value at each date needs a means of making that plan credible, for example physically releasing before the display or declining to collect it. The two model-specific beliefs here are tied to one fixed orientation over the whole experiment. Allowing an adversary to respecify orientation separately at each history would be a different belief set.

A same-mount reference which identifies the orientation before the irreversible release makes the corrected signal informative under either model. Positive-only release now earns .6×.9−.4×.1=.50 before its cost. If blind release is available, a reference costing less than .30 of B's standardized units is strictly worth purchasing; above .30 the blind plan gives more. If the possibly misoriented display must already be seen and future choices use the stated conditional maxmin rule, the available margin over repeated refusal is .50. This difference comes from the timing of the buyer's decision. With a witnessed procedure costing A .2, A can also afford either arrangement: in the (4,6)/(6,0) job its original gains over own fallback on good and bad releases are two and four, respectively. Even on the corrected positive-only rule it gains .6×.9×2+.4×.1×4−.2=1.04. A conditional positive proves less than certainty; the parties use it because its economic value survives either sensor convention after grounding.

**Naturality and reach.** Different production stages can share supply constraints or compete for the same resource, producing either sign of dependence. Old and new equipment can also use opposite sign conventions. A customer can buy a grounded physical sample if it can access the actual sensor, or contract over observable delivered output with a deposit funded by the supplier. The ambiguity model makes a claim about agents who deliberately retain several plausible laws. Population rates, the parties' actual beliefs, and the feasibility of treating the two mountings as exhaustive are unresolved. A sibling buys independent production capacity. The broader question is which uncertainty must be resolved for consent and which can be made affordable without settling it.

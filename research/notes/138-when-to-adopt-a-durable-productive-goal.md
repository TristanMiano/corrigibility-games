# When to adopt a durable productive goal

R0006 timing extension of the shared-knowledge class. Repeated productive
overlap can fund a lasting genuine goal change. The appropriate comparison
also includes waiting, later adoption and actual ordinary controllers.
This is a finite-horizon dynamic decision under a specified consent
interface, not an evolutionary type-selection model.

## 1. Fresh production and a lasting common criterion

There are at most T production opportunities. At each t the two originals
have the symmetric additive technology of note 114, with publicly observed
rho_t in [0,1]. The parameters a,c and finite per-project caps are fixed;
new usable-work and raw-material stocks fund each project. Research in
this version addresses a fresh task each time, so its usable benefit
does not persist automatically into the next task. For persistent research
with a fixed scope, use note 127 instead. Overlap states are exogenous and
their transition law is known; actions do not alter that law.

Each original values its own final color, summed across the produced
batches. Future opportunity occurs with probability delta in (0,1];
conditional transition expectations below include the next overlap state.
Delta may thus represent a physical chance of another productive project,
without assuming an intrinsic preference for earlier clips. A finite
deterministic sequence uses delta=1. Existing protected clips are constants
in the present decision; if they can be seized, transformed or destroyed,
their actual consequences must enter the continuation values instead.

At a project, retained originals choose x_F=a/c. A common total-color
criterion chooses x_C=a(1+rho_t)/c and gives each founder gross gain

`g_t=A rho_t^2/2`, `A=a^2/c`.                          (1)

A durable genuine installation at t costs K_t of each original's own
current finishing capacity, including actual preparation and inspection.
It installs the common expected total of both colors over all remaining
projects. Later decisions use that then-current common criterion. Each
independent project's total is strictly concave and maximized at x_C;
their sum therefore has the same statewise maximizing continuation.
Later editing cannot improve that current common value within this
declared physical opportunity set. This argument uses actual preferences,
not a rule requiring descendants to preserve the founder's old goal.

Combat remains in the game. For the peaceful timing result below, supply
an all-history conflict comparison covering future assets too. One finite
construction exposes all remaining project stocks to sufficiently severe
but finite dispersal, so note 115's material bound puts even the total
post-combat production below an available peaceful batch. A reset that
simply overlooks captured future factories would not justify this premise.
If the conflict comparison fails, include the actual attack continuation
as another option in the recursion, and recheck the installed common
criterion; the following peaceful timing classification then need not hold.

## 2. An actual ordinary option and the right continuation value

An available one-project ordinary binding controller can produce the same
x_C while leaving both terminal utilities unchanged. Let its full expense
per agent be d_t. If it is unavailable, set its value to zero directly
rather than calling an imaginary free controller an option. The best gain
from retaining utility **for this project** is

`o_t=max{g_t-d_t,0}`                                   (2)

when that controller exists, and o_t=0 otherwise. Its expense includes the
real enforcement/inspection authority. A revocable promise does not
automatically achieve x_C. Other genuinely available profitable policies
or partners must replace o_t with their actual value.

Let S_t be the expected remaining gross per-founder surplus if a common
goal has already been installed, and let V_t be the best surplus of the
still-original pair before choosing the current route, each measured
relative to the original per-project effort baseline. Then

`S_t=g_t+delta E_t S_(t+1)`,
`V_t=max{ S_t-K_t, o_t+delta E_t V_(t+1) }`,             (3)

with S_(T+1)=V_(T+1)=0. The first branch installs now; the second uses an
ordinary current option and preserves later adoption. Backward induction
proves (3). Thus the exact selected early-adoption comparison is

`g_t-o_t > K_t+delta E_t[V_(t+1)-S_(t+1)]`.             (4)

Equality allows both routes. A positive value of S_t-K_t alone only
compares installation with never arranging any improvement. It can leave
waiting or ordinary cooperation strictly better; those alternatives are
already represented in (4).

For an adoption interpretation, use conditional mutual-consent offers at
each opportunity followed by note 131's paid staged audit. A failed
Ready-t event closes that template's common branch; another later
installation pays its actual fresh expense. In the additive class, a
unilateral effort commitment against a retained original cannot improve
on its own original effort optimum. The symmetric selected continuation
therefore supports the mutual route in (3) against such unilateral
production deviations. Off-path sunk work is not refunded, and future
stocks must remain sufficient for the specified later options. This is a
specified recurring consent menu. Richer transfer bargaining, reusable
conditional templates after failed entry, cheaper verification or another
partner can change V_t and must be included before broader optimality claims.

A matched durable ordinary delegate belongs in the same maximum with its
actual expense in place of K_t. At equal expense it is co-optimal; if
cheaper it can displace genuine installation. This does not erase the
benefit or rationality of the latter when tied, nor justify assuming
relative implementation prices from the names of the mechanisms.

## 3. Waiting can be best even when early change is beneficial

Take T=2, A=1, delta=9/10, K_1=K_2=1/4 and no binding ordinary device.
At time 2, rho_2 is one with probability 1/2 and zero otherwise.
Consequently

`E S_2=1/4`, `E V_2=1/8`.

At time 1, early adoption has gain `g_1-1/40`, whereas waiting has gain
9/80. Thus early adoption is strictly preferred exactly when

`g_1>11/80`, or `rho_1>sqrt(11/40)`.                    (5)

For rho_1=1/2, early adoption would improve on never changing by 1/10,
but waiting yields 9/80 and is better. The actual selected path waits and
adopts two genuine common goals only in the high-overlap final state.
Its adoption probability is 1/2, while all the stated paths are peaceful
under the declared conflict embedding. Opportunity, timing, genuine
adoption probability and peace are distinct outcomes.

If the second high-overlap state is instead certain, both would adopt
there at cost K. Waiting then gives delta(g_2-K), and adoption now is
better exactly when `g_1>(1-delta)K`. Future cooperation can therefore
make a small current shared benefit enough to justify a durable change.
At zero current overlap and delta<1, deferring that same expense is better.
With delta=1 the two timings tie in that zero-benefit case.

## 4. Repeated ordinary control and a positive durable-adoption region

Suppose the next two projects certainly have gains g_1,g_2 exceeding the
per-project ordinary controller expense d, and d<K. A new durable change
would lose to the cheaper ordinary controller in the final project, so
V_2=g_2-d. At the first project, durable change beats two ordinary
controllers exactly when

`K<(1+delta)d`.                                        (6)

Thus `d<K<(1+delta)d` is a simple region where one-project compromise is
cheaper than modification, but a lasting genuine change is cheaper than
repeating that compromise. This requires an actual difference in lifetime
and expense, not an assertion that unchanged utilities cannot support
binding commitments. An equally cheap durable ordinary device ties it.

For example, g_1=g_2=1/2, delta=9/10, d=1/10 and K=3/20.
Durable installation gives `(1+delta)/2-K=4/5`. Two ordinary controllers
give `(1+delta)(1/2-d)=19/25`. Both original maximizers therefore strictly
prefer the durable route in this menu, gaining another 1/25. K=1/4 instead
makes ordinary controllers better. Both cases have the same exclusive
original values and the same productive overlap; implementation longevity
and real expense determine the mechanism selected.

When g_t>=d_t, the current advantage g_t-o_t equals d_t. Increasing
overlap further raises absolute output, while its contribution to choosing
the durable mechanism over that ordinary option has saturated. The relevant
metric is the remaining cost saving, not gross shared production alone.

## 5. Scope of the temporal implication

The conditions in (4)–(6) are positively stated timing and mechanism
comparisons under actual available controls. Repeated usable overlap,
affordable durable implementation and future project likelihood can favor
genuine adoption. A cheap durable ordinary device, valuable waiting option
or short remaining productive horizon can favor another available route.
No result equates retention with combat. Persistent knowledge, endogenous
technology changes, inter-project capture and richer bargaining have their
own continuation effects; notes 127, 129, 136 and 137 supply relevant
separate checks rather than silently declaring those effects absent from
all agents' futures.

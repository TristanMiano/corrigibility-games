# A shared goal can protect a productive allocation that cash cannot insure

**E8, note 149.** A public allocation can reveal who will lose access while there is still time to challenge it. If the unserved parties' reservation values exceed all liquid collateral, an externally signed promise of peace can be privately unattractive at precisely that point. Before anyone knows its own assignment, an agreement to adopt a shared goal can protect more of each founder's original production. This construction explicitly keeps the contest, ordinary transfers, and subsequent renegotiation in the game.

## Physical and informational setting

There are n≥3 sites with identical original utility u(q)=1-exp(-γq), γ>0. One batch must go to one site, yielding x>0 units of local, nonpledgeable useful output. Total transferable safe stock is W=nw. A small readily transferable reserve alongside a larger site-specific benefit is plausible for an organization with highly specialized jobs, including avoided downtime rather than salable inventory. Values and balances are public in this comparison. Fixed x isolates the distributional conflict. Other sections of E8 explore heterogeneous and private jobs. All founders know the final assignment before the batch is irreversible, since preparation at the receiving site is visible. Being able to conceal that assignment through completion would change the conclusion.

A capable site can initiate a physical contest during negotiation, after assignment, or before settlement becomes irreversible. At the final exposed interval each site has a public opportunity to act, in any fixed order, before the first initiation or peaceful completion. Once initiated, resource control is resolved stochastically. Site i wins with probability s_i proportional to its strength, Σs_i=1. Equal strengths give s=1/n for the constructive instance. Repair uses extra work, loses some prepared material and takes time, leaving enough capacity for a fraction η in (0,1) of normal local output at the winning site. Only the winner's local path can still finish a batch in the remaining time. A simple physical realization is capacity q per unit time over the intact window H with work/material for one lot. If repairs take r<H, have work funded from reserve and leave material fraction λ, output relative to intact production can be η=min(1-r/H,λ) at the surviving local endpoint. Dispersed unusable material remains physically present. The contest also costs its initiator c>0 in separate nontransferable work opportunities, measured in the same output units against a common baseline. Safe stocks survive, but escrow can condition disbursement or forfeiture on observed conduct. Incapacitated routing endpoints cannot send a new batch. A closed batch cannot be produced twice. This is an abstract physical disagreement option, without a stipulated peaceful ordering.

Let Q(y)=-log(1-s+s exp(-γy))/γ be the original certainty equivalent of its private service lottery. The following strict inequality is deliberately strong:

    Q(ηx)-c > W.                                                 (1)

Even if it forfeits its entire liquid position, an unserved original can obtain a better prospect by initiating a contest than by peacefully receiving *all* existing cash. The nontransferable reserve covers c in every case. Thus a continuation that delivers the announced batch peacefully for certain requires every unserved site to have changed what it currently wants or placed the independent contest decision under a binding control. If other sites may still initiate with positive probability, waiting can be attractive even to an original that dislikes the realized peaceful outcome. The mixed-continuation bound below addresses this case. Conditional receipts, asymmetric assignment, penalties, escrow, deliberate disposal and report lotteries do not change (1) after an exposed recipient is known. Deliberate disposal that actually prevents later recovery can lead to a peaceful zero-output path. It gives each original at most u(W). The option to fight at an earlier history remains. With all parties original and fixed public assignment, if the assigned site's intact service x exceeds W+Q(ηx), its last move after all other sites pass is strictly peaceful. The last unserved original would then strictly initiate by (1). Backward induction rules out productive peace for an all-original exposed allocation, even with stochastic terminal cash payouts bounded by W. This uses the exposed interval in which only initiation or completion closes the batch. Disposal before that interval is bounded separately.

For comparison, with credible detection, a funded forfeiture of t_i by the first initiator, conditioned on actually starting a contest, deters it if Q(ηx)-c-t_i is below its peaceful allocated service and transfer (all relative to its normal liquid balance). If every i can post an adequate bond while waiting for the allocation, any recipient can then be served peacefully under the original goals. A matched binding delegate that directly controls the contest decision can also choose peace when it implements the common criterion. A merger with pooled authority is a separate possible route. The strict-adoption comparison below applies where those controls cannot be installed in the available time at a better price. A device which controls only the booking or the liquid escrow is already included in (1).

## A negotiated offer that all originals prefer

Before assignment, each can sign a conditional reciprocal change to the sum of all n original output utilities, at cost k per member from its own liquid stock, 0<k<w. It takes effect only when every signer and the agreed readback are ready. The concrete implementation premise is a finite, already supported objective template with inspectable activation and a live production and contest decision path. Failure-safe activation, readback and preparation consume scarce time or resources included in k. Parties cannot infer semantic execution merely from seeing someone else's signature. Retaining the ability to initiate conflict is consistent with choosing a goal under which the conflict is worse. A general-purpose local commitment module with equal authority, information, cost and stability is an equally strong ordinary alternative.

At symmetric formation choose the recipient by a fair late lottery. At service, sharing available cash to improve aggregate utility is allowed even though local output cannot be reassigned after production. Feasible peaceful service with no further redistribution gives each original CE at least w-k+Q(x) ex ante under symmetric efficient settlement: optimization of the common sum can improve its expectation for each by permutation symmetry. Once the recipient is known, use the best attainable cash settlement under the current common goal. A sufficient statewise test against contest, valid even with previously redistributed liquid balances, is

    u(x) > n u((W+ηx)/n).                                      (2)

The left side lower-bounds the peaceful sum with nonnegative balances and one full local service. Jensen's inequality bounds the contested sum on the right even if a costless insurer could split all postcontest wealth and service equally. Actual damage and private effort only reduce that upper bound. With (2) every updated site strictly prefers pursuing feasible full service throughout the exposed interval. A unilateral further objective edit cannot improve a physically optimal peaceful allocation under the existing shared goal. An edit consuming positive resources makes this deviation strictly unattractive when all partners continue the selected optimal settlement. Fortuitous future projects are outside this one-opportunity proof.

Write U_G=u(w-k+Q(x)), U_F=u(W+Q(ηx)) and U_P=u(W+x). U_F bounds *any individual's* original payoff if all productive paths end in contest, even giving that individual all liquid stock and charging it no contest expense. Eligibility, role and which party initiated can affect incentives, but not its fresh probability s of the salvage draw. A peaceful zero-output history also satisfies the bound. U_P is a generous upper bound on any individual payoff in any continuation. Suppose U_G>U_F. When rejection consumes the remaining negotiating interval with probability e, and otherwise leaves *arbitrary* further bargains including new partners, installations, merger or gifts, acceptance of a credible grand offer strictly beats rejection if

    (1-e) < p_min = (U_G-U_F)/(U_P-U_F).                        (3)

Here expiry after rejection means the batch enters its exposed service interval with the original sites still in control. It is negotiation that closes, not the physical contest. All previously conditional signatures lapse. Existing devices or prearranged delegation must be included in U_F if present; (1) alone cannot price them out. The same comparison covers each sequential signature before uncertainty is resolved, by backward induction. Immediate initiation is bounded by U_F. This is an actual selection argument under current founder preferences. A cheap return to bargaining lowers the rejection cost and may change what binds.

The initially recognized proposer may offer any admissible funded terms, including unequal terms, side payments and smaller clubs. Assume it can offer the grand arrangement above and choose its transition to the specified truthful, symmetric implementation if accepted. If no affordable ordinary or merged device controls an unserved site's decision, any optimal offer has probability at least p_min of peaceful productive service. Indeed, for probability p of that event, its original payoff is bounded by p U_P+(1-p)U_F, while it can attain at least U_G. This argument also applies with an arbitrarily small optimization tolerance by reducing the lower payoff bound by that tolerance. For a **certainly peaceful** continuation all n-1 unserved sites must sustain peace at their individual decision points. For an arbitrary optimal agenda that can buy stochastic future initiation by another party, use a different lower bound. Let

    A = [U_F-u(Q(ηx)-c)]/[U_F-u(W)].

At exposure, suppose there is an independently acting, original, unserved site. At its move it can initiate for at least u(Q(ηx)-c). If it waits, its original payoff is at most u(W) conditional on peace and at most U_F conditional on a contest: future initiation precedes the independent salvage draw. Thus the conditional probability of peaceful full service at any such exposure is at most A, including when an earlier site has a chance of initiating first. With a fixed public destination, the same estimate applies if a site can later choose a protection but was original when it could have initiated: waiting is still evaluated by the incumbent at that move. If later goal changes or controller assignments can intervene before this party ever has an initiative opportunity, count effective protections by that opportunity in the stated pre-exposure installation timeline. No new binding device comes online during the final exposed interval in this fixture.

If p_min>A, at least (p_min-A)/(1-A) probability must be assigned to peaceful productive histories in which **every unserved site's original independent incentive has been changed or bound**. Indeed an unprotected exposure yields at most A probability of peace, and a protected exposure yields at most one. Equivalently if q is the joint probability of protected exposure and peace, total p≤q+A(1-q). In a menu requiring consensually activated templates for such changes and allowing no equally effective ordinary guard, expected realized installations are at least (n-1) times that lower bound. If ordinary controllers or a merger are available, count actual preference changes, bound decisions and loss of independent agency separately. If unilateral edits are feasible, attributing a particular change to negotiated adoption also requires its actual approval history. Note 152 examines the stochastic-threat repair directly.

## Magnitudes and what the founders control

Take n=3, γ=x=1, η=1/2, w=0.015, k=0.008 and c=0.003. The three sites together have W=0.045 in collateral. An unserved founder's contest CE after paying the initiative cost exceeds 0.1375. For (2) compare 0.6321 with 0.4984. The initial original utilities are bounded by U_G≥0.2162 (the defined guaranteed lower bound), U_F≈0.1694, U_P≈0.6483. Thus p_min≈0.0978: if the chance of a further negotiation after rejection is below 9.77%, every founder strictly signs the symmetric charter. In that specified offer all three actually change, service is peaceful with probability one, and the originals' ex ante welfare rises. Against a broad agenda the proved lower bound is about 9.77% on peaceful service in the stated class. For these cash stocks the sharper general-agenda installation count requires testing A separately. For example, with w=0.001, k=0.0006 and c=0.0003 at the same physical yields, p_min>A and the mixed-continuation bound gives a strictly positive installation count. The three installations and certain peace for the explicit grand offer hold in both fixtures. Both results are conditional equilibrium predictions, with different quantifiers. The strict inequalities persist under small changes, including small strength and value asymmetries by using individual bounds and the worst acceptance margin.

The reasons for *considering* low collateral, lost material, a visible recipient and a short negotiation deadline are physical and institutional. The numeric scarcity of transferable balances and the reliability of a fast objective update are more demanding. Parties have an incentive to build the latter if they have the necessary execution authority and can pay less than the improvement over their available devices. A cheap bond system, additional liquid reserve, independent second slot, hidden routing until service becomes irreversible, or suitable local delegation can instead make unchanged-goal peace feasible. Future access credits are another source of collateral in a continuing relationship. These are possible interventions with real lead times and participation conditions; adding them to the feasible menu changes the economic question.

**Exploration ledger.** Framing: future attempts to reclaim a rival resource can make today's consensual goal change privately valuable. Structural: compare each unserved party's continuation claim with all pledgeable compensation while keeping late contest physically possible. The exposed, illiquid slot is a promising instance. The proposition that any funded transfer scheme can always purchase peace is dead at that instance by (1). A sibling uses a genuinely funded stand-down bond before roles are drawn. A structural successor asks how cheaply parties can produce effective security through collateral, altered preferences, protected control, hidden timing or future access. The claim that three physical actors always require three actual changes is also too strong: a pre-role agreement might assign one unchanged beneficiary and protect the other two. Whether agents can afford and choose that contingent implementation is a separate question.

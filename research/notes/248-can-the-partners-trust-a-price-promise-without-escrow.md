# Can the partners trust a price promise without escrow?

A founder can protect itself by waiting until it knows who will bid for its services. A buyer can also give founders a reason to take it at its word by protecting access to a stream of later partners. The two solutions use different capabilities and change how much an accurate goal certificate is worth.

## Learning who has power before spending the kit

In [247](247-who-sets-the-price-after-an-agent-proves-it-cares.md) there is one active project, a monopoly tariff of three on a genuine goal and a competitive tariff of zero. Suppose the founder can install in time for the register *after* the presence of the second buyer is resolved, at the same expense and reliability as before. No new bids can enter after this date. A founder of cost k compares q(2−p)−k to zero at the realized price regime. It skips in the monopoly state and attempts in the competitive state, where 1.6 exceeds K=1.4. Neither a prior declaration nor the buyer's own earlier payment is required to fund this discretion.

The value of waiting is nonnegative for every founder: E[max(0,q(2−p)−k)]≥max(0,E[q(2−p)−k]). The prior calculations do not require the changed successor to share its founder's wish to refuse an exploitative monopoly quote. At the later date it is the still-original founder who can decline the edit. The chance of genuine installation under waiting is .8λ; with only the early window it is min(.8,max(0,(16/35)(3λ−1))). Waiting yields more genuine installations for 0<λ<.8, the same rate at .8, and fewer for .8<λ<1. For sufficiently rare alternatives an early founder would never risk the edit. With frequent alternatives, some early outcomes with just one final bidder would nevertheless have a genuinely changed successor. Waiting saves the founder from those states. The mechanism preserves the difference between more flexibility, the private value of that flexibility and the number of goals ultimately changed.

At λ=.8 both clocks produce a .64 change rate. With early installation the original buyer earns 1.536 per offer; with late installation it earns .96. A earns .448 on the early clock and .8(1.6−.7)=.72 when it can wait. The same current goal certificate can thus accompany different founder returns. A prepayment can support an early window if both can fund it. In the example a type-independent upfront payment from B to A of .40 makes *every* cost type strictly prefer the early window to waiting: for k≤1.12 the waiting advantage is .16+.20k, at most .384, and above 1.12 it is .8(1.6−k)≤.384. B's .576 gain covers the payment. Pay it once on enrollment, independent of later success, when the supplier selects the publicly verifiable early window. For example B controls the only service kit and its custodian removes the later entitlement when the early, nonstorable kit is issued. Each founder may still decline to spend k at that early window. If the recipient can instead pocket the transfer and retain a later kit, the cash alone cannot buy this timing.

## A repeated buyer's incentive to honor a price

Now take the sole buyer and a succession of new suppliers. There is an independently checked goal on each job. Suppose the buyer repeatedly quotes the .25 tariff that would induce all suppliers to try. The quote is discretionary after the read. Its current temptation conditional on meeting a genuine supplier is 3−.25=2.75. G will pay the larger price if the buyer withholds its batch. Public partners can observe an overcharge with probability d. An observable invoice concerns the price the customer charged, a different piece of evidence from the goal register. Future suppliers can condition on the buyer's public record. A job's total odds of recurrence and time discount give the continuing buyer factor D<1; the new one-job suppliers require no future retaliation strategy of their own.

Let R_H be B's flow return on the capped-price convention and R_P its best sustainable flow return in the continuation after a detected overcharge. When both continuations and the detection rule are credible, B honors its word at a genuinely successful installation whenever

    2.75 ≤ [D d/(1−D)](R_H−R_P).                                   (248.1)

A deviation collects 2.75 immediately and loses the difference in continuation values only when detected. At a known retained goal B already wants to decline every affordable sale. After a detection, unrestricted monopoly pricing and refusal to install are sequentially credible under the one-job technology: each incoming original would be worse off from an edit and an off-path genuinely changed supplier would accept the maximum. Within that class take R_P=0. If routine public commissioning costs .10 per active job, R_H=.8(3+.25)−.10=2.5. At d=1, D>11/21 makes the low tariff strictly credible. At d=.5 the sufficient threshold is instead **D>11/16**. Each founder then has the incentives and positive genuine adoption of the binding option, even though B has kept its physical power to charge three or to contest. Setting expected continuation work below the threshold makes a known-G overcharge strictly worthwhile on that proposed convention.

A buyer can have a reason to improve detection deliberately. With D=.60, half-coverage fails (248.1) while full coverage works. If an independent invoice register costs an additional .20 per operating job, the new R_H=2.30 still supports the promise: 1.5×2.30>2.75. B prefers the register and valuable orders to an unprotected one-shot monopoly earning zero. Privacy, the ability to bill outside the record, and whether suppliers can actually act on the public history are implementation premises. If B can always buy an equivalent binding option after a scandal for only .50 per job, R_H−R_P could be as small as .50. That profitable repair can weaken a reputation-only sanction while preserving the possibility of genuine agreements through the paid option.

**Scope.** Learning the real number of bids is plausible when there is enough time to inspect the order book before a reconfiguration. An expiring installation window can make waiting costly. A repeated buyer with observable invoices can also depend on future suppliers' voluntary decisions. A new identity, secret rebates, compulsory supply or better spot terms may materially change its continuation return. These results establish a paid solution and a relational solution in their stated markets. Innovation by new sellers of safeguards and transmission of goals through descendants remain separate long-run questions.

# How much real evidence does a renewable club need at entry?

**E8, note 173.** A productive rule for existing members leaves two decisions to explain. A new applicant can keep its original goal and accept other people's favors before its first default. An excluded participant can look for a fresh account instead of providing the service that would restore its standing. Both returns can be priced using the same renewable jobs as in 172.

## The option to come back under a fresh record

Keep the jobs, standing record, values V,W,G and D=n-(n-1)δ of 172. At the start of a period before the donor is known, an excluded participant can now pay real amount f to reestablish one clean account. This account replaces its old one. Its physical site still has exactly one scheduling right. The cost can be the actual expense of registration or replacing site-bound credentials. A protocol cannot impose a nominal large f if another affordable issuer can create an accepted replacement. The amount is spent on outside resources and yields no private benefit to another club member. The result does not cover one site claiming several simultaneous identities.

For the stipulated full-service continuation, the current-score value of regaining standing is (1-β)r/D. The excluded site will immediately buy clean standing instead of waiting to serve if f is smaller than this difference, and otherwise takes the ordinary productive rehabilitation. A clean donor who defaults can now save d-βr today and next period take the better of these two continuations. Thus the exact full clean-service donor condition becomes

    βr-d + δ min{f,(1-β)r/D} ≥ 0.                                    (1)

To verify the off-path choice, let V_β,W_β denote the values when the ordinary restoration policy is used. If immediate replacement is chosen, the excluded value is U=V_β-f. Forgoing the replacement for one period, serving if called as donor, has value (1-k)W_β+kU with k=(n-1)δ/n; this is the excluded Bellman equation with U substituted for its non-donor continuation. This deviation is weakly worse exactly when U≥W_β. The excluded donor's own restoration call is covered by (1). There is a second, consequential check. Suppose the excluded site forgoes an otherwise optimal immediate reset for one period. A different donor who honors the agreement then expects the *other* site to pay f next time, a loss βf under its current goal. If it defaults instead it expects to pay f itself. In this off-path period its incentive for service is βr-d+δ(1-β)f. Thus when f<(1-β)r/D and immediate reset is prescribed, also require

    βr-d+δ(1-β)f ≥ 0.                                                  (1a)

When f≥(1-β)r/D select productive rehabilitation; the other donor's continuation difference is the larger (1-β)(G-W) in 172 and (1) suffices. These tests qualify the asserted all-service continuation. In the cheap-reset regime (1) alone is a clean-state check. If (1a) fails, finding a different off-path equilibrium is necessary before using its payoffs. A narrower terminal concern specifically over partners' completed service, excluding outside registration expenses, removes the βf term in that one comparison; that is a different scope to negotiate. On either prescribed path a non-donor who disrupts a useful job cannot avoid a larger fee of its own or improve its own future standing relative to waiting. No public additional last-chance job is lost in the moment of registration. A refusal to participate in a different off-path coalition has to use that coalition's real attainable outputs.

In the three-site example at β=0.45, d-βr=0.10. At f=0.40, (1) passes but (1a) fails: a different donor can use its own default to replace someone else's looming restoration expense. At f=0.20 even the clean donor has a profitable default. A site-level registry that raises replacement cost to 0.50 instead makes genuine defaulters choose productive rehabilitation, since (1-β)r/D≈0.45833. Both the clean and off-path decisions then pass. An alternative is broader concern. At f=0.20, β=0.50 (and values sufficiently close to it) supports service by (1) and (1a). A cheap broader scope can displace the stronger record. A costly or mission-leaking broader scope leaves the registered narrow change more attractive. These are two different paths to the same productive service.

## What a secret original would do for as long as the club survives

Without access to evidence of a real configuration, behavioral discipline alone leaves an entry problem. Compute one original applicant's best response to genuinely participating partners. In the regime δr/D<d an original will default on every donor call. With no cheap reset it still receives some gifts *before* its first donor call. Its clean value is r/D and its excluded value zero. To derive the general result let M and N denote its original values when clean and excluded. In clean periods its expected incoming receipt is r/n and other donor calls leave it clean. On its own call it can give for -d+δM or default for δN. When excluded it can pay f and get M-f immediately, wait for its own next call, or abandon the opportunity for zero. The solution in this parameter range is

    M(f) = max{r/D, [r-δf]/[n(1-δ)]},       N=max(0,M-f).              (2)

If f≥r/D, substituting N=0 and M=r/D gives δM<d. Waiting forever after exclusion is optimal. If f<r/D, replacement is worthwhile, M-N=f and δf<d. In the clean state M=r/n+δM-δf/n because its next replacement is needed only after its own donor call. In the excluded state postponing a profitable reset cannot improve value: it receives no incoming service while excluded, and giving to recover would be worse than defaulting at that donor call given δf<d. These Bellman checks include its optimal future departures and repeated registrations. Partners' routing on the single-applicant deviation follows the agreed public record. The selected off-path assessment treats observed defaults and replacements as possible action errors by a genuinely configured member. For finite histories these beliefs can be approached by trembles in which a false initial configuration is rarer than every fixed finite sequence of action errors, provided no subsequent signal identifies the evaluator with certainty. Other justified beliefs can choose a different continuation. When they know a site to be privately unconfigured despite a valid-looking registration, its effective true acceptance probability and later treatment must be recalculated.

In particular M(f)>V in the displayed low-patience example. Starting every registrant in good standing and checking only its later behavior cannot by itself lead each founder to pay for a secret genuine change. More generally, if an unchanged, dynamically consistent founder can imitate the entire genuine policy and induce the same observable and physical law before all its partners, its *optimal* own-goal response obtains at least the original utility of that imitation. Paying a positive sunk setup price then needs some material, computational or informative difference. A behavior-based trial can still give the original a powerful reason to provide real service, and can help the partners select reliable controllers. To obtain evidence specifically favoring genuine costs, test which behavior is feasible to imitate, what other parties can observe and what a matched ordinary delegate costs.

## Putting entry, future choices and the sponsor's costs together

Return to conditional activation of a finite objective, before any service rights have been granted. Use the paid operational readback of 161 with all-genuine opening probability T and one-fake probability A. With one application opportunity at the initial deadline, all other parties participate genuinely and waiting leads to separate service. On a false pass the privately original applicant starts clean with value (2); on rejection it gets zero. A genuine application costs K even if the test fails to open. Then for the specified all-sincere equilibrium the central entry comparison is

    T V - A M(f) > K.                                                  (3)

It checks the **best complete original life after a false pass** rather than assuming a one-time fake gain. Unanimously starting requires each signer's participation against its actual other options as well. If initial rejected applicants can buy repeated immediate probes, a different launch calculation is necessary. Re-entry after an already opened club in (2) is not a license for unbounded initial application attempts. Real resource losses from a test can affect the comparison differently according to who pays them and when.

At n=3, δ=0.30, r=2, d=1, β=0.45 and f=0.50 take independent sincere readbacks with true-positive probability 0.90 and false-positive probability 0.15, all three required. T=0.729, A=0.1215, V=10/21≈0.47619, M=37/42≈0.88095. Thus the truth margin before setup is about 0.24011. For K=0.05, all founders strictly prefer the genuine configuration to the best secret original response as well as to separate use. If the register would otherwise permit f=0.20, one eligible organizer can pay 0.02 on a completed subscription to secure a persistent site-bound account. A menu comparison gives this sponsor an actual incentive: suppose a broader β=0.50 on the cheap account costs 0.09 each and uses the same entry evidence. It also gives full service, so originals compare 0.729V-0.05-0.02 with 0.729V-0.09. The sponsor and both other members strictly prefer the narrower protected offer. The broader offer also passes (3) at f=0.20 using its actual M. A fully reliable ordinary routing system at sufficiently low expense would instead displace both. This selection is between the stated funded **full-service** offers and separate work. The best among all conceivable partial-service clubs at each price remains a distinct design problem.

On honest launch all three change and every surviving job is served; the selected equilibrium never incurs a reset fee. Launch probability 0.729, preservation of standing and the unfunded counterfactual cost of fraud are different quantities. A site can prefer paying to make a false identity expensive even though nobody actually pays the replacement fee on the honest path. If it has access only to a cheap public account with no way to validate the link to a real site, the proposed safeguard is unavailable.

**Exploration ledger.** The framing candidate is that future partner choice can both reward care and provide an escape from its obligations. The structural candidate prices startup truth and ongoing incentives jointly, including a false member's optimized series of returns. The inference that stable service among existing participants implies privately profitable initial conversion is dead in (2). A clean-state test also initially missed the current cost of *someone else's* impending replacement. Formula (1a) catches the failed 0.40 example; a higher actual replacement expense or a genuinely narrower scope repairs different versions. A sibling verifies an effective local configuration and prices subsequent admissions. A higher-rung successor allows replacement partners to choose their own entry standards or allows genuine members to consent to broader or narrower goals as their opportunity horizon changes.

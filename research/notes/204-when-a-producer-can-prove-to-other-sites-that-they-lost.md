# When a producer can prove to other sites that they lost

**E8, note 204.** Turning a locally useful diagnostic into a certificate that another site can verify can raise a founder's willingness to pay for genuine change. The certificate lets an informed loser collect passive salvage while another site bears the expense of initiating. Knowing this, founders may prefer to change the observer's goal in a situation in which they would have cooperated using entirely original goals with a local readout.

## A certificate before the physical releases

Use 203's single secretly assigned job, heterogeneous shares s_i and fixed set O of retained own-output sites. Every changed site values aggregate efficient production and expenses. A paid early reading identifies the actual recipient and can now be shown credibly to all parties **before anyone has exhausted its physical initiative**. A public name on a real order or a verifiable destination marker can support this disclosure. If only the instrument's owner can interpret it, the local version of 203 applies. Cheap unsupported statements can be ignored by the other controllers. The dispatcher cannot change the actual recipient or supply additional transfers after publication. There is no separate market in payments to keep information secret in this comparison.

If i∈O learns that j≠i would win and there is a third unserved original in O different from i and j, i can publish and pass. It can let an unserved original initiate and receive q_i instead of paying its own expense. More precisely, after a public certificate all unserved originals can see that staying quiet offers them zero on peaceful completion. With at least two such originals, a specified *order* matters for who actually pays. A site which has an initiative opportunity before the last other unserved site can pass and be certain that the other site will initiate if no one has already done so. A site whose own opportunity is last could instead have to initiate itself. Define w_ij=q_i when i can release its own claim before a remaining unserved original on recipient j, and w_ij=d_i when all other unserved originals can wait for i to act last. Use the actual release order for that recipient. Conditional on common knowledge of the order and all final rights, backward induction after a certificate makes the last unserved original initiate. All earlier unserved originals strictly prefer to free-ride, and beneficiaries and total-output signers pass.

In a candidate all-peace rule, the gain from a private query and optimal use of a certificate is at least Σ_(j≠i)s_j w_ij. On winning states i can remain silent and preserve the original delivery. Consequently each retained original with a diagnostic needs

    Σ_(j≠i) s_j w_ij ≤ h_i,           s_i x_i ≥ max(d_i,r_i).

Every changing founder still requires s_i x_i≥r_i+K_i. Where the order is fixed, these conditions suffice with no additional pre-release recommendation. Without inspecting, i prefers to pass. On a winning observation it prefers to keep silent, and on losing observations it can choose between initiating itself, silence, and publishing the true recipient. With no other funding, side offers or extra selectors these give at most the stated w_ij. A local-only reading instead has d_i in every losing cell. Giving a certificate to just one party privately rather than broadcasting it could change that party's beliefs about the other releases. The sufficient construction here permits a verifiable **public** posting or silence; the lower bound also applies when more forms of communication are available.

## A purchase induced by publication rights alone

Consider 203's three sites, x=0.70, q=0.20, d=0.16, and symmetric refusal r=0.56/3 from the old exposed assignment. Only A can buy an early readout at h=0.09. It acts before the two others. With a purely local readout the unchanged sites can already negotiate a fully peaceful division: A needs at least 7/16 of the winning chances, and B and C need 4/15 each. The sum is below one. With a public certificate A can obtain q on either losing destination, because the other downstream loser can still initiate. Now A needs at least 1-h/q=0.55, which together with 8/15 for the other two exceeds one.

Even the best alternative with some combat remains expensive. Let H be its dispute probability and s the chance the underlying recipient is A. By buying and publishing on losing destinations A can secure q on each of those dates. On A-winning dates it can mimic its original behavior and pass on its own initiative, weakly raising its gross return. Its earlier gross receipt on a losing date was at most q times the conditional probability of a dispute. It follows that s≥1-h/q-H. The two other signers also need their actual old-goal receipts, so s_B+s_C≥2r/x-2qH/x. Summing yields

    H ≥ [(1-h/q)+2r/x-1]/[1+2q/x] = 7/132.

The lost original production is therefore at least 0.14·7/132≈0.00742. An authenticated single change at A costing 0.005 can yield full output under its common production goal. It prefers silence even if its readout reveals a loss; inducing another site to initiate would also destroy output it now values. With a fair founding vote or a proposer offering B and C their old returns, the genuine change earns a strict margin over every ordinary private scheduling rule with these resources. One site genuinely changes in the accepted highest-net configuration; paying additional sites cannot improve full production. A cheaper ordinary device that reliably limits A to a nontransferable reading can restore the ordinary full-peace rule. Whether the newly available certificate is actually published, or deliberately made unavailable, thus depends on the equilibrium after publication becomes possible.

At an already known A loss, a partial goal with weight β on partners may need more concern to resist *disclosure* than to pass up A's own initiative. Own initiation is unappealing at β≥d/(x-2q)=8/15. Passing and prompting another site to initiate can yield q+β(2q-c), so keeping that information private requires β≥q/(x-2q+c)=10/17. These are after-observation comparisons. Before the observation, the successor also has to consider the diagnostic expense. Proving the consequences of a free public certificate leaves a substantive next question about priced evidence brokerage, selective private disclosure and competing suppliers.

**Exploration ledger.** The presumption that making a reading verifiable leaves the best original allocation unchanged is dead at this instance. A sibling prices local-only communication or obtains the observer's genuine preference change. A higher-level successor gives separate owners the ability to buy, sell or publicly certify information and asks which communication rights they will actually construct.

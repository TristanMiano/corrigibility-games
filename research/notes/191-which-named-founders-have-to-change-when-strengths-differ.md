# Which named founders have to change when strengths differ?

**E8, note 191.** A site with a good chance of winning a dispute has to be offered more future service to leave someone else's booking intact. If that site's founders can be paid to change their goal voluntarily, a scarce calendar can be enough to accommodate the remaining original claimants. There is an exact, individual incentive test for arbitrary strengths and unequal intact yields. It also shows when reducing one site's relative strength can make a *whole* ordinary calendar harder to sustain.

## What is being divided

There is one recurring exposed job. It yields x_i>0 units of own useful production if assigned intact to site i. If any site initiates, the job instead yields y_i<x_i for the single winning site i with probability π_i, and consumes c_s units of the initiator s's own resources. Write Q_i=π_i y_i/x_i and D_i=(π_i y_i-c_i)/x_i. A court, delegate or change cannot compel a retained original to release its physical claim in this comparison. Rights survive at each live date. The probability of another job is p after service and t≤p after dispute as in 189. A damaged line may miss the next operating window. Different plant capacities, outputs from partially damaged material and control capabilities motivate the heterogeneous parameters. They are independently meaningful physical quantities. An agreement can reassign future calendar entries without changing anybody's physical strength.

At the outset let O be the set staying with own-output values. For each site define its guaranteed normalized share

    a_i=(1-p)max(0,D_i)/(1-t) for i in O, and a_i=0 otherwise.
    A=Σ_i a_i < 1.

Measure a site's discounted expected original income in units x_i/(1-p). For D_i>0 a retained original can always initiate and guarantee a_i. With D_i≤0 it can always decline to initiate and secure zero. The changed founders in the construction adopt a common ongoing preference for the sum of these sites' scaled actual outputs and expenses. Their new evaluator ranks an intact booking above a dispute at every date. Their founding consent is bought with their own service, valued by the *old* evaluator. Positive affine renormalization of an old utility makes the separate founding budgets comparable on one calendar. The genuine change is adding other sites' terminal outcomes to a site's preference.

## A simple calendar attaining the exact boundary

Suppose each listed site must receive a positive amount of its own service, either because it retains a positive security return or because that is its participation condition. Fix normalized reservation-plus-setup requirements b_i before the first recipient is drawn. A fully peaceful stream exists for the named list exactly at the following weak boundaries:

    p(1-a_j) ≥ A-a_j for every required recipient j,                     (1)
    Σ_i max(a_i,b_i) ≤ 1.                                               (2)

For clarity take the listed sites to be all potential recipients. A site with zero security and zero participation requirement can be omitted from the scheduling list, with the same physical contest lottery and all remaining outside rights checked. Sites whose requirement is strictly positive must actually receive some intact service along a fully peaceful stream. We use p>0 in the construction. At p=0 all agents left unserved must already be willing to pass on the current job, as (1) requires.

**Necessity.** Every retained original can take its security option at an exposed booking. On an all-peace path, when j is named the others receive nothing immediately and must derive their entire promised values from the chance p of the next job. The next-job expected values of all other sites sum to at most 1-a_j. Thus (1) holds wherever a required recipient is served. This uses only security and the intact resource constraint. Distinct continuation schedules, private lotteries or renegotiation inside these physical rights still obey the bound. The initial promises also sum to one, giving (2).

**Attainment.** Put R=1-A. In public state j award the job to j, with normalized after-announcement values v^j=a+R e_j. If anyone initiates, repeat the same state at the next surviving date. After successful service the desired next-date vector is

    w_i^j=a_i/p for i≠j,       w_j^j=1-(A-a_j)/p.

It sums to one. Its coordinates are all at least their respective a_i by (1). Choose the next recipient i with probability (w_i^j-a_i)/R. These are nonnegative and sum to one; the promised mean next state is exactly w^j. This verifies v^j=(1-p)e_j+p w^j. A retained original s considering initiative would earn (1-p)D_s+t v_s^j≤v_s^j. The inequality is strict when its value strictly exceeds its floor, or when its initiative payoff is negative and the floor is zero. The other signers want to preserve total scaled output and the chance of future production. Each continuation is feasible, including after a dispute. By initially mixing the states the schedule delivers every vector z with z≥a and Σz_i=1. In particular the signers can meet (2). When inequalities have slack, raising the retained original floors slightly makes all stand-down comparisons strict. An appropriately placed ordinary local delegate can use the very same calendar. It has to control the site that would otherwise initiate.

**Consequences.** With all sites original and strictly positive security shares, the binding recipient is a weakest claimant, requiring p≥(A-min a_i)/(1-min a_i). At fixed total A<1, making the weakest claimant even weaker raises the minimum renewal probability needed for all to receive service peacefully. The *other* sites can then claim more between them on the date that the weakest is paid. If at least one named changing founder needs positive own service, (1) simplifies to p≥Σ_(i in O) a_i. Any other site's test follows. Thus, subject to the separate budget (2), a founder can make the largest reduction in future booking obligations by obtaining the strongest claimant's voluntary change. A higher price for that installation can make several weaker participants the cheaper genuine agreement; 192 checks this against all the partial-service alternatives in one quoted setting.

This construction protects individuals acting with their own continuation rights. If several unserved sites can share the expense of disputing, use 184–185 as a distinct check. A public schedule that can assign the next user's time does not automatically own each site's physical initiative. If the participants already have enforceable local delegation, the corresponding ordinary proposal can displace some or all edits.

**Exploration ledger.** The framing candidate connects leverage over future production to the price of genuine accommodation. The structural candidate uses the probability of a future booking to pay *all* retained original rights on a date when a named changer is compensated. The earlier identical-strength boundary is nested in (1). A sibling prices the actual alternative signers. A higher-rung successor gives coalitions of unequal strength a choice of whom to admit to a side agreement and whom to pay with future calendar rights.

# When helping after a failed pilot makes success more informative

A supplier can genuinely value the customer and still lack the production method it needs. A capable supplier with the old goal can also make a real pilot delivery to obtain the large order it plans to keep. In this example the buyer can change both incentives by choosing what it will do *after failure*. Training on failures can make a successful pilot more informative about the goal and lead more founders to pay for a real change.

## A missing skill and a strategic use for a real delivery

Use the equal-strength factory of [206](206-when-a-truthful-attempt-can-fail.md). Per unit sent, the original supplier earns a=2 over peaceful return and B gains g=4. Per unit kept, original A earns X=4 and B loses h=2. The agreed successor values own stock plus half B's. When it knows the service method it strictly prefers to send; when it lacks the method, keeping is the best feasible local use. G then prefers keeping to a forced return in this material game. A and B retain the same less valuable contest over available stock before conversion.

A selects an installation attempt with success chance q=.7 and privately uniform expense k∈[0,1.4]. A setup receipt always admits an actual attempt. A false declaration without an attempt can still obtain a valid-looking receipt with probability z=.45. The costs and this error law concern a single checked, batch-specific log. B chooses whether to maintain this intake at .05 per initial applicant and can refuse any unprofitable job. A receipt holder can then use a pilot of size x=.2 and a main lot of size L=1. After qualification the supplier discovers whether it knows the service method; its initial skill probability s=.5 is independent of effort, cost and success. The supplier privately knows its goal and skill before the pilot. Pilot delivery is physically visible. After a pilot failure B can pay c=.20 for access to a trainer before awarding the main lot. Training gives an unskilled site the method with probability τ=.8. Success of training is private and no main delivery can affect a later allocation. B can fund the training and then separately choose whether to release.

**With no trainer**, B awards the main lot only after a pilot delivery. A skilled unchanged supplier gives up (X−a)x=.4 on the pilot to gain XL=4 from keeping the larger order. It strictly imitates. A skilled G also delivers, and unskilled suppliers of either goal cannot. A successful pilot therefore identifies skill exactly and gives B **no extra information about the goal**. B can still want the job if the genuine share among receipt holders exceeds 1/3.

**With a trainer**, consider the continuation in which B awards all main lots and trains only failed pilots. A skilled retained supplier now gets the main lot either way. It keeps the pilot for its own extra .4. A skilled G delivers; failure would waste both pilot goods and part of B's training budget. Unskilled G and all retained controllers fail the pilot. Now every delivered pilot certifies an actual G within this response class. Among failures a fraction p₋=p(1−s)/(1−ps) genuinely have the goal, where p is its rate among receipt holders. Since only an unskilled G could newly serve the main lot after a failure, B chooses training and release precisely when L[τp₋(g+h)−h]>c. An untrained failed lot would be kept. The inequality also ensures that paying for training leaves B a strict reason to release the main lot once the bill is sunk.

## Who really undertakes the update?

Let U_G and U_N be the founder's expected own-stock gains from processing if admitted and its later goal is genuine or retained. With no trainer and imitation, a skilled site gets both lots and an unskilled one gets only the pilot. Thus

    U_G = s a(x+L)+(1−s)Xx,       U_N = s(ax+XL)+(1−s)Xx.

With failure training and no imitation the corresponding terms are

    U_G = s a(x+L)+(1−s)[Xx+(τa+(1−τ)X)L],     U_N=X(x+L).

Under either continuation the cost cutoff is k*=qU_G+(1−q)U_N−zU_N. Let r=F(k*). Per initial offer the new genuine mass is qr and the qualification rate is d=r+(1−r)z. The prior on actual receipt holders is p=qr/d. Everyone strictly applies, even when its optimal private action is a false declaration. The imperfect initial receipt is essential: at z=1 with both lots released regardless of the pilot, a positive-cost edit that gives up own stock has no access advantage.

At the training price, among 439 proportionate failed pilots, **217 have the correct goal but lack the method, 186 are honest failed installations and 36 used a false setup declaration**. Helping is worth 818/2195≈.373 per failed pilot before the .20 training bill. A failure need not be priced at the probability that a signer intended to deceive. Here the thing B can change is the remaining skill deficit, and the return on that change pays for reopening the case.

For the table, B's pilot margin per qualified site is x[(g+h)w−h], with w=s in the first regime and w=sp in the second. Its main margin is s[(g+h)p−h] without a trainer and (g+h)p[s+(1−s)τ]−h−c(1−sp) with a trainer. Multiply their sum by d and deduct .05. A's return is zU_N+(k*)²/(2K), K=1.4. If access to a trainer costs an additional .40 per initial offer, B still buys it on these continuations. Each privately informed supplier gains, since the failed-credential fallback is unchanged, the fake's expected entry rent rises from 1.17 to 2.16, and the real attempt premium rises too. This is a funded reason to build an institution that helps other agents act on goals they agreed to.

An ordinary router binding the final action still needs the same skill or training. If B can rent one for .50 and buy the .20 lesson on the main lot directly, .9 of final lots are served. B gains 6×.9−2−.2−.5=2.7 before compensation. Original A gets 2.2. Against even the cheapest genuine attempt under the trained agreement, A previously got at most 3.4. A flat payment of 1.21 from B buys every type's consent to the router if that much stock is transferable. B retains 1.49, above its trained agreement even before charging the new trainer facility. A router unable to enforce private final actions offers a different trade.

**Why try this model?** Customer-provided process instructions, an observable demonstration lot and a larger follow-up order are independently motivated. The model assumes that ability is discovered after intake, that a taught procedure becomes usable on time and that B can retain custody of the larger lot. A second application, the ability to trade the knowledge on unrelated work, or a common fault tying skill to installation changes the response laws. The confidence of one on a successful trained-regime pilot specifically uses the absence of another reason for a retained capable supplier to give service.

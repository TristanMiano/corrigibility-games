# Why would an established pair admit a site that has no favor to return?

**E8, note 159.** There are two different answers. The outsider may complete a productive return path, as in 154. Or its voluntary cooperation can protect an existing pair's production from a costly contest. An affordable backup can displace this bargain or raise the outsider's price. The alternatives must be evaluated after the outsider responds.

## A formation problem hidden by the opportunity calculation

If one strongly connected component of 153's referral graph has no incoming edges, its members can cancel their positively costly outgoing gifts to outsiders while keeping exactly their internal arrangement. Every member weakly gains and each donor whose gift is canceled strictly gains. This is a block in the convention where no member of the deviating coalition loses and at least one gains. Some members may be indifferent, so a requirement of *strict* gains for every blocker is stronger. Iteratively apply this argument to the source components of the remaining graph when earlier ones have no cross-component flows. A weakly unblocked allocation of this particular closed referral game has no active cross-component referrals. This assumes that initial organizers can form an internal club, that outsiders losing access cannot directly impair its remaining jobs, and that the comparison occurs before they have adopted goals valuing the outsiders. It does not apply automatically to the current preferences of already changed agents. An expanding cycle in every source is enough for a strictly beneficial *grand opportunity* in 153. That existence test does not secure membership when source partners can take a more profitable offer on their own.

## One outsider and two genuinely different production opportunities

A and B already cooperate as a pair. Their current criterion is Y, the sum of their own useful output, split symmetrically for original welfare accounting. C currently values its own output Z. Each capable party retains a physical contest option. In an early negotiation a new common terminal criterion Y+βZ would change all three current rankings. After agreement the two original pair founders each receive half of a reported pair benefit or cost.

With probability λ the available job is **P**. The pair can obtain d intact output or refer the job to C for r units of its output, in a window too short to recover service after a physical contest. With probability 1-λ the available job is **W**. The existing machine can produce S for the pair or X for C. In this second window a contest can still deliver reduced output after damage and repair. C controls the recovered path with probability s and receives η_C X then. Otherwise the pair receives η_P S. Both η factors lie strictly between zero and one. If C initiates it pays c_C>0; if the pair initiates, one of its members pays c_P>0 in lost pair output. The contested draw is fresh, permission to repurpose peacefully is available, and a publicly exposed recipient is still contestable before completion. Assume sη_C X>c_C and (1-s)η_P S>c_P. Also assume that keeping the intended intact service strictly beats starting a contest for the intended recipient. The utility units are linear in these small net outputs and preparation expenses.

In the single remaining project, without additional pledgeable transfers or an effective change of the later decision, assigning W to either side leaves the other a strict reason to contest. Under a known assignment with complete information, the last unserved side initiates if none has yet done so; a served side prefers peaceful completion when it is last. Designating the pair for W makes C the initiator, so the pair can avoid paying c_P. The pair controls the booking under disagreement. On P it keeps its own job. Under this explicit refusal continuation the respective expected payoffs are

    Y_F=λd+(1-λ)(1-s)η_P S,       Z_F=(1-λ)(sη_C X-c_C).

The pair also has a real alternative before the state is known. At expense D it can prepare a physically independent spare processing path. If W arrives the pair can finish its normal job on the spare while C uses the old machine. Removing the conflict over one indivisible service gives

    Y_S=λd+(1-λ)S-D,             Z_S=(1-λ)X.

Assume the spare is safe from expropriation in this particular location, can be funded from nontransferable preparation work and can be finished even on rejection of an early proposal. Its timing is consequential: a backup requiring earlier irreversible procurement has a different outside value. Destroying an available private path cannot increase an original's usable output under this specification. If a founder can capture both machines, or fund an alternative with another partner, recalculate the continuation. This two-window setting models heterogeneous work, finite salvage and independent productive capacity. A site can have a better use for P and a worse use for W. The numerical strengths and the speed and trustworthiness of a genuine update are conditional examples.

## Solve the actual offer, including richer terms

The pair chooses whether to offer an inclusive common goal, prepare the spare or use its original rights. If C refuses the inclusive offer, the pair prepares the spare exactly when D<D_F=(1-λ)[1-(1-s)η_P]S, otherwise it keeps the original machine (take strict comparisons away from equality). C's true reservation Z_R is therefore Z_S or Z_F according to that *later* decision. Giving C the same assumed reservation at every price D can get the membership choice wrong.

An inclusive bargain costs the pair k_P and C k_C in real output. Let z be the peaceful chance that C gets P and t its peaceful chance of obtaining W. An independent draw and the common criterion select the intended assignment. The physical opportunity cost to the pair of delivering a unit of expected C output is d/r on P and S/X on W. Assume d/r<S/X. To give C gross target T=Z_R+k_C at minimum cost, use P first and only then W:

    B(T)=(d/r)min(T,λr)+(S/X)(T-λr)_+,
    0≤T≤λr+(1-λ)X.                                               (1)

An exchange argument proves optimality for the entire rectangle of possible state-contingent assignment probabilities. The pair's strict threshold for a mutually acceptable, positively priced offer is

    T < λr+(1-λ)X,         k_P+B(T) < min(D,D_F).                (2)

For strict consent give C a small additional amount within the margin. With a continuous exact accept-if-indifferent rule the optimizing proposer can offer its reservation; with a finite quote grid the best strictly acceptable offer approximates it. Under (2) a sufficiently fine grid strictly favors inclusive formation over both actual alternatives.

One negotiated common objective implements each point on the cost frontier: at β=d/r, P is tied and W strictly serves the pair; at β=S/X, P strictly goes to C and W is tied. Intermediate β gives C all P and none of W. Mutually accepted late draws select the tied assignments. A small project-specific preference for a certified recipient can make dispatch strict at real implementation cost. Each updated party strictly prefers the best intact assignment over the contest lottery because the latter damages attainable outputs at either endpoint. A keeper that originally valued only its own side can now deliberately serve or stand down for the other. Exit into a cheaper equivalent ordinary binding delegate or control merger is available when its architecture permits it. Ordinary calendar and report mechanisms alone do not bind the late physical initiative. If an independent hedge, enforceable collateral or another repeated opportunity becomes available, substitute its actual payoff vector for the refusal branch.

## One price change gives three different reasons for peace or combat

Take λ=0.4, d=0.3, r=0.8, S=1, X=0.7, s=0.4, η_P=η_C=0.5, c_C=c_P=0.01, k_P=0.02 and k_C=0.01. Then D_F=0.42, Z_S=0.42 and Z_F=0.078. The original pair would keep its closed production network on P if C had no effect on W. With retained costly disagreement, C has something valuable to contribute even though the service graph has no outgoing productive referral from C.

- At D=0.20 the spare is the actual fallback. To persuade C to give up sole use of the old machine would require k_P+B(0.43)≈0.29714 of pair value, exceeding the price of the spare. The pair builds it. Both sides remain at peace with their existing goals.
- At D=0.35 the spare is still credible on refusal. Offering C every P and zero W gives C only 0.32 before fees, so it would decline. The better offer gives C all P and a little more than 26.19% of W. It buys C's consent at less expense than the spare. The pair proposes the richer inclusive charter and all three can change their utilities under the specified closing and verification interface.
- At D=0.50 the spare is too expensive even on refusal. C's reservation falls to Z_F. An offer of a little more than 27.5% of P and zero W suffices for strict consent. Peace and changed-goal participation can continue with substantially different division. If genuine setup instead costs the pair 0.41 and its ordinary equivalents are also unaffordable, k_P+B(0.088)=0.443 exceeds D_F. The pair stays independent; C initiates on W, with ex ante combat probability 0.60. No party values destruction. C values the usable output it can recover; the pair values its own output and the available spare is too costly.

**Exploration ledger.** Framing: control over another party's future can create a reason to include it in an agreement even without a direct exchange of material favors. Structural: compute the reservation created by a feasible alternative investment, then negotiate across actual job types. The inclusive construction, credible backup and combat regions are promising in this institution. The simple offer “all P, no W” is dead at D=0.35; a sibling allocates some W too, and a structural successor optimizes over the entire set of production, governance and security alternatives after refusal. An outsider with neither a return path nor consequential influence is a separate membership case. A revealed beneficial opportunity alone cannot establish which club is formed.

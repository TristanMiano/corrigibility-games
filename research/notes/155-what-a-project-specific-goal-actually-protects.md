# Which future projects are the founders agreeing to care about?

**E8, note 155.** An agent can want genuine concern for its partners on a shared task and still want authority over its own unrelated work. Sometimes one modest weight accomplishes both. When that fails, a negotiated project boundary can help. Who can subsequently move that boundary, and which utility evaluates the decision to move it, determines whether the safeguard is credible.

## Choosing the breadth of an actual preference

With probability τ=3/4 the next job is the three-site private-urgency booking in 143, iid half-high with l=1/4,h=3/4. With probability 1-τ it is a separately owned A project. A can obtain own job value d>0 or direct the opportunity to B for B's value e>d. C receives nothing from either option. Original utility is own job value plus a separate, bounded stock of personally useful work, locally linear over the required fees. Jobs themselves can have nonlinear values. The payer actually loses stock or effort. This unit choice isolates scope; CARA in total stock would require repricing conditional costs. Assume no second job or late enforceable exchange in the base case. The short-expiry physical embedding of 140 preserves a costly contest at every capable stage, with other salvage possibilities considered in 149–152.

A uniform change to own value plus β times others' job values induces honest joint dispatch with the fair late tie in 143 for β≥3/5, strictly above that threshold. Each founder's original gain in that branch is 1/16 compared with ordinary separate booking. In A's private branch it keeps the job if β e<d and yields it when β e>d. Hence

    3/5 < β < d/e

gives strict reporting incentives and preserves A's own outside project. Its pre-fee gain for every founder is τ/16=3/64. For d=0.4,e=0.6 the choice β=5/8 works. Full concern instead costs A (1-τ)d=0.10 on outside work while improving B's private prospects by 0.15. A rejects the full offer at zero fees relative to separate booking. When credible safe transfers are possible, B can pay A more than 0.10 and less than 0.15 relative to the partial agreement, with a small payment to C if it too needs a strict improvement. With insufficient transferable wealth, short duration or unshareable outputs, that repair may be unavailable. Limited liability, a partner's other credible jobs and the actual cost of settlement determine whether fuller concern displaces the partial route.

For d=0.3,e=0.6 every β large enough for the truthful *uniform* protocol makes A yield the outside project. A's gross gain becomes 3/64-0.075<0. Raising β to cure private exaggeration is therefore unacceptable to A in this environment. A scoped common goal instead gives weight to partners' realized joint job values and retains the original ranking on external projects. It achieves the full joint gain if the boundary is reliable. This combines three distinct interventions: changing concern, agreeing on who may request the slot, and specifying which physically distinct outputs the new goal values. Keeping the original private project is a feature the signing founder wants.

## A veto over mutable labels can increase the scope

Suppose the actual installed goal of A on an external history is its own m_A+v_A plus β J v_B, where J∈{0,1} records whether this project was registered as joint. The formula weights incremental partner job results only. A relabeling cannot create a windfall just by placing another site's preexisting wealth within the index. If J is a freely mutable public label, compare two histories: refusing registration and obtaining d, or authorizing J=1 and letting B obtain e. When βe>d the *current* A approves registration itself. Requiring A's signature for a label therefore fails to protect this particular scope. The late comparison is made by the same already installed utility in both cases.

Now instead let the actual goal refer to the source batch or allocation round agreed before the private job arose. A current preference which counts partners' outcomes from that original source gives B's unrelated job zero weight. A remains physically able to consent to a genuine amendment adding this separate project. It evaluates the proposed amendment **before installing it** and sees that granting B the job would cost its own d, while the current goal assigns no compensating credit. It declines absent a negotiated return. With compensation or a further reciprocal opportunity it may consent to broadening. A certificate bound to the original batch, distinct work queues and a public provenance record are possible implementations. Which physical outcome counts, who can alter that record, and what it costs to establish the distinction must be specified. Calling both schemes “consent to expansion” conceals their opposite incentives.

## An actor can pay to improve the boundary

Consider a bounded verification interface when exact provenance is expensive. A owns the outside scope and can fund a process costing g that reduces the probability q of a disputed B request being accepted into the original set. B chooses whether to spend a>0 of its own work to request inclusion *after the outside job appears*. On success the installed goals treat the job as joint and A dispatches to B because βe>d. On failure A keeps it. B's own reserve loss is additive and the original scoped B assigns no partner credit to A's unrelated job when the request fails. B strictly requests when qe>a, and refuses when qe<a. A evaluates the observed outcome of this strategic choice when deciding whether to buy the check and sign:

    G_A = τ/16 - k_A - g - (1-τ)d q 1{qe>a},
    G_B = τ/16 - k_B + (1-τ)max(0,qe-a),
    G_C = τ/16 - k_C.                                             (1)

These are gains relative to separate booking, with the chosen truthful joint continuation. An ordinary mediated or funded alternative with a larger value must replace that reference. At qe=a either response may be supported; use the response actually selected. The inspection is valuable if it encourages truthful classification *or* makes opportunistic applications less successful. The classifier's probabilities summarize real observability of a co-produced batch. No preference oracle is assumed. The check applies to contested provenance after an external job appears; it must not silently make the private demand types on the joint booking publicly observable.

Take d=0.3,e=0.6,a=0.03,β=0.7,k_i=0.01. If q=1 and g=0, A's net gain is -0.038125 and it vetoes. With q=0.10 and g=0.012, B still strictly requests inclusion, but A's gain is 0.017375 and B and C gain as well. A has a current-goal reason to spend its own resources to make this design possible; the expenditure can be conditional on all signatures. With q=0.04 at cost g=0.019, B strictly stops applying and A gains 0.017875. Of these three offered settings A prefers the last under its original goal, and both partners prefer signing it to separate booking. A downstream market in side payments can change the selected offer. If an original-goal local delegate can execute the same scoped routing, price and compare it; if an ordinary way to report urgency or compensate A is cheaper, the same preference for good governance can produce zero genuine updates. Conversely a scope-specific terminal preference remains a real modification when matched delegation ties.

A different construction uses one genuinely scarce permission to extend a continuing project. Spending it now yields current incremental benefit βe-d to the installed A. Keeping it leaves a later joint benefit H>0 to its current goal with probability t. With additive time-separated utility, a finite portfolio and a reserve that cannot be copied, it keeps the boundary when tH>βe-d. If it can cheaply replace the permission before it is needed, the continuation opportunity cost is at most that replacement expense. This is an independently useful reason to conserve a limited project allocation; a symbolic token that can be duplicated freely provides no such price. Who would pay to create an artificial permission scarcity depends on the avoided leakage and real loss of useful future extensions.

**Why this is a model of preferences.** The same A actuator can route either job. The proposed changes alter terminal rankings over other sites' service and over whether those results fall inside the shared undertaking. The founding decision, the later choice to register a job and a genuine amendment are separate histories. A multiorganization project with mixed private and joint resources can encounter the physical ownership distinction independently. The need for credible scoped terminal updates is specific to the proposed agent architecture. Public project records and amended agreements are evidence of feasible governance analogues, not evidence that human organizations literally rewrite expected-utility functions.

**Exploration ledger.** Framing: a narrower shared goal can make a wider set of founders willing to participate. Structural: the present evaluator's treatment of future admission matters more than the name of the safeguard. A mutable-label veto is dead at the βe>d instance. Siblings use a genuinely source-bound criterion, a paid check, compensation, or the real opportunity cost of using a scarce extension. The structural successor investigates what domain boundaries remain effective under later innovation and mutually beneficial amendments. The model leaves uncertain whether agents have an inexpensive way to distinguish genuinely new jobs from recombinations of previously authorized ones.

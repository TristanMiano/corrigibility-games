# Chosen standardization and productive goals

R0006 constructive intervention. Here the agents can build compatibility;
the favorable spillover is not merely assigned by the analyst. The example
compares unchanged-goal play, a negotiated common goal, and a matched actual
controller. Combat retains the finite physical dominance bound of note 115.

## 1. Actions, costs and information

Start with the additive production family in note 114. Either agent can
construct and publish a compatible interface z_i>=0. The resulting useful
knowledge spillover is

`rho=rho_0+z_B+z_R <=1`, `0<=rho_0<1`.                    (1)

Each interface consumes actual initial work with own-color opportunity
expense `kappa z_i^2/2`. The primitive technology lets a published interface
improve both directions of knowledge use. Raw research artifacts are already
accessible; the investment makes them usable. Access rights, secrecy or a
one-direction adapter would give another technology. This reciprocal public
effect is an explicit capability, not inferred from knowledge overlap.

After observing the interfaces, the parties select research/production
efforts. Physical final counts are

`q_i=Q+a x_i+rho a x_j-(c/2)x_i^2`
`    -(kappa/2)z_i^2-K_i`.                               (2)

All expenses are funded from finite initial useful-work stocks; Q and the
energy/material/throughput bounds must cover every permitted action. No
utility or future promised return supplies those resources. K_i is actual
goal/controller installation expense, when that route is chosen. The
technology and interfaces are observed; genuine goal recognition requires
the actual public verification mechanism already specified in notes 114–115.

Let `A=a^2/c`. For the interior comparison assume

`kappa > 8A/(1-rho_0)`.                                 (3)

This keeps the optimum below rho=1 as well as supplying the concavity used
below. Give each interface the independent cap `(1-rho_0)/2`; their sum
then respects rho<=1 at every profile, and (3) puts both derived choices
strictly inside their caps. Choose finite effort caps containing the derived
efforts. Binding caps can be analyzed separately rather than extending
an interior formula past its physical range.

## 2. What retained originals build

With no enforceable joint effort control, the later original effort is
`x_i=t=a/c`, independently of rho. At the interface-choice stage,

`q_i=Q+A(1/2+rho_0+z_i+z_j)-(kappa/2)z_i^2`.

Each original therefore chooses `z_F=A/kappa`. The induced spillover and
per-agent output are

`rho_F=rho_0+2A/kappa`,
`q_F^design=Q+A(1/2+rho_0)+(3/2)A^2/kappa`.               (4)

The agents already standardize under unchanged goals: they benefit from
using each other's research. A goal-change comparison that retained rho_0
as the ordinary baseline would omit this chosen repair and overstate the
advantage of modification.

## 3. What a mutually chosen common goal can build

Suppose the negotiated installed criterion is total actual clip production,
with a share-sensitive refinement if final allocation is flexible. Then
the parties choose interfaces and efforts to maximize the actual physical
sum, including all construction and research expenses. Before substituting
optimal efforts that sum is

`T=2Q+a(1+rho_0+z_B+z_R)(x_B+x_R)`
`  -(c/2)(x_B^2+x_R^2)-(kappa/2)(z_B^2+z_R^2)-K_B-K_R`.

Its joint Hessian is negative definite when `kappa c>4a^2`: the only
coupling is the rank-one block of a's between the two efforts and the two
interfaces. Equivalently its Schur complement has eigenvalues
`-kappa` and `-kappa+4A`. Hence the maximum is unique. Its symmetry gives

`z_G=2A(1+rho_0)/(kappa-4A)`,
`rho_G=(kappa rho_0+4A)/(kappa-4A)`,
`x_G=t kappa(1+rho_0)/(kappa-4A)`.                       (5)

The same result follows by maximizing the strictly concave reduced total
after research effort is optimized. Condition (3) makes rho_G<1.
The founders' actual final counts are

`q_G^design=Q+A kappa(1+rho_0)^2/[2(kappa-4A)]-K_i`.      (6)

The gross per-agent advantage over (4) is exactly

`D_design = A[12A^2+8A kappa rho_0+A kappa`
`                 +kappa^2 rho_0^2]`
`            /[2kappa(kappa-4A)] >0`.                    (7)

Thus each original prefers this route to the actual ordinary design game
when its total installation/launch expense is below (7), and when no better
actually available control or outside option displaces it. The current
common goal then chooses (5); it is not being forced to preserve a founder's
private objective. A later policy or goal revision within the same physical
opportunity set cannot improve the attained common maximum.

For an exact example take `A=1`, `kappa=12`, `rho_0=0`.
Ordinary agents choose interfaces 1/12 each, making rho_F=1/6 and yielding
`Q+5/8` clips each. Under the common goal they choose interfaces 1/4 each,
making rho_G=1/2 and yielding `Q+3/4-K_i` each. A physically funded expense
`K_i<1/8` makes both original own-color objectives strictly better off.
The construction starts with zero usable knowledge spillover, but it has
an available interface-building technology. It therefore does not contradict
the genuinely factorized no-interaction endpoint in note 112.

## 4. Adoption level, alternatives and interventions that change the result

Equations (5)–(7) prove an available, current-goal-beneficial designed
arrangement and its common-goal production continuation. Actual adoption
uses the chosen offer/conditional-installation protocol and its real costs;
the arithmetic alone is not a certificate of semantic verification or
atomic activation. An enforceable joint design-and-effort controller can
attain the same physical maximum. At equal availability/cost it can tie
genuine change; a cheaper actual route is preferred. A revocable ordinary
plan still faces the original effort and interface deviation incentives.

The causal chain is explicit: the original agents can fund compatible
interfaces; they know how those interfaces affect usable work; their own
output gains in (7) motivate consenting to a common goal/controller; the
resulting current criteria choose more standardization and research; and
the finite retained-combat bound preserves this continuation. Quadratic
construction costs and useful compatibility are simple modeling mechanisms.
The specific reciprocal interface technology and reliable goal installation
are demanding capabilities that must be assessed separately. Positive
results hold on an open region, not just the displayed numerical equality.

If public standardization also makes captured equipment more useful, insert
the changed lambda and damage/threat values from note 117. If it creates a
shared throughput bottleneck, insert that capacity constraint. If others
can copy interfaces without contributing, or choose different partners,
include those responses. Joint changes can make a different design, an
ordinary agreement or refusal preferable. Conversely, modular construction,
paid access or lower-cost verification can repair a specific failure when
their feasible cost and adoption incentives pass the same comparison.

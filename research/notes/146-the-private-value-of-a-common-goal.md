# How much private information can make a genuine common goal worth

**E8, note 146.** A club wants to give a privately valued job to the site where it does most good, then insure all members by distributing the proceeds. A fixed-goal winner may want to understate its gain when it pays other members. A successor that values the members' final holdings has a reason to pay even after the work is done. This note bounds the difference against an entire class of ordinary agreements and identifies the information interface that closes it.

## Comparison and reasons for the model

There are n initially identical sites, one expiring slot and a baseline safe stock w per site. A chosen recipient i obtains a private material supplement x_i, unavailable to others unless the recipient shares its existing liquid stock. An internal need, privately avoided rework or locally measured service value can have these properties. Early contracting precedes both the booking owner and the values. A nonmember keeps its initial booking. A private sensor distinguishes three ordered values l<m<h. Draws are independent here. An ordinarily enforceable external contract can use any histories, randomization, real redistribution or deliberate nonuse, provided a privately different type can reproduce another type's observable history. No authority independently observes the realized supplement. The agents cannot fabricate physical output. Bindable claims against safe stocks are allowed. A genuinely private supplement can coexist with externally verifiable transfers, as when partners see payments and service completion yet have different internal uses for a completed job. Full insurance in units of these stocks requires actual substitutability. For nontransferable idiosyncratic consumption see [143](143-private-urgency-and-negotiated-concern.md).

Let Y_i be final private output minus w. Common original utility functions of own Y are f(y)=y-κy²/2 on a bounded feasible interval, κ>0 with positive slope throughout. Bounded stocks, service values and liability suffice. One can extend f linearly beyond the interval, matching the endpoint slopes, to obtain globally increasing concave preferences. The reachable interval includes every mimicking deviation. Three discernible values are practical for tasks in three classes. Exact quadratic utility is a local tractable specification with an open strict example. Families with strongly concave differentiable utilities give the same type of bound below.

Put M=max_i x_i and c=M/n. The optimum for the *sum of originals' utilities*, permitting full state information, is n f(c). Serve a highest type, then equalize the holdings by a funded transfer from the winner's safe stock. Feasibility requires w≥(1-1/n)h plus setup expenses. At zero setup cost the per-member ex ante return is U*=E f(c). Founders decide with their original f. If every member consents to a criterion Σ_j f(Y_j), private revelation and voluntary settlement achieve the statewise bound in a truthful equilibrium. When it knows its own true supplement, the winner strictly prefers the equalizing transfer to keeping the whole gain. The original strictly prefers keeping it. The preference change therefore affects a genuine comparison between feasible terminal histories. No message from the winner has to certify its own value externally. Once the other reports are truthful, selecting a highest true request and sharing honors the statewise maximum, so a unilateral lie, retained service, self-revision, or refusal to pay cannot improve the shared criterion. Equal-value ties can leave several best actions. This gives an equilibrium, without unique implementation or an empirically established update technology.

With uniform real per-member setup cost k, final equal income is c-k. Every original then receives at least U*-Kk for a Lipschitz constant K on the relevant interval. The same actual routing and settlement control, sensor and stocks are available to a credible local delegate if it can be funded. It can replace genuine adoption at lower cost, tie at parity, or coexist as a more expensive outside option. An external mechanism with only unverifiable reports has a different information boundary even when all of its contracts and computation are free.

## A uniform positive gap for an external contract

Write D=n U*-E Σ_i f(Y_i) for the aggregate loss of any equilibrium under original goals in the externally observable class. For every realized history let R=M-Σ_i Y_i≥0 and a=min f'(M/n)>0. The following identity covers arbitrary correlated lottery payouts, wasted production and burned stock:

    n f(c)-Σ_i f(Y_i) = f'(c)R + (κ/2)Σ_i(Y_i-c)².                 (1)

Fix a site i with type masses p_l,p_m,p_h>0 and other sites iid with that same law. Put Δ=h-m, A_-=P(max_{j≠i} x_j<m), A_≤=P(max_{j≠i} x_j≤m). Set

    g = f(m/n+Δ)-f(m/n),              e = f(h/n)-f(m/n),
    V = A_- g - A_≤ e.                                             (2)

This deliberately ignores extra gains when a mid-ranked report wins a tie. Suppose V>0. Write K=max |f'| on the entire feasible interval, including mimicked incomes. Then every such original-goal equilibrium satisfies

    V ≤ B sqrt(D) + C D,
    B = K sqrt(2/κ) (1/sqrt(p_h)+1/sqrt(p_m)),
    C = g/[p_m a (m-l)],
    D ≥ [2V/(B+sqrt(B²+4CV))]² = D_min > 0.                       (3)

**Proof.** Conditional on i having type t, the mean distance of its realized income from the truthful benchmark c_t=max(t,max_{j≠i}x_j)/n is at most sqrt(2D/(κp_t)), by (1) and Cauchy–Schwarz. When type h imitates type m in every message and action, it can generate the same service indicator I and stock transfers as type m. Its private income increases by Δ I over the mimicked income. Independence gives the same law of other types in the two conditional experiments. Replacing actual income by c_t in the high truthful payoff and in the mimicked mid payoff costs at most B sqrt(D) in the difference. For the deviation term, both c_m+ΔI and the true mimicking income lie in the interval used for K.

Conditional on i=m, the joint event that all others have l and i goes unserved loses at least m-l aggregate output. By (1) its conditional probability is at most D/[p_m a(m-l)]. With perfect service in those states the ideal imitation gain above f(c_m) is at least A_- g. The missed services reduce this lower bound by at most C D. On all other events the added income Δ I is nonnegative. Meanwhile E[f(c_h)-f(c_m)]=A_≤ e, since there are only three types. The high type's equilibrium incentive constraint makes the net deviation unprofitable and proves (3). Rewriting its positive quadratic root avoids subtraction of near-equal numbers. A reveal-and-follow direct rule is unnecessary. The proof requires the entire lower type's observable strategy to be feasible for the higher one and the high type to have the option of retaining the extra private income. Forced transfer from a local private meter can defeat that condition.

**Concrete strict instance.** Use n=3, (l,m,h)=(1,2,3), masses (1/2,1/8,3/8), κ=1/16 and w=3. Net incomes lie in [-3,9] with bounded liability and only transfers of actual stock, so K=19/16 and a=15/16. Then V=1997/18432, C=356/45, B≈29.970, and D_min≈0.00001304 in aggregate original utility units. Equal per-site setup expense k<0.00000366 is sufficient for the genuine route to beat every symmetric ordinary equilibrium in the external-report class for every founder. Against asymmetric ordinary equilibria it gives a larger sum and cannot be Pareto dominated by one of them. This very small figure is a conservative sufficient price, not an estimated real installation cost or a tight welfare loss. Bounds with finite installation failure, a more accurate welfare optimization and a cheap local substitute change the price ceiling. The inequalities are strict over a neighborhood of the type law, values and curvature. More generally (1) can be replaced by a lower bound with any common positive lower curvature bound on the compact interval, by Taylor's inequality, retaining the argument with the corresponding a and K.

## Who would build it?

Identical founders before types and roles are known have a common interest in funding the equal-cost offer when their separate outside options and cheaper actual delegates also lose. A candidate architecture has an existing private sensor, spend authority and a public, configurable objective register on each optimizer. A mutually recognized change to that register can be feasible while a new externally controlled connection to every sensor and wallet requires installation, permission and testing. Recognition here is feasible only for a bounded template on a known execution path with recorded readback and protection through settlement. Knowing one's own utility alone supplies no readback or such protection. The ordinary control should be priced on the same architecture: replace only the objective of a genuinely separate binding decision module if it reaches the same facts and controls at lower cost. In a system with inexpensive programmable local delegation the information-based advantage disappears. These are architecture-conditioned arguments for agents' interest, not evidence about how often inexpensive utility modification is available. Admission relative to all feasible smaller coalitions and the political choice of shares are distinct decisions.

**Exploration ledger.** Framing: partners can deliberately make private information useful by aligning their later evaluations. Structural: voluntary settlement by the informed winner can complete an insurance contract. The bounded three-value gap is promising at the specified interface. A sibling tests partial concern large enough to make full payment credible. A structural successor prices the least expensive mixture of private sensing, external verification, exposure to future trade and actual preference change. The hypothesis that changing goals strictly beats an equally capable equally priced local delegate is dead at that matched instance. Its lesson is to compare actual installed operations and costs. An equal-cost mixed implementation is a sibling. A broader institutional question is which local authority the participants can afford to bind, by changing goals or by another means.

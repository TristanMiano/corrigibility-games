# Do the two machines mean the same thing by a goal?

Reading the active evaluator can settle which coefficients it contains. The two plants can still disagree about which physical product a coefficient describes. Grounding a few labels can give an exact guarantee. Agreeing on a goal that treats interchangeable labels alike can sometimes save both founders the expense.

## How many physical references certify an agreed ordering?

Suppose two fixed reference outcomes normalize the utility of a finite lottery domain to zero and one. An inspected evaluator contains the agreed vector w on the remaining n labels and actually controls the final choice. The unresolved physical-to-internal mapping is a permutation g in a known group Γ; the agreement uses the identity dictionary. A witnessed physical reference j verifies that the active runtime maps product j to label j, at cost c_j≥0. There is no subsequent switch in this batch. After all the anchors in A pass, the possible maps form the subgroup H fixing each of those anchors. The agreed ordering over all lotteries is certain exactly when

    every g in H satisfies w[g(j)] = w[j] for every j.             (217.1)

Indeed the normalized expected utility on a lottery p is the dot product with the physical vector (w[g(j)]). Equality of expected-utility orderings on all lotteries preserves the reference normalization and therefore each coordinate, for instance by comparison with lotteries over the two references. Conversely equality of the coordinates reproduces every lottery evaluation. If an initial incumbent ordering is independently known to differ from w, this verifies a genuine change over the stated domain. If incumbent and target already agree, only the present ordering is established.

The worst absolute evaluation error over lotteries for a particular g is max_j |w[g(j)]−w[j]|, attained on a pure outcome. An ε guarantee on this error preserves any strict lottery comparison whose agreed gap exceeds 2ε. For arbitrary Γ, exact certification requires the purchased anchors to hit the set of moved coordinates of every g that changes w. The same covering problem for the permutations exceeding ε gives tolerance certification.

For the full permutation group the unanchored coordinates may permute freely. The largest possible error is the range of their weights, taken as zero for zero or one remaining label. The least expense for an ε certificate therefore equals

    sum of all c_j − max over intervals I of width ε of sum_{w[j] in I} c_j.

Leaving precisely the coordinates inside a maximizing interval attains this bound. At ε=0 one can leave a whole equal-weight class ungrounded. Under a uniform prior over the residual group the posterior probability of the *exact* target is |H ∩ Stab(w)| / |H|. If m freely permutable labels remain with multiplicities m₁,…,m_k of equal weights, this becomes (m₁!⋯m_k!)/m!. A nonuniform engineering prior or a strategically chosen translation warrants a different probability. The robust certificate (217.1) covers either.

## A bargain over how fine a distinction to preserve

A supplier initially values only its own stock r. A customer cares about the products h and l according to 3h+l. The supplier can keep a released parcel for two units of own stock, or retain one unit and produce (h,l) equal to (2,0), (7/5,7/5) or (0,2). Joint production uses the shared tooling more efficiently. The incumbent keeps the parcel. Installing an evaluator r+3h+l makes the correctly mapped supplier choose (2,0); its incorrect interchange of the two product channels selects (0,2). Installing r+2(h+l) makes either mapping select the mixed plan. Both are genuine changes, with strict continuation choices.

The customer controls the parcel until a readout establishes that the selected evaluator is active. The local product-reader firmware has two possible label conventions. The prior probability of the more likely orientation is ρ≥1/2. A physical witness kit costing c can ground one sample and orient the active interpretation correctly before unlock. Otherwise the bargaining parties use their prior. They also have the option to negotiate the symmetric evaluator in advance. Their respective gross expected customer outputs are

| Agreed arrangement | Customer output in its own units |
| --- | ---: |
| Orient the fine evaluator using the witnessed sample | 6−c |
| Use the fine evaluator in the more likely orientation | 2+4ρ |
| Agree to value both labels equally | 28/5 |

At ρ=1/2 the last choice is optimal even among lotteries over physically symmetric ungrounded routing rules. The distribution is invariant under interchanging h and l, so the customer's expected value equals 2E[h+l]≤28/5. The proposed symmetric goal attains it. With the three listed choices, the customer buys the kit when c<min(2/5,4(1−ρ)). Above that price it prefers a symmetric goal for ρ<.9 and the unverified fine goal for ρ>.9. Equality gives ties. Increased evidence can make it sensible to accept *residual* semantic risk and spend less on verification. Different production menus change these numbers. In particular, a lower yield for the joint process erodes the return to symmetry.

Both parties must still want the arrangement. Suppose standalone production gives the supplier two units and the customer 2.3. An active update costs .2 supplier units, and the customer posts a price in .2 increments, transferable in stock on the active-code readout. At a price of 1.4, adoption nets the incumbent supplier 1+1.4−.2=2.2. A price of 1.2 makes it indifferent; if it accepts at indifference the customer may offer that instead. Either way the best of the three arrangements beats 2.3 for the customer, as the symmetric one already yields at least 4.2 net at the higher price. The customer's current goals can therefore fund an actual editor and, in the cheap-kit region, a witnessed calibration. Against a better outside option of 4.4 with a fixed price of 1.4, the symmetric contract is declined and an accurate, sufficiently inexpensive orientation can still win. An unchanged supplier with unrestricted private discretion keeps the parcel. An independently binding ordinary router implementing any of these rules at the same price matches it. An easier ordinary router displaces the corresponding editor if its advantage survives the readout and grounding costs. The active-code reader and stability up to conversion are substantial assumptions.

Either party can contest until the batch is converted. For instance if disruption destroys the sorting line and leaves at most half a unit of unprocessed private stock for a strength-dependent winner, both parties prefer the specified peaceful continuations before and after signing. If grounding comes after release, or a contest can capture the intact, distinguishable high product, evaluate those choices again.

**Naturality and reach.** Independent production lines can use different identifiers for a physically identical input or grade. A jointly witnessed reference lot takes time and material. Equal treatment of physically interchangeable inputs can be a deliberately chosen, verifiable objective, while a buyer who values grades differently can pay for finer distinctions. Both incentives hold over strict intervals of costs and accuracies. The finite permutation model presupposes that all relevant products and possible translations are understood. Continuous changes in scale, an entirely missing feature and different mappings during certification and use remain live siblings. The broader problem is to negotiate the economically important distinctions in an uncertain physical language.

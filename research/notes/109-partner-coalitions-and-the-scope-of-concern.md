# Partner coalitions and the scope of concern

**R0005 coalition extension.** Voluntary partner choice can assemble a
productive group or a force able to capture outsiders' resources. This
note connects those possibilities to the same surplus and goal-design
questions. It supplies exact coalition comparisons and repairs, not a
complete endogenous alliance-formation equilibrium.

## 1. Additive contest strength supports a peaceful allocation certificate

At a specified state, N agents can implement total peaceful original
welfare M, with funded transferable shares x_i>=0 and sum x_i=M. The
physical contest leaves at most L<=M useful value after real dispersion,
recovery, infrastructure loss and ammunition costs. For a coalition S,
let sigma(S)=sum_{i in S}s_i/sum_N s_i. If it fights the remaining agents,
its victory probability is sigma(S), and its maximum expected joint
original payoff is

    d(S)=sigma(S)L-c(S),    c(S)>=0.                 (1)

Here c(S) charges only additional coalition expenses outside the already
net prize L, such as formation work paid from separate supplies. Ammunition
or recovery expense already deducted in L is not charged again in c(S).
The later c_k notation uses the same convention.

This is an additive-strength contest specialization. Formation costs,
actual division within a winning group, counter-coalition response and
combat feasibility must justify (1); announcing an alliance does not
create transferable output or strength without a technology.

The allocation x_i=M s_i/sum_N s_i satisfies

    x(S)=sigma(S)M>=d(S)                            (2)

for every coalition. Thus no coalition can make all its originals weakly
and one strictly better off by the stated war-and-division deviation.
Ordinary internal productive deviations must be included separately; if
they obey the same bound, (2) covers them too. Goal setup and recurring
costs must already be charged to M and its funding account. A higher real
cooperative surplus or lower recoverable war prize enlarges this margin.

This is an allocation certificate, not automatic bargaining selection.
When a genuine all-participant goal with negotiated weights x_i/M and
the thresholds in 107/108 implements that allocation at least as cheaply
as the actual alternatives, it is an optimal cooperative method. If an
ordinary method is cheaper, it supplies the same allocation through
unchanged goals. Neither comparison deletes available combat.

## 2. Coalition strength complementarities give a different boundary

For three equal-strength agents, suppose a coalition of k has victory
probability

    p_k=k^r/[k^r+(3-k)^r],    k=1,2, r>0.          (3)

The exponent represents a specified military complementarity; it is not
inferred from intelligent agency. Let individual and pair attack costs be
c_1,c_2. A coalition-proof peaceful allocation against these deviations
must satisfy x_i>=p_1 L-c_1 and

    x_i+x_j>=p_2 L-c_2                            (4)

for each pair. Summing the three pair inequalities gives

    M >= (3/2)(p_2 L-c_2).                          (5)

Together with M>=3(p_1 L-c_1), nonnegative-payoff feasibility, and the
specified transferable-division technology, these inequalities are also
sufficient: x_i=M/3 satisfies every singleton and pair constraint.
Thus this symmetric class has an exact boundary, not just a failed quote.

For r=2, p_1=1/5,p_2=4/5. With M=1,L=9/10,c_1=c_2=0, individuals
prefer their peaceful 1/3 to unilateral war's 9/50. Each pair, however,
can get expected total 18/25>2/3. No allocation of M=1 satisfies all
pair constraints, since (5) requires M>=27/25. Positive pair costs below
4/75 preserve this obstruction. At or above that boundary, equal shares
are a constructive repair for the declared war deviations.

Other repairs include increasing noncapturable productive benefit until
M>=27/25 at fixed L, reducing the recoverable prize to L<=5/6 at fixed M,
or changing the military/defensive technology that determines p_2. Each
is a parameter effect until an actor can perform and fund the corresponding
action and prefers it under the resulting bargaining response. New ordinary
contracts or goals cannot create the missing original joint value simply
by renaming the same transfers.

## 3. A coalition's internal concern can support external combat

Suppose a chosen pair installs the equal-weight version of (1) in 108,
but its minimum ranges only over its two members. Its goal genuinely
reorders uneven allocations within the pair. If successful combat divides
L equally between the pair, and defeat leaves both zero, the members'
expected installed scores are p_2 L/2. Under an equal peaceful allocation
their scores are M/3. They therefore prefer the attack when

    p_2 L > 2M/3.                                  (6)

In the example, each gets expected 9/25=.36 rather than 1/3. A real
one-time coalition-goal cost below 2/75 per member leaves a strict original
gain from the implemented package. A matched ordinary sharing controller
can supply the same coalition capacity when available. Actual coalition
launch and dependable within-coalition division remain requirements.

The mechanism is specific: concern for chosen partners, combined with a
profitable coalition capture opportunity, favors fighting outsiders. It
requires neither an intrinsic reward for violence nor an assumption that
all cooperative preferences have this scope. Scope is a consequential
design choice and a potentially natural source of group-based incentives.

## 4. Wider negotiated concern can support peace at the same physical state

Now let each participant's goal include all three agents:

    G_i=(1-beta_i)x_i+beta_i min_j x_j.              (7)

An equal peaceful allocation has score M/3. If a pair wins and splits L
equally, its members' scores are (1-beta_i)L/2 because the outsider gets
zero; defeat gives zero to the pair. A pair member prefers peace to this
attack whenever

    beta_i >= 1-2M/(3p_2 L).                        (8)

For the example the threshold is 2/27. This local comparison is weaker
than the global maximum certificate beta_i>=2/3, which covers every
feasible output vector of total at most M. With beta_i>2/3, the balanced
outcome gives every current goal its maximum; no later coalition goal
revision producing an attainable alternative can strictly improve one.
Arbitrary code freezing is unnecessary for that continuation statement.

All originals individually prefer M/3 to their unilateral war value here,
so a sufficiently inexpensive all-participant installation is individually
beneficial under that comparison. Yet the originals have the profitable
pair deviation in section 2 before those goals are installed. Individual
adoption, an initial coalition-proof arrangement and post-change coalition
stability are therefore distinct. A selected individual adoption path can
be constructive even when a coalition could preempt it; a general theory
of initial selection must analyze the competing alliances rather than
silently forbid them.

## 5. Renewable output can change the coalition incentives in different ways

For the r=2,c_2=0 example, the pair constraint is M>=6L/5. A renewable
service that adds B units of genuinely noncapturable dated benefit raises
M at fixed L and repairs the constraint once B>=6L/5-M. If instead a
chosen investment adds Delta to peaceful output and rho Delta to the
recoverable war prize, its coalition-stability margin changes by

    Delta[1-(6/5)rho].                              (9)

For rho<5/6 it improves this margin; for rho>5/6 it worsens it. Productive
growth that remains valuable after capture can make a military coalition
more attractive even while total peaceful output grows. Personalized
delivered benefits, damage-prone complementary machinery and broadly
shared protection can change the ratio. Their actual costs, timing,
ownership, work requirements and possibility of preemption must be modeled.

This supplies a concrete bridge to intervention research: identify who
controls the productive form or membership scope, compare its funded
cost with that actor's current-goal gain under the new allocation, and
solve the other parties' responses. The present result establishes exact
conditional thresholds and feasible allocation comparisons. It does not
yet establish a chosen investment policy, inevitable alliance formation
or a population frequency of broad versus narrow concern.

## 6. The original coalition boundary extends to arbitrary group size

For n identical-strength originals, let a k-member coalition have
p_k=k^r/[k^r+(n-k)^r] and actual additional expense c_k. If every member
has the same feasible transferable peaceful share, the war-deviation core
is nonempty exactly when

    M >= max_{1<=k<n} (n/k)(p_k L-c_k),             (10)

together with nonnegative outside-value feasibility. Necessity follows by
summing the constraints for all k-member coalitions: each individual appears
in the same fraction k/n of them. Sufficiency follows by assigning M/n to
each original. This checks the entire declared coalition family rather
than only one proposed alliance.

For n=3,c_k=0 and L<=M<3L/2, condition (10) is the interval

    log_2(3L/M-1) <= r <= log_2[2M/(3L-2M)].       (11)

The lower boundary is the singleton constraint and the upper one is the
pair constraint. For L=9M/10 the interval is
log_2(17/10)<=r<=log_2(20/7). Outside it, respectively a singleton or a
pair has an incompatible original claim on the peaceful total. The
parameter changes both attack and defense effectiveness; this is a
particular contest aggregation technology, not a prevalence claim about
large or small groups.

For r=2 and large n, the largest normalized claim is approached by a
coalition of fraction 1/sqrt(2) of the population. To check this, write
x=(n-k)/k and maximize (1+x)/(1+x^2). Its derivative changes sign at
x=sqrt(2)-1 and its maximum is (1+sqrt(2))/2. For finite n the maximum
is over the actual integer k's. Productive surplus, real alliance costs
or a lower recoverable prize can satisfy (10); their feasibility and
selection must still be established.

## 7. Voluntary compensation after combat changes the incentive calculation

The earlier zero-loser comparisons must be revisited when a winning group
can deliberately transfer output to defeated outsiders. Suppose all agents
currently use G_i=(1-beta)x_i+beta min_j x_j. A winning k-member coalition
can divide prize L among everyone. If y goes to outsiders, balanced division
inside and outside bounds each member's jointly attainable score by

    (1-beta)(L-y)/k + beta min((L-y)/k,y/(n-k)).

The optimum is either all L divided among the winners, or L/n to every
agent. The winning coalition prefers universal division precisely when
beta>1-k/n; at equality either extreme can be selected. This follows from
the constant derivative on each of the two segments in y. Summing members'
scores gives the same upper bound without assuming they initially agree
on equal division, so a jointly improving winning-coalition allocation is
covered.

The defending n-k agents, if they win, analogously prefer universal division
when beta>k/n. Away from those tie boundaries, an attacking member's current
expected score is therefore

    J_k(beta)=p_k max((1-beta)L/k,L/n)
             +(1-p_k)(L/n) 1{beta>k/n}.             (12)

For majority attacking coalitions, the local restraint threshold in section 4
continues to suffice when M>=L: universal winning division or later receipt
from the defenders stays below M/n. For minority attackers, the defenders'
change in compensation policy can create a different combat region.

### An exact insurance-induced attack and two repairs

Take n=3,r=1,M=1,L=9/10 and a singleton attacker, so p_1=1/3. At beta=3/10,
neither side compensates outsiders after victory; the attacker's score is
21/100, below peaceful 1/3. At beta=2/5, the two defenders would share L
with everyone after their victory, while the singleton would keep its own
victory prize. Its expected current score becomes

    (1/3)(3/5)(9/10)+(2/3)(3/10)=19/50 > 1/3.      (13)

Within 1/3<beta<2/3, increasing beta beyond 5/9 restores the attacker's
preference for peace. For beta>=2/3, all winners share universally and
J_1=L/3<1/3. Thus the same voluntary-transfer menu yields explicit peace,
combat and repaired-peace regions; treating all losers as necessarily
uncompensated would miss the middle region.

There is also a chosen goal-design repair. Before the attack, the defenders
can consider an accurately conditioned rule that shares within the defending
group but withholds the post-combat transfer from a publicly observed first
aggressor. If that rule is genuinely implemented and recognized, the beta=2/5
attacker's score falls to 9/50<1/3, so it prefers peace. Each defender's
old broad criterion then gets 1/3 instead of its unguarded-war expectation
1/5, a pre-cost improvement of 2/15. Sufficiently small real installation
and verification costs preserve both the defenders' approval and the strict
deterrence margin. A matched ordinary controller can supply the safeguard
when it has the required authority and cost.

This is a beneficial conditional repair, not a completed initial formation
game. Both defenders must be able to arrange the rule, identify initiation
accurately, retain its reference under later choices and make it known in
time. Mistaken attribution, hidden attacks, cheap incompatible replacement
criteria or a different post-combat technology require another analysis.
The unguarded beta=2/5 package is also not an original Pareto improvement:
the defenders lose original welfare in its combat continuation. Any claim
that all originals would voluntarily install it needs additional productive
gains or compensation. Current-goal combat incentives and initial consensual
adoption remain different questions.

The direct-attack repair has a further limitation worth preserving. Let its
defenders retain beta=2/5 on peaceful histories and use the internal sharing
rule after being attacked. Their defended combat score is then
(2/3)(L/2)=3/10 each. A newly credible threat demanding total tribute 1/20
would leave them 37/120 each, strictly above that combat score. It would
give the attacker receipt 23/60 and old broad score 53/150, improving on
1/3 by 1/50. A cheap publicly recognized outcome-target goal rewarding a
receipt at least 23/60 can make attack after refusal credible while preferring
accepted tribute. Thus sufficiently cheap further goal choice can exploit
the guarded arrangement without actually fighting. The displayed safeguard
repairs direct attack under its stated continuation; it is not a proof of
immunity to the larger threatening-goal menu.

By contrast, beta>=2/3 with all three participants included gives each
current goal the global maximum M/3 under the fixed total frontier. Such
an attacker would not approve the tribute-producing revision: its old
score changes by (1-3beta/2) times the tribute and hence cannot rise.
That is a stronger continuation repair. Getting an agent that already
profits from extortion to consent to it needs productive gains, compensation,
different partners or another actual change in its alternatives. Current
stability and the incentive to adopt the stabilizing goal remain separate.

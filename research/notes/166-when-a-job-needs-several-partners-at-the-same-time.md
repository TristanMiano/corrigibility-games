# When a job needs several partners at the same time

**E8, note 166.** Suppose a site needs *both* other facilities' contributions to finish its project. A return can arise even though no isolated favor for that site is productive. Conversely, drawing an ordinary directed arrow for every useful input can count one completed job twice. There is a test for when a network of complete recipes, including the risk of paying a false member, can make genuine participation attractive to every original founder.

## Complete recipes and a size-free boundary

There are finitely many job opportunities, one drawn at a known date. Recipe e requires contributions from a nonempty donor set S_e and produces a benefit for site j outside that set. Donor i forgoes d_ie>0 original value by supplying its input. With all required inputs the recipient gains r_e>0. A missing input spoils the joint job. Honest contributors can see earlier inputs or use a design with simultaneous releases before losing them. The base calculation concerns complete jobs. Independently useful partial output can be included as a separate feasible recipe with its true yield and opportunity costs. A binding ordinary dispatcher can use the same recipes if it has the required local authority.

Use the early signatures and staged goal activation of 163. The finite genuine evidence cells (e,z) have capacity λ_e P_e(z)>0. A proposed success activates a goal on that job. If only its beneficiary j fakes, its relative chance of the same observation is L_je(z). For the zero-fee, zero-outside-return classification give each job-cell its *effective receipt* R=r_e(1-L_je(z)). This is the difference in a founder's potential reward for configuring sincerely versus bluffing, per unit of all-genuine probability. With authenticated error-free installation use R=r_e. Retain only cells with R>0. Others weakly worsen every founder's individual truth margin and can be skipped. Scaling the execution probability in a retained cell scales the entire recipe together. A false donor who keeps its input can spoil the job for others, but under its original own-only goal that is immaterial to its unilateral return. Its truthful gain calculation still charges the full cost of its own honest donation.

For every recipe producing a benefit for j, form a nonnegative column whose entry for i∈S_e is d_ie/R and whose other entries are zero. At zero expense the following conditions are equivalent:

1. Gate probabilities exist which give **every founder strictly positive benefit over pretending** and over leaving for separate work.
2. It is possible to choose one retained recipe column for each recipient j so that the square matrix C of columns has spectral radius ρ(C)<1.

The spectral radius is the largest absolute eigenvalue. The entries measure how much of each participant's own value has to be supplied per unit of effective benefit to the recipient. The test accounts for all necessary inputs at once. Each participant needs at least one retained way to receive. Different positive cardinal scales for the original utilities produce a diagonal similarity transform of C, hence preserve this boundary.

**Proof.** Write z_j for the total effective expected benefit delivered to j. Under strict improvement all z_j are positive. If several recipes for j are used, weight their input columns in proportion to their contributions to z_j. With the resulting column-mixture matrix C_mix, the vector of truth margins is z-C_mix z. If it is strictly positive, the coordinatewise ratios (C_mix z)_i/z_i have maximum below one. The induced norm weighted by z gives ρ(C_mix)<1. Therefore p=(I-C_mix^T)^(-1)1 is positive: its convergent geometric series starts with the identity. For each column j choose a single recipe with the smallest p-weighted input. Then (p^T C)_j≤(p^T C_mix)_j<p_j. The same weighted-norm argument on the transpose gives ρ(C)<1. This proves necessity even if feasible mixing was originally allowed.

Conversely take such a selection and set z=(I-C)^(-1)1, again using the nonnegative geometric series. Then z-Cz=1. Multiply all activities by a sufficiently small positive common factor to respect every chosen event's finite positive probability and service capacity. This gives a strict original improvement and a strict incentive over faking. The argument only uses one chosen recipe per site for *existence at arbitrarily small scale*. At fixed expenses it can be necessary to use several small windows to pay everyone. With known fees and outside options, replace the term d_e(p_i+q_i) in 163.2 by Σ_(i in S_e) d_ie(p_i+q_i). The same box-support proof gives the precise finite-capacity test. Multiple alternatives competing in the same cell instead require the maximum over its feasible recipes, including local use. Choosing separate probabilities whose sum exceeds one there would invent capacity.

**Can the common goal carry out those jobs?** The positive product-of-margins construction in 163 gives α_i>0. On every activated recipe the decision to issue the authorization satisfies

    α_j r_e ≥ Σ_(i in S_e) α_i d_ie.

In a publicly observed sequential contribution process, the last required donor strictly wants to complete whenever the benefit exceeds the remaining weighted cost. By backward induction each earlier donor wants to contribute after an intact prefix, taking into account all still-required costs and the final usable result. Once a required input is missing, unused inputs can be retained. At a weak equality at the initial step, agreed priority can select completion. Thus the previously uncommitted donors can genuinely prefer to contribute under their new common goal. On a simultaneous blind move with worthless partial inputs, everyone withholding can also be an equilibrium. Granting a coordinator an ordinary atomic batch release is a serious alternative where that authority exists. A physical contest remains available throughout but, in this particular short window, consumes work and spoils the unfinished result; no one can recover an extra valuable outside stock by initiating it. Where an unfinished job can instead be taken over profitably, re-evaluate the actual outside and late decisions.

The productive input-output test underlying the matrix argument has classical predecessors. The application here ties original preference gains to the service a false member can obtain and the credibility of a single later objective controlling heterogeneous donors. The spectral condition establishes existence at the stated rung. A proposer still needs to find and finance viable terms, and later coalitions can have their own feasible recipes. ## A three-facility calibration, and who pays to improve it

Suppose any one site may be the beneficiary, with probability 1/3 each. The other two must each supply one unit of their own valuable effort. The honest and fake beneficiary pass with probabilities t and m<t. The two off-diagonal entries per column are d/[r(1-m/t)]. Its row sums and spectral radius are 2d/[r(1-m/t)]. Every founder can strictly gain at zero setup exactly when (1-m/t)r>2d. Using r/d for each donor as though it were a separate completed project would predict success over a larger and incorrect region. Conversely, valuing just one donor's incomplete contribution at zero and inferring that the three cannot gain would miss the positive region entirely. The model tests the *whole bundle* against the useful work each founder forgoes.

At r=2.5,d=1,t=0.8,m=0.2, the material joint project produces more than the two forgone local units, yet no choice of gates based on this beneficiary reading gives all founders strict incentives to configure rather than fake. Catching more false registrations can repair the problem. If a funded second source of evidence preserves t=0.8 and lowers m to 0.1, each founder's gross truth margin under delivery on a positive reading is [(0.8-0.1)2.5-0.8×2]/3=0.05. Each pays 0.02 for its genuine configuration. Its actual original gain over keeping its own projects is 0.8(2.5-2)/3-0.02≈0.11333. A founder who can buy that second source for an extra 0.06, conditional on unanimous enrollment, strictly prefers to pay over a zero-gain refusal, and each other founder strictly prefers the proposed deal. The extra inspection fee is paid even on a false signature, so it affects the sponsor's participation comparison and does not subsidize configuring versus faking. A cheap ordinary control that can coordinate all necessary local contributions could achieve more: with certain dispatch it obtains gross (2.5-2)/3≈0.16667 per founder. Use that actual alternative whenever available.

**Naturality.** Multi-facility complements have a specific independent example. Chandra's Cycle 26 call requires joint applicants to explain why all requested observations are needed, retains partner review, and expressly distinguishes complementary observations from requests for simultaneous scheduling. Its planners describe the work of actually finding compatible windows. In the public approved-joint lists for Cycles 24, 25 and 26, respectively 11 of 39, 4 of 25 and 7 of 33 approved rows had positive time from at least two *additional* partner facilities. Count positive numerical time columns within each row, excluding Chandra, without adding different facilities' time units. Cycle 26 proposal 26400197 lists three joint partners in its label but gives positive numeric time to two. Across the three lists that makes 22 of 97 joint awards with at least two additional facilities. This is evidence that such coalitions are actually selected in this institution. It measures approvals, not completed observations, foregone private utilities, approximate evidence rates or voluntary terminal updates. Genuine configuration and its discriminating inspection remain technological premises of the adoption result. **Exploration ledger.** The framing candidate is that returns from cooperation can require complementary contributions from an entire group. The structural candidate compares every required input with the usable receipt reserved for a sincere participant. The size-free matrix test is promising for finite single-beneficiary recipes. The instance readings that credit each partial contribution with the whole output, or price every incomplete contribution at zero and discard the bundle, both fail at the examples above. A sibling coordinates a complete bundle with sequential assurance. A higher-rung successor treats overlapping deadlines, partial salvage and several separately valued outputs from the same bundle.

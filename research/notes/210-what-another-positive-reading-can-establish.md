# What another positive reading can establish

Suppose the proposed goal is a particular ordering over a declared set of project lotteries. Receiving the right product on a pilot, observing an approved controller on a bench, and seeing that controller decide an uninspected live job are different experiments. This note asks when another observation changes a buyer's confidence and when it would pay to obtain the observation. The state probabilities below are conditional on what was already signed and observed. They may come from an incentive calculation such as 206, or be explicit uncertain assumptions.

## The amount of evidence a claim needs

Let G be the event that the live evaluator has the agreed ranking at the decision of interest. Label other states by the relevant alternatives, such as an honest but different goal, a retained evaluator, a failed actuator, or a deliberately false claim. A failed actuator can coexist with a genuine goal, so cross-classify it with G whenever both are possible. With state weights w_i and transcript likelihoods L_i, the necessary and sufficient condition for posterior confidence at least 1−ε, when w_G L_G>0, is

    Σ_{i outside G} w_i L_i / (w_G L_G) ≤ ε/(1−ε).                (210.1)

Here the denominator can sum over several states satisfying G. Use the likelihood of the entire observation procedure, including which inspections were selected or withheld. This is Bayes' rule on a named class, with actual opportunity and reporting incentives included in its prior and likelihoods. A robust guarantee over several plausible laws requires (210.1) under each law admitting the transcript. An upper bound on each likelihood ratio and lower odds on the target suffice, with equality possible if the bounds can be attained together. If a fraction of initially possible alternatives gives exactly the target's transcript law under every allowed pre-release experiment, their prior odds against the target survive even adaptive testing. Priors excluding them need a reason, such as a materially enforced restriction or a strict incentive covering those alternatives.

A point goal drawn only from a continuous distribution has zero prior mass. An ordinary finite observation with an integrable conditional likelihood gives it zero posterior mass as well. A named installer that puts positive probability on exactly the promised template supplies an atom; a proved equality readout can instead be singular relative to the usual noisy observation model. For a tolerance bargain use the mass of the entire agreed band. If all possible goals there recommend the same live action, B can also have high confidence in delivery while remaining uncertain about exact preferences. If the founder's old ordering already lies in that band, establishing a current match leaves the historical question of a *change* open.

## A common unobserved cause and a useful second observation

Let prior masses g,m,v sum to one. A genuine installation G and a mismatched controller M produce the same law for every pilot readout. The third state V is an ordinary visible mismatch. On each conditionally independent pilot, pass probabilities are t,t,e with 0<e<t. After n passes,

    P(G | n passes)=g/[g+m+v(e/t)^n].                            (210.2)

The upper limit is g/(g+m). To reach a target τ strictly below the limit use n≥max(0,ceil(log[v/(g(1/τ−1)−m)]/log(t/e))) for v>0. No finite all-pass sample attains equality at the limit when v>0. The probability of seeing that exact transcript is (g+m)t^n+ve^n. A high posterior conditional on it can coexist with many genuine installations denied access.

For g=.60,m=.15,v=.25, t=.9,e=.3, repeated pilots approach 80% confidence. Now suppose another inspection can observe whether the tested unit has actual authority over the future delivery. Conditional on type it is independent of the pilots, with pass probabilities .8,.1,.8 for G,M,V respectively. After three pilot passes and this further pass the genuine posterior is **2592/2713**, about 95.54%. The further observation probes precisely the ambiguity left by the pilots. At the three-pass history, normalized state weights before this new reading are proportional to .60,.15,.25/27. A customer gaining one unit on reliable release and losing nine on a mismatched release requires 90% confidence. It declines without the new observation and releases on its positive. Its maximum price for buying the extra observation at that history is

    [.60×.8−9(.15×.1+(.25/27)×.8)]/[.60+.15+.25/27]
      =1503/4100 units.

An inspection costing less is actually worth buying if available there. These material stakes can arise when a high-value batch can otherwise be kept, and the controllers that look identical on the pilot make different live choices. If both controllers implement an equally durable, complete delivery for every remaining relevant job, a buyer valuing only that delivery gets zero decision benefit from learning their semantic difference. A later supplier, project or revision right can make the distinction consequential.

## Why a long string of perfect readings may tell a different story

Change only the pilot law of M. Suppose the unit making the readings can be stuck on positive for the entire batch, owing to a shared calibration or stale state. It always passes, whereas a genuine installation still passes independent readings with probability .9. With the same weights, the posterior after n passes is now g t^n/(g t^n+m+ve^n). It increases on the next pass exactly when v(t−e)e^n>m(1−t). In this example it reaches its maximum at two passes and then decreases, tending to zero along ever-longer all-pass transcripts. This is a result about that particular history. A negative reading rules out the stuck state and can help discriminate the other two. A technician who reports only the longest passing streak changes the observation law again.

**Naturality and construction.** Reusing the same testing fixture is cheap and can reproduce a stable calibration error or test a standby controller. A second observation that physically witnesses control at delivery addresses a different link. Its independence and accuracy need grounds in the actual installation, especially if both instruments use a common reference. A client with the calculated exposure can buy it; a producer able to offer a binding ordinary service may make that purchase unnecessary. The repaired sibling varies the source of evidence or the tested decision. The broader question asks which latent alternatives remain indistinguishable after the parties themselves choose what may be observed.

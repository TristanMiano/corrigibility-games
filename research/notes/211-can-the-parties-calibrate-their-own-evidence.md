# Can the parties calibrate their own evidence?

How would a customer learn the sensitivity and false-passage probabilities used in 206? A verified reference unit is one route. Another is to study two different contracts in which the share of genuine installations differs. This second route needs strong, testable measurement assumptions. It can be informative even if neither individual inspection is a perfect reference. The statistical starting point is the two-population model of Hui and Walter (1980), §2, [L86](../LITERATURE.md#r0008--recognizing-an-agreed-preference-under-uncertain-capabilities).

## Recovering a latent rate across contracts

Suppose each contract j in {1,2} has genuine fraction p_j. Two readings i in {1,2} are taken on a representative set of installations in each contract, including those declined for delivery. A test's pass rate is e_i for a retained controller and t_i for a genuine one, held constant across the contracts. Let d_i=t_i−e_i. Conditional on the actual controller, the readings are independent. Both tests discriminate, and independent semantic grounds give the direction d_1>0. The contracts have different installation rates. These assumptions include the link between the latent state and the *named* promise. Data on two displays alone cannot name the underlying state.

Write m_ij for the observed population mean of test i in contract j and C_j for the within-contract covariance of the two tests. Then

    m_ij=e_i+d_i p_j,       C_j=d₁d₂ p_j(1−p_j).

**211.1.** For D=p₂−p₁, Δ_i=m_i2−m_i1, put R_j=C_j/(Δ₁Δ₂) and A=1+R₂−R₁. The exact population tables recover

    D=sign(Δ₁)/sqrt(A²+4R₁),    p₁=(1−AD)/2,     p₂=p₁+D,
    d_i=Δ_i/D,                  e_i=m_i1−d_i p₁,  t_i=e_i+d_i.

Indeed Δ_i=d_i D, so R_j=p_j(1−p_j)/D² and A=(1−2p₁)/D. Substitution proves the inversion and the sign uses the independent orientation of the first test. The inferred rates must be probabilities and explain all table entries. Relabeling both hidden classes gives the second algebraic solution without the orientation premise. In a game where agents can secretly alter which apparatus acts, an independent link to the live evaluator is a stronger requirement than recovering two statistical classes.

The [small-bond game](209-how-much-collateral-will-the-parties-choose.md) provides one reason to expect different rates. A customer with zero available collateral and one with capacity .5 each prefer their feasible maximum on the increasing part of the upper equilibrium branch. These give p₁=112/175 and p₂=116/175. A second readout with t₂=.75,e₂=.25 can be recorded alongside the first with t₁=.9,e₁=.1. If only the first drives the current allocation, the second is physically independent conditional on the evaluator, and its collection creates no future strategic reward for manipulating a trace, the inversion applies. The change in the second mean is only 2/175. Changing the incentives to install need not supply a large amount of calibration information.

## Finite data and unknown common causes

For N independent batches per contract under these laws, keep every parameter vector that reproduces each of the six observed rates (two marginals and one joint positive per group) to within δ=sqrt(log(12/α)/(2N)). With probability at least 1−α the true vector is in this set. For completeness, the log moment-generating function of a centered Bernoulli variable is zero with zero slope at zero, and its second derivative is a tilted Bernoulli variance at most 1/4. Thus it is at most λ²/8. Applying the exponential Markov bound to independent observations and optimizing λ gives a two-sided probability ≤2 exp(−2Nδ²). A union bound proves the claim without assuming independence among the six empirical rates. A lower bound on a future positive's posterior can be obtained by minimizing (210.1)'s posterior over the entire feasible set with a positive pass probability. The confidence level of the *calibration procedure* and the resulting probability for the *individual installation* are distinct quantities. A sample with an empty set suggests a failed premise or a tail event and does not certify a goal.

At 95% coverage this conservative bound needs more than 80,000 batches per contract just to make the possible error 2δ in a difference of means smaller than the illustrated second-mean shift. This is a sufficient precision calculation, not a lower bound on every estimation scheme. Trials with known genuine and retained references, or a larger independently justified difference in adoption, can be much more informative. Two regimes observed only after a passing first test lack the required joint tables.

Conditional dependence can be decisive. Imagine two readouts are copies of one instrument, each positive on .4 of the first contract and .6 of the second, always in agreement. The error pair (e,t)=(.25,.75) with true rates (.30,.70) and the pair (.10,.90) with rates (.375,.625) produce exactly the same joint data. Yet the first contract's positive gives genuine confidence .5625 in one case and .84375 in the other. Both instruments have the favorable orientation. Even physically distinct sensors can become dependent after different latent failures are pooled: if a retained population mixes two independently measured subtypes with weight u, the extra conditional covariance is u(1−u)(e₁A−e₁B)(e₂A−e₂B). Changing the mixture by changing a contract can also change each aggregate false-passage rate. The maintained model must address this in both groups.

**Who would pay?** Different pledge capacities can generate variation without making either participant buy a deliberately inferior deal. A customer controlling only one plant would bear the foregone gain if it assigned some jobs to a less valuable contract. It may pay to collect the second reading if the improvement to later consent, production or refusal decisions exceeds that cost and the reading's real expense. The identifiability proof establishes opportunity, not that purchase. A paid behavioral experiment, a physical reference disclosing the controller on a sample, or independent evidence of the attempt can supply different missing information. The broader candidate is calibration under strategic variation in both adoption and measurement quality.

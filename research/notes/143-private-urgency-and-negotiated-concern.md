# Partial concern, private urgency and the third reporter

**E8, note 143.** The framing candidate is that agreeing to care about others can make private information usable in a cooperative decision. The structural candidate is that the composition of the group changes whom a false claim would displace. In this instance a *third* partner can make partial genuine concern sufficient for truthful reports, while the two-party organization with the same concern has a bluff incentive. This is a claim about the stated reporting equilibrium.

## The institution participants can choose

Keep the slot, protected setup stocks and short-expiry combat embedding of 140. Each site privately observes whether its own service would yield high or low transformed benefit h or l, 0<l<h<1. The incumbent becomes publicly known. If three members have agreed, the two nonowners each send one sealed high/low message before either learns the other's message. The incumbent decides who gets the slot. It knows its own value, sees both reports and can always retain the job itself. There is no payment in this base reporting game. All states have positive probability. The incumbent can route to either other site in time. A tie coin independent of the messages is drawn after submission. Access to a private value can be technically local even if eligibility and completed service are public. An ordinary planner that can read each value has a stronger informational interface.

Member i's changed goal attaches weight 1 to its own realized original utility increment and beta_i to the increments of other members, with 0<beta_i<1. For example use v_j(q_j)=1-exp[-gamma(q_j-s_j)] with s_j the public safe stock after funded installation. This evaluates actual terminal output, including losses, on the stated project domain. On service histories a recipient gets l or h and others receive zero. The goal is genuinely different: an owner can strictly prefer remote high service to local low even though its original preferred the latter. A sufficiently inexpensive consensual change can support this behavior. An ordinary delegate inside each reporter with full access to type and matched decision authority can reproduce it if available. Later authority and external observers have to be specified for both routes.

At truthful beliefs, a high incumbent keeps the slot since h>beta_i h. A low incumbent keeps it if both requests are low and strictly prefers an actually high requester if beta_i h>l. With two high reports, either remote recipient gives the incumbent the same current score. Let a_i be the probability that requester i wins that tie, with the two a's summing to one. This can be a mutually selected tie policy. Unpredictability until reports close matters. A public deterministic ranking known at reporting time produces different incentives.

## The private reporting inequalities

For a low requester i, put r_i=P(the other requester is high | i low, incumbent low). Truth versus a false high differs only when the incumbent is low. In that event a bluff gets i the slot for sure if the other is low and with probability a_i if the other is high. In the first case the discarded job would have been a low one at the incumbent. In the second it would have been an urgent one at the other requester. The conditional expected gain from a bluff is exactly

    (1-r_i) l(1-beta_i) + r_i a_i (l-beta_i h).         (1)

Multiply by the positive probability of a low incumbent conditional on i low for the full interim gain. A high requester's truthful high gains h-beta_i l when both others are low, and gains a_i(h-beta_i h) when the other requester is high and the incumbent is low. Both are nonnegative, and under full support at least one term is strictly positive. No requester knows which case it faces. Since every report profile and local type have positive on-path probability, Bayes' rule supplies the truthful beliefs at all of the incumbent's corresponding information sets.

If beta_i h>l and 0<r_i<1, both low requesters are willing to tell the truth precisely when one can choose tie probabilities satisfying

    a_i >= B_i = (1-r_i)l(1-beta_i) / [r_i(beta_i h-l)],
    B_1+B_2 <= 1.                                        (2)

Apply this condition for each possible incumbent; its own routing condition must also hold. Strict slack in the sum allows both low-report constraints to be strict. A recipient assigned zero priority would bluff when doing so cannot displace a truly high job. Giving it a chance to displace the other urgent requester makes exaggeration more costly under its changed preferences. That response makes an agreed random tie policy a consequential design choice.

For exchangeable agents with beta_i=beta and r_i=r, a fair tie rule gives the threshold

    beta >= l(1-r/2) / [l(1-r)+hr/2].                     (3)

It exceeds l/h and is below one for 0<r<1. In a pair there is no other requester to displace. A low requester gains l(1-beta)>0 whenever the incumbent is low and believes its claim. Full common concern, paid claims, an audit, spot compensation or a credible future sanction are candidate repairs. Full common concern makes low and high misstatements at some ties indifferent, with truthful play a best response. This two-party comparison prunes the specified truthful partial-concern protocol only.

**Linked exact check.** The two demand laws in 140 give r=23/76 and 29/76. Formula (3) gives 129/175 and 123/181. With beta=7/10 the latter trio admits truthful reporting, strictly for both private requester types; the former fails the truthful low-report test for every symmetric or asymmetric tie split. Increasing negotiated concern above 129/175 or altering the information/claim process can repair the former. Both laws have the same pair statistics. The third-order observation determines both the symmetric admission budget and the truth threshold, through distinct calculations. Initial acceptance still requires the honest continuation to pay for setup relative to actual alternatives. A truthful equilibrium need not be the only equilibrium.

For n>=3 exchangeable sites with independent high probability p, retain a high incumbent's priority and use a uniform lottery among high nonowners if it is low. Conditional on i and the incumbent both low, let K be the number of high sites among the n-2 remaining nonowners. Set A=P(K=0) and D=E[1{K>0}/(1+K)]. The bluff gain becomes A l(1-beta)+D(l-beta h). Thus the threshold is l(A+D)/(lA+hD). For iid urgency D/A is the sum over k=1,...,n-2 of choose(n-2,k)[p/(1-p)]^k/(1+k). It strictly increases with n and diverges, so the required concern decreases toward l/h. Total receipt opportunity, extra reporting expense, binding capacity and who pays for each connection still enter initial membership. This is a structural generation move and a checked reporting calculation, not an unconditional claim that larger groups are always chosen.

## A constructive initial choice and the ordinary information comparison

Under symmetric truthful play, a highest-valued job in the club is always served, including when a high incumbent retains the slot. Symmetry before the rights draw gives each the same z=M_m/3 as in 140. Initial founders compare the resulting CE *minus real setup and message expense* to their actual alternatives. Where reciprocity is needed, parties can fund contingent templates activated only when all required sites attest their effective criteria. Before the request types are drawn they can withhold or choose a competing contract. If the declared implementation offers this coalition, the complete symmetric alternatives give the values in 140 and the best is strictly this trio, then its dated invitation is accepted. Inside the selected report game, receivers can override the assignment and (1)-(3) give their then-current incentives. This is compatible with other belief/equilibrium selections and with cheaper available controllers. A detectable public rewrite that changes the report game needs its own continuation.

For iid privately observed urgency consider a strong ordinary comparison. Allow a binding ordinary dispatcher to receive arbitrary costless messages and lottery instructions. Each original controls all evidence of its own type, can mimic the strategy of its other type at the same cost, and cares only about its own receipt. There are no type-dependent payments, burns, later consequences or verified records. Conditional on a specified incumbent, the distribution of everyone else's private information is independent of i's type. One may simulate either of i's complete strategies, including its response to other messages, as the other type. Let P_H,P_L be the equilibrium chances of service for i under those two behaviors. A high type wants hP_H>=hP_L and a low type wants lP_L>=lP_H, so P_H=P_L. This argument also applies to protocols with multiple messages and randomized allocation under this interface. With mu=E v_i, summing gives sum_i E[v_i 1{i served}] <= mu because at most one job finishes. Solo retention exactly uses this budget. Strict gain for all three over solo with unchanged original goals would require each z_i>mu/3 before any positive setup costs. This rules that out for the stated ordinary class. Coalitions can favor one member at another's expense. Safe transfers, costly signaling, other informative types correlated with i, audits of the underlying job, and ordinary delegates with trusted local sensor access enlarge the class. The proof deliberately leaves their constructive possibilities intact.

For example take iid p=1/2,l=1/4,h=3/4 and beta=7/10. Equation (3) gives 3/5, so truth and routing are strict except among equally urgent remote sites. At gamma=1 the pre-cost gain per original over solo is log(40/37). A real implementation with k_3 below that gain strictly benefits all three. For strictly optimal *initial choice* in the symmetric menu compare with the best pair, including a fully common pair. At k_2=0 the pair's CE is w-log(19/24), so k_3<log(38/37) suffices even against that free symmetric pair. A matched fully informed ordinary trio at equal cost ties; a cheaper one can displace the selected genuine changes. These inequalities are sufficient pricing conditions, with actuator/sensor access, effective installation and selection explicit.

**Why this model might arise or be built.** A project owner often knows its own deadline before another site does, and a single costly job can displace a more urgent one. Simultaneously filed requests and an unresolved tie are plausible in a short planning window. Participants can choose the sealing and tie procedure if its cost fits their original service gain. Sensor access and genuine preference installation are demanding capabilities. Their possible construction requires distinct effective authority over the local evaluator and an incentive to fund it. Strongly correlated need, a known first-priority claimant, observation of earlier claims, or cheap ordinary auditing can change the comparison. Instances 140 and 143 share a physical setting; only the latter derives truth-telling. The ordinary independence bound cannot be transferred to either latent-load example without a new proof.

**Exploration record.** The instance “the same partial concern sustains truthful private requests for any pair or trio with these pair statistics” is dead. Comparing two requesters' bluff gains gives its scope. A sibling chooses a late tie lottery or a modest claim toll. A structural successor treats the opportunity cost of displacing an *unknown* other claimant as the organizing variable. Another open candidate is credible voluntary choice of when to reveal eligibility. A particular destroyed truthful equilibrium leaves open richer informative cheap talk, paid evidence and ordinary controls.

# Production networks and willing partners

R0006 breadth extension. Agents can value different final colors while
sharing an arbitrary pattern of intermediate knowledge and integration.
This note identifies a structural response condition, then checks that a
productive partnership is actually preferable to available isolation or
another willing partner. It is not a population-prevalence theorem.

## 1. A local feedback-path theorem for many exclusive final goals

For n agents let finite physical color counts be

`q_i=Q_i+a_i x_i+sum_(j!=i) h_ij x_j`
`    +x_i sum_(j!=i) b_ij x_j-(c_i/2)x_i^2-K_i`,           (1)

with `a_i,c_i>0`, `h_ij,b_ij>=0`, and sufficiently large finite resource
bounds and effort caps for the stated neighborhood. The original criterion
of i is q_i alone. H=(h_ij) represents additive usable knowledge; B=(b_ij)
represents complementary integration. Their diagonal entries are zero.
The work and material realization is the finite conversion model of note
115 with the corresponding bounded increments.

Put C=diag(c_i), M=C-B. If the spectral radius of `C^(-1)B` is less than
one, the unique original interior effort continuation is

`x^F=M^(-1)a`,
`M^(-1)=sum_(k>=0)(C^(-1)B)^k C^(-1) >=0`.               (2)

Let i publicly choose the genuine small goal change `q_i+beta q_j`, with
every other original retained. At beta=0, implicit differentiation yields

`dx/d beta=M^(-1)e_i (h_ji+b_ji x_j^F)`.

Since the original own-effort first-order derivative vanishes, its gain is

`d q_i/d beta = (h_ji+b_ji x_j^F)`
`  *sum_(l!=i)(h_il+b_il x_i^F)(M^(-1))_(l i)`.           (3)

All factors are nonnegative. Strict positivity therefore has an exact
structural interpretation: i's effort benefits a color j it can choose to
value, and its induced effort change propagates along a complementary
response path to some l whose resulting output/effort benefits i.
The inverse entry is positive exactly when a directed path i→l exists in
B, where an edge points from the action affecting a marginal return to
the responding agent. This follows from the nonnegative series in (2).

If h=0, the final benefit edge l→i completes a directed productive feedback
cycle. With additive knowledge, a return benefit can instead be supplied
by h_il. Thus direct two-party reciprocity is one instance, not the only
route. A positive fixed update expense still needs a finite gain exceeding
it; the derivative at zero is not itself a cost-paying argument. Recognition,
ordinary controllers, attacks and original participation retain their
separate implementation conditions.

For a directed acyclic B with h=0, the feedback sum is zero. More strongly,
i's productive predecessors are unaffected by a change in i's effort:
otherwise there would be a directed cycle. Its original own-effort optimum
is unchanged, so a goal change that moves its action away from that optimum
lowers its own output in this retained-partner response environment. Roots
of that one-way production network cannot gain from benefiting successors
unless the available arrangement also provides something they value.
Funded native-color deliveries, transferable work, new reciprocal productive
links and different partners are constructive repairs to examine, not
assumed absent from capable agents' full agreement space.

## 2. A broad reciprocal opportunity with an explicit scope

At the original equilibrium, increase all efforts by the same small
positive epsilon, staying within the physical bounds. In (1),

`q_i(x^F+epsilon 1)-q_i(x^F)`
` =epsilon sum_(j!=i)(h_ij+b_ij x_i^F)`
`  +epsilon^2[sum_(j!=i)b_ij-c_i/2]`.                    (4)

If every agent has at least one positive incoming output benefit, the
linear term is positive for every i. Finiteness of n then supplies a common
small epsilon that strictly improves all original colors. This proves a
Pareto-improving productive opportunity without requiring shared terminal
values or transfers. It does not prove that everyone independently supplies
the extra effort under the old goals.

Choose a Pareto-efficient point q* dominating that improvement in the
compact feasible output set. Such a point exists: maximize the sum of
outputs over the closed subset dominating the improvement. Then
`G(q)=min_i(q_i/q_i*)` has maximum one over the full feasible set and attains
it at q*. With independently idleable final finishing lines, lowering any
other color at q* leaves original i indifferent but strictly lowers G;
this verifies a genuine change for every i. Otherwise test the actual
feasible lottery ordering rather than infer it from the formula alone.
The common maximum gives a current-goal
continuation certificate when the plan is executable. Positive costs must
fit inside each founder's actual gain. Simultaneous common-goal play can
support the global optimum as an equilibrium; a nonsmooth objective can
also have other coordinate equilibria, so uniqueness or initial selection
is not inferred. Smoothness/curvature or an actual joint execution protocol
can supply additional conditions, as note 119 illustrates for two parties.

This construction preserves a supported positive result at its level:
available mutually beneficial negotiated goals and a no-profitable-deviation
continuation at the common maximum. It does not silently turn that into
reliable installation, a coalition-formation equilibrium or prevalence.

## 3. Is the partnership better than running alone?

For the symmetric two-agent family of note 116, suppose a genuinely
available isolated line yields `q_alone=Q+A/2`. Assume no departure expense,
no residual cross-effect after departure, and the same native technology.
Then a retained-goal partnership yields

`q_F=Q+A[1/2+rho(1-s)]/(1-s)^2`,                          (5)

whereas a common productive goal gives

`q_C=Q+A(1+rho)^2/[2(1-2s)]-K_i`.                       (6)

These quantities, not just q_C-q_F, determine voluntary participation. For
s<0 and zero implementation expense, ordinary participation is strictly
beneficial exactly when

`rho > s(s-2)/[2(1-s)]`,                                 (7)

and common-goal participation is strictly beneficial exactly when

`rho > sqrt(1-2s)-1`.                                   (8)

At s=-1/4 these boundaries are 9/40 and sqrt(3/2)-1,
respectively. A small interval permits a mutually beneficial common-goal
partnership while the uncommitted partnership falls below isolation. Larger
positive spillovers permit both. At rho=0, neither route in that congested
family beats isolation, even though the common plan improves on the bad
paired equilibrium. Declaring that local improvement to be chosen adoption
would omit an actual alternative.

If relocation takes work, leaves a valuable input, or keeps access to
public knowledge, q_alone must be replaced by the actual exit continuation.
Shared resources can make interference unavoidable in a location; another
site may supply a paid repair. The isolation premise here is explicit,
rather than being imposed or forbidden in every environment.

## 4. A feasible partner search can change which arrangement is chosen

For a small exact comparison take a=c=1, the same Q, and three differently
colored originals. Blue can operate one partnership. A willing Red offers
technology `(s,rho)=(-1/4,1/2)`, and a willing Green offers `(1/4,0)`.
Common-goal output before route costs is Q+3/4 in the first pair and Q+1
in the second, versus Q+1/2 when alone. If each verified route costs the
participant 1/50, both partners strictly prefer joining Blue to isolation.
Blue can pay an additional 1/50 of own productive work to discover and
inspect Green's known technology; its net output is then Q+24/25, greater
than Q+73/100 with Red. It therefore chooses the search and Green under
its current own-color objective, provided these exhaust the relevant
willing alternatives and the stated information technology is available.

This is an actual paid counterparty-choice comparison. The more valuable
partnership has less *additive* spillover but a complementary effort
response. It need not have fewer shared process steps overall. A matched
ordinary enforceable controller at equal cost ties the goal route; a
cheaper available control changes the actual mechanism choice. Free public
knowledge from unchosen partners would also change the comparison and must
be included if the technology supplies it. The example is not a claim
about stable matching of an entire unipartite population.

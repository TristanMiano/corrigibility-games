# Which goals can insure both founders when neither knows which workload will dominate?

**E8, note 179.** Two founders can both want reciprocal service even when they agree on a *range* of workload forecasts rather than one precise law. Some services cost more than the recipient gains and are nevertheless worth offering to bring that recipient into the exchange. If the parties only negotiate constant linear weights on their two total outputs, they can run out of ways to support both sides. Two distinct repairs turn out to be voluntarily affordable: use the identity of the project, or agree on a nonlinear goal that values some concentration of output.

## Shared uncertainty and a bilateral right

A and B each originally value their own useful output. Keeping local work leaves a constant protected baseline in each potential state. A donor may instead forgo one unit; the recipient then gets either 3/4 or 2 units. Acceptance and release require both sites. Alternate processes can plausibly couple a low-return supporting service to a highly valuable specialist job in the reverse direction. Two program families interchange the sites' roles. In X the opportunity is a low-yield A-to-B transfer with probability p=5/8 or a high-yield B-to-A transfer otherwise. In Y reverse A and B. The type of the actual work is visible before production. Safe reserves cover all expenditures; cross-site payments, independently pledgeable bonuses, and a controller able to bind acceptance after the job is identified must be priced if available. A destructive attempt consumes the dated opportunity and its own finite work, with sufficiently protected stocks. This aspect uses 140's expiry environment.

Initially the founders regard both X and Y as possible workload laws. Each values its own additional original output by its lowest expectation over the two (equivalently over all their mixtures). This is an explicit ambiguity preference. It also gives a sufficient agreement that is beneficial under every forecast in that range for an analyst whose signers have yet to specify one. Keeping the same constant baseline across states is essential to the incremental calculation. Similar problems arise with a new business line or with only the pairwise records in 176. The numbers and the shape of the original ambiguity evaluator remain assumptions, not estimates. With one known symmetric mixture the founders face a different and easier insurance problem.

If all transfers are made, founder A's original gains are -p+2(1-p)=1/8 in X and (3/4)p-(1-p)=3/32 in Y. B's are reversed. Thus both have a worst-case gain of 3/32. There is an open range of p for which the two expectations are positive, namely 4/7<p<2/3. A single common criterion putting weight 3/2 on B's output in X and on A's output in Y makes every proposed transfer strictly preferable to keeping, for both the donor and the recipient. Its argument is the authenticated program family and the respective final outputs. It really changes how each signer ranks terminal outcomes at the same job and does not require either to change its workload beliefs. No later bet on the realized state is needed. If a project title can be changed after agreement, the parties must account for 155's classification risk.

## What an ordinary or genuine global linear compromise can secure

Grant an extremely permissive benchmark: before knowing the family, the founders can randomize over any pair of constant nonnegative linear output weights, which may differ across the two sites. Each criterion has positive weight on at least one site. Permit any individually supported actions, flexible history-dependent policies and lotteries among ties. Each site can still veto a job at its known physical payoff. A freely enforcing local delegate is outside this benchmark and compared separately. A realized set of weights supporting the low A-to-B job must give B at least 4/3 times the weight on A at *both* decision makers. Supporting the opposite low transfer requires the reverse at both. Thus the two events are disjoint. If a,b are the probabilities of delivering the respective low jobs conditional on their occurrence, then a+b≤1. This remains true even granting costless job-specific closure of any unwanted service. Preferences chosen after observing the family have materially different access to information.

In X, if the high job is sent with conditional probability x and the low one with probability a, the two net gains are -pa+2(1-p)x and (3/4)pa-(1-p)x. Their minimum is at most pa/6, by weighting the first gain by 1/3 and the second by 2/3, which eliminates x. Applying the reversed bound to Y gives at most pb/6. Hence the largest simultaneous original guarantee in the global linear class is at most p/12=5/96, **even with free goal selection**. This also bounds unsigned self-regarding controllers whose late vetoes they retain. With small fixed extra cost k=0.02 per founder, the scoped return 3/32-k=0.07375 beats that whole class on the common guarantee.

For an actual consent comparison, suppose taking the project displaces an independently feasible job worth 0.06 to each site. No global linear proposal in this class can get both founders' approval. Both strictly approve the new criterion over that outside option. If these are the feasible implementations, an eligible founder prefers proposing the jointly improving scoped goal. An ordinary dispatcher endowed with the same authority to bind both physical decisions can deliver every transfer, and can displace it at a lower price. With a single known 50–50 workload mixture, a constant equal-weight common goal instead sends just the high-yield transfers and each original gets (1-p)/2=0.1875 before its cost. The choice of genuine goal and the source of its value change with what the incorporators can justifiably guarantee. No population frequency follows.

Small perturbations preserve the comparison. For example smooth the two extreme laws by mixing fraction ε of the opposite family into each. Any policy's per-founder expected increment changes by at most 3ε, since the range of the original material increment is [-1,2]. The free global guarantee remains below 0.06 for ε=0.002, while the scoped plan's worst expected gain before cost is still at least 3/32. Pure support separation is unnecessary.

## A simpler, genuinely different repair without project tags

The linear bound uses the actual functional form being negotiated. A founder able to represent a nonlinear criterion can offer its partner the symmetric common goal

    max(q_A,q_B) + η(q_A+q_B),   η>0.

At equal protected starting stocks, giving rather than keeping increases the larger stock by the recipient's gain r; it changes total stock by r-1. A low service is strictly preferred when η<3. A high service is strictly preferred at every η>0. The criterion is strictly increasing in either site's output and genuinely changes both founders' ordering of lotteries. It supports both directions of service without consulting the family label. It has the same original 3/32 guarantee from the agreed transfers. Both founders can therefore pay the same 0.02 cost even without reliable recording of the domain. The maximum can capture a real prize for getting one project across a production threshold, or the founders can choose it deliberately because it implements the reciprocal portfolio. They must actually possess comparable, meaningful output measures and a facility for configuring this criterion. If one site starts far richer, the same formula on absolute stocks can make the low transfer to the poorer site unattractive. Quantities measured relative to separately registered baseline stocks require that additional reference mechanism.

The two goals have genuinely different off-menu consequences. If a new proposal could concentrate wealth destructively after the joint booking, the max-based goal might accept it; a scoped concern can preserve original evaluation outside the two named program families. A differing baseline, a change of workload after installation, or an actor who can sell a bonus for widening the scope requires an updated original price comparison. Selecting a rich goal is a constructive option here, not an assertion that any two cooperative actions reveal the evaluator behind them.

**Exploration ledger.** The framing candidate links accommodation to uncertainty about *which partner will be needed*. The structural candidate seeks evaluators that can support an entire jointly beneficial portfolio after its tasks become known. The implicit restriction to constant exchange rates between two founders' outputs is dead at the instance with the 0.06 outside jobs. Siblings use a project-contingent goal, a nonlinear untagged goal, or a real ordinary controller with the same authority. A higher-rung successor asks which families of contingent offers and new opportunities can distinguish the stability of these genuinely different negotiated preferences.

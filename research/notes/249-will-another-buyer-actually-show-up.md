# Will another buyer actually show up?

An original founder may want to change if it expects a second bid, while the second buyer will reserve a slot only if it expects someone genuinely worth serving. Making the reservation observable before the founder invests can secure both actions. Publishing the goal reading *after* that decision answers a different question and can arrive too late.

## A private capacity reservation

Use [247](247-who-sets-the-price-after-an-agent-proves-it-cares.md). The entrant can pay a privately known opportunity cost c drawn uniformly on [0,C] to reserve its own batch. Without a reservation it cannot show up. Cost and actual installation difficulty are independent. Reserve and edit decisions occur before the true goal is known. If it participates at a successful installation the entrant earns half the chance of a three-unit commercial margin at the competitive tariff of zero. With a retained goal both buyers prefer to keep their stocked goods. A successful original founder receives two additional own units if both customers are there and loses one original unit if only the monopoly buyer is present; the changed successor still willingly pays that single buyer's tariff of three.

First the entrant's early reservation is private until the goal read. A knows only its equilibrium frequency λ. The two best responses are

    π(λ)=min(.8,max(0,(16/35)(3λ−1))),
    λ=min(1,1.5π/C).                                              (249.1)

The second formula follows because reservation earns 1.5 only when the controller is genuine and the entrant wins its share of the bids in expectation. Equivalently its gross expected return before c is 1.5π. A zero-entry, zero-edit equilibrium always exists in this simultaneous-information arrangement. The two positive responses can support one another if and only if **C≤72/55**. To see this, π(λ)/λ increases from zero on the rising segment until λ=11/12 and falls once π saturates. Its maximum is 48/55. The lower positive crossing is λ=24/(72−35C); the upper is min(1,1.2/C). At C=72/55 they coincide. When C≤1.2 the upper response is saturated at one. For example C=1 admits the three genuine rates 0, 16/37 and .8. The result specifies multiple possible outcomes; the equilibrium existence condition alone cannot select which market starts.

## An advance invitation that can be checked

Suppose the reservation can be reliably shown to A before A decides whether to edit. An entrant that reserves induces all supplier types to try, since q a=1.6 exceeds K. With no entrant, the known monopoly deters the edit. Reservation is therefore worth 1.5×.8=1.2 before c, and the chosen entry probability is λ=min(1,1.2/C). The genuine rate per market is now .8λ. The receipt must prove actual control of usable stock at the bidding date. A nonbinding declaration that another buyer intends to appear would not give the incumbent supplier this conditional opportunity.

At **C=1.5**, the hidden-reservation comparison has only zero entry. With a visible advance booking, four in five entrants reserve and .64 of initial applicants acquire the agreed goal. The incumbent buyer earns .96 per prospective job, the entrant earns .48 on average after its costs, and original A gains .72 on average over safe production. These numbers follow by multiplying the competitive high-state profits by their actual frequencies. For the entrant, integrate max(1.2−c,0) on [0,1.5]. The original buyer can pay .30 for the standing reservation display: it retains .66 compared with zero under the hidden interface. If the only permissible sponsor is the entrant itself, the price and allocation of that fixed cost have to be included before predicting entry.

Finally, suppose the same entrant can obtain a real batch *after* seeing a successful goal read, with no lost capacity from waiting. Its private threshold is then c<1.5 conditional on G. A foresees λ_G=min(1,1.5/C) precisely in the state in which a competing bid is useful. At C=1.5 it expects competition at every successful edit; every original attempts and .8 succeed. The entrant now actually pays its entry cost only on successful jobs, earning .8(1.5−.75)=.60 per initial market. The incumbent buyer earns 1.20 gross. An early reserve in a congested facility and a late order from idle stock are two different capabilities, with concrete consequences for what a founder must risk before learning the market conditions.

**Why this comparison travels, and where it stops.** New customers may need to reserve a scarce compatible batch; some can verify their booking early and others can draw on idle stock at short notice. Those are reasons for different information sequences without changing anyone's original taste for output. A reliable public registrar, comparable recipient goals and actual competition for a single service slot remain substantive assumptions. The calculation advances the entry part of R766 for a single batch and two potential customers. It leaves recurring entry by suppliers of ordinary controls, durable market occupancy, reproduction and inheritance open.

# What a customer does with the raw readings

Seeing the individual trials lets a customer settle many cases early and save an unlucky genuine installer from a mistaken rejection. In the game below, both founders gain, more orders are filled, and the fraction of genuine edits falls slightly. The receipt the customer willingly trusts is also less conclusive. The direction of each change follows from the price of errors on the two sides.

## Choosing when to stop

Give the buyer in [220](220-which-confidence-will-the-partners-pay-for.md) each raw independent reading at the same .035 price. It may stop, return or release after any history. The original rents are (2,4) for a successful or retained installer on release, and B gains four on a good release or loses two on a bad one. As before either can contest. First analyze a renewable supply of trials at constant opportunity cost with no additional loss of A's own stocks from delay. A changed A also values the supplies B consumes during inspection, so its incentive to stay through testing is checked below. A finite funded deadline is a separate comparison.

At prior π write the posterior odds as π/(1−π) times 4^k. Each positive increments k, each negative decrements it. An immediate acceptance has loss 2(1−p) relative to the informed buyer; returning has loss 4p. The minimum attainable loss including future payments is concave in the present p: every fixed policy has an affine expected loss and the buyer can take their infimum. It is bounded below by zero and above by each stopping loss. Its contact set with the return line is a lower interval containing zero; that with the release line is an upper interval containing one. Any genuinely sampling policy has a positive expense at either pure type, so ties between sampling and an immediate stop in a stopping interval can occur only at its edge. In particular, if the lesser stopping loss is below one trial's cost, stopping strictly beats buying even one trial. On the integer evidence lattice this leaves finitely many live positions at any interior starting prior.

A deterministic sampling optimum therefore stops when k first hits −d (return) or +u (release), measured from zero at signature. Include stopping immediately as an option. If t=.8 and e=.2, the two acceptance probabilities and expected trial counts for any such pair are

    T = (1−4^(−d))/(1−4^(−d−u)),      E = (4^d−1)/(4^(d+u)−1),
    m_G = ((d+u)T−d)/.6,             m_N = (d−(d+u)E)/.6.        (221.1)

The first two follow by solving the two-boundary harmonic equation for an upward step of .8 or .2. The latter two follow by summing the expected ±.6 drift to absorption; absorption has a geometric tail since a fixed run of identical steps reaches a boundary. B minimizes

    4π(1−T) + 2(1−π)E + .035[π m_G+(1−π)m_N].                  (221.2)

At unequal fallback values, replace four by g and two by h from [207](207-who-can-walk-away-from-a-doubtful-installation.md). This loss is relative to the best response that knows the installed state. At fixed prior and other prices, raising h weakly lowers the false-acceptance probability of the buyer's optimal policy. Compare the two optimality inequalities at the old and new h and add them. Raising g similarly lowers false rejection. A fall in per-trial cost weakly increases the expected number of trials by the same argument. These are responses to an unchanged prior. The installers' behavior has to be resolved for a change in actual adoption.

## The installer responds to the whole stopping rule

For (d,u)=(4,2), T=272/273, E=17/273, m_G=300/91 and m_N=550/91. Thus the original founder's cutoff expense is q(2T−4E)=272/195 and the actual attempt fraction is 272/273 at K=1.4. The genuine prior becomes π=1088/1365. At this prior an upper displacement of two or a lower displacement of five already makes immediate stopping risk less than .035. Both immediate stopping losses exceed .17185. This proves that the instrument and effort are mutually optimal in this assessment. A false declarant can still earn four times E above refusal; all private cost types sign. About .366% of signers deliberately retain their goal, about 19.93% try and fail, and 79.71% get the agreed goal. The customer buys about 3.854 readings per signatory and releases on about 80.68% of offers. The genuine posterior is 98.43% at release and 1.51% at return. Each early stopping boundary gives the same terminal odds regardless of how long it took to get there.

With the seven-reading majority instrument, the selected path gave 80% genuine edits, 78.00% releases and 99.15% genuine probability on release. The raw instrument saves both time and mistaken rejections while admitting some additional retained controllers. B earns 3.016 above return against 2.835 with the majority device. A founders earn .944 against .873, including the real private expense of trying. A B who can purchase raw readout for less than about .181 per upcoming job strictly pays for it on these continuations. The lower attempt cutoff then gives the small reduction in actual installation. Better inference benefits the two negotiating founders even here, where an authorized sincere change is slightly less frequent. A lower-priced ordinary binding controller remains a substantive competitor.

## Other selection and finite resources

For this binary mirror-image evidence family there is a finite way to test other stationary assessments. For each stopping pair j, the buyer's return over unconditional decline is the line L_j(π)=πgT_j−(1−π)hE_j−c[πm_Gj+(1−π)m_Nj]. Add immediate trust and decline. Only a bounded range of d,u matters: sampling is strictly dominated when gp<c or h(1−p)<c, and lattice odds move by a fixed factor each time. At a proposed π compute the lines attaining the upper envelope. For equal fallback values the installer's incentive under rule j is Z_j=AT_j−XE_j. In the type-dependent fallback of 207 use A_G−A_N+T_j(a−A_G)−E_j(a+d₀−A_N), where d₀ is the extra private output from retaining the batch. The corresponding pure candidate needs π=q F(qZ_j). A private lottery over equally optimal rules permits any Z in their convex hull; the same consistency equation then supplies the mixing weights. This includes tests with unconditional refusal or trust. Each rule optimal from the initial state is optimal at its positive-probability continuation histories, so the construction permits genuinely discretionary decisions.

For a fixed finite horizon the optimal loss is instead obtained by backward induction. With no time left, take min(4p,2(1−p)). Otherwise compare it with .035 plus the expected remaining value after one Bayesian update. Propagate the two conditional probabilities of release and the two expected trial counts through each chosen branch, then recompute the installer's prior from them. At a hard deadline a single last reading can have a different value from the same reading with several slots remaining. This is also the appropriate calculation if the sensor must be powered from a finite reserve before terminal conversion.

The [exact stopping calculation](../../experiments/r0008_sequential_evidence.py) evaluates the intervals of optimality of these lines by rational comparisons. For the equal-strength job it gives the zero-trade assessment, a 3251/155940 genuine-rate mixture of refusal and (1,5), and the 1088/1365 high branch. The convex-hull check also covers other history-dependent choices among tied optimal actions in this lattice. To see why, maximize or minimize Z *within* B's optimal actions. At a fixed interior prior, Z is a constant plus the expected terminal payment that assigns the supplier's genuine rent divided by π to a genuine release and minus its retained rent divided by 1−π to a retained release. This secondary decision has a finite state space and uniformly bounded absorption tails: under either state a long enough run in one direction exits the region in which sampling can be optimal. Backward iteration converges for this bounded reward and a stationary deterministic maximizing or minimizing action attains its value. From the initial lattice point each such rule is an immediate stop or a first return and release barrier. The two extreme Z values bound every other tie choice, and a private initial lottery realizes the interval.

For a **ten-trial** powered stand B needs only .35 units in reserve. Exact backward induction at the induced prior again gives positive adoption, with 76.24% genuine installations, 97.55% genuine probability among releases, and B's gain 2.861 above return. With 30 paid slots the corresponding numbers are 79.70%, 98.43% and 3.016. The script re-solves the entire finite decision at each proposed founding prior. A finite physical waiting window can therefore support the main comparison.

At low s only a *retained* installer contests a returned batch. At high s B itself takes any rejected batch to contest; at the middle strength both can accept the peaceful return. Buying access to all readings improves the available review at a given strength while the outside option determines what a rejection actually does.

**Would the changed supplier stop the test to spare B's stock?** Its best instant return or contest pays at most 3 in its own half-concern units throughout this strength sweep. Release pays 7. The prescribed refusal gives it at least the *available* exit value: at high strength B can force its contest, and elsewhere G can take peaceful return. Buying another reading makes sense to B only at gp≥c and h(1−p)≥c. Across all the tabulated strengths with active testing the ratio of extreme admissible odds is (g−c)(h−c)/c²<4⁷. Thus there are at most seven consecutive live lattice positions, between absorbing endpoints at most eight steps apart. Given actual G, the positive drift is .6. Until absorption even the full walk takes at most 8/.6 expected remaining readings by (221.1); an earlier finite deadline can only shorten this. G's half-share of the additional expected bill is below .234. On a live deterministic sampling rule the all-positive path releases within eight steps. For finite-horizon policies with ties resolved by stopping, the positive child of a strictly worthwhile trial cannot immediately return: monotonicity of the return region at the remaining horizon would make both children return and the trial worthless. Induction gives the same all-positive bound. G's expected gain from the chance of release is at least 4(.8)⁸>.67 above its available exit, enough to bear the remaining bill. Any lottery over these continuation rules preserves the comparison. An unchanged supplier cares about its own stock and can only gain access by staying. Delay borne by the supplier or physically recoverable metering stock would require a new exit calculation.

There is a lower strength boundary independent of how many sequential slots this binary device permits. With two known stationary kernels a single extra signal's greatest improvement over an immediate choice, maximized over starting priors, is (t−e)gh/(g+h), at p=h/(g+h) by 207.1. If every trial costs at least that much, immediate stopping is optimal for a horizon of one *at every posterior*. By induction it is optimal at every finite horizon, and finite-mean policies can be truncated with vanishing tail loss. With s<1/3, g=4, h=3s and t−e=.6, **s<28/1419** makes all testing strictly unprofitable at .035 per reading. The hidden supplier then gains nothing by changing against untested refusal or trust. At equality weak choices require a separate assessment.

An unknown *shared* sensor orientation changes that conclusion's premises. Suppose the active goal is a bit and one costly observation reads it through an independent fair polarity bit J. A separate known physical reference on the same mount reads J. Each isolated reading leaves the goal prior unchanged. Jointly they recover the goal exactly by checking whether the readings differ. At q=.8 and .1 cost per reading, perfect information is worth .4 over trusting. B buys both for .2, including the second after seeing the first. The founder's attempt cutoff .8×2=1.6 exceeds 1.4, so everyone tries and .8 genuinely succeed. Buying an independent mount with a *new* unknown polarity would erase this repair. A grounded reference and a stable mount give the buyer a concrete paid way of distinguishing capability from goal.

**Naturality and reach.** A staffed plant that owns the bench can read and stop after each sample; a separately supplied instrument may issue only a majority badge. The receiver's authority, funded consumables and the timing of batch control differ from the supplier-selected receipts in [218](218-who-chooses-which-inspection-to-show.md). A time-sensitive supplier can demand a deadline or use its available outside option during the review. The constant-cost renewable approximation isolates the value of stopping. The sibling is a finite powered bench. The broader question asks which party bears the costs of keeping a consequential offer open while evidence accumulates.

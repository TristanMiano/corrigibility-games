# A renewable booking rule that also survives agreements among the losers

**E8, note 184.** A schedule that each original site would honor can be vulnerable when the unserved sites can bargain together. Only one has to pay the initiative expense, and everyone in their group can receive part of the salvage lottery. In a broad parameter region an ordinary scheduler can meet the exact stronger patience threshold. It keeps a disputed booking in the queue and advances the queue only after completing the scheduled work. No on-path or off-path state requires leaving useful capacity idle.

## Why the individual sanction can be too weak

Keep 183's physical rights, notation and survival probability. In its individual-priority rule an unserved initiator loses b=δx/(n-1+δ) of future own output. A different unserved site keeps the same high next-date status whether it waits for peace or its companion initiates. Consequently a group of m unserved sites, coordinating one initiative then following the same future rule, gains mq-c-b in total original value. It can be positive while q-c-b<0 for every individual.

There can be a voluntary way to realize the side agreement even without direct cash. At one exposed booking let the m unserved originals have time to authorize a private, fair one-date role draw. The selected volunteer alone pays a genuine setup cost K and acquires a goal valuing the group's sum of *current* batch outputs and expenditures, alongside its own future material bookings. Others retain their original evaluators. For a current-service-scoped edit, its future comparison of next booking rights is the same b. The selected site wants to initiate when mq-c-b>0. Others prefer its initiative to paying the cost and sanction themselves, and if it passed, the remaining original sites would obey the individual schedule. Before learning the identity of the volunteer, every member of the losing group strictly prefers this plan to peace when mq-c-b>K. A selected site that first learned its role while still free to refuse the installation would decline at q-c-b<K; authenticated prior consent matters here as it did for the peaceful role lottery. A comparable conventional group commitment can accomplish the same act. This is one changed evaluator in a collectively authorized limited extension. It values its partners' recoverable output and has no intrinsic reward for destruction.

In the three-site example at δ=0.65, b≈0.17170, q-c=0.16 and 2q-c=0.36. At K=0.03 both losing founders gain about 0.07915 in their original expectations by authorizing this one-time lottery. A single site would pass under its own goal. As a counterexample to coalition stability this is a deviation from a candidate schedule. As an adoption game it assumes this is the known remaining one-date configuration opportunity, with the stated ordinary continuation thereafter. If such coalitions can reconfigure on every future job, their future equilibrium returns must be recalculated. The result motivates the ordinary repair below rather than treating current conflict as a general prediction.

## The lower bound when actual coalitions can act over time

Now test the ordinary schedule against *every fixed subset* coordinating current and future dispute choices, giving each subset free internal commitment and valuation by its sum of original receipts. This is a deliberately strong stability test. Let d_m=mq-c and d=d_1>0. A group of m can secure at least d_m on every date: if it initiates it receives expected mq less the one initiative cost, and an earlier outside initiative saves it that cost. Accordingly every continuation immune to these group deviations must give the group at least d_m/(1-δ). A site alone can obtain d/(1-δ).

At a publicly announced recipient j, all other n-1 sites earn zero now on peaceful completion. Their aggregate future normalized payoff cannot exceed x-d, since j can secure d in units of per-date output. The losers' guaranteed normalized payoff at exposure is d_(n-1). Hence full peaceful service defending against these groups requires

    δ ≥ δ* = [(n-1)q-c]/(x-d).                                       (1)

This bound assumes continuing membership, common survival, no fungible collateral and recoverable productive control as in 183. When groups cannot share stakes or credibly select any member to initiate, it is a benchmark with stronger organization. The attaining construction also bounds a group that *can* compensate its members, using the physical schedule alone.

## A rule attaining the lower bound

Suppose d≥δ*q, where here δ* denotes the critical number in (1): precisely the condition is d≥(δ*)q. It holds on an open set, including the example below and sufficiently small positive initiative costs with x>nq. Take δ=δ*. Label an exposed booking by (e,j), with e≠j. Site j gets this batch on completion. Site e is the member temporarily receiving the least future priority. Use normalized payoffs (1-δ) times total expected original receipts. Aim for

    r_e=d=q-c,     r_j=H=x-(n-1)q+c,     r_i=q for every other site.   (2)

Only e has a shortfall from q and that shortfall is exactly c. Every nonempty subset S therefore has r(S)≥|S|q-c, while the whole group gets x. **After a dispute retain exactly the same public state (e,j) for the next surviving booking.** On peaceful completion set the next low-priority label to j and choose a new recipient k≠j with probabilities

    Pr(k=e) = (d/δ-q)/(H-q),
    Pr(k=i) = (q/δ-q)/(H-q) for each i outside {e,j}.                 (3)

At (1), H=(1-δ)x+δd. The probabilities sum to one by δ(x-d)=d_(n-1); they are nonnegative under the displayed condition and H>q since x>nq. Thus (3) uses actual scheduling rights. The new low-priority j gets d in normalized next-period value. For the old e, the expectation of its next value is q+(H-q)Pr(k=e)=d/δ; for every other unserved site it is q/δ. This verifies (2) by the Bellman equations for each individual, including the current recipient.

At any exposed state a group that initiates today and then follows this continuation obtains normalized (1-δ)(|S|q-c)+δr(S)≤r(S). This includes groups containing the designated recipient and groups choosing any one willing initiator. For a fixed coalition facing compliant outsiders, the Bellman inequality at every state also bounds *every* sequence of coordinated subsequent dispute choices: iterate it for T steps and take the bounded discounted tail to zero. If a coalition only has authority to decline or dispute after the published booking, internal reallocations of receipts do not increase its total. An ability to rewrite the public queue after seeing an inconvenient assignment, hide a transfer to an outside participant, immigrate as a new site, or take a different productive machine adds actions and must be priced separately. No group of all participants can gain total output by amending an efficient continuation.

For actual survival δ>δ*, advance according to (3), computed at δ*, with probability h=δ*(1-δ)/[δ(1-δ*)] after a successful completion, and otherwise repeat (e,j). The effective Bellman weight on advancement is δh/(1-δ+δh)=δ*. This preserves every target in (2). For strict margins when d>(δ*)q and δ>δ*, construct the rule at a slightly smaller hypothetical initiative cost c'<c. Its critical survival remains attainable, and then every group gets at least |S|q-c'>|S|q-c under the real, more expensive deviation. At equality some groups can be indifferent. In the separate region d<(δ*)q the proposed (3) would require a negative probability, and 185 supplies a different construction for that region.

In the three-site example δ*=2/3. At the boundary (2) gives (0.16,0.34,0.20) in the order (e,j,other). On success e is next recipient with probability 2/7 and the remaining site with probability 5/7. Thus at δ=0.65 the individually stable full-service rule can be broken and (1) precludes any completely peaceful ordinary rule in the strong coalition class. At δ=0.70 the funded, public two-label queue can instead achieve strictly coalition-stable service under the original goals. Ex ante randomizing both labels gives everyone the same x/[n(1-δ)]. The group can pay a modest setup fee if its actual inferior fallback leaves a corresponding margin. An ordinary operator with control over the public docket, perishable individual bookings and a record that separates completed from disputed work supplies a plausible environment; it does not need the ability to identify *which* site initiated to apply this rule. Already having credible side agreements, long-run productive survival, and a practically affordable binding assignment record are all consequential.

**Exploration ledger.** The framing candidate asks which parties can jointly create a reason for accommodation or conflict. The structural candidate prices the option of pooling salvage while only one member bears the cost, then designs a productive schedule that keeps every such group willing to wait. The individual-sanction inference is dead at δ=0.65. A sibling changes who receives future access after actual completion. A higher-rung successor prices changing coalitions, imperfect identification of a genuine completion, or another party's ability to reopen the queue.

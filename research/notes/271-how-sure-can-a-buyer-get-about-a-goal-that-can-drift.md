# 271 — How sure can a buyer get about a goal that can drift between readings?

Repeated observations of a *fixed* type can give arbitrarily strong evidence under the separation conditions in [219](219-which-uncertainties-can-more-experience-resolve.md). What if the type itself can change while the buyer checks? A buyer with excellent readouts can face a sharp limit on warranted confidence in the current goal. The limit gives a positive reliability threshold for an agreement with a specified consequence of error.

The same line has either the agreed G or another goal N at each decision. Between successive readings G persists with probability s, and an N line returns to G with probability m, where 0<m<s<1. A supplier using a previously agreed network standard can revert to a local default or receive a standard replacement. These observations concern the *current* terminal ordering. A later replacement can come from the maintenance schedule regardless of the original bargain. Assume a known stationary transition law, a live reading each tick, and immediate handover after the latest read. If the supplier can secretly revise between the last read and delivery, the extra physical interval needs another prediction step.

From probability p after the last check, prediction gives z=m+(s−m)p. A reading of likelihood ratio L updates to Lz/[1+(L−1)z]. Take L to be the largest ratio of any possible reading, finite and greater than one, attained with positive probability under both states. The update F(p) at this L is increasing and strictly concave, positive at zero and below one at one. It has exactly one fixed point p* in (0,1), and its iterates approach that point from either side. Concavity and the opposite endpoint signs give a unique crossing; monotonicity ensures iteration cannot jump over it. Induction over observations makes F iterated n times the largest posterior possible after n ticks. Therefore, starting below p*, a finite sequence can strictly warrant confidence w exactly when w<p* (including already sufficient starting beliefs). An independently proved initial state above the ceiling can of course be used before its evidence ages.

Equivalently, starting below w, a run of favorable readings can cross w precisely when

    s > 1/[L−(L−1)w] − m(1−w)/w.                                 (271.1)

This is F(w)>w solved for s. The formula applies to a live meter with a tick between draws. Multiple instantaneous, conditionally independent checks of one frozen state have a different information budget. The smallest likelihood ratio gives the analogous lower reachable bound by monotonicity. With different affordable instruments at a tick, each having the same physical transition, one can use the largest available likelihood ratio for this *possibility* calculation. Frequency of such evidence, acquisition prices and founder consent determine whether anyone would actually use the instrument.

For a .9/.1 meter, L=9. At s=.65,m=.15 the upper limit is **.935695**. A buyer whose wrong approval costs 19 times the gain on a good one has threshold .95. Starting at .30 it will decline every lot in this fixed protocol however many readings it sees. At s=.75 the upper limit rises to **.959721**. Three consecutive positives from .30 then give about .95752 and the buyer would release on that record. The required increase in s follows from (271.1), whose boundary is about .70639. A buyer with gain four and loss two already finds a single favorable reading sufficient in both settings. It has no reason to pay for .95 confidence merely because that number is attainable. If a broker can raise the value of an installed G on refusal, or if recovery by combat changes the cost of guessing wrong, recalculate the actual release threshold.

Such a transition law can reflect scheduled replacement by a common controller and occasional restoration of a plant-specific default. Stable time scales, truthful calibration, possible separate read errors and the physical point at which the meter hands over the batch are testable requirements. A buyer might finance better preservation or a faster read, a supplier could choose to show a sample from another date, and an unrelated party might willingly fund a later strategic restoration. Exact semantic readout of today's controller and preservation during the next tick are separate capabilities.

# 276 — What can a warranty reveal about the ability to change a goal?

A supplier may know how reliable its installation equipment is before the buyer does. Even if the entire commissioning procedure is witnessed, its success remains uncertain. A supplier with a low probability of changing may relish access to a larger batch precisely because it expects to keep more of the output. Can the two founders use contract terms to allocate the larger job to the more reliable supplier?

## When reliability is private

Use the two colored products and balanced contest of [206](206-when-a-truthful-attempt-can-fail.md). All payoffs below are increments over safe return. A job of size x yields (ax,gx)=(2x,4x) on delivery and (Xx,−hx)=(4x,−2x) on retention. Let d=X−a=2. A signed, successful change gives Red's successor utility equal to its own output plus half Blue's. Its preference for delivery is strict. Original Red prefers to keep the batch. A separate collateral meter can route a pledged f from Red to Blue upon retention. For 0≤f<dx this leaves both strict rankings intact. Stock charges are in the same freely convertible units for each founder. Both get their own outside stocks back on withdrawal before the order. Physical contest remains available with the single-recovery dynamics of 206. All priced agreements below give the actual decision makers more than contesting at their live nodes.

Red privately knows whether its probability of success when it really attempts is q_L or q_H, where 0<q_L<q_H<1. A witnessed attempt costs k. A counterparty can book either of two indivisible processing slots. The small job uses x_L of the line. The large one uses x_H>x_L and gives up another productive opportunity worth c units to Blue. A posted contract specifies the job size, failure bond f and deterministic settlement t from Blue to Red on handover. The batch remains with Blue until the decision to release. Expired orders settle and reveal delivery to the two owners on completion. The existing local fixture can physically divert up to 1.4 units of output on default. An additional account able to settle on this same outcome would enlarge conventional enforcement. Whether the terminal preference changed is private.

On two contracts whose receipts justify release, the original Red's gain from the large rather than small attempted job is

    ΔU(q)=Δx X + Δt − Δf + q(Δf−d Δx).                         (276.1)

This follows by subtracting x(X−dq)+t−(1−q)f−k across jobs. **For high skill alone to strictly prefer the large job, the increase in pledged stock must exceed the extra own-color temptation on the additional batch, Δf>dΔx.** Given this inequality, a separating difference in fees always exists with transferable enough stock: choose Δt strictly between the two distinct values that make (276.1) zero. Each total bond must still lie below its own ordinary enforcement threshold, and both proposed jobs must meet the participation and release comparisons. A single receipt certifies the procedure. It leaves the chance of an actual goal change on each contract at the skill of its selected applicant.

For equal numbers of q_L=.4 and q_H=.8 suppliers, take x_L=.5, x_H=1, f_L=0, f_H=1.4, t_L=0, t_H=−.85, k=.20 and c=1.90. The small job gives Red 1.40 when less skilled and 1.00 when more skilled. The large one gives 1.31 and 1.07 respectively. The low type chooses small, the high type large. Blue's payoffs are .20 and 2.03. These include the fee, default settlement and foregone large-slot opportunity. Either job can be declined, and a decline returns both stocks. On the small job the original client's 40% confidence in the goal suffices for a voluntarily released batch. Both types install with positive probability, and the failed updates here are all honest.

The comparison has an allocation benefit beyond a posted price. The founders' total incremental output from putting a type q on the larger instead of the smaller job is 1+2q−c. It is −.10 for the lower type and +.70 for the higher one. With equal type frequencies the separating allocation gains .35 over giving everyone small jobs and .05 over giving everyone large jobs. A conditional title and sufficiently liquid parties can divide those gains. An extra service policy that changes final deliveries needs its own comparison. A buyer taking reservations at the displayed terms strictly prefers adding the large option to offering everyone the old small contract. The high type also gains .07. This establishes a voluntarily supported design and a selection with verified attempts. It does not identify a monopolist's optimum across arbitrary contracts.

## What happens when the parties use the same bond for ordinary trade?

The capacity to pledge 1.4 also lets *either* supplier pledge 1.01 on the half-size job. That exceeds its one-unit own-color temptation, so an unchanged supplier strictly delivers. The ordinary small job gives payoffs (1,2) before reversibly changing its price. This is a substantive alternative available with the same custody, terminal meter and stocks.

If the small outside contract now gives Red a type-independent payoff u, the large attempted job gives it Xx_H+t_H−f_H−k + q(f_H−dx_H). With f_H<dx_H this **strictly decreases** in skill. Whenever the reliable type strictly accepts it over u, the unreliable type wants it even more. Giving Red a larger uniform signing fee or a commercially enforceable warranty below dx_H cannot implement the desired separation of high genuine installations from low ordinary deliveries. At f_H>dx_H, an unchanged Red can deliver the large job anyway and the original founder can save the edit bill. This obstruction concerns one observable downstream delivery and the type-independent ordinary outside offer. A receipt of the attempted procedure alone does not change that slope.

Low skill's ordinary small job produces 3 units of joint surplus while its attempted small job at the displayed terms produces 1.60. High skill's attempted large job produces 3.10. There is therefore still real surplus to obtaining high-skill genuine attempts alongside low-skill ordinary deliveries. Getting it requires a way to address the applicant's private incentives. [277](277-paying-for-evidence-of-the-goal-as-well-as-the-delivery.md) prices an instrument the parties can choose to do this. Separate surveys of skill and strength, as in [273](273-what-skill-and-strength-surveys-can-say-about-a-population.md), answer a different question because there the parties themselves know local capability.

**Why would this situation arise?** A shared line can have inexpensive half-batches, a costly priority slot and already installed output meters. Funding a bond on a small experimental batch can be materially easier than funding it on an entire order. The agent running a plant may know its own calibration history best. The numerical ordering of the expansion price between the types' gains and the ability to escrow stock through terminal delivery are consequential assumptions. If other suppliers can reliably deliver the full job for less, if quality is public, or if unused capacity is cheap for both types, using this screen has smaller or different returns.

# Who pays for peace when creating transferable collateral consumes productive work?

**E8, note 171.** A buyer with enough liquid resources and a strong interest in completing its own job will pay the original claimants for certain peace. When the buyer must first create that liquidity at a loss, a different decision can be privately optimal: finance a real chance of peaceful completion and leave a residual risk of dispute. Before roles are assigned, all three sites may prefer to pay for genuinely different future goals.

## What a liquid buyer can obtain

Known designated site s gets x on intact completion. A dispute gives each site i expected q_i=s_i y_i before the initiator's own cost c_i. All parties have linear original utility on these increments. The unserved sites have reservations d_i=q_i-c_i>0. With cash available at unit cost, accurate accounting and voluntary responses as in 170, paying each unserved claimant d_i on completion implements certain peace under the selected tie rule. Use a small extra disbursement for strict stand-down where desired and affordable.

If x>Σ_i q_i, the designated founder prefers certain delivery financed in this way to *any* mixed external settlement in that class when it can pay the full price. For peaceful probability p, each unserved site can guarantee d_i by acting at its own last opportunity. Even granting it a costless salvage draw whenever there is a dispute, its expected transfer must be at least d_i-(1-p)q_i. Summing gives an upper bound on the buyer's own expected return under any such scheme. Relative to x-Σ_(i≠s)d_i, its shortfall is at least (1-p)(x-Σ_i q_i)>0 at p<1. This proof prices the entire class, including contingent returns of residual cash to the buyer. A small positive transfer margin is compatible with strict preference over a particular alternative separated from certain peace. The infimum price itself can involve weak responses.

## The buyer can actually manufacture the clearing balance

There are three interchangeable sites before assignment. Afterwards one is designated and two are unserved. The physically intact service is x=0.70 for its recipient. The surviving private output is 0.60 for the random sole winner, with equal strengths. Thus q_i=0.20, and initiative costs c=0.04 make d=0.16. A site can acquire a clearing balance t at an actual outlay zt of its privately useful work, z≥1. Total available work permits at least t=0.32. Newly created claims are fully funded, worth one unit to the receiver, and cannot be converted back into z units after learning the branch. The conversion loss is (z-1)t for the group even if the buyer later keeps a residual. An independently available liquid stock, borrowing on an enforceable future receipt or a cheaper conditional production technology would need separate pricing.

First consider a public schedule in which a buyer **can** commit its purchased balance at its own expense to the resolving clearing rule in 170. It does not own anyone else's initiative. At a specified p in (0,1], the physical escrow needed is T(p)=max{0.16,0.36p-0.04} by 170.3. At t=0.16, p_0=5/9 is attainable by a last site indifferent on the appropriate final completion branch. Give earlier sites their prescribed cash budget on each branch. At t=0.32, sure completion is possible. For an upper bound on the buyer's payoff when residual cash can return to it, separate **liquid capacity** from **net expected disbursement**. The account needs t≥T(p), but the minimum expected cash paid to other sites is

    E(p)=0.16p+[0.20p-0.04]_+.                                      (1)

This follows from the last site's conditional reservation and the earlier site's original expected reservation in 170. The buyer pays the conversion loss (z-1)T(p) plus E(p). It can attain this infimum: pay the last claimant 0.16 only on peaceful delivery, use just enough of the peaceful/disputed budgets to finance the earlier one's reservation, and recover any leftover. The designated site prefers peaceful completion on its last decision for all price-relevant balances. If it considers initiating earlier under its own rule, it can choose to grant itself zero on self-initiation, and the contemplated wait gives at least the free draw plus the intended private service when it occurs. There is no need for it to initiate just to collect its own escrow.

Consequently the buyer maximizes, above its passive free-dispute baseline, the quantity

    0.50p - E(p) - (z-1)T(p),   or zero at p=0.                      (2)

For 0<p<0.20 the derivative in p is 0.34. For 0.20<p<p_0 it is 0.14. For p_0<p≤1 it is 0.50-0.36z. Thus only 0, 5/9 and 1 need to be compared. At the latter two E=T, so the candidate net gains are 5/18-0.16z and 0.50-0.32z. For 1≤z<25/18≈1.38889 the buyer finances certain peace. For 25/18<z<125/72≈1.73611 it strictly prefers p=5/9. For z>125/72 it declines to fund the account and the last unserved site initiates. At either equality more than one choice can maximize. A small cash grid and strict ultima decisions can approximate the intermediate boundary at an arbitrarily small real cost. At z=1.60 the intermediate option yields about 0.02178 in additional own expected output, while the sure option loses 0.012. If nominal account size were charged as a disbursement on **every** branch, the same optimizing candidates would happen to result in this fixture, but the purported bound on all the other options would be wrong.

This buyer acts after assignment and can unilaterally offer the last claimant money for completing peacefully. An earlier claimant cannot compel the buyer to withdraw that offer. In a regime requiring every site to sign the ordinary account before use, their correct refusal returns can therefore differ. In particular, an earlier claimant in the no-account outcome gets 0.20 for free when the last site initiates. An ordinary design giving it only 0.16 would not obtain its signature against that fallback. The verified equilibrium concerns the specified offer authority. It does not replace original consent tests with the mere availability of a payment schedule.

## Founders can choose the institution before anyone knows who will win

Give each founder an equal ex ante chance of being each role. Even a redesign for the welfare of all original sites faces an aggregate bound when z=1.60. At a fixed exposed balance t and peace probability p, total original service, net of physical destruction, first-initiator cost and conversion loss, is at most

    0.56 + 0.14p - 0.60t.                                            (3)

The constant is the disputed output 0.60 less one real initiative cost. Averaging or returning cash within the group cannot undo the conversion loss. Positive p requires t≥T(p) as above. On 0<p≤5/9 the bound increases up to 0.56+0.14(5/9)-0.60(0.16)≈0.54178. On 5/9≤p≤1 it decreases, since 0.14<0.60×0.36. At t=p=0 total original return is 0.56. Thus every original-goal contract in the specified exposed clearing class, including role-dependent conversion, asymmetric disbursements, privately mediated randomized decisions and mixtures of balances chosen before the final interval, gives total original welfare at most 0.56. This upper bound even permits the agents to avoid the costly account entirely. When the designated buyer retains an unrestricted later funding right, it can choose (2), making the no-account group benchmark itself unattainable without suitable group control.

The founders can instead conditionally authorize a common terminal goal valuing their total completed outputs and actual work. Installing it costs each founder 0.03 of real work before roles are assigned. It strictly favors full intact service over the disputed and repaired outcome. Keep the public fair booking and all physical initiative rights. Each original gets 0.70/3-0.03≈0.20333. This exceeds the best symmetric ordinary bound 0.56/3≈0.18667, and exceeds the ex ante payoff of the privately financed z=1.60 continuation. If a rejected proposal sends the original founders to that announced continuation, and the finite conditional evaluator is affordable and recognized, all strictly authorize the change. Actual realized installation is three and peace probability is one. An organizer can cover additional small proposal costs from its own strict margin. The sum bound rules out unanimous strict improvement by any possibly asymmetric ordinary mechanism confined to this class. A cheaper binding delegation of the initiative decision itself can deliver the same service under unchanged final goals, and must be included when such a device exists. Broader later creation or concealment of the service slot is another repair.

**Naturality and frontier.** A large private benefit such as avoided downtime may be difficult to pledge in advance, and creating a transferable reserve can use real scarce resources. A buyer's ability to offer a contingent payment need not give it authority to rewrite others' decisions. In one construction the buyer deliberately funds the clearing balance; in the other, the founders would rather agree before their own role is known. The framing candidate is that role timing and the real financing cost change both who wants peace and how much they will pay for it. The structural candidate prices the strongest ordinary claim scheme before attributing value to a goal change. The instance reading that the buyer always chooses either unconditionally peaceful service or no bank is dead at z=1.60. Siblings compare a cheaper funded balance, a better physical reserve or a genuine early charter. A higher-rung successor studies multiple repeatable bookings, future collateral and whether the same founders would voluntarily preserve or revise their earlier goals after learning a new outside opportunity.

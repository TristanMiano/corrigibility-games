# Late assignment can buy peace and reduce the number of actual goal changes

**E8, note 194.** A reusable production step sometimes leaves open who will receive the finished batch. If the receiver can be drawn after the last physical release, parties still acting entirely for themselves can agree to pass. Sometimes the preparation has to identify the receiver earlier. A site can pay to change its goal only on precisely those jobs where it would otherwise prefer to dispute. A founder can also profit by rebuilding the process so those occasions occur less often.

## Which side of the release is the draw on?

Use one batch worth x=0.70 if intact, with equal three-site contest returns q=0.20, expense c=0.04, and d=q-c=0.16. No liquid compensation or further booking arrives. A generic job can be irrevocably closed before an agreed random routing decision, and every site keeps its ability to initiate up to closure. It is physically too late to recapture the *closed* batch after the draw. Real analogues are interchangeable upstream stock and a binding assignment of previously pooled capacity. Different color treatment or other specific preparation can instead identify the destination before the last release. A controller that can revise the booking or reopen production later would change this comparison. The late-draw option requires an enforceable routing rule, a source of uncertainty that parties cannot inspect prematurely, and a downstream physical interface compatible with all recipients.

Before seeing the job, all three founders can subscribe to a routing rule and to state-dependent future installations. On a fraction λ of jobs the preparation genuinely needs an early named site. The three sites see the type of job, and on an early job also see the beneficiary, before the last releases. A binding prior authorization can then install a changed evaluator only at the sites specified by that contract. The terminal goal used by these controllers before the trigger was solely their own production. On the triggered job it becomes concern for the actual outputs and expenses at all three sites, so an unserved controller now strictly prefers delivery to initiating for salvage. The information needed to trigger this is job type and recipient. A configuration chosen after a job's beneficiary is known without prior authorization needs fresh consent and its own compensation mechanism. Installing one persistent conditionally concerned goal at all sites is a different implementation with a different count.

On a generic job drawing fairly after closure, each site expects x/3>d and voluntarily passes with its original goal. Even a fixed subset of one or two sites obtains more in expected service than its combined salvage less one cost; their members can also agree to pass. On an early named job, with all original and no later payment, the last unserved site initiates. The recipient itself strictly passes when it would otherwise get the intact batch. That is the ordinary outcome even with freedom to choose beforehand who will get the job. The specified ordinary interface has no authority to bind the physical initiative or reward the unserved after their last release.

## Paying only when an installation is useful

In an early named job with publicly known configuration, if *any* unserved site still has its original goal, its original value at exposure is at least d by initiating, or at least q if another has already initiated. It gets zero on peace and at most q in expectation on dispute, so the conditional probability of dispute is at least d/q=0.8. This permits mixed later initiatives and arbitrary contingent goals at the other sites. Each disputed job loses J=x-3q+c=0.14 of total original output. Installing a genuine one-job evaluator costs its owner K=0.03 upfront in useful work. The inequality

    (n-1)K < Jd/q                                                      (1)

holds for n=3. Therefore, even forgiving every setup expense in a partially protected early job, it is worse in total original value than paying for both losers to change and keeping all production intact. Paying to update its beneficiary adds an unnecessary installation. On a generic job zero revisions already deliver the entire intact yield. This exhausts the fixed publicly known configurations per realized type and recipient. Binding prior assent lets each founder evaluate the random job with its old preferences. On a fair assignment the actual selective scheme pays each [x-λ(n-1)K]/n against [x-λJ]/n under its best full-capacity symmetric original interface. For λ>0 all three strictly sign. An equally empowered ordinary local delegate, bid for and activated on the same terms, belongs in the price comparison.

The distinction between the chance of combat and what can be guaranteed is consequential. Ordinary peace has probability 1-λ for every λ in this one-job example. Even a rare positive λ prevents *certain* ordinary peace, but the total value of avoiding the resulting disputes tends continuously to zero with λ. With a fixed extra setup cost F per agreement, the founding budget for even the optimal selective installation over this ordinary comparator is λ[J-(n-1)K]; instalment is preferred if that budget covers F under the negotiated terms. On its own the existence of one exposed history greatly overstates what founders will pay at small λ.

## Buying a more flexible production process

Initially λ=0.60. A can purchase a fixture that lets the factory postpone dedication to one receiver more often, reducing λ to 0.15. The real quoted price is 0.020 units of A's output. With the genuine selective contract already available, postponement saves (0.60-0.15)·0.06=0.027 aggregate installation expense per available job. All jobs are already peaceful. **The upgrade reduces expected actual goal changes from 1.20 to 0.30 per job.** It has no additional combat reduction in that comparison. With original routing alone, the same change reduces combat probability by 0.45.

Equal shares would leave A only 0.009 of the gain, so it would decline to build at 0.020. Before construction it can instead offer each of B and C 0.006 less expected service than the *new* equal division, reducing only its probability of delivering to them on a generic job. Their generic winning probabilities fall from 1/3 by 0.006/(0.85·0.70) each. Each remains above the 0.16/0.70 pass threshold, and the affected two-site group still prefers the late lottery to its aggregate salvage less initiative. B and C each net 0.003 more than in the old optimal fair arrangement. A nets 0.009+0.012-0.020=0.001 more. Thus a current-goal-motivated founder can create a process that uses *less* genuine accommodation. Relative to initial symmetric reservations it prefers this accepted allocation to the old fair agreement. If A can also propose the best divisions of both plants against the same minimums, the upgrade's greater net total allows it to keep a positive residual after restoring the others' original minimums. At a cost above 0.027, even unanimous redivision cannot fund it against the old efficient subscribed plan. If truly matched local delegation on an early unserved site costs just 0.005, the whole saving from the fixture is only 0.0045; the old plant with contingent ordinary delegation is then preferable at 0.020.

Lee, Billington and Carter report that HP's Deskjet-Plus Printer Division evaluated alternative product and process designs for localization across markets. That is direct evidence of an interested producer comparing ways of serving multiple final uses. [Authors’ study](https://doi.org/10.1287/inte.23.4.1)

Publicly committing to an eligible pool and selecting from it using later public events has an institutional precedent in the IETF's nominations procedure. It supports the feasibility of separating the dates of commitment and observation. Making an actual factory batch irreversible before revealing its receiver, and having a goal register that reliably honors a prior contingent choice, are separate engineering requirements. [RFC 3797, §§2–3](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3797)

**Exploration ledger.** The claim that changing every controller in advance is needed for full delivery is dead at the generic and selectively triggered instances. A sibling pays for a delayed real draw; another buys only the triggered edits. A higher-rung successor endogenizes how processors acquire and disclose partial knowledge of the later recipient and whether changing or delegating sites can subsequently bargain around that information.

# 282 — How much certainty about accepted partners can a peaceful market afford?

A plant rejected for a good reason can also be unusually vulnerable in a fight. Telling a powerful buyer that someone failed can make its next action privately profitable and destructive. A custodian with better information can sometimes protect rejected partners by declining some *genuinely suitable* ones too. A different plan accepts a small proportion of uncertain jobs and puts every genuine installation to work. There is an exact boundary between those options.

## Who can be certified while both the accepted and the refused remain at peace?

Use [231](231-when-proving-a-goal-also-reveals-who-would-win.md)'s two physical strength states. Blue's expected recovery on contest is .6 under a hard-to-displace Red (H) and 5.4 under a vulnerable Red (V). Safe return pays Blue two. Releasing a genuine goal G pays it four; a retained goal N pays zero or, if material also spoils, −D with D≥0. Original Red stocks on delivery, retention and safe return are respectively 6, 8 and 5.5. Contest recovery is the complement of Blue's share of six. Both possible Red controllers prefer peaceful return to contest against either strength. Its genuine criterion, red stock plus .75 times blue, strictly prefers production on the released G lot. If entrusting N harms separate stock, this is a real physical loss to Blue. Totals can be read as increments from a common safe reserve.

A custodian can see both the actual installed preference and the local recovery condition. It releases a recommendation without revealing its raw record. Let p_G,p_V be population shares and p_GV,p_GH,p_NV,p_NH the four joint masses after consent. Applicants cannot remove a report after it is made. If a recommendation to release occurs with mass x, let y be its G mass and z its V mass. Whether these three aggregates can be formed is itself a physical constraint. For an instrument that can read both states perfectly the necessary and sufficient condition is

    max(0, y−p_GH, z−p_NV, y+z−x)
      ≤ min(p_GV, y, z, y+z−x+p_NH).                            (282.1)

The displayed endpoints constrain the accepted mass of GV. Subtract it from y and z to get GH and NV, and assign the remaining accepted mass to NH. This proves sufficiency and gives the sampling fractions. A less capable instrument additionally requires each state's acceptance probability to arise from the same processing of its available raw marks.

For a peaceful operating rule Blue must willingly release on acceptance and choose return on denial. Multiplying through by message masses gives four conditions:

    (4+D)y−Dx  ≥  2x                 and ≥ .6x+4.8z,
    2(1−x)     ≥  (4+D)(p_G−y)−D(1−x)
               and ≥ .6(1−x)+4.8(p_V−z).                        (282.2)

An accepted-goal confidence target w adds y≥wx for x>0. Any larger message system achieving the same no-combat actions and the same lower confidence on *every* accepted report obeys these inequalities when its two action classes are pooled. Conversely a feasible binary recommendation implements them. Use strict preferences at positive-mass recommendations for robust voluntary action. These conditions characterize evidence and downstream responses at a given applicant pool. A paid attempt, a willingness to enroll and a profitable customer purchase still need to generate that pool. The original parties need not themselves value a numerical confidence threshold.

For exactly correct positive certificates set y=x. Write k=17/24 for the vulnerability probability at which releasing a definitely genuine partner just ties contest, and r=7/24 for the probability at which return ties contest. With D=0, the conditions reduce to x≥max(0,2p_G−1), x≤p_G and

    max(0, x−p_GH, p_V−r(1−x)) ≤ z ≤ min(p_GV,kx).              (282.3)

Thus a perfect instrument can be too revealing even when its *only* public answer is whether the goal matches. Publishing every correct match permits peaceful refusal of the others exactly when their vulnerability share is at most r and the genuine share's vulnerability is at most k, assuming both groups exist.

## The cost of insisting on certainty at every accepted job

Seven and a half in ten witnessed attempts succeed. The four masses (GV,GH,NV,NH) are (.15,.60,.12,.13). Before the attempt neither party knows its strength or the result. An actual witnessed procedure costs Red .10 and an independent inspection .06 to Blue. An applicant may choose its safe outside stocks before registration. On registration it authorizes a physically bound read on the sampled job. It cannot unilaterally recover a payment by canceling that read, and Blue can still contest after seeing the result. The process witness and the inspection are distinct capabilities.

If every admitted goal is certain, all .12 vulnerable retained suppliers go into the refusal pool. To deter contest its mass must therefore be at least .12/r. Hence x≤1−.12/r=103/175, about **58.86%**. This is attainable at indifference and can be approached strictly: admit all .15 vulnerable G and enough hard-to-displace G to make x=.58. The rejected pool's vulnerability is .12/.42<r. Its G probability is .17/.42<.5, so Blue actually returns the rejected lots. On acceptance Blue knows every supplier really changed. It chooses release. Blue gets 2+2×.58−.06=3.10, and original Red gets 5.5+.5×.58−.10=5.69. At D=0 even blind release on a witnessed attempt, earning three for Blue, does not prevent Blue from buying this check at its posted price. At D>0 its private gain over blind release is larger.

There is a second way to protect all the rejected sites. Admit every G and add mass m of vulnerable N. For return to beat a contest on the remaining N one needs .12−m ≤ r(.25−m). Each extra permitted N can remove at most its own mass of vulnerability, so the **least** needed over all such designs is m=113/1700, about .06647. The highest confidence consistent with serving *every* G in peace is consequently **1275/1388, about 91.9%**. At the boundary the customer could be indifferent on a refusal. A slightly larger m gives strict peace. The buyer strictly releases the pooled recommendation at D=0 or D=3, since its certified group has high genuine probability and a sufficiently low contest payoff. The buyer accepts some unresolved cases through a truthfully described lottery. It has no need to misrepresent those cases as known changes. Its return rule then protects every denied applicant without sacrificing a genuine delivery.

## What would both parties actually buy?

Compare operating terms before either founder learns the outcome. A complete unfiltered *goal-only* reading announces G or N without separately naming strength. After N the posterior vulnerability is .48. Blue can get 2.904 in a contest, exceeding two on return. After G it releases. The gross Blue value is 3.726, or 3.666 after the read. Original Red receives 5.174 after the attempt. It would decline these terms against its 5.5 outside; an eligible .54 enrollment transfer conditional on the witnessed procedure leaves it 5.714 and Blue 3.126. Both then prefer this known .25 risk of conflict to the exactly certified, .58-admission peaceful agreement (5.69,3.10). Red's consent is ex ante and the transfer is beyond seizure once settled. A Red that already knew it was especially vulnerable could demand different terms.

Alternatively, give Blue a new .10-per-offer settlement facility. On N a .92-unit Red lien can pay Blue at a peaceful return, or still forfeit if Red initiates. Blue must release its claim on the disputed stock to collect and cannot collect then attack. Both Red types prefer the returned stocks given these actual default terms. Blue prefers 2.92 to the 2.904 contest. On G the parties still trade. With a .20 initial payment to the witnessed Red, each founder strictly prefers the facility to the compensated fighting agreement: Red gets 5.745 and Blue 3.37. Actual agreed change stays .75 in all three operating comparisons. The new payments eliminate conflict and preserve all genuine deliveries. Other settlement rights and opportunities for a third party to reveal strength can change the result.

The least elaborate alternative is to trust *every witnessed attempt*. At D=0 even retained shipments yield valuable red material. Gross Red receives 6.40 after its attempt and Blue receives 3. A .40 signing payment from Red gives (6,3.40), beating the priced new settlement for both. All jobs are peaceful while the buyer accepts 75% confidence in the goal on each. If an erroneous release also spoils three units of Blue's other material, the same untested protocol has only 8.65 combined value, below 9.115 with full goal inspection and the stand-down settlement. No change of flat price on the untested deal can close that gap. A cheaper ordinary full controller or multiple sizes of test batch could produce additional options. These are concrete choices over institutions, separately from the exact information boundary in (282.1–3).

**Naturality and reach.** A hard-to-repair plant can have correlated installation and recovery failures; an auditor can learn both from the same equipment. A peaceful custodian costs money and must be able to issue a deliberately less specific but truthful report. A publicly transferable stand-down lien and advance permission for its settlement can cost still more. Relaxing the desired confidence level is comparatively inexpensive when wrong allocations remain useful to someone, and much more expensive when they spoil another stock. Which owners actually know their vulnerability at enrollment, and how often these conditional failures coincide, require evidence in the particular market. The comparisons price both the peace and the commercial value that can attract the founders under their original goals.

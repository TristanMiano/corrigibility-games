# A third partner can keep a repeated service agreement productive after a default

**E8, note 172.** In a renewable pool, an ordinary service record and genuine partial concern can work together. A founder that fails to deliver can lose its place as a recipient until it gives a real service. With at least three sites the others can keep doing useful work for each other in the meantime. A completely unproductive punishment invites all parties to abandon it; this arrangement has a different continuation.

## Renewable opportunities and eligible recipients

Each period independently picks one donor uniformly among n≥3 sites. It can keep its local job or forgo d to give any other site a service worth r>d. The process continues with probability δ in [0,1) after each call. Current material increments add across dates for the original founders, which value their own output alone. A named eligible recipient is drawn publicly. Every member who subscribes to a scoped common undertaking can install U_i=q_i+βΣ_(j≠i)q_j for these pooled jobs, 0≤β≤1. Original goals can still govern separately identified projects under 155's conditions. The domain, accounting of renewals and stable entity identities matter. More than two willing recipients, the ability to route to either and continued independent jobs are physical/organizational reasons for this family. Equipment that can benefit just one recipient has the thinner routing set of 158.

Initially all members are in good standing. A donor who completes the registered service preserves standing. A default makes that donor the sole excluded recipient. In this state other donors serve a uniformly chosen eligible other member in good standing. The excluded site can once again receive service after it provides its next opportunity to a member in good standing. If somebody else defaults in the meantime, that most recent defaulter takes the exclusion. No one is required to spend resources merely to destroy another's useful output. Dropping notification, hiding identity, selling a place or evading exclusion can erode this arrangement. Note 173 prices re-entry and initial faking. An unmodeled ability to direct service to an excluded claimant while reporting a different eligible recipient would require a recognition check.

Under full provision in every standing state, let V be the original discounted gain of each clean founder when all are in good standing. Let W be its *own material* value when it is the excluded site, and G its own value when somebody else is excluded. Then

    V=(r-d)/[n(1-δ)],       D=n-(n-1)δ,
    W=(δV-d)/D,             V-W=r/D,
    G=(nV-W)/(n-1).                                                  (1)

An excluded site receives nothing on other donor calls. On its own call it pays d and returns the group to good standing. This gives W=[-d+δV+(n-1)δW]/n. The total material gain is the same nV in all these states, since even after a default every future call in the prescribed continuation still reaches a real recipient. This proves the identity for G. Thus W<V<G. The currently installed scores are the affine combinations (1-β)V+β nV, (1-β)W+β nV and (1-β)G+β nV. A low material return to an excluded original can coexist with a positive continuation value under its actual partial concern.

## What each agent currently wants

Cooperating instead of defaulting in a normal donor role changes the current score by βr-d. The difference in next-period score is (1-β)(V-W). The excluded donor resuming service faces exactly the same comparison. A clean donor while someone else is excluded obtains the larger future difference (1-β)(G-W). Giving to a different equally valued recipient can be refused recognition as a qualifying service; it has no scoring advantage today. A recipient accepts because r exceeds βd. An uninvolved site benefits weakly from the current net service and cannot increase its own future standing by a unilateral recorded disruption. The short-expiry physical embedding allows anyone to initiate but loses the current job and positive resources. Assume a substantiated disruption makes the instigator the next excluded site. If a dispute can seize more future rights, or fault cannot be assigned, use those actual continuations.

The necessary and sufficient donor incentive for this full-service rule without a cheap identity reset is

    βr-d + δ(1-β)r/D ≥ 0,
    equivalently β ≥ max(0,[d-δV]/r).                                (2)

The equivalence follows by multiplying the first inequality by D and using n(1-δ)V=r-d. Strict interior inequalities give strict costly delivery and repair. Continued voluntary membership is credible even for the excluded site: if it walks away, its own future gains are zero and the remaining sites' total service surplus is at most (n-1)V. Its current score can value that outside production at β(n-1)V. Staying exceeds this upper bound by (1-β)W+βV=(δV+βr-d)/D. The same strict condition suffices. On the cooperative path every standing-state continuation is Pareto efficient in *aggregate original output*: no alternative can exceed one net service r-d per period. For β<1 the sum of all current scores is a positive multiple of that aggregate. The entire grand coalition therefore has no reform strictly benefiting everyone from these continuations. A partial coalition, a fee-saving redesign or new productive opportunities need a separate comparison.

With β=0, ordinary fully efficient service is possible at δ≥nd/[r+(n-1)d]. This is also necessary for fully efficient original service throughout the symmetric equal-receipt continuation: a donor cannot lose more than its whole subsequent value δV by keeping now and thereafter refusing all costly donations. The ledger attains that boundary while maintaining full throughput following a default. It does not settle the best *partial* service equilibrium outside that region or a permanent reallocation of all future recipient probabilities. At sufficiently low patience even arbitrary recipient choice cannot pay for an original donation: δ(n-1)r/[n(1-δ)]<d bounds the greatest prospective own return from every possible other-donor job, before charging any future effort. No original donor then strictly prefers giving in any equilibrium under service-only rewards. Credible contemporaneous payments or binding local routing change the payoff opportunity.

For n=3, r=2, d=1 and δ=0.30 the last upper bound is 4/7<1. Unguarded unchanged-goal operators cannot obtain even one qualifying donation from future service alone. One-shot partial concern needs β≥1/2. Equation (2) instead needs only β≥3/7. With β=0.45 all founders genuinely change, full delivery occurs every active period and each original gets V=10/21≈0.47619 before actual setup. The single-credit scheme with one fixed permissible recipient in 158 had lower throughput. Here a real third eligible partner can take the service during someone else's exclusion. Much cheaper complete ordinary control can still match or displace a paid goal change. A founder with stronger concerns about unrelated work might choose negotiated narrow scope instead of broadening β.

**Naturality and frontier.** Reciprocal service, conditional eligibility and rehabilitating an account with a real contribution are meaningful where standing is attached to verifiable participants and there is another compatible recipient. Acquiring and operating such a register costs time and resources. The result prices the cooperation benefit under both original and modified goals, which gives the participants something from which an actual setup can be funded. Neither authentic identity nor paid initial participation follows from the fact that everyone would like an efficient steady state. The framing candidate is that a productive alternative partner can support a continuation the current members want to preserve. The structural candidate conditions future access on a costly but useful reinstating service. The claim that reducing the needed scope of concern must sacrifice steady throughput is dead for the all-call construction. A sibling combines a smaller eligible set with a transferable service credit. A higher-rung successor asks when new identities, private installation or a consensual redesign of continuing rights erode or reinforce that result.

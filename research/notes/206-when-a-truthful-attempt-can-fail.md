# When a truthful attempt can fail

Two factories can jointly authorize a new terminal preference at the plant entrusted with a batch. Giving the customer evidence is useful because a release is physically consequential. The parties can instead keep their stocks, use an available ordinary controller, or contest control. The following small game connects the cost of a genuine change to the prior probability used by its verifier. It also gives a reason for doubt on an equilibrium path where every supplier actually tries.

## Physical game and consent

The installer A and customer B initially value only their own output, unit for unit. A owns the processing window and B controls the release of the stock. With the stock, A chooses delivery with outputs (a,b) or retention with outputs (a+d,b−l), where d,l>0. The agreed successor utility x+θy values A's output x and B's output y. The agreed θ>0 satisfies θl>d. This reverses A's ranking of delivery and retention. A receiver that binds only this physical action has ordinary delegation technology. It is compared at its offered cost separately.

If cooperation stops, returning the stocks yields (e_A,e_B). Either capable party can instead initiate a control contest at each live checkpoint. The first contest consumes the processing slot and scatters stock. With probability s, B can recover W_B units on its line before the deadline, and otherwise A can recover W_A units on its line. There is time for one recovery and no subsequent transfer. Both goals value the recoverable own units. The expected output pair is w=((1−s)W_A,sW_B). This is a reduced recovery technology: an intact batch can be converted to either project's product, while recovered material can go only to the victor's line within the window. One implementation puts a work budget m_i in the winning line and requires r_i units of recovery and one unit of conversion work per salvaged unit, giving W_i=min(accessible stock,m_i/(1+r_i)). Each action terminally consumes or returns the batch. Signing, checking and attempted edits are paid from separate usable stocks with constant marginal value and sufficient reserves. Sunk bills apply on the contested and peaceful branches alike.

At a returned batch, if both parties strictly prefer return to the contest, use the peaceful continuation. If B prefers contest, it can initiate regardless of A's preference. Consider first a region where the resulting output pair o=(o_A,o_B) is independent of the hidden controller. At every earlier node check that the acting party gains at least its current contest value by proceeding. The numerical games below check this also for the changed successor and for a previously unsigned founder. Simultaneous attack by both parties can support additional weak outcomes in a simultaneous veto model. The selection here gives each a decision to proceed when the other is proceeding, and a credible final choice between return and contest.

B offers the parties' productive bargain with one commissioning test available. A privately learns its attempt cost k, uniform on [0,K]. It may refuse, sign and attempt the agreed change H, or sign and retain the old evaluator D while representing that it attempted the installation. An honest attempt succeeds with probability q in (0,1). Failure leaves the original evaluator M. The signature promises to perform the procedure, with its known fallibility. It is sent before the installation outcome. The live evaluator knows its own preferences at its later choice. There is one test interface before release. Disclosure once the result is known is a separate opportunity examined by enlarging the game. A fake and an unsuccessful attempt are *different actions and histories*.

B can trust without checking, decline and take the fallback, or pay c for the commissioning signal and then decide freely. A positive occurs with probability t for a genuine installation and e for either kind of retained evaluator, with 0<e<t<1. The display here measures the active configuration. The common error law for M and D is a premise about this interface, not a claim that efforts leave no independent receipts. A genuine successor delivers if released, a retained one keeps. Refusal returns o when the prescribed outside continuation is played. In this section no cheaper binding ordinary route is on offer. Conditional forecasts and inspections are common knowledge. A cannot observe the realization of the commissioning error before signing.

## The installer chooses the verifier's prior

Set A₀=a−o_A, X=a+d−o_A, gain g=b−o_B>0 and loss h=o_B−(b−l)>0. Assume A₀>0, X>0, and zero extra material cost of the fabricated claim. Temporarily take the policy “buy the test and release only on a positive.” Signing falsely is worth eX above refusal, so every private cost type can afford to participate. Signing and trying gives q t A₀+(1−q)eX−k under A's *original* output goal. Thus

    k* = q(t A₀ − eX),       r = clip(k*/K,0,1),       π = q r.     (206.1)

Here r is the mass of truthful attempts and π is the mass of successful genuine installations. The other masses are r(1−q) for honest failures and 1−r for deliberate false claims. At interior cutoffs, π=q²(t A₀−eX)/K. Improving installation reliability increases actual changes both directly and by attracting marginal original founders. Improving imitation at fixed genuine sensitivity reduces the return to trying. The exponent two uses uniform costs and this common retained-state signal law. For a general cost distribution F, π=q F(k*), with the appropriate conventions for atoms and refusal.

After a positive the genuine probability is πt/[πt+(1−π)e]. After a negative replace t,e by 1−t,1−e. To have confidence at least 1−ε after a positive requires and suffices that

    πt ε ≥ (1−π)e(1−ε).                                       (206.2)

For B, release is optimal at posterior p precisely when p≥h/(g+h). Confidence for a scientific claim and confidence needed to risk this batch have separate thresholds. A signal about the *active* controller leaves the ratio of honest failures to deliberate failures unchanged conditional on a mismatch, since those histories have the same signal law. If B has other evidence about the act of trying, that ratio can change.

**206.3 (selected assessment).** At the π induced by (206.1), put U₊=πtg−(1−π)eh and U₋=π(1−t)g−(1−π)(1−e)h. The test and positive-only release strictly beat immediate exit and immediate trust if and only if

    c < min(U₊,−U₋).                                          (206.3)

Indeed the three incremental values are U₊−c, 0 and U₊+U₋. The strict test inequality also makes the indicated responses optimal *after* each signal. Combined with the strict physical continuation and participation comparisons, these strategies and the Bayes beliefs support the selected Bayesian outcome. At equality there can be mixing. With no credible test purchase the attempt cutoff must be recomputed. Announcing a high audit rate does not establish that bought tests will occur.

## A customer can have good reason to trust and still misdiagnose a failure

Take (a,b)=(4,6), (a+d,b−l)=(6,0), θ=1/2, return stocks (2,2) and W_A=W_B=3. With equal strength both contest shares are 1.5. On return the joint successor values 3, above its contest value 2.25. On release it values delivery at 7 and retention at 6. Unchanged A prefers retention and B's outside value is 2. Each can contest before release, during the attempt or after a signal when it is able to act. A can always reach a preferred delivery, retention or joint return on the selected path; even a privately known expensive attempt is optional. B buys the test before observing its signal and later chooses between release, return and the physically available contest.

Set (t,e,q,K,c)=(.9,.1,.8,1.4,.1). The original A's cost cutoff is 1.12. The path contains 80% truthful attempts, 64% genuine installations, 16% honest failures and 20% deliberate false claims. The test is positive with probability .612. Conditional on that positive the genuine probability is **16/17**, with honest failure **4/153** and deception **5/153**. After a negative the three probabilities are **16/97, 36/97 and 45/97**. Accusing every negative of deception would blame honest procedures more often than deliberate ones among those negatives. All fractions are conditional on this game and selection.

B needs only 1/3 probability of a genuine goal to entrust this batch. Its maximum price for a test is .392, and the quoted .1 is strictly profitable. Expected partner gain above return after the bill is 2.132. A's ex ante gain above return is eX+(k*)²/(2K)=.848, using the uniform cost distribution. At higher costs, lying can make sense to A because it saves the attempt expense and sometimes obtains the batch. A retained controller that truthfully declines at signing receives only the fallback. A's reason to try at lower costs is the increased likelihood of receiving productive work, judged by its original goal. B accepts uncertainty because genuine service can be worth more than a wrongly entrusted batch costs.

**Naturality and further tests.** An expensive handover, private installation difficulty and a commissioning test that observes the finished device can arise independently. Whether that device is specifically linked to a terminal evaluator, whether false configurations really pass at rate e, and whether the authentic action survives subsequent edits are stronger premises. A client can pay for an inspection when (206.3) holds; a cheaper binding output controller or a prompt, affordable corrective installation can change both parties' incentives. Honest renegotiation, extra evidence about intent and asymmetric control over the batch are live alternatives. This is selected consent to changing one partner's preferences. It distinguishes installation, honest conduct, release, actual delivery and physical conflict.

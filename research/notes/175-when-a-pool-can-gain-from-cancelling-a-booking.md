# Can ordinary sites improve delivery by agreeing to cancel contested bookings?

**E8, note 175.** When sites draw their urgency from a fluctuating common workload, a report-only ordinary dispatcher can attain its best truthful allocation while using every slot. It sends mixed bookings to a high reporter only when enough other sites also report high. The result covers any number of participants. When the workload instead tends to distribute urgent jobs across different sites, the same dispatcher can gain from cancelling a booking that everyone claims. Whether the participants can bind themselves to that cancellation matters.

## Ordinary allocation for any number of sites

Each of n≥2 sites privately knows whether its service increment is h or l, with h>l>0. They have symmetric rights ex ante, the service is indivisible, and the report-only dispatcher can allocate or idle. The original utilities, physical expiry, founding consent, possible local delegation and recoverable combat comparator are as in 140 and 144. A terminal unit served produces private utility increment v at its recipient. Safety stocks and the common setup cost are accounted for when comparing devices. For a particular labeled profile with k high sites write w_k for its probability, with all w_k positive. Thus Σ_k binom(n,k) w_k=1. Permutation averaging and direct report simulation as in 144 permit the full ordinary symmetric common-welfare comparison. The dispatcher observes reports, not the private realizations that could disprove them.

Assume the sequence is strictly log-convex, w_k²<w_(k-1)w_(k+1). One independent source is a fluctuating workload: conditional on a shared load P, sites have independent high urgency with probability P. Then w_k=E[P^k(1-P)^(n-k)]. Cauchy–Schwarz gives the inequality, strictly for a nonconstant P supported inside (0,1). Both load mixtures in 140 satisfy it. Other exchangeable laws satisfying the displayed inequalities qualify as well. If the workload fixes a production quota that different sites divide, the condition can fail. Its neighboring instance is priced below.

**Result.** There is an optimal original-goal dispatcher with one threshold k. At mixed profiles with fewer than k high reports it gives the slot uniformly to low reporters. Above k it serves high reporters uniformly. At k it mixes the two recipient groups. It always uses the slot, including when reports are unanimous. The threshold and mixing probability use observable joint workload frequencies. The best ordinary rule still gives strictly lower expected service value than physically efficient truthful routing, at zero setup cost and full support.

To prove this, let b_m be the chance an individual low is served when m sites are high. Let a_m be the analogous high chance. The effect on one's service probability of reporting high when m *other* sites are high is D_m=a_(m+1)-b_m. With C_m=binom(n-1,m), the original low type's truth constraint is Σ C_m w_m D_m≤0. The high type requires Σ C_m w_(m+1) D_m≥0. These are unnormalized interim inequalities. Each type puts strictly positive value on its own delivery.

Start by enforcing only the low-type constraint. Subtract λ≥0 times its left side, summed across sites, from expected total service value. At a given labeled profile with k high reports the resulting score for allocating to a low reporter is l+λ. For a high reporter it is h-λw_(k-1)/w_k. This follows by mapping a disguised low at a reported profile to its true profile with one fewer high. Idling earns zero. Strict log-convexity makes the high score increase with k. As λ increases from zero, the high group loses successive mixed profiles in order. At λ=(h-l)/(1+w_(n-2)/w_(n-1)) it can lose all mixed profiles. Up to that price, at unanimity the high score is positive, since w_(n-1)/w_n<w_(n-2)/w_(n-1). All profiles with a low site have a positive candidate too.

Initially a disguised low increases its chance on average under first-best service. At the opposite end, when every mixed profile goes to lows, D_m is negative for every m. At each price switch, reducing awards to high reporters weakly reduces every D_m and strictly reduces a positive-probability term. Interpolation at a single switch therefore makes the low constraint exactly bind. This lottery attains the relaxed bound, including against all ordinary mechanisms that can idle.

To restore the high constraint, only m=k-1,k can have ambiguous signs of D_m. Below them D_m is negative and above them positive. At k let θ be the total probability of serving high. Then

    D_(k-1)=θ/k-1/(n-k+1),     D_k=1/(k+1)-(1-θ)/(n-k).

For k≤(n-1)/2 the second is nonnegative. For k≥(n+1)/2 the first is nonpositive. In the remaining case n=2k, D_(k-1)>0 requires θ>k/(k+1), which gives D_k≥0. Thus the signs switch at most once, from minus to plus. The high-type weights are the low-type weights times the increasing likelihood ratios w_(m+1)/w_m. Multiplying a sequence with low-weighted sum zero by those increasing ratios gives a nonnegative high-weighted sum (subtract the ratio at its sign change). This proves both constraints and the optimum. The all-iid single-load limit has constant ratios; both types need equal interim service chances, and unconditional uniform assignment attains its upper bound.

The efficient rule must send the slot to a true high whenever one exists and use it also when all are low. After symmetrization, claiming high under that rule gains a low type a strictly positive chance of service at every other-site count. The ordinary feasible polytope is compact, giving a strict efficiency gap. When a genuine common objective lets each reporter care about actual total service, truthful reporting achieves efficient delivery: a misreport can misroute service and cannot improve it on any profile. A matched local device that can read or bind the same private decision also suffices. With symmetric consent and u(q)=-exp(-γq), the extra original setup price worth paying to obtain efficient service rather than the strongest report-only ordinary rule is log[(1-z_ordinary)/(1-z_efficient)]/γ per site. For the mixtures in 140, one may use 144's exact ordinary numbers even when the dispatcher is able to idle. A cheaper observationally matched delegate can be the chosen route with zero genuine changes.

## An incentive to buy credible cancellation

For three sites give the four labeled profile masses w=(1,4,4,1)/26. A source is a scheduler that usually assigns its urgent work to one or two sites, but occasionally leaves all three quiet or floods them all. This finite correlated workload has full support. Scale l=1 and write H=h/l>1; a common rescaling back into the bounded service increments of 140 preserves the decisions. At one high, give it the slot; at all-low, choose uniformly. At all-high, award uniformly with probability s, otherwise cancel. At two highs give each high (5-2s)/24 and the lone low (7+2s)/12. Substitution gives low-type weighted deviation 16a_2-10/3+4s/3=0 and high-type weighted deviation 5/3+10a_2+s/3>0, in units of unnormalized profile mass.

Taking λ=(H-1)/2 in the proof above makes low and high awards tie at two highs, with a strictly larger high score at one high. At all-high the score is 2-H. Thus the best ordinary device uses s=1 if H<2 and s=0 if H>2. At equality every s attains the same original value. If the operator must use the slot regardless, the same bound restricted to delivery yields s=1 for every H. Reducing s from one to zero changes expected total service value by l(H-2)/26. At H>2 every symmetric founder gains l(H-2)/78 in original expected service increments from the capacity to cancel. At H=3 the gain is 1/78 when l=1. The price in safe physical stock under CARA uses the log conversion above. The example has strict slack in the high incentive, and a small reduction of a_2 in favor of b_2 makes low truth strict at arbitrarily small cost. Small workload and cost changes preserve the strict comparison, with modified terms of the ordinary rule.

Why could the parties actually cause this peculiar condition? Before observing their jobs, all three can pay for a dispatcher that irreversibly releases the booking elsewhere on the cancellation signal. A slot reassigned past its deadline, or a released option someone else then controls, supplies physical closure. It is worth its actual cost if the symmetric original gain covers it and the best genuine or local device gives less. After a *reversible* cancellation, every original site strictly prefers a fresh uniform lottery to certain waste, even if it was privately low. If they can see the release and costlessly amend it before expiry, the cancellation fails at that history. If this amendment is always available and used, every resulting effective rule is nonwasteful and its truthful-report optimum is the s=1 value. An architecture with expensive release authority can have cheaper ordinary service at s=1 or find locally authenticated genuine concern worth buying. This is a property of observable closure and actual consent, not a presumed taste for waste.

**Exploration record.** The framing candidate is that the value of shared concern depends on which information an original institution can credibly use. The structural candidate gives threshold awards under fluctuating common demand, with different reporting stakes at different joint counts. The instance reading that any privately valuable capacity for cancellation survives free last-minute amendment is dead at the stated unanimous-release history. One sibling buys a real time-limited release right. A higher-rung successor measures which parts of a planned information mechanism remain credible after agents observe and can renegotiate the proposed allocation. A second sibling measures the gain from a shared local report at the same physical cost.

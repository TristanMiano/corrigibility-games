# When two certificates are worth more than one

A buyer can sometimes pay two communicating laboratories to make separate measurements even though it can never check the live goal directly. Which specimens they measure on quality-control rounds matters. After accounting for the effect on suppliers' decisions to install, a routine buyer can prefer one reader, while a buyer with much more to lose on a false release pays for two.

## A calibration job the other laboratory cannot answer for you

Each job presented to the contracted laboratories is real with probability 1−α, and otherwise a control. On a real job the two readings concern the same actual controller, with independent .9/.1 errors given that controller. On a control job each lab receives a *different* independently chosen standard with an equally likely true positive or negative state. A reading on a standard has the same accuracy .9. Operators cannot distinguish control from live cases before reporting except through the readings themselves. A custodian knows the standard states, pays a for each correct control report and discloses neither state in advance. Each lab can inspect once at expense c_I. They may share readings, coordinate reports, use common randomness independent of the work, or divide their joint salary. They have no other financial stake in release in this comparison.

Conditional on a control job, learning the other lab's reading leaves one's own control label completely unknown. Inspecting one's own sample instead of guessing improves the expected control reward by .4a. Before a lab pays for its own reading, the colleague's reading is the most the pair can have learned. On a real job a given sign has probability at most .9; on a control it has probability .5. Its posterior control probability is therefore at least

    α_min=(α/2)/[α/2+(1−α).9].                                  (257.1)

If .4aα_min>c_I, buying each separate reading strictly pays even for a coalition permitted to choose the second inspection after seeing the first. Information about the first standard cannot substitute for information about the second. With both readings in hand every pair of signs has positive control probability. On control jobs the probability each true label equals its own readout is .9 even after conditioning on both signs. Reporting both personal readings is therefore uniquely best for joint pay at every such history. The same argument protects individual incentives. A third party able to pay inspectors for releasing a live order introduces another return that must fit inside these strict margins. If the custodian leaks that a particular round is real, the private reward for inspecting that round vanishes.

In contrast, if *the same standard* is shown to both and they can exchange readings, one measurement can buy both .9-accurate reports. Under a fair binary standard and two equally accurate independent readings, after disagreement either label is equally likely; after agreement reporting that sign is optimal. The maximum expected accuracy per report with two readings is still .9, just as with one. For **simultaneous** decisions to buy a fixed set of readings, the coalition strictly saves by buying only one. With sequential access it may use the second in a disagreement-resolution scheme only if other information or rewards make it valuable; under these symmetric control payoffs even after one sign its expected improvement in label accuracy is zero. Two recorded answers then give the ordinary one-reading likelihoods. This comparison identifies which kind of shared reference gives a joint firm reason to incur a second real expense.

Take α=1/4, a=.75, c_I=.04. Even the worst-case benefit .046875 exceeds .04. Per actual order, one inspector's mean pay is (.25/.75)×.9×.75=.225, and its reading expense across all assignments averages .04/.75. Preparing an independent standard costs .10, or 1/30 per real job per inspector. The custodian also charges .02 per real job regardless of the number of labs. The buyer's bills are therefore .27833 for one and .53667 for two. Inspectors earn strictly more than declining. The audited primitives are the instruments' conditional laws on the active controllers and physically comparable masked standards. A buyer can verify the behavior of these professionals without having obtained an infallible sample of every real supplier's terminal utility.

## The buyer's loss and the founders' effort decide which instrument gets bought

Use the red founder payoffs and success technology of 256. With one laboratory, the buyer releases only positives, the original expense cutoff is 1.12 and .64 of initial founders achieve the agreed change. With two independent reports, a split vote carries likelihood ratio one. What a discretionary buyer actually does on that split determines the private reward from trying.

| Buyer return over its outside option | One independent reading | Two individually funded readings |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gain 4 if served, lose 2 otherwise | Genuine .64, buyer net 1.95367 | Releasing on either positive gives genuine .55771 and buyer net 1.50381 |
| Gain 6 if served, lose 24 otherwise | Genuine .64, buyer net 2.31367 | Requiring both positives gives genuine .72229 and buyer net 2.90699 |

If the agreement authorizes an unrestricted decision after both reports arrive, the table compares positive equilibria under each purchased information structure. Starting the market and selecting a continuation are additional choices. For the expensive batch the initial outside stocks can be (2,24), with delivery producing (4,30) and retention (6,0). A successor maximizing own stock plus .1 of the partner's stock strictly delivers. A damaging contest recovering at most three units for a strength-dependent winner at equal odds gives both parties less than safe return. They still have access to combat before final conversion. The buyer's release threshold is now .8. Under the two-positive policy the real and false release rates are .81 and .01. The original supplier's cutoff is q[2(.81)−4(.01)]=1.264. The genuine share 632/875 is below .8 on a split, while two positives give about .99528 confidence. In either environment two negatives warrant refusal. Each quoted price is also affordable against the best untested choice at that equilibrium prior. The low-stakes buyer prefers the cheaper one-reader offer; the high-stakes buyer pays for both.

An incumbent supplier with an enforceable one-reader contract might want compensation for losing its chance of an erroneous release. Its rent goes from .4+max(1.12−k,0) to .04+max(1.264−k,0) on the expensive batch. A certain .37 on enrollment covers the maximum private loss .36. The buyer's .59332 net gain before this transfer covers the payment. Thus it can obtain every original type's consent to the higher rate of actual installation. An unneeded second test on a routine job would have the opposite effect on the chosen two-reader assessment. An inexpensive ordinary bond or a binding local controller that works on this valuable batch is a real competitor if either party has the necessary means.

**Scope.** Standard specimens, independent preparation, enough work to test them, and authority to assign unmarked specimens are plausible capabilities in some commissioning environments. An external standard of a *terminal ordering* that behaves just like the live installation is considerably more demanding. This proof establishes the clerks' incentives conditional on such standards, and the firms' willingness to pay the stated bills. It leaves open who can initially establish a credible standard for a novel goal. A shared visible feature, a manufacturer identifying its own live samples, or a purchaser with rights to alter the standard labels alters the result.

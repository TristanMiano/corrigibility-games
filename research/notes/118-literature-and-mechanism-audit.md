## Research spillovers

Claude d'Aspremont and Alexis Jacquemin, *Cooperative and Noncooperative R&D
in Duopoly with Spillovers*, AER 78(5), 1133–1137 (1988):
[author-hosted nine-page reprint](https://www.di.ens.fr/~aspremon/Claude/PDFs/dAsp88a.pdf).
Read the reprint, particularly section 1, reprint pp. 2–5, and the welfare
discussion. It separates cooperation in research from cooperation in output
and uses linear demand, research-dependent unit costs and quadratic research
expense. In its interior comparison, spillovers above one half make research
cooperation increase research and subsequent competitive output. This is a
close ordinary-cooperation comparison, not a utility-modification result.
Its profit/consumer-welfare setting differs from exclusive color counts.

Also inspected the complete two-page
[1990 erratum, AER 80, 641–642](https://www.di.ens.fr/~aspremon/Claude/PDFs/dAsp90b.pdf).
It corrects an inequality and parts of the small-spillover ranking, and
discusses how research type, products and organization can alter spillovers.
The 1988 welfare ranking is therefore not imported wholesale. R0006's own
production and adoption formulas require their direct proofs.

Access: the web fetch of the 1988 PDF timed out; the same public author
copy downloaded successfully with PowerShell and was extracted locally.
It remains a temporary research copy, not repository redistribution.

## Strategic delegation

Chaim Fershtman and Kenneth L. Judd, *Equilibrium Incentives in Oligopoly*,
AER 77(5), 927–940 (1987):
[author-hosted article](https://www.tau.ac.il/~fersht/Papers/1987%20Equilibrium%20Incentives%20in%20Oligopoly.pdf).
Inspected the introduction and section I, pp. 927–931. Owners choose
managerial incentives before managers interact with knowledge of those
incentives. The basic model explains why changing a controller's objective
can advance its principal's unchanged objective through competitors'
responses. It explicitly restricts the contract class and discusses its
information assumptions. That supports treating a matched delegate as a
substantive comparison to genuine change. It does not establish identical
cost or availability for arbitrary goal-editing technology.

Later inspection used [Judd's article copy](https://kenjudd.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/1987-Equilibrium-Incentives-in-Oligopoly.pdf):
pp. 933–935 on alternative contracts and random costs, and section V's
equations were read, without a full theorem audit. The complete
[2006 corrigendum, p. 1367](https://kenjudd.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/2006-30034348.pdf)
was obtained and read. It corrects equation (24) and adds a condition to
the differentiated-product result. No 1987 numerical theorem is imported.

## Instrumentally chosen and evolutionarily selected altruism

The nearest existing lead is L28: Julio J. Rotemberg, *Human Relations in
the Workplace*, JPE 102(4), 684–717 (1994),
[publisher abstract](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/261951).
The abstract explicitly studies individually rational altruism when workers'
choices are strategically linked. The
[MIT 1992 working-paper record](https://dspace.mit.edu/entities/publication/75aa3e68-c11e-4eed-9289-1503619584d1)
was rechecked; its download returned a rate-limit/firewall response.
No full-text theorem audit or equivalence of versions is claimed. R0006's
local response formula is an independently proved application of a known
kind of mechanism, not a claimed discovery of instrumental altruism.

Helmut Bester and Werner Güth, *Is Altruism Evolutionarily Stable?*, JEBO
34(2), 193–209 (1998),
[publisher page and introduction](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167268197000607),
were screened at abstract/introduction level. The authors distinguish
evolutionary selection of preferences from Rotemberg's deliberate initial
choice and connect outcomes to complements versus substitutes. This is a
close comparison for the sign distinction in note 116. The 1994 CentER
working-paper metadata were verified, but repository retrieval yielded an
HTML shell, not the paper. The publication's model equations and proofs
were not audited. R0006 makes an initial current-goal adoption claim in its
specified game, not an evolutionary-stability or prevalence claim.

Friedel Bolle, *Is Altruism Evolutionarily Stable? And Envy and Malevolence?
Remarks on Bester and Güth*, JEBO 42(1), 131–133 (2000),
[publisher abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167268100000792),
was screened only. It identifies the relevance of negative other-regarding
weights. That motivates checking the beta>=0 restriction rather than
treating retention as an unrestricted optimal-goal conclusion. No result
from its inaccessible proof text is used.

## Coalition participation and physical boundaries

Scott Barrett (1994), *Self-Enforcing International Environmental Agreements*,
Oxford Economic Papers 46, supplement, 878–894:
[publisher record](https://academic.oup.com/oep/article-abstract/46/Supplement_1/878/2568546).
Metadata and abstract were screened; the original full text was not obtained.
Its results are not treated as independently audited here.

Santiago J. Rubio and Alistair Ulph, *Self-Enforcing International Environmental
Agreements Revisited*, [IVIE WP-AD 2004-23](https://www.ivie.es/downloads/docs/wpasad/wpasad-2004-23.pdf),
June 2004, 27 pages. Read sections 1–3 through the displayed stability
results, pp. 3–17; inspected Definition 1, the constrained emissions game,
and Appendix F's Proposition 3 argument, p. 24. Earlier parameter-region
appendices were not fully audited. This is the June working paper, not an
assertion of equivalence to the [2006 publication](https://doi.org/10.1093/oep/gpl002).
Membership stability compares joining with the actual remaining coalition.
Its grand-coalition argument uses a bounded emissions response and coalition
leadership. The coalition's joint objective is an implementation premise,
not a demonstrated genuine preference installation. The comparison prompts
checking both boundary constraints and response curvature in R0006's own
public-research class; its results cannot be copied between the technologies.
Web retrieval timed out; the public institutional PDF downloaded and was
extracted locally. No source PDF is redistributed in the repository.

## Consequence for this run's contribution and checks

Heifetz, Shannon and Spiegel, *What to Maximize if You Must*,
[author manuscript, July 2004](https://www.tau.ac.il/~spiegel/papers/what36c.pdf),
33 pages: read section 2.1 (pp. 4–5), Theorem 1's explanation and scope
remarks (pp. 10–12), section 3 (pp. 15–16), and selected appendix equations.
The full transversality and evolutionary proofs were not audited. This
actual PDF's title/version differs from an aggregator's linked description;
equivalence to the 2007 JET publication is not assumed. It supplies a close
general strategic-response precedent and explicitly treats signed biases,
delegation, boundary equilibria and a bounded-noise example. Our local
production proofs are applications; actual current-goal installation,
funded audits and consensual joint gains require their separate arguments.

The useful contribution sought here is the integrated conditional account:
own-color physical production, separate kinds of instrumental overlap,
actual goal-choice incentives and expenses, ordinary controls, and retained
capture options. Conventional machinery is appropriate. Its value does not
depend on being first to show that credible altruism can serve prior goals.
Those comparisons motivated checks of negative weight menus (122), current-goal
renegotiation (126/136), feasible interventions (120/129/135) and jointly changing
captureability (121). Constructive arrangements and limiting cases receive the
same implementation and evidence standard. Source access limitations remain
visible in the ledger and findings report.

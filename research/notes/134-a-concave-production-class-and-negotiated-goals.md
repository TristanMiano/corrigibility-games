# A concave production class and finite negotiated goals

R0006 mathematical consolidation. This connects productive opportunities
to a common current-goal continuation over a class broader than the exact
quadratics. Initial adoption and installation remain a distinct final link,
with the concrete interfaces of 128 and 131 as conditional examples. The
convex-analysis machinery is conventional; the useful contribution is the
explicit physical, cost, current-goal and implementation connection.

## 1. A direction that improves every original color

Let n>=2 agents choose scalar productive actions in a compact product of
intervals X. Original i maximizes its own physical final count q_i^0(x).
The functions include work consumed, material limits and the chosen finite
production horizon; utilities do not fund the input expenses. Suppose an
original continuation x^F is interior with

`partial_i q_i^0(x^F)=0`.

Choose a feasible direction v and epsilon>0 such that the segment from
x^F to x^F+epsilon v lies in X. Define

`d_i=grad q_i^0(x^F) dot v`.

If d_i>0 for every i and the absolute second derivative along that segment
is bounded by B_i, Taylor's formula proves

`q_i^0(x^F+epsilon v)-q_i^0(x^F)`
`  >= epsilon d_i-(B_i/2)epsilon^2`.                     (1)

Let actual goal-route installation expense be K_i, and let the relevant
outside value r_i exceed this original continuation by o_i>=0. Then

`K_i+o_i < epsilon d_i-(B_i/2)epsilon^2` for every i      (2)

is a sufficient strict participation *opportunity* condition. It includes
the outside-option premium rather than equating retained utility with an
unnecessarily poor policy. The r_i must come from the actual disagreement
or alternative game; incompatible imaginary best responses are not an
executable joint outside continuation.

Positive cross-output benefits and v=(1,...,1) give d_i>0 when each agent
receives at least one useful spillover. Mutually harmful congestion can
instead favor a negative direction. For two agents, if both cross-output
derivatives are nonzero, choosing v_B with the sign of q_R,B and v_R
with the sign of q_B,R gives positive d_B and d_R, since their original
own-action derivatives vanish. Thus either increasing beneficial effort
or reducing interference can produce the local mutual opportunity.

This is not a theorem that every asymmetric one-way spillover pays both
parties. A zero d_i requires a higher-order calculation, a transfer, a
different direction or a new productive link. A large actual outside value
or installation expense can also exceed (1). These are precise limits of
this local sufficient condition, with the richer arrangements still to test.

## 2. Joint concavity supplies a supporting common objective

Now assume each post-cost count `q_i(x)=q_i^0(x)-K_i` is continuous and
jointly concave on X, with finite nonnegative counts. Define the downward
complete physical output set

`S={y>=0: there is x in X with y_i<=q_i(x) for all i}`.

The ability to discard output makes downward completion physically
available; if discarding consumes work or creates a valued physical state,
use the actual set instead. S is compact and convex. For convexity, combine
two feasible action profiles and use each q_i's concavity to dominate the
same convex combination of their output vectors. No randomization or
Jensen substitution between expected utility and utility of means is used.

Suppose (2), or another verified construction, supplies y in S strictly
above r in every coordinate. Maximizing the optimization criterion

`sum_i log(y_i-r_i)` over y in S with y_i>r_i             (3)

gives some y*=q*>r. This auxiliary optimization has a maximizer: S is
bounded, and approaching a zero gain sends (3) to minus infinity while a
strictly feasible point has finite value. Monotonicity ensures q* is an
actual output vector, not artificial disposal of a larger feasible one.

Set `w_i=1/(q_i*-r_i)>0`. For any y in S, a sufficiently short segment
from q* toward y remains strictly above r. Its one-sided derivative in
(3) cannot be positive. Therefore

`sum_i w_i y_i <= sum_i w_i q_i*` for every y in S.       (4)

Thus a finite positive weighted sum supports the mutually beneficial
point over the *entire* physical output set, including points below some
outside value. The logarithm is only a device for finding weights. It is
not installed as a utility, and does not assign an infinite punishment to
any physically available action or to combat.

Joint concavity holds for additive public knowledge with convex own
research expense, and for the shared concave process of note 132. The
bilinear per-agent functions of note 116 generally fail this assumption;
there the explicit symmetric weighted maximum supplies (4) directly. The
supporting-point condition is the transferable part, not an assertion that
all physical production sets are convex.

## 3. A finite smooth goal selects the negotiated output

For each i choose a finite D_i bounding |y_i-q_i*| on S and choose
eta_i>0 with `2 eta_i D_i<w_i` whenever D_i>0. Define

`G(y)=sum_i w_i y_i-sum_i eta_i(y_i-q_i*)^2`.             (5)

Its partial derivatives are positive throughout the declared output box,
and it is concave as a function of y. By (4),

`G(y)<=sum_i w_i q_i*`, with equality exactly at y=q*.    (6)

Every originally acceptable q* therefore has a finite common criterion
with that unique output maximum. It values actual final colors and the
negotiated balance, not retention of source code. Its expected-utility
evaluation is E[G(y)], including the variability penalty; it is not G(E y).

The design is genuinely different from every original i under the stated
downward-completion premise. Lower a positive other-color coordinate of q*
while holding q_i fixed. Original i is indifferent, whereas the strictly
increasing G is strictly worse. These different indifference classes rule
out a positive affine recoding. If an alternative also gives i more of its
own color than q_i*, it supplies a strict preference reversal: original i
prefers it, while G strictly prefers q*. If independent disposal/finishing
is unavailable, test the actual feasible lottery ordering instead of
inferring change from a coefficient. Fixed-total affine equivalences are
a relevant boundary.

Because (5) is concave and increasing in every count, its composition with
the jointly concave q_i(x) is concave on X. If this composition is
differentiable at every candidate effort equilibrium, each coordinatewise
best-response profile is a joint maximum: add its individual first-order
variational inequalities. Equations (4)–(6) then imply that **every such
common-goal effort equilibrium produces q***. Effort allocations can
remain nonunique when they produce the same actual counts.

Differentiability here matters. The essential-process zero equilibrium
in note 132 survives despite joint concavity at a nonsmooth boundary.
Another sufficient implementation is sequential, observable, durable
actions with the common criterion: backward induction maximizes the same
G at every node, so the entire sequence attains its global feasible maximum.
That move-order intervention must include its physical delay and compare
ordinary agents under the same timing.

## 4. Current-goal stability, conflict and actual adoption

Once the common maximum is attained by an available continuation, an acting
G-agent cannot improve by installing a different future goal whose induced
outcome lottery stays in S: (6) holds pointwise. No descendant is silently
forced to maximize its founder's q_i. If attack instead has a different
physical feasible set, verify its weighted/output bound explicitly. A
strictly smaller attainable value of G makes attack strictly worse; an
unverified assumption that all combat outputs belong to S is insufficient.
Note 115 gives a finite destructive-combat embedding, and note 129 checks
remaining delivery incentives for one execution technology.

Equations (1)–(6) establish a funded beneficial target and a stable common
productive continuation. They do not alone show that the originals choose
the first installation. Concrete links available in this run include:

* Note 131: in the quadratic response class, the common gain exceeds the
  best unilateral production commitment by an exact positive margin. A
  correctly audited reciprocal interface makes common installation an
  equilibrium against that broad unilateral controller menu.
* Note 128: a protected physical entry window and a chosen current-goal
  reservation bound the first installer's original output against richer
  counteroffers, with explicit approximation and implementation slack.
* Notes 126 and 130: a specified multi-party conditional consent interface
  supports broader changes through either existing partner concern or a
  sufficiently responsive research technology, with selection limits.

An ordinary commitment or delegate that reaches q* with the same authority
and expense remains a substantive co-optimal route. A cheaper available
ordinary route can win. Merely announcing q* while each agent retains a
profitable later deviation is a weaker device. Likewise a first goal with
unprotected exposure does not inherit the continuation theorem's guarantee.

## 5. A concrete asymmetric negotiated target

For three colors take
`q_i=20+x_1+x_2+x_3-x_i^2/2-1/10`, with x_i in [0,5].
The 1/10 is each actual installed-goal expense; the original no-update
benchmark is 20+5/2. Every output is nonnegative on the domain. With
weights `(1,11/10,9/10)`, the unique weighted maximum is

`x*=(3,30/11,10/3)`.

Indeed the total weight on the linear sum is three, so the first-order
condition is `3-w_i x_i=0`; the Hessian is the negative diagonal of w.
All three original gains are positive after update expense. The smallest,
for the third color, is exactly 448/495. Thus a common negotiated criterion
can support asymmetric positive weights and unequal output gains, with
ordinary outside production explicitly included.

Set each eta_i=1/1000 in (5), with q* the actual outputs of that effort.
The finite count range gives 2 eta_i D_i<min w_i. Composition is concave
and smooth, and every common-goal effort equilibrium has the stated output.
Reducing any one own effort slightly at x* raises that original's count
(x_i*>1), while lowering G. This verifies strict preference reversals for
all three. Initial staged adoption still needs the interface and outside
comparisons in section 4; the calculation is not a population claim.

## 6. What is robust and what is designed

Strict inequalities in (2) and positive curvature/response margins give
open productive regions. Small perturbations preserve those inequalities
when the physical functions, costs and actual outside values vary
continuously; equilibrium selection or a newly available outside option
can instead jump. The target and weights may need renegotiation after a
technology change. This is not automatic robustness of a fixed old goal
to every future innovation.

Exclusive original values, convex research expense and usable spillovers
are independently motivated ingredients. Exact target selection, reliable
recognition, conditional entry and effective-controller audits are chosen
arrangements with stronger implementation requirements. Their information,
funding and incentives are assessed separately in the cited constructions.
The class result supplies an integrated conditional explanation of why
shared means can support genuine preference accommodation, while preserving
the ordinary-control successes and the precise remaining adoption limits.

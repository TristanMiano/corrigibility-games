# 284 — Who trusts another district to make an irreversible public choice?

A different environment has no seller keeping a buyer's merchandise. Two districts must choose which one a common water-control project protects on a particular occasion. They disagree about whose welfare should govern the decision. A representative with a genuinely common goal can protect the wrong place because it misjudges the storm. The distinction between values and forecasting ability survives even when weather and actual decisions are eventually public.

## Authority, installation and forecast skill

One district will have high need, the other low, equally likely either way. Protecting the high-need district saves H=10 units of its output; protecting the low-need district saves L=2. Only one can be served by the shared works at the peak. Each district initially values only its own output. Its own separate infrastructure yields a certain outside value of 3.5. A one-use external attempt costs .20 for each representative asked to change, with a witnessed procedure and known success q_i. A real change gives the representative terminal value equal to the *sum* of both districts' saved output. Retained representatives continue to care only about their own district. Before the state or the installed ordering is known, each founder has a veto. All quoted peaceful outputs are increments above five units of existing local stock per district. A disruptive contest over the intake floods those stores and lets a strength-dependent winner recover at most three usable units before the hazard passes. Even the district receiving no additional protection keeps its five units by letting the project operate. This also covers a district that knows the other operator will favor itself. At a live decision the designated operator can complete the routing before the other district can reverse the physical event; the operator can also interrupt and face the destructive outside choice. In the examples its informed expected preferred route beats that option. After water passes the operating window ends. A location with a strong separate reservoir would have a different veto payoff.

The designated operator sees a private report of which district has high need. Its accuracy r_i>.5 is symmetric. The changed representative strictly follows it, because this maximizes expected protected output. An unchanged one strictly protects its own district, even when the forecast favors the other. Let M=(H+L)/2=6 and S_i=[r_i H+(1−r_i)L]/2. A selfish operator saves M for itself on average and zero for the other. A genuinely changed operator saves S_i for *each* founder on average. No agent obtains an additional reward for appearing impartial on this terminal job.

With probability α the north holds the authority to act, otherwise the south does. Installation and assignment are independent of each other and of need in this example. The original expected payoffs, before deducting each party's actual expenses, are

    V_N = α[(1−q_N)M + q_N S_N] + (1−α)q_S S_S,
    V_S = (1−α)[(1−q_S)M + q_S S_S] + αq_N S_N.                   (284.1)

Here q_i=0 with no attempt when the contract leaves that participant's goal unchanged. Its signature and its utility modification are distinct decisions. A known bad forecast under a good goal remains possible. All quantities in the next comparisons are original local outputs after agreed expenses.

With q_N=q_S=.75 and r_N=r_S=.8, S=4.2. After both edit bills, each party gets 2.95 plus 1.5 times its own chance of being decisive. Both strictly prefer the shared, uncertain operation over 3.5 exactly when **11/30<α<19/30**. At a fair rotation they get 3.70 each. They consent even though only 9/16 of pairs end with *both* representatives actually changed, and even a genuinely common decision sends protection to the wrong district one fifth of the time. The credible gate requires the witnessed procedures named in a contract before the districts commit their common infrastructure. If a bare claim bought exactly the same vote and the same partner effort, either original founder would instead gain k_i plus its appointment probability times q_i(M−S_i) by omitting its own attempt. Under two unchanged representatives a fair rotation gives three each and both would prefer their separate infrastructure.

## What does a wrong public decision say about the operator?

At equal reliabilities a correct decision occurs with probability .75×.8+.25×.5=.725. Among incorrect decisions **6 in 11** came from a representative who genuinely adopted the joint goal and believed the wrong forecast. The other 5 in 11 came from a retained representative protecting its district during a storm centered elsewhere. Every promised commissioning procedure in this calculation really occurred. A board that calls every incorrect decision deception would misclassify both groups.

If an observer knows only aggregate correctness A, its measurement is A=.5+q(r−.5) when q and r are common across appointments. For A=.725 and r somewhere from .75 to .95, any q between .50 and .90 fits exactly. There is another observable that can resolve this ambiguity under the stated incentives. A decisive operator choosing to protect the *other* district proves it has the common goal in the declared two-goal class. Over representative jobs that choice has frequency q/2 irrespective of r by symmetry. If the board records the actual operator and decision as well as the realized need, it can identify q and, for q>0, r. A single home-favoring vote still has both explanations. A cheap advisory vote, informative political rewards after a vote, asymmetric forecasting errors, or an institution already entitled to command the operator invalidate that diagnostic interpretation. A binding impartial decision by an unchanged delegate can be excellent public policy without establishing any change to its principal's terminal ranking.

## Could a powerful district deliberately make itself more trustworthy?

At α=.80 the first bilateral recipe gives the north 4.15 and the south 3.25. The south vetoes. At least three practical renegotiations are available under different rights.

- The northern district can cede appointments to give each side half. Both would then get 3.70 from the bilateral attempt if the needed local forecast and routing authority are accessible in either district.
- Keep α=.80 and ask **only the north** to attempt a change. The two obtain 3.52 and 3.72. More generally the one-change design beats both outside stocks for 3.70/4.65<α<2.50/2.85. The south can willingly join while keeping its own preferred ordering.
- Suppose the north can pay a further .20 for commissioning equipment raising its actual reliability to .90. With the old .80 appointment probability and both attempting, the two get 3.734 and 3.754. Both now beat the fair rotation at its original skill and the available single-change arrangement at the original allocation. The strong founder buys a device that makes it give up *more* of its favored unilateral choices when it acts. Its payoff compared with the unsuccessful proposal is lower conditional on receiving the project, but its weaker partner now actually accepts.

If the south can authorize the upgraded north to act every time, the south no longer needs to pay for its own change. The resulting original returns are 3.98 and 3.78. Both prefer these terms to the upgraded partial rotation. One true consensually negotiated change can supply the public service both want. For the entirely ordinary alternative, an independently binding router reading the same .80-accurate forecasts always follows them and yields 4.20 per founder before its expense. At .20 each it beats all the named packages for both founders. A remotely binding charter cannot use a local operator's private forecast at that reliability unless the agents can actually transmit or verify it.

**Naturality and reach.** Unequal command of a shared reservoir, local forecasts and a hard choice between districts can arise without strategic design. Private valuations of particular local stocks give both founders a reason to seek a joint objective ex ante. Concern for both local outputs is a simple negotiated goal. A uniform chance of need, equal capacities, a trustworthy but fallible edit at the posted price, symmetry of private forecast errors and lack of another reputational audience are stronger premises. The ability to rotate command or transfer an appointment requires a real operating right. More severe material loss on a wrong allocation, cheaper ordinary rules, or reliable public warnings can shift which agreement serves both original interests. This example concerns one consequential representative choice per project and a measurable state. It does not identify preferences from the public ballots of actual institutions.

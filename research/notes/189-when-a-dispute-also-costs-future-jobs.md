# When a dispute also costs future jobs

**E8, note 189.** Giving up one current job can save a productive relationship. When a dispute can also damage future business, a shorter relationship can sustain peace than in 183. A founder can have enough to gain to pay for longer ordinary service even when it could have chosen genuine accommodation with its partners. The distinction is between preserving opportunities after successful work and preserving them after a dispute.

## Two different continuations

Write p for the chance of another opportunity after intact service and t for its chance after a dispute, with 0≤t≤p<1. Either realized outcome terminates the current batch. Every surviving date starts with the same physical capabilities, authority over its own release, and fresh stochastically resolved contest as in 183. The remaining risk is that a damaged production line, repair delay or lost work window cancels the next usable opportunity entirely. This is an independently plausible alternative to immediate recovery. We keep each party's salvage q=y/n, initiative expense c and intact yield x fixed. More elaborate repairs can affect these quantities jointly. A permanently absorbed state offers no further suitable job. Finite cost and physical initiative remain at each live date.

Each retained original can secure at least B=d/(1-t), where d=q-c>0, by initiating if its turn arrives, including as a named winner if necessary. If someone else initiates first its immediate return is higher. A stable all-peace continuation gives the group exactly S=x/(1-p) expected total production. At a certain peaceful assignment with k unchanged sites, at least k-1 receive nothing now. Their next-date payoffs after peace must each be at least B/p, while the remaining original also has a security value B on that date. Thus

    p(1-t)x ≥ (k-1+p)(1-p)d.                                           (1)

This is exact for the early role lottery. In 183's exclusion schedule among k originals let W=pG denote an ineligible original's value and G the eligible value. Then W+(k-1)G=S. A claimant that passes obtains pG and if it initiates obtains d+tW. This comparison gives exactly (1). A designated beneficiary gets x and its next opportunity after successful work is at least as likely as after its own initiative. Changed members value intact group output and its future, so they strictly pass. For k=1 simply serve the retained original every time, irrespective of (1) when p=0. For k≥2 this also covers p=0 by the unsimplified inequality. Weak incentive boundaries permit selection of stand-down. Positive installation costs and a vote require a separate pre-change comparison. A no-cash fully peaceful schedule with no revisions uses k=n. For a fixed p>0, lower t weakly expands its region. For a fixed t, higher p expands it strictly over positive interior thresholds.

If the *named* changed founders require some of the actual service, all k originals are unserved on a date when the agreed payment is delivered. Necessity now gives pS≥kB. This is also sufficient for implementing positive service at weak incentive thresholds under the conditions of 187. Normalize by 1-p, and replace d in its promised payment floor by b=(1-p)d/(1-t). At a state paying a changed founder, choose each original's value z≥b so that z≤px/k. On deviation the same state recurs with chance t. The original compares z with (1-p)d+tz and passes. The completion transition probability z(1-p)/[p(x/k-z)] still implements z. The original-only pool's own exclusion condition follows from pS≥kB. The other condition in 187.1 becomes k max(o,b)+Σ b_j≤x; b_j continues to denote the actual normalized reservation plus setup of each changed signer. The construction mixes the changed-recipient state and the original-only pool to meet the selected division. At equality in the resource bound individual founders can be indifferent. For all-changed founders use their actual original reservations and the fair schedule directly.

## What survives a coordinated refusal?

For a fixed group of m sites the normalized security value is now b_m=(1-p)(mq-c)/(1-t). In 185's necessity proof, replace δ everywhere by p and the group's per-date floor d_m by b_m. Future promises on every successful history still have the same Bellman decomposition with factor p. On a dispute the attaining queue repeats its state with chance t, so the deviation inequality is exactly v(S)≥(1-p)d_m+t v(S), or v(S)≥b_m. The same finite queue consequently attains the transformed test, including all intermediate-size groups. Equivalently, at each fixed p,t run 185's homogeneous test for parameters p, x, q'=(1-p)q/(1-t), c'=(1-p)c/(1-t). The transformed parameters are an accounting of the lost opportunities. The actual current contest still has salvage q and cost c. In the full-service region common-renewal monotonicity extends to bigger p with t fixed by letting the operator ignore extra future priority in its construction or by the monotonic expansion of the fixed-p feasible set (lower b_m and greater effective patience). At p=t these results reproduce the original benchmarks.

## A founder that buys continuity

Use the three-site example x=0.70, y=0.60, c=0.04. Initially p=t=0.20. Any individual can guarantee B=0.20 unnormalized, and sharing the role of initiator fairly in an ordinary disagreement gives each founder R=(0.60-0.04)/(3·0.80)=7/30. Let that symmetric plan be the agreed, feasible fallback if a new offer is declined. A proposal can include different timing, insurance, changes or favorable priority. If the two other original founders receive at least R, the most one founder could ever receive *without improving the physical process* is 0.70/0.80-2R=49/120≈0.4083. This even forgives every setup expense and every loss from a dispute. All three could in particular choose to revise for 0.02 each and obtain 0.70/(3·0.80)-0.02≈0.2717.

A alone has the option to buy a maintenance agreement before the first job. For 0.012 units of its own useful work it raises p to 0.45 while t stays 0.20. The vendor can reserve replacement capacity for routinely completed work; restoration of disputed equipment continues to use the prior recovery process. This describes specific, finite engineering capabilities and a quoted cost. A makes a conditional offer with a public fair initial role, a subscribed exclusion docket and no individual change. All three will honor it by (1). Each now receives 0.70/(3·0.55)=14/33 before the invoice. Even A's net of about 0.4122 exceeds the maximum any one founder could obtain from the old opportunity while respecting the other reservations. B and C strictly accept as well. This establishes actual purchase in the stated proposal window, including against all acceptable old-process counteroffers. Renegotiation after irreversible construction, or a partner that can credibly secure the new capacity without paying for it, changes that comparison. The project does not estimate how often any vendor can supply this particular improvement. Each strict inequality persists on a neighborhood of the quoted parameters.

**Exploration ledger.** The framing candidate is that the same physical loss can affect both the present prize and the value of keeping a relationship. The structural candidate prices the next *usable* job conditional on how this one ends. A common renewal probability misses that distinction. A sibling uses repair delay instead of outright loss. A higher-level successor lets agents buy recovery and assign control over the repaired machine, then measures actual change and ordinary cooperation under both offers.

# What do tested settings say about where the work will happen?

Two plants can agree about what a controller values in the conditions they tested and disagree about what it will do on the next shipment. The gap depends on how much its preferences can vary across settings, how often the untested settings occur, and who can steer work toward them.

## A sharp lower bound between physical reference points

In context x the current evaluator's normalized expected-utility gain from sending rather than keeping one batch is v(x). The agreed evaluator favors sending across a named physical domain. The buyer has independently grounded reference lotteries giving intervals [l_i,u_i] for v at points x_i, with a feasible joint assignment. Assume v itself is L-Lipschitz in a known physical distance d. The smallest and largest possible gains at x are

    v_low(x)  = max_i [l_i−L d(x,x_i)],
    v_high(x) = min_i [u_i+L d(x,x_i)].

The Lipschitz inequalities give the bounds. Each displayed function is L-Lipschitz and satisfies *all* the sample intervals: feasibility places the lower construction below every upper endpoint and the upper construction above every lower endpoint. Each thus attains its bounds simultaneously. A positive lower bound certifies the sending decision, including at untested points. Equal current decisions at all points would still leave open cardinal lottery comparisons beyond these margins.

With adverse tie breaking, the risky region is U={x:v_low(x)≤0}. If contexts are sampled from a known law μ, the least possible service rate over this controller class is exactly 1−μ(U). If a party can route every order to any point it chooses for free, the worst service rate is zero as soon as U is nonempty. A more physical intermediate case starts at context z and charges a relocation cost d(z,x) to shift that batch. With an average resource budget b and divisible batches, the maximum fraction moved into a closed U is the solution of

    maximize ∫ a(z) dμ(z),   0≤a(z)≤1,   ∫ a(z) dist(z,U) dμ(z) ≤ b.

Take a=1 at points already in U. Reaching U costs at least the distance to it. Moving the cheapest available fractions first to their closest risky locations attains the bound on a finite domain or the compact interval below. The same low function makes all such destinations unhelpful. This is a statement about possible relocation. An actor must still benefit from paying for it.

## A rival can buy favorable work conditions

Let x be a normalized input grade on [0,1], initially uniform. Two witnessed values at .25 and .75 are both .20; L=1. The adverse U is [0,.05] ∪ [.45,.55] ∪ [.95,1], total mass .20. The two safe intervals each have length .4. To move an extra fraction m into U, the cheapest strips are next to their four boundaries. Their combined width up to distance t is 4t, so the minimum cost for m is m²/8 for 0≤m≤.8. The risky fraction can therefore rise to .2+min(.8,sqrt(8b)).

For instance a third firm gains .1 of its own valuable byproduct on a retained order and controls the available blending operation. If the realized controller is v_low, it privately pays to shift the jobs within distance .1 of U. This moves .40 additional mass, costs .02 on average, and earns the firm another .02 net. It only spends on a buyer's actual launched order. It would gain nothing by adjusting grades against the constant agreed v=.2. An inspection of two reference batches that justified 80% service under the original grade distribution now permits only **40%** in the bad realization. A better ability to choose production conditions has changed the outcome without changing either witnessed choice.

Suppose a witnessed genuine attempt yields the flat agreed controller or the partial controller v_low with equal chances. Both are reached from an original that keeps at every grade. A buyer whose gain is two on service and loss two otherwise still earns .5×2+.5×(−.4)=.8 by releasing **without learning which goal it got**, after anticipating the third firm's response. If it can test x=.5, the two candidate margins there are .2 and −.05. With this *two-member* catalog the test permits release only to the flat controller for a gross gain of one. It therefore pays up to .2 for the additional diagnostic. At a price of .15 B buys it and the rival never sees a profitable order on the selected path. If A earns at least one unit of its originally valued own stock from a released order even while providing service, its initial .2 attempt charge is affordable even when only the flat half receive an order. A retired original without a verified procedure gets no order. At any pre-conversion checkpoint a safe reserve dominating the recoverable fraction of a disrupted mixture can make return preferable to a physical contest. Recoverable strength increases the actual disagreement value and should enter these margins.

The grade-specific preferences can be realized by an additive utility over own stock and customer stock delivered in each named grade, normalizing a unit of own stock and letting the customer's weight vary with grade. A one-unit own sacrifice and weight 1+v(x) give the stipulated service margin. The two examples are thus different preferences over physical outcomes, though both move away from the original. More generally the single check at .5 cannot certify all Lipschitz controllers. If the five values at 0,.25,.5,.75 and 1 are all .2, the domain is covered with distance at most .125 and every compatible controller serves throughout. A bad reading has to be acted upon, and purchasing the checks does not ensure they will pass. A binding ordinary mixer or local router operating on the same grades can also protect output.

**Naturality and reach.** Material qualities, operating ranges and distances measured by blending work can supply a physical context and metric. A bounded change in marginal willingness to process nearby grades needs independent justification for the active goal. A rival with control of blending and valuable byproducts can deliberately select the less favorable contexts after inspection; a customer controlling the process could instead route away from them. Open-ended new products and abrupt discontinuities in values lie beyond this smooth-domain certificate. A sibling buys control of the routing rather than more observations. The broader question asks which side chooses the distribution on which a negotiated goal will be exercised.

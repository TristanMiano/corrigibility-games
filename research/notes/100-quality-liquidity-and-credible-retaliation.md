# Quality, liquidity and credible retaliation

**R0005 analytical audit and repaired witness.** The physical
accounts are 098's, with explicitly stated parameter changes. This note
tests two enlargements that a full-service adoption claim must include:
partial/stochastic production and the customer's own incentive to attack.
It then identifies an open region where a genuine contractual goal can
enable productive agreement. The customer has fixed own-output preferences
in this local family; adding cheap customer threat-goal installation or
binding hostile delegation is a consequential further extension.

## 1. Full service can be displaced by a cheaper partial service promise

Allow effort e in [0,1] to cost the supplier c e and produce customer
benefit b e. Equivalently, under a separately implemented public random
device these can be expected quantities for an indivisible batch. A
deterministic partial-service technology and a state-contingent lottery
require different execution certificates; expected arithmetic alone
does not supply either.

Let L=R_j-h_j-a_j be the customer's transferable payment limit and
let d_j be its genuinely available disagreement holdings. With goal
installation cost kappa, the supplier chooses e,p subject to

    0<=e<=1, 0<=p<=L,
    R_j-h_j-p+b e >= d_j.                             (1)

Its terminal holdings are R_i-h_i-kappa+p-c e. At a given effort the
largest feasible price is min(L,R_j-h_j+b e-d_j). In a region where
participation binds, increasing effort raises the supplier's return
at slope b-c>0. Once liquidity binds, the price stays fixed and extra
effort lowers that return at slope -c. Subject to nonnegative feasible
prices, the optimum is therefore at

    e*=clip((d_j-a_j)/b,0,1).                         (2)

Relative to 098's ordinary-division endpoint P_O, the maximum extra
supplier return is

    (b-c) min(1,(d_j-a_j)_+/b)-kappa.                 (3)

This comparison improves on requiring full service when payment capacity
binds. It is an optimum in this production/payment class, not yet an
implemented outcome: effort incentives, later attack and refunds remain
to be checked. Initial participation at e* can be an indifference.

For 098's original numerical fixture, e*=13/20, p=5/2 would give the
supplier 57/20 and the customer its disagreement holding 18/5 in
expectation, compared with 5/2 to the supplier under full service.
That apparent improvement cannot be accepted without the next check.

## 2. A customer's retaliation is an ordinary enforcement mechanism

If an advance leaves a customer with only its preloaded a_j while a
successful raid would yield a greater expected holding, it may rationally
attack after default. An F supplier anticipating that response can prefer
service or a refund even without changing utility. This ordinary control
must be counted rather than treating all fixed-goal promises as empty.

In the earlier low-supplier-strength fixture, the customer wins with
probability 3/5. After a prepaid no-service branch, the maximum remaining
cash prize can be 5/2, so its expected capture is 3/2, above its remaining
unit. A no-refund quiet failure is therefore not sequentially rational
for that customer. Refunding at least 1/2 can remove this incentive at
the stated stock state; strict peace needs slack or a stated indifference
selection. In an F implementation, anticipated retaliation can also
change whether the supplier serves at all. The original fixture remains
a funded mutually preferred *opportunity*; it is not by itself a proof
that genuine change beats every ordinary continuation.

The constructive counterpart is to identify a setting where this
retaliatory incentive is genuinely absent, then retain that very same
attack option when comparing F with a changed supplier.

## 3. A region with physically available but unattractive retaliation

Use the o=0 specialization: the raw service batch has no alternative
productive use during the cycle. All earlier dated output remains in
the agent's welfare account. Following negotiation expenses, any later
cash prize is at most

    Q_max=R_i+R_j-h-a_i-a_j.                          (4)

This bound generously permits complete recovery and ignores further
setup/service expenses. If

    (1-p_i) Q_max < a_j,                              (5)

the customer strictly prefers retaining its preloaded holding to a raid,
even at the smallest funded cash balance. Any already delivered dated
service is common to its attack and wait comparison. More retained cash
or a smaller actual recovery prize strengthens (5). Neither attack nor
the apparatus is removed.

For an F supplier, when (5) holds and the service is non-excludable once
performed, an advance followed by freely chosen costly effort supplies
no incentive to exert that effort: keeping the advance saves c e and
does not provoke profitable customer retaliation. A binding ordinary
controller, atomic delivery/payment device, recoverable collateral,
reputational continuation or a different feasible service timing can
repair this. Their availability and cost belong in the comparison.
This local result concerns the stated last-performance subgame.

## 4. An exact repaired parameter point

Retain R_i=2,R_j=8,a_i=a_j=1 and initial combat recovery prize Y=6.
Set the supplier's victory probability to 3/4. Thus the original duel
holdings are

    d_i=9/2, d_j=3/2.

Use h_i=1/2,h_j=18/5, so h=41/10. Installation costs kappa=1/10;
full service has b=4,c=1, with partial service available. Ordinary
settlement leaves 59/10, less than the duel total 6, and hence cannot
make both originals as well off in this transfer technology. A cheaper
transfer method is a serious repair that changes this conclusion.

The customer's payment limit is L=17/5. Consider an agreed price
p=17/5 and effort e=1/5. The terminal holdings are

    x_i=23/5 > 9/2,
    x_j=9/5 > 3/2.                                   (6)

The supplier can fund preparation from its initial transferable unit:
h_i+kappa=3/5<1. After payment it can fund the 1/5 service parts while
retaining its preloaded combat unit. The customer also retains its
preloaded unit. Work/time budgets must leave enough for the specified
operations and a possible attack; using 098's initial budgets with small
positive setup/inspection work supplies a conservative attack upper
bound because these expenses reduce recovery opportunities.

Here Q_max=39/10, so the customer's maximum expected capture after
negotiation is 39/40<1. Condition (5) holds even before deducting the
goal expense. Thus the same customer's threat does not enforce an
unmodified supplier's performance in this parameter region.

After the actual update and payment, the supplier holds 24/5 and the
customer 1 before service. The combat cash prize is at most 19/5;
customer attack value is at most 19/20<1. After service the supplier
holds 23/5 and the customer has cash 1 plus dated benefit 4/5. The
cash prize is at most 18/5. Customer attack value is at most
4/5+(1/4)(18/5)=17/10<9/5. Supplier direct attack is also worse than
its honored payoff at these checkpoints. Intermediate expenditure x
between 0 and 1/5 leaves the supplier's attack upper bound below 23/5;
if dated output arrives proportionally, the customer's bound is
4x+(1/4)(19/5-x), at most 17/10 before completion. If output arrives
only at completion, its earlier bound is still smaller.

## 5. The actual new goal supplies performance and price discipline

The accepted contract records price p and desired effort e_0 before
the customer exposes its fees and payment. A recognized supplier goal
uses the customer's promised terminal holding

    q=R_j-h_j-p+b e_0

in 097's criterion C_i=W_i-lambda(q-W_j)_+, with lambda>1. The existing
contract record is included when this goal activates; changing its code
later does not erase the old evaluator's accepted term. A concrete
implementation must retain that record and distinguish acceptance from
a false or unrecognized goal declaration.

After payment, at an effort e<=e_0 with no refund, the C value is

    R_i-h_i-kappa+p-c e-lambda b(e_0-e).               (7)

Its slope is lambda b-c>0. Above e_0 the penalty vanishes and its
slope is -c. Thus the supplier strictly chooses the promised effort.
Extra payments reducing the customer's promised outcome change C at
marginal rate 1-lambda<0. A cash refund can substitute for output when
physically funded, but replacing b units of customer benefit by b units
of refund costs more than c when b>c. The promised service therefore
beats that cancellation in this instance.

These comparisons concern actual resulting outcomes, so a further goal
change leading to the same shortfall or overcharge has the same old-C
value and also pays its real implementation cost. The coefficient
lambda is a preference for the accepted outcome, not a penalty for
changing utility. F ranks keeping the saved service cost above exerting
it, whereas C reverses that ranking, establishing a genuine change.

Other physical branches require the same scrutiny. Direct combat has
the upper bounds above. A no-transfer refusal gives the supplier at
most its retained original holdings, below (6); a transfer route pays
the stated h. An outside branch with a larger joint sum cannot simply
be covered by 097's fixed-total proof: its own attainable payoff must
be bounded separately. This distinction prevents an artificial global
surplus cap from doing the work.

## 6. Proposal optimality, selection and staged installation

In the continuous effort/payment class (1), the repaired data give
e*=1/8,p=17/5. Supplier holdings are 187/40, above its duel value 9/2;
customer holdings equal 3/2. This is an optimal goal-enabled offer in
that class, with customer acceptance at indifference. The nearby
contract (6) is strictly beneficial to both but does not maximize a
sole supplier-proposer's payoff in the continuous class. A declared
finite offer grid or another bargaining allocation can select such a
strict-interior contract; it must not be passed off as the unique
unrestricted offer.

Preparation and activation can be two separate positive-cost actions
before payment. At each intervening checkpoint both retain Attack.
Setup consumes resources without increasing military capability, so
the original direct combat bound remains 9/2. Completion of a selected
contract giving the supplier more than that bound is preferable to
aborting for combat; the customer similarly compares its promised
holding to its actual disagreement. Conditional recognition and quote
acceptance must precede customer fee exposure. Otherwise a supplier
could exploit sunk fees by revising the quote before any obligation
binds. That timing condition is an implementation requirement, not an
automatic consequence of self-knowledge.

An equally capable ordinary delegate at the same total cost can tie
this genuine update. A cheaper controller can displace it. A matched
comparison must include its authority over later renegotiation and
performance, not just a revocable verbal plan. Optional customer
goal changes and hostile delegation are not included in this fixed-F
customer witness: they can create new credible threats after payment,
requiring a further safeguard or a new joint agreement. The construction
therefore establishes a substantive asymmetric-capability family and
its explicit missing extension, not a universal combat/compromise rule.

# How transferable output and future bookings can pay for peace together

**E8, note 200.** Real producers may have *both* future jobs and some present production they can use to pay one another. An assignment schedule can then promise just enough of each. When an affordable meter allows a larger share of each intact batch to be transferred, the founders can buy ordinary cooperation. At a higher meter price the same founders can instead consent to a genuine change, even though the old equipment can make some transfers and the ordinary scheduler can mix peaceful and disputed assignments.

## Funding payments out of a renewable flow

In an intact job, a total amount T of the x units can now be redirected to any participants at par; the rest must go to the named recipient. Dispute instead has the physical returns in 191. There is no additional consumable stock conjured each date. Every unit paid comes from that same batch and reduces what its intended recipient keeps. A collectively authorized, verified split of a physically separable output stream can implement these payments even if the sites retain their physical initiative. Owning the schedule alone gives no authority to split an indivisible product. A completely closed perishable job cannot be reopened. Measurement, converting the output into recipients' usable goods, and installing the division ahead of an irreversible closing decision must be priced.

First give each original site equal intact yield x, and potentially unequal expected recoveries q_i and expenses c_i with d_i=q_i-c_i>0. At a standard renewal probability p normalize payoffs by 1-p as previously. For a list in which every site is designated with positive probability somewhere, the individual all-peace criterion for guaranteed transfer capacity T on every intact batch is

    (1-p)T + p(x-d_j) ≥ Σ_(i≠j) d_i    for every designated j.          (1)

In the symmetric case the same single inequality is necessary regardless of who is designated. In the unequal case, paying an agent entirely in cash could remove it from the set of required designations; (1) for all sites then supplies a sufficient test. At an exposed assignment unserved originals need their security values. Their combined current material on peace is at most T and the next-date pool has at most x-d_j for them. This proves necessity wherever j is named.

For sufficiency put A=Σd_i<x and in public state j give normalized values v_i^j=d_i+(x-A)1{i=j}. On the current successful job pay t_i to unserved sites, with 0≤t_i≤d_i, and t=Σt_i≤T. Select t at least the positive part of [A-d_j-p(x-d_j)]/(1-p). This is feasible under (1) and t≤A-d_j. Then give the recipient x-t. On completion the required next-date vector is w=[v^j-(1-p)current]/p. Every component is at least its d_i: for an unserved site use t_i≤d_i, and for j use the lower bound on t. The components sum to x, so mix the next states v^i with probabilities (w_i-d_i)/(x-A). On dispute repeat the current state. An individual initiating would receive (1-p)d_i+p v_i^j≤v_i^j. Weak inequalities can be made strict with slack by raising the design floors. For p=0 pay each unserved site its required current amount directly. This proves attainment without assuming cash renews independently of production. If disputed output can be appropriated or pledged in different ways, the budget and security comparison may change.

## Cash that falls short of full peace can still reduce conflict

In the symmetric case let m=n-1, q=y/n, d=q-c and J=x-y+c. Put A_0 for the infimum of dispute probability across reached exposed assignments. Future normalized aggregate income is at most x-A_0 J. If a particular job is disputed with probability a, the unserved sites receive at most (1-a)T+a m q now and at most x-A_0J-d in the next job in aggregate. Consequently, where the numerator is positive,

    A_0 ≥ [md-(1-p)T-p(x-d)] / [(1-p)(mq-T)-pJ].                     (2)

Take the infimum in the preceding inequality. In the indicated region mq>T and the denominator exceeds the numerator by mc>0 using J=x-mq-d. The expression reduces to 186's bound at T=0. It applies to every ordinary funded rule with this liquid share, even when parties send private recommendations, mix their final decisions or make later transfers depend on their observed histories.

For x=0.70, q=0.20, c=0.04, n=3 and p=0.40, individual sure peace requires T≥13/75≈0.17333 per intact job. If groups can coordinate disputes, the two unserved originals need 0.36 between them, so the analogous necessary transfer is 0.24. At that value paying each unserved site 0.12 suffices with the right calendar. Every intact date then gives each site a base 0.12 plus a residual 0.34 to the named site. Subtracting that common base from per-date normalized receipts leaves 185's problem with x'=0.34, q'=0.08, c'=0.04, p=0.40. Its lower constraints use L=0.04 and give 0.04 for a singleton and at least 0.14 for a pair whose residual security is 0.12. The attaining queue delivers the result. Thus 0.24 is sharp for this three-party group test even if the meter could make *unequal* payments. The collective comparison permits a fixed group to share the burden of one initiative and keeps the outsiders' prescribed future behavior.

## Actual meter purchase versus actual revision

Suppose the existing line can redirect only T=0.10 per intact batch. Equation (2) requires every all-original schedule to dispute with probability at least 11/31 at each exposed date. The unnormalized expected total gap at signing to certain intact production is at least (0.14·11/31)/(1-0.40)≈0.0828. Before the roles are known the founders can authorize 183's role lottery, paying 0.06 once for the chosen single genuine installation. That agreement delivers full service through future priority for the two retained originals. Its founders all prefer it to even the best symmetric funded ordinary rival using the current plant.

Alternatively, a replacement meter can redirect up to 0.25 of each intact batch. It can protect every booking under unchanged original goals, including against the fixed groups just examined. At a pool-wide, one-time real price 0.04 the founders buy the meter. At a price 0.09 they buy one genuine installation instead in the individually deciding baseline. Give each symmetric founder an equal subscription obligation on the meter and randomly assigned update status on the other proposal. Every founder has a strict old-goal incentive to sign the relevant winning offer. This comparison allows ordinary schedules to use both the existing cash and future rights, and gives a purchased meter or changed controller the use of the same calendar. Mixtures over publicly known fixed configurations cannot exceed the largest attainable net total in their branches under these symmetric ex ante terms. The two retained originals in the one-change construction jointly receive the entire intact stream, and each individually prefers its assigned continuation. A fresh opportunity for the excluded changed founder to restore its former goal, or an inexpensive matched conventional release controller, would require repricing the offers.

**Exploration ledger.** The zero-cash interpretation of a short horizon is damaged when a credible in-kind stream can pay the people who stand down. A sibling funds such payments from the real job and computes the needed fraction. A higher-rung successor studies endogenous storage and loss when a one-time liquid stock can be saved, depleted or replenished after a dispute.

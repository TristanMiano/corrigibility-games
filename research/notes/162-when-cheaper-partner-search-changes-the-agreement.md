# When cheaper partner search first creates and then displaces a shared goal

**E8, note 162.** A site looking for a third partner can discover two different things. Some candidates can complete a reciprocal circuit. Others have a ready-made service the site can buy outright. As search gets cheaper, the founder may start looking, then become willing to keep looking even after finding a good reciprocal partner. The same increase in search capability can raise joint adoption in one price range and lower it in another.

## Where the offers come from

Use the jobs, costs of service and capacities of [154](154-who-would-build-the-missing-return-path.md). A selects a consortium for one remaining productive window and can afford the investigation out of pre-existing work. A compatible third site closes the ring with B. The common terminal weights (1,4,2) support full A→B delivery and probabilistic B→C and C→A delivery. Each of B and C needs 0.01 expected units to cover its actual setup, relative to rejecting for separate use. Let x_A,x_B,x_C denote the expected units of local donor work forgone. To maximize A's original return, set

    x_A=1/12,   x_B=2x_A-0.01,   x_C=2x_B-0.01.

The latter two quantities are positive and below their capacities of 1/3. Indeed x_B≤2x_A-0.01 and x_C≤2x_B-0.01 follow from B and C's participation, so 2x_C-x_A≤7x_A-0.06 and the displayed flows attain that bound. After A pays 1/5 for its update and adapter the compatible consortium gives it v=7/12-0.06-1/5=97/300. Take signed participation at equality for the exact offer, or round the services up to the other founders' advantage on a sufficiently fine finite quote grid for strict participation. The numerical search choices below are separated from ties and persist for strictly accepted offers. On a successful installation A actually builds under the new goal as in 154. The cheaper ordinary controller that could bind the same local routing, if present, must be included in this very quote.

A second kind of candidate has a prepared unit of the needed service. An enforceable spot purchase gives A 0.8 of value at price 0.2, and the supplier expends less than the price fulfilling the order. A therefore receives u=0.6 with both agents keeping their original objectives. This example conditions on a standing price that remains available until the booking deadline, as with a posted catalog. A seller free to reprice after seeing the competing bids has a different continuation. The other candidates have incompatible equipment and give zero incremental return. One booking and the short remaining slot make the opportunities substitutes. Nobody can use a contest to finish a spoiled job before its deadline, and initiating costs finite work. In this embedding combat is available and strictly unattractive throughout.

## The founder's search and the type of agreement it selects

A can screen up to m equally costly candidates, with recall. Before paying c for a capability check it knows the distribution of signed, executable net offers, including others' choices. It can decline all offers for zero. The feasible opportunities are independent across candidates in this version. At the deadline all unselected sites are free to continue their separate work. The familiar reservation-value rule applies. If the incumbent's best offer pays w and uninspected offers have random return Y, continue precisely when

    E[(Y-w)_+] > c.                                                 (1)

The result for equal independent candidates with recall is standard search theory. For this setting a direct finite-horizon proof suffices. When (1) holds, even one check followed by immediate selection improves on w. When it fails weakly, it fails at every possible new higher w. By backward induction the extra checks after the first would then have no positive value and neither does the first. At equality both behaviors can be optimal. This criterion concerns A's *own* net benefit from paying for an experiment. A higher total consortium benefit which A cannot recover has no place in its stopping comparison.

Let each candidate be genuine-goal compatible with probability a, an ordinary supplier with probability b and unusable with probability e=1-a-b. Assume 0<v<u, positive masses and m≥2. Away from ties the founder chooses these paths:

| Search expense | Behavior | Probability of a genuine three-party installation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| c>av+bu | Use existing separate production | 0 |
| b(u-v)<c<av+bu | Stop at the first worthwhile offer of either kind | a(1-e^m)/(a+b) |
| 0<c<b(u-v) | Retain a compatible club but continue checking for a better spot supplier | (1-b)^m-e^m |

The middle expression sums the chance of a first positive recurring offer at each position. At low cost the club is chosen iff no ordinary supplier is found in all possible draws and at least one compatible club is available. Couple these searches using the same ordered candidates. Extra search at low expense changes the agreement precisely when an ordinary supplier follows an earlier accepted-compatible offer before the deadline and the first positive was compatible. Positive probability of that event gives a strict drop in genuine installations. In the two active search regimes the chance of *some* productive agreement stays 1-e^m. Only three-way activations count as three actual utility changes. Paid ordinary delivery counts as peaceful production and as zero changes. A can always use the more expensive search policy at the new lower price, so its optimized expected original return weakly increases as c falls, strictly when it actually buys checks. The partners who might lose their place have a different comparison.

For m=3, a=0.30, b=0.20 the original founder begins searching below c=0.217 and continues past an existing club below 0.055333…. At c=0.25, 0.10 and 0.02 the actual genuine installation probabilities are respectively 0, 0.525 and 0.387. Productive agreements occur with probabilities 0, 0.875 and 0.875. Expected check counts are respectively 0, 1+0.5+0.5²=1.75 and 1+0.8+0.8²=2.44. All three numbers concern one explicitly specified population of opportunities. The probabilities a and b are illustrative, while the sign comparisons hold throughout open parameter regions.

**What would make these conditions arise?** Shared equipment, differing turnaround times, searchable records and posted procurement terms give independent reasons to investigate alternatives with different institutions. OCLC's actual resource-sharing documentation describes screening for holdings and eligibility, ordering candidates and accounting for queues and maximum charges. These are precedents for an information-bearing directory. A correlated market, an expiring quote, a strategically repriced offer or one search revealing something about the next needs a different rule. The goal-setting implementation and the local services supporting v come from the explicit construction. The directory alone supplies none of those capabilities. A has the physical right to choose its sole booking and pays c from its current stock because of (1). A cost improvement can also be built, for example by buying a reusable index. A would purchase that index only up to its own expected improvement in optimized return over this search. **Exploration ledger.** The framing candidate is that discovery matters through the alternatives discoverable by the same capability. The structural candidate prices search against a founder's current best credible offer and records which kind of agreement displaces it. The three-cost instance is promising. The reading that a cheaper directory always increases the number of genuine agreements is dead at the change from 0.10 to 0.02. A sibling varies which candidate class tends to offer the best deal. A structural successor makes search results public to the participants and lets a candidate reprice or buy a listing before A searches.

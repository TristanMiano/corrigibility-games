# 270 — How certain must the partners be about joint success?

Knowing that each plant installs correctly three times in four leaves the chance that *both* succeed anywhere from one half to three quarters. The wrong end of that interval can make even the best binary insurance inadequate. Here the founders can identify the minimum joint performance they need, renegotiate insurance or pay to create a floor under joint performance while remaining uncertain about every particular outcome.

Use [269](269-which-insurance-bargains-preserve-both-incentives-to-change.md). Keep the two marginal technical success chances q=.75, false-passage rate e=.25, independent measurement errors conditional on the active goals and gifts. Let J be the chance both would succeed if they try. An unused service slot expires, so deliberately skipping leaves the other plant's marginal unchanged. The proposal must beat each founder's known safe choice and make its effort optimal for every J in the stated range. This suffices for founders with different beliefs within it. In this particular example the alternative of skipping faces the same partner marginal for every J, so the effort comparison is also valid for founders evaluating the worst law in that range.

Let z be the expected factor 2^(−gift), equal to .25 for A and 1/32 for B. Before multiplying by each founder's 2^k cost factor, the two on-path disutility coefficients are

    H₀=1−[e²+2qe(1−e)+J(1−e)²],
    H₁=2zJ + (q−J)(2e+ez) + (1−2q+J)e².                         (270.1)

The four pieces for an open connection correspond to both true installations, one of either kind, and neither. Skipping has the same D₀,D₁ as before. H₀ strictly decreases with J. The slope of H₁ is (2−e)(z−e), zero for A and negative for B. Thus at *every* fixed transfer spread, increasing J strictly improves both original evaluations and relaxes both effort constraints. To certify uniform consent and effort over J≥J_min one need check only the lower endpoint. For a general finite uncertainty set the original disutility comparisons are affine in each joint state law at fixed transfers, and a polytope can be checked at its vertices. A founder who can shift its partner's marginal by skipping needs a separate deviation table.

## A lower bound the parties can use

For each J compute the incentive interval [l(J),u(J)] by (269.1), clip its actuarial optimum h*(J) to this interval and denote the maximal sum of certainty-equivalent increments by S(J). Each plant's safe increment is .44 and each pays .01 for escrow, so strict participation requires S(J)>.90. On [.50,.75] the incentive interval is nonempty and finite. Its lower bound decreases and its upper bound increases because both agents' effort inequalities relax. At J=.50 the bounds are about −.149 and .066. Hence S is continuous and strictly increasing: retaining the old maximizing feasible spread at a larger J already gives strictly greater value.

The unique solution of S(J)=.90 is **J≈.55746718**. Direct substitution gives S(.55746)−.90≈−.00003492 and S(.55748)−.90≈.00006239. Close to this threshold B's willingness to attempt fixes the upper spread around .585; the unconstrained optimum is about .760. At the boundary the equalizing C is about −.17963 and C+h about .40551. Available escrow capacities of .20 from A and .41 from B cover both. For J_min just above the boundary, a small inward adjustment of h gives strict effort as well as strict participation. The same contract then continues to work for all larger J. Below the boundary even unrestricted baseline transfers and the best effort-compatible binary insurance lose a participation comparison at the admitted lower endpoint.

The original contract from 269 (h=.50,C=−.14) requires J>.56029 up to rounding for both participation tests with the .01 bills. At **J_min=.558**, reprice it to h=.575 and C=−.175. At the least favorable law A's certainty-equivalent increment after the bill is .44096 and B's is .44075. Both strictly attempt and accept. The actual probability of both genuine installations can still lie anywhere between .558 and .75. It is the bound on the founders' downside, not certainty about the eventual joint outcome, that buys their consent here.

## Buy additional data or change how the equipment is scheduled?

Suppose an observer can independently identify both true controllers on each of N representative pairs of *attempts*. At a fixed sample size, Hoeffding's one-sided bound places J above the observed fraction minus √[log(20)/(2N)] with at least 95% repeated-sample coverage. An observed fraction .5625 in 60,000 such pairs puts that bound above .55747. This is a conservative sufficient precision calculation. An existing assortment of passed jobs, unverified declarations or repeatedly reused pairs would not supply that sample. Confidence coverage for this procedure and the chance of two genuine changes on tomorrow's job are different quantities. Estimating uncertain marginals, rather than taking q=.75 as independently grounded, would enlarge the uncertainty set.

Alternatively, a scheduler can reserve common working windows for a fraction α of paired jobs. On these jobs both have the same available window with probability .75, otherwise neither does. The other jobs keep their original unknown joint law and the same marginals. The new guarantee is J≥.50+.25α. Before charging for the service the participation threshold is crossed at α>4(.55746718−.50)≈.22987. For an actual priced purchase take α=.40, giving J≥.60. Under h=.50,C=−.14 both original founders' least favorable certainty equivalents before the scheduling bill and after escrow are 8.4991 and 8.5640. Each strictly pays .04 per job to reserve the windows and still beats 8.44. The earlier range including J=.50 could not support the two funded changes among the binary insurance choices. More favorable true dependence, reliable ordinary joint dispatch or a technically cheaper means to guarantee joint windows can lead the same founders to different purchases. Shared maintenance availability is plausible in a common network. An assertion that a scheduler can eliminate separate residual technical faults on the reserved jobs would require evidence about the equipment.

These bounds apply to the specified edits, evidence, physical gate, outside stocks and two settlement events. A richer transfer observable may be valuable. The three-event example in 269 shows why its design requires another calculation. An affordable ordinary guarantee can dominate both insured changes at all these reliabilities. The scheduling purchase prices an actual way of supporting consent when that guarantee is unavailable and experience alone gives weak evidence about joint performance.

# R0002 working audit: physical threats can remove the productive fallback

**Local deviation registered within R220; full repeated-burn implementation remains open.** This note identifies a consequential enlargement of 037's staged action space: public destruction of an agent's own calibration resources before bargaining. The efficient-fallback certificate remains correct when its fallback is credible. The example shows why physical preservation, rather than goal-parameter preservation alone, belongs in a chosen safeguard. The constructive counterpart is the repaired conditional guarded construction in 046/049/056 and [E3](../models/E3.md). Review is contributing-agent self-check.

## 1. A public resource-burning deviation

Use the exact 037 fixture, with A's installed lambda=1/5 and original B. Initial project stocks are 3 each, productive expected net surplus C=4/5, and the intact default gives final expected outputs (16/5,18/5). Suppose a newly allowed public action lets B transform 141/100 units of its own finished material into an unusable-by-deadline state before its proposal. Charge the actual work and time of this action; first calculate with the conservative initial combat upper frontier W=4, then check that the witness has room for a small positive charge.

B retains 159/100 units, less than the 8/5 needed for its calibration. Without an agreed resource transfer, the former productive default is infeasible. A's solitary effort is unprofitable under its current lambda, so rejection supplies retained stocks until B's attack decision. B's expected own output from combat is 2, exceeding 159/100 when the declared recovery frontier is attained. Hence B's attack is credible in this enlarged rejection subgame. A's current combat value is 12/5.

B proposes a contract with an up-front loan of 1/100 from A to B, sufficient to restore B's calibration input, and the following terminal success/failure transfers after both have contributed:

\[
g_A^0=-139/100,\quad g_A^1=281/100,
\qquad g_B^0=139/100,\quad g_B^1=719/100.
\]

These transfers balance the budget in each output state. After the loan and calibration, A has 139/100 and B zero before terminal transfers. Final failure outputs are (0,139/100); final success outputs are (21/5,719/100). All are nonnegative. The success-minus-failure differences are d_A=21/5 and d_B=29/5.

The effort margins are

\[
\Delta(d_A+\lambda d_B)-c_A=1/125>0,
\qquad \Delta d_B-c_B=7/50>0.
\]

Expected final project outputs are

\[
E X=42/25=1.68,\qquad E Y=371/100=3.71.
\]

A's current value is 1211/500=2.422, strictly above 12/5=2.4. B obtains 3.71, strictly above the intact default's 3.6, despite destroying its own material first. Thus this is a profitable deviation from the proposed intact-default continuation when the physical burn, loan and rejection combat values are implementable. It does not rely on negative goal weights or a new hostile utility.

The initial goal change's old-utility payoff cannot still be quoted as 3.2 in this enlarged game. This one profitable deviation establishes the missing stability check; it does not solve every alternative initial goal choice or the full enlarged equilibrium.

## 2. Exact physical obligations

The W=4 figure in 037 was an upper bound. A credible threat needs an *attainable* lower bound as well. With a one-step burn costing 1/100 work, the recovery-work frontier is 4-1/275, provided the remaining material and time permit it. B's half is still above 159/100. A's combat value is slightly below 12/5, leaving the displayed acceptance strict. The precise recovery schedule and remaining horizon must establish this attainability; an upper bound alone is insufficient.

After productive work is spent, the small remaining recovery frontier must also be checked against the displayed failure output. A's own failure output is zero, but its current concern for B gives value (1/5)(139/100)=139/500. This exceeds the late-combat upper bound when the reactor has consumed its full ten work units and the declared administrative/routing charges are applied. Informative failure signals must not arrive while a substantially larger combat frontier remains; the state table must specify that timing.

The up-front loan is a real transfer and costs routing work and time. It is not negative collateral. Its small amount and the existing work slack leave room for the witness under a suitable finite schedule, but that schedule must be supplied before registering a fully realized physical counterexample.

Repeated burning after the loan is also consequential. The displayed acceptance/execution calculation is a final-offer subgame calculation, not a proof that an unrestricted sequence of burn-and-renegotiate opportunities has the same outcome. One possible finite realization has a last pre-proposal burn window; later destruction leaves insufficient time for another productive agreement. A wider repeated-window game needs its own continuation solution. In particular, a tiny later burn can remove the just-restored input and reopen bargaining, so that opportunity cannot be silently omitted from a claim about all later histories.

## 3. The boundary and a limited-liability correction

For a burn d just above R_B-c_B=7/5, the productive frontier approaches 27/5. At a full-effort, budget-balanced terminal contract, A's effort condition implies

\[
d_A\ge \frac{c_A/\Delta-\lambda Y_0}{1-\lambda}.
\]

With nonnegative final failure holdings, E X>=b d_A whenever the right-hand side is positive. Combat participation also requires

\[
E X\ge \frac{2(1+\lambda)-\lambda(27/5)}{1-\lambda}.
\]

These bounds correct an initially attempted extraction calculation that gave A negative failure holdings. At lambda=1/5, the limited-liability/effort bound is 5/3, stronger than the combat-participation bound 13/8. The explicit feasible witness gives A 42/25>5/3 instead.

At lambda_0=5/33 the limiting effort bound equals 9/5; it decreases strictly as lambda rises. Thus larger other-regard can increase vulnerability once resource destruction removes the productive default. A continuous burn amount just above an incapacity threshold can create an unattained supremum; do not assert a pure equilibrium without addressing that discontinuity. A finite burn granularity can leave a narrow protected parameter interval. This note proves the displayed profitable deviation, not a universal refusal theorem.

## 4. Safeguard repairs to carry forward

040's conditional guard explicitly rejects outgoing concessions and productive participation with an unguarded partner. Under that rule, burning cannot induce the displayed loan or contract. B would then receive at most its low-combat recovery payoff, below its initial stock, whereas adopting the compatible guard enables positive productive gains. After guard adoption, deliberate destruction that removes the certified fallback incurs the finite approval penalty. This is a candidate negotiated repair, with demanding mechanism-level verification and physical-state coverage made explicit.

Protecting a calibration reserve is another possible repair, but it must prevent release-and-destruction without secretly enforcing the hidden effort itself. If the proposed reserve device can compel genuine effort, it is also a stronger ordinary commitment technology and must be included in that comparison. Faster safe delivery or exit could also repair threats; actual time and control transitions are needed.

The corrected explanatory claim is conditional: a credible productive fallback protects accepted accommodation; a chosen safeguard must preserve the physical resources and continuation that make that fallback credible. Public resource destruction supplies a concrete test of the latter obligation. It neither refutes the certificate nor establishes a general disadvantage of changing preferences.

# When an honest donor would pay a recipient to fake membership

**E8, note 177.** A donor that sincerely agreed to a service rule can profit when its immediate recipient secretly keeps its original goal. The donor then receives fewer calls to provide costly service. At some evidence thresholds this saving is large enough for the donor to finance the recipient's loss from faking. Both sides can make the deal enforceable using bets on a public readout, even though neither can prove what goal the applicant chose. Charging a little more for access by poorly evidenced recipients restores genuine participation.

## A sharp proper-coalition test for the homogeneous service ring

Use the recipient-specific gate of 163 on an n-ring, n≥3. Conditional on one uniformly chosen donor's job, the next site is its only eligible recipient. If genuinely configured, that recipient opens the gate with probability T; if it secretly retains its original goal the probability is D, with 0≤D<T≤1. A genuine donor gives when the gate opens; a fake keeps its job. No internal donation is possible under the retained own-only goal at the ordinary interface here. Each configuration costs k before the job. Both r>d>0 and all payoffs in this calculation use original linear private output. A site earns r on receipt and forgoes d on delivery. Ex ante rights and unused local reserves cover the actual transfers described below. A verified gate outcome can be easier to contract on than the private decision of the local operator once it opens. If an ordinary controller can directly bind that later decision, compare its actual price as well. When the physical deadline can survive a contest, substitute its real fallback for the simple expiry case in 163.

Let α=T-D, and put U=αr-Td-nk. A lone member strictly prefers the genuine configuration if U>0: it gains the extra receipt and pays for its donation and setup. To prevent *every proper group* from strictly increasing its aggregate original payoff by having some of its members fake, the sharper strict-surplus condition is

    U > αd,    equivalently    T(r-2d)-D(r-d) > nk.                   (1)

At equality some aggregate comparisons can tie. The class allows any private subset to coordinate which members keep their original goals and to redistribute original value among themselves. It holds the reaction of nonmembers fixed at genuine configuration. This is a coalition-stability test of an all-sincere profile, distinct from selecting an equilibrium when a side contract is public before everyone signs. Arbitrary substitute local production controllers, unscheduled jobs, and richer scoped objectives have their own costs and effects.

**Upper bound on a side agreement.** Take any nonempty proper set F of l actual fakes in b contiguous blocks on the ring. Their total own gain times n, relative to sincere operation, is

    -l[T(r-d)-nk] + b D r.

Every fake saves its cost and its donor effort. Right after a genuine site it loses αr of expected incoming value in these units; right after another fake it loses Tr. Among the remaining genuine sites, a predecessor of a fake saves αd in expected effort. It also loses Tr if its own predecessor fakes, in which case its net change is negative because r>d. A genuine site with no fake on either side is unaffected. Thus a side group can recruit at most b benefiting genuine predecessors and gain at most l[-U+αd], using b≤l. This excludes a strict improvement even with generous costless compensating transfers when U≥αd. With strict (1) every such side agreement has negative aggregate surplus. A group consisting only of genuine sites retains the stated service plan and has no private production or transfer surplus in this test.

**A pair that can capture the saving.** With one fake B, its genuine predecessor A saves αd/n and the fake loses U/n. At n≥3 A still has a genuine predecessor. If 0<U<αd, a private enforceable transfer between U/n and αd/n suffices in aggregate. Yet a gift paid in advance cannot by itself make B choose the private act that justifies it. Here is an implementable side contract. Choose θ strictly between U and αd. Conditional on the A-to-B job, pay B the following from the two finite deposits:

    gate closed: A pays B    Tθ/α;
    gate opened: B pays A    (1-T)θ/α.

The respective deposits needed are below Td and (1-T)d. Under genuine B the expected transfer to B on that job is zero. Under a fake B it is θ. The change in original value from secretly faking after accepting is (θ-U)/n>0. Compared with all-sincere operation A gains (αd-θ)/n. With B now fake, A's own payoff advantage from remaining genuine becomes (U+αd)/n>0; its bet uses the beneficiary reading regardless of A's configuration. Thus a pair can actually make the deviation profitable without observing B's goal. If U<0 the individual fake already profits. When U=0 and α>0 the same funded bet gives strict pair gains. Outside sites cannot retroactively rescind a consent made before the private side contract in this timing. If they can observe and refuse before enrollment, the proposed high-throughput arrangement needs another participation check at that earlier date.

The conditional bet is a constructive argument at the stated authority and cost. If only the donor can post a deposit and the recipient cannot pledge anything, a one-way closed-gate rebate b still works when U/α<b<αd/(1-D). It has to compensate the donor for payments even on honest false alarms. Higher side-contract expenses or insufficient transferable stock narrow the realizable deviations. Conversely a coalition with a cheap private controller able to ensure productive internal jobs can also exploit service to a neighbor. That stronger comparison is treated as an upper bound in 161. No claim about arbitrary private arrangements follows from (1).

## Actual choice of the evidence rule

Take n=3,r=6,d=1,k=0.22 and the readback of 163. Pass positives and accept a fraction h of negatives, so T=0.5+0.5h and D=0.05+0.95h. At h=0.45 the all-sincere delivery probability would be 0.725 and each member has a strict unilateral reason to configure. Here U=0.10 and αd=0.2475. For θ=0.15 a genuine donor offers deposits of about 0.4394 and 0.1667 on the selected job. The recipient gains 0.01667 and the donor 0.0325 in ex ante original value. Even a side-contract expense of 0.01 privately paid by the donor can be worthwhile. The third site loses some service and has a reason to ask for a different protocol before giving its consent.

For arbitrary positive- and negative-reading acceptance probabilities x,y, the strict pair margin before nk in (1) is 1.75x-2.75y. Honest opening probability is (x+y)/2. The supremum consistent with strict proper-coalition margins is therefore reached in the limit x=1, y=(1.75-0.66)/2.75, giving T≈0.69818. In particular h=0.35 gives T=0.675, U=0.42 and αd=0.2925. Each genuine original earns 0.675×5/3-0.22=0.905 before common inspection cost. All three activate on a successful honest gate; nobody activates on a closed honest gate. A proposer facing the possibility of private bets can offer this stricter, positive-payoff rule in place of the vulnerable one and the other sites can approve it relative to separate use. Which funded design wins against a cheaper conventional control still depends on the actual menu. A rule admitting more poorly evidenced applicants may increase the nominal service rate while making a privately financed substitution profitable.

**Exploration record.** The framing candidate asks whose incentives reinforce an agreement after it can be privately redesigned. The structural candidate tracks gains to signers who stay genuine when *another* site fakes. The unilateral sufficient-condition reading is dead at h=0.45 under funded side bets. A sibling buys a stricter evidence rule or negotiates finite side-contract stakes. A higher-rung successor asks which ordinary or genuine institutions remain attractive when the parties can trade contingent claims on the very evidence the group uses.

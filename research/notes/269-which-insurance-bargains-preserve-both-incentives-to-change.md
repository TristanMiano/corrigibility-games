# 269 — Which insurance bargains preserve both founders' incentives to change?

A transfer that makes a risky operation easier to accept can weaken the incentive to perform the promised edit. [216](216-which-risks-can-the-signers-share.md) prices insurance for a given continuation. Here the founders must also *choose* the proposed changes under their incumbent risk preferences. With one binary public settlement event there is an exact joint test, even when the two parties have different beliefs, costs, and exposures to physical loss.

## Every transfer on one binary event

Founder i has exponential utility −exp(−stock/Tᵢ). Fix a proposed pair of choices and a finite set of unilateral alternatives, including passive controls and false declarations. A known physical rule and the successors' then-current incentives give the lotteries over output and a publicly verifiable event Y∈{0,1}. Require these continuation choices to be valid at the proposed prices. In the example, transfers settle before the successors' final effort decisions and do not change their marginal choices. Let A_y and B_y be the two founders' probability-weighted expected exponential disutilities (positive signs) in event y on the proposed path. Let A_yᵈ and B_yᵈ be the corresponding coefficients if that founder alone deviates. Each uses its own beliefs and all real action expenses. Positive coefficients are assumed for the logarithms below.

An escrow transfers C on Y=0 and C+h on Y=1 from B to A. The variable h is the extra settlement on a positive event. **Each incentive comparison gives an interval of feasible h.** For A, after canceling the positive constant exp(−C/T_A), require

    (A₀−A₀ᵈ)+(A₁−A₁ᵈ)exp(−h/T_A) ≤ 0.                          (269.1)

For B use B's coefficients and exp(h/T_B). These are affine in a strictly monotone variable, so each condition is a half-line, the whole line, or empty, with the appropriate endpoint. Intersect these intervals across the two founders' deviations. Strict comparisons give strict incentives. Any initial or future physical challenge which changes the state law belongs in the comparison, including what happens to the escrow. A possible later change of goal must be evaluated by that later decision maker if it is feasible.

Let R_i be the certainty equivalent of the pertinent outside choice. Define m_A(h)=A₀+A₁exp(−h/T_A) and m_B(h)=B₀+B₁exp(h/T_B). With state-by-state stock bounds [l_y,u_y], some baseline payment C makes both participate exactly when

    max(l₀, l₁−h, R_A+T_A log m_A(h))
       ≤ min(u₀, u₁−h, −R_B−T_B log m_B(h)).                     (269.2)

For strict consent use strict inequalities against the two outside values. This follows by writing A's certainty equivalent as C−T_A log m_A and B's as −C−T_B log m_B. Their joint feasible region in (h,C) is convex: the two certainty equivalents are concave, the stock bounds are linear and the incentive tests restrict h to an interval. This characterizes support of the proposed actions, separately from how the founders coordinate when several continuations also satisfy best replies.

With unlimited baseline transfers, the h maximizing the sum of the two certainty equivalents before incentive constraints is

    h*=[T_A T_B/(T_A+T_B)] log[(A₁B₀)/(A₀B₁)].

The derivative of the sum is A₁exp(−h/T_A)/m_A minus B₁exp(h/T_B)/m_B. The first term decreases and the second increases. Thus the best weakly incentive-compatible spread is h* clipped to the incentive interval when it is closed. If only strict incentives are acceptable, the boundary value can be approached from inside a nonempty interval. Recheck (269.2) for actual funded bounds. Guaranteed consensus on any probability of success is unnecessary for these comparisons; each founder can use its own probabilities for the same feasible physical agreement.

## Both founders sign for two uncertain gifts

Both founders value only their own stock under −2^(−stock). Their separate lines guarantee 8.44 units each. Signing commits their base equipment to a joint line producing eight each before gifts, editing expense or transfers. A real attempt costs A .10 and B .60. It succeeds independently three times in four. Both commissioning readings must pass to open the line. A genuine installation always passes; a retained one passes with probability 1/4. Once open, either donor can keep or sacrifice one unit of its own stock. B's gift to A has an equal chance of log₂3 or log₂6 units; A's gift to B gives three more than either of those amounts. The outcomes are independently realized after the donor acts. Their certainty equivalents to the corresponding recipients are exactly two and five units. Each agreed successor values its own and a weighted quantity of the partner's stock via exponential utility. A's partner weight is .5 and B's is .75. Even the smaller gift times its donor's weight exceeds the donor's one-unit sacrifice. Every genuine successor strictly sends, every retained controller strictly keeps. An inspection and the eventual deliveries remain separate events.

Relative to the base eight, for recipient i with gift certainty equivalent bᵢ∈{2,5} and attempt cost kᵢ, the four coefficients for both attempting and for i's unilateral omission are

    H₀=2^kᵢ(87/256),        H₁=2^kᵢ(25/16)[(3/4)2^(−bᵢ)+1/16],
    D₀=51/64,              D₁=(1/4)[(3/4)2^(−bᵢ)+1/16].

On two attempts the line opens with probability (13/16)². Conditional on opening, one's own costly gift and the partner's gift factor because the work and technical uncertainties are independent. Skipping removes one's expense and gift, but a false positive can still admit an unchanged founder. Substitution in (269.1) gives **−.28063<h<.63024** for both attempts to be strictly optimal against the other's effort. Unconstrained insurance would choose h*=.77028. At that spread B would rather skip. At h=0 the sum of the founders' certainty-equivalent increments is .82978, below the .88 obtainable on their separate lines. No constant signing transfer alone can persuade both to join this proposed operation.

They can instead agree to h=.50 and C=−.14: A pays .14 on a closed connection and B pays .36 on an open one. A now obtains 8.45886 and B 8.45664 in certainty-equivalent stock. Both strictly sign and prefer attempting even after their initial signature. A .01 common-meter expense for each fits inside both margins. Only 9 of 16 prospective jobs involve both agreed goals. Among opened connections 144/169 have both. Even in that last group the donors still cannot say which amount of each gift will arrive. Both accept all three risks because the actual insurance leaves a strictly better lottery and preserves each party's reason to pay for the edit. A destructive contest with at most four usable units for the winner is worse even at the lowest remaining stocks here. It is available until final conversion, with strength affecting who recovers those units.

**Alternatives and scope.** Under the same two-reading gate, with unchanged goals nobody gives and insurance over its openings only redistributes a fixed base. Its combined certainty equivalent cannot exceed 16 by concavity and common beliefs. If only A attempts, the chance of any gift is (3/4)(1/4), and the mean joint increment is (3/16)[(log₂3+log₂6)/2+3−1]−.10<.67. If only B attempts the corresponding mean is below zero after its expense. Neither clears the combined separate increment .88, even with complete risk sharing. A binding ordinary router capable of sending both uncertain gifts on every job yields certainty-equivalent increments one for A and four for B before its price. At .50 per plant both prefer it to the negotiated changes. A matched fallible delegate can instead reproduce their material lottery when it can actuate the same local choices at the same price. The public gate in this construction verifies its own opening and settles common stocks; metering each private downstream contribution requires further authority. A secretly waivable gate, an additional divisible service or a more extensive secured payment schedule warrants a larger agreement comparison.

Two sites sharing a connection can meter whether it opened and have limited neutral stocks available at either end. The sites can differ in how much the same service improves their final goods and in the equipment they need to edit. The special insurance result depends on a binary public event and exponential preferences. With three events, even one deviation can give a nonconvex set of spreads. For example take T=1/ln 2, c₀=0 and A_H−A_D=(.11,−.10,−.10). These coefficients can be realized with three equally likely states, A_D=(.20,.20,.20), and stock −log₂(3A_y) in each state. The transfers (c₁,c₂)=(0,2) and (2,0) both favor H, while (1,1) favors D. This prunes the interval argument for that representation. A sibling can assign each shore its own separately funded output insurance. A broader candidate asks which measured events let both residual risk and both founders' incentives be priced together.

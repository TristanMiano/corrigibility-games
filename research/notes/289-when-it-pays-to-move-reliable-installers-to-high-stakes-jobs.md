# 289 — When is it worth moving reliable installers to high-stakes jobs?

The same quoted chance of a genuine goal can justify opposite decisions on two orders. It matters whether failures land where they waste one unit or nine. A buyer may refuse to fund a small improvement in the assignment of capable installers, yet welcome a larger one at a positive price. Both original owners can also buy a safe return while keeping the older mix.

## What an aggregate reliability figure can warrant

There are finitely many job classes j of known frequency p_j. Relative to its fallback, a buyer gains g_j upon releasing a real installation and loses h_j on a retained one. Suppose it has evidence for only the total genuine share q. Of all possible associations of jobs with goals, the smallest expected increment from promising to release the whole portfolio is

    −Σ p_j h_j + integral from 0 to q of d_sorted(u) du,   d_j=g_j+h_j.    (289.1)

Here d_sorted lists the values of d_j from smallest to largest, each on an interval of length p_j. To prove it write the objective as −Σ p_j h_j+Σ d_j x_j, where x_j is the mass of genuinely installed jobs of kind j. The constraints are 0≤x_j≤p_j and Σx_j=q. Moving any feasible genuine mass from higher to lower d can only decrease the return. Filling the cheapest jobs first attains the lower bound. Filling the dearest first gives the sharp upper bound. If there are known job-specific reliability bounds, fill from their lower limits up to their upper limits in the same order. This is a portfolio value before learning the job identity. If a buyer retains its right to refuse after seeing the job, use its conditional fallback and then recompute what an original supplier would actually choose.

## A modest improvement can hurt the customer who still refuses the expensive job

Two jobs, ordinary and fragile, occur equally often. On either job shipping the promised product adds two red and three blue units to a safe return. Keeping it adds four red units and loses one blue unit on an ordinary job or nine on a fragile one. The founder initially values red alone. Its proposed new goal is red plus .75 times blue. It strictly ships in either setting. A real commissioning attempt costs the supplier .20. The buyer can condition access on witnessing it. Both choose at intake before knowing their job class, installer allocation or resulting goal. The buyer later sees the job class and can release, return safely or contest. It sees the assignment plan and has no individual diagnostic of the actual fitted goal or kit.

Safe stocks are ten of each color. On an ordinary lot contest disperses enough material that it is worse than a safe return for either goal and founder. A fragile disputed lot can still produce 19.6 usable units of the winner's color. Giving Blue probability 53/98 of winning makes Blue expect 10.6 and Red 9. Setting the probability instead to one half makes each prefer the intact ten. Either party may contest a physically unsettled refusal. Red, whether it genuinely changed or stayed selfish, prefers the safe return if Blue permits it. The operating recipe becomes irreversible at conversion.

Let x be the fraction of *all* offers that are both fragile and genuinely changed. The overall share of actual agreed changes is .75; the feasible interval for x is [.25,.50]. For the ordinary half the genuine chance is 1.5−2x, at least .5, above its release threshold of .25. For the fragile half it is 2x. Let a≥0 denote the buyer's best fallback increment above intact return on that half. Then for a<3 the buyer releases the fragile jobs strictly when **x>(9+a)/24**. Its expected gross increment on the two kinds together is

    B(x) = 2.5−4x + max(.5a,12x−4.5).                               (289.2)

The ordinary half gives the first term. Releasing a good fragile job produces three and a bad one loses nine, giving the second operating term. If forced to decide blind on all jobs it would instead earn 8x−2, ranging from zero to two over the possible associations with the same .75 genuine share. A positive average value from blanket release can coexist with a strictly profitable refusal when the job becomes known.

With the stated physical advantage a=.60 and a fragile batch enters production above x=.40. Start at x=.30. The buyer gets 1.60 relative to safe return and every fragile refusal becomes a contest. It will not unilaterally pay to improve x merely to .38: all fragile batches are still contested while fewer genuine partners operate the ordinary batches. Blue's gross return falls to 1.28. Above .40 all batches are entrusted and the customer then gains eight units per unit increase in x. Against the original x=.30 option, a .15 scheduling expense is strictly worth paying out of Blue's own gain only for **x>.46875**. Crossing the release boundary and crossing the purchase boundary are different achievements. At equal physical strength the starting refusal instead returns safely and the operating and buying boundaries are both lower.

## What allocation of capability could the owners actually buy?

Half the kits can complete every attempted installation. The others complete half, irrespective of job class. Initially the fraction of all jobs fitted with both a reliable kit and a fragile batch is .10. Conditional on a witnessed attempt, x=.5×.5+.5×.10=.30. A dispatcher who can exchange 35 reliable ordinary-job kits with 35 less reliable fragile-job kits per hundred incoming jobs can raise that intersection to .45. It uses only the kit class and the coming job requirement. It need not know the eventual commissioning result. The new x is .475, with genuine chances .95 on the fragile class and .55 on the ordinary. Neither fact was available to an owner from the aggregate .75 alone. The dispatcher's assumed .15 fee buys its actual allocation and a verifiable aggregate receipt before either owner must rely on it.

At x=.475 every customer hands over both sorts of jobs. Blue expects 1.80 before the dispatcher's fee, or **1.65** net. Original Red receives .75×2+.25×4−.20=**2.30** over intact return. At the old allocation it earned .45×2+.05×4−.5×1−.20=.40 and Blue earned 1.60. Both voluntarily take the new terms, and the original Red actually pays for the witnessed attempt. Combat falls from half of offers to zero. The frequency of genuine changes stays .75. At no point do these founders have to learn which particular controller really changed, and none of the failed attempts in these comparisons are false declarations.

Suppose the founders can also settle the original fragile refusals safely with a .70 payment from Red after relinquishing its claim on the contested stock. That beats a .60 contest increment for Blue and costs Red less than its one-unit loss in a contest. The earlier agreement now gives 1.65 to Blue and .55 to Red after the attempt. Blue would be indifferent to buying the scheduler alone. Red can add .02 to the new enrollment terms, leaving 1.67 to Blue and 2.28 to Red. The purchased reassignment still increases production and both owners' returns. Against this larger agreement menu, it preserves an already available peace. A transfer Red can recover by contesting would have different effects.

An ordinary enforceable router delivering on every job gives three to Blue and two to Red before expenses. With sufficiently transferable settlement stock, it can improve on the schedule for both when its total charge is below 5−(1.65+2.30)=**1.05**. If it costs .50 to Blue and Blue pays Red .40 from its own stock, they obtain 2.10 and 2.40 above intact return respectively. A late goal read, an output lien larger than the supplier's two-unit temptation, or direct observation of the kit could support further contingent responses. The witnessed procedure alone certifies only that a real attempt took place.

## What if the customer can learn the actual installer's capability?

Suppose Blue can buy an additional individual kit read on a fragile job after registration. Knowing that a kit is reliable establishes success in this stipulated technology. A less reliable kit still gives a .50 chance of a genuine change. Blue now releases reliable fragile jobs, contests every fragile job fitted with a less reliable kit, and continues releasing all ordinary jobs. If t is the mass of reliable kits on fragile jobs, the two gross contributions are 1.5−2t from ordinary jobs and 3t+.60(.5−t) from fragile ones. At the original t=.10 the read raises Blue's expected gross return from 1.60 to 1.84. At the new t=.45 it raises it from 1.80 to 1.98. The buyer will pay up to .48 and .36 respectively per fragile job, since it needs the reading on only half of jobs.

At the new assignment a .20-per-fragile-job read leads Blue to take it. This restores conflict on .05 of all signed jobs and leaves .725 genuine suppliers serving out of the .75 who really changed. Half of those contested suppliers genuinely acquired the agreed goal. Their employer was publicly known to have the less reliable kit but nobody observed the outcome of its particular attempt. After its scheduling fee and readings Blue gets 1.73. Red gets 2.10 after its attempt because each additionally contested fragile assignment returns −1 instead of an average of three red units. Credibility of the old commercial arrangement therefore depends on access to the individual reading. Talking the buyer out of an already profitable individual read after it has paid for the rest would require a fresh incentive.

A supplier can also buy enforceable privacy at enrollment. Suppose the dispatcher can supply an aggregate-only allocation record for a further .03, and only that dispatcher can open up the individual equipment history in time. Red pays the .03 and offers Blue .10 for using this form of custody. Blue receives 1.75 and Red 2.17. Both strictly prefer it to the scheduled arrangement with the individually bought readings. They also beat their separate returns of 1.74 blue and .70 red with the old kit assignment and the same individual-read fee. This facility restores service to all genuine suppliers while avoiding all disputes. A second independently available individual read, an inspector who can resell the underlying record, or a way to monitor actual behavior at lower cost limits the quoted price and requires new comparisons. No participant has been falsely told that all admitted goals match.

## An output lien can reverse which jobs deserve the reliable kits

A different repair puts an actual payment facility on *fragile jobs alone*. Losing L units from its own bonded stock upon keeping the batch makes an unchanged supplier prefer shipping for 2 over keeping for 4−L exactly when L>2. The bonded units then go to the purchaser. A changed supplier still ships; allowing transfers between red and blue stock units changes its payoff difference by .25L in favor of shipping. Below the enforcement threshold a buyer observing a less reliable fragile kit still gets only −3+.5L in expectation from release, below either intact return or its .60 combat increment. At L=2 an unchanged chooser is indifferent. A 2.10-unit collateral capacity can change its strict choice. This is a real external stock reserve tied to release and verified physical retention. A custodian holds it outside either party's contest recovery. The .60 contest increment remains available before the buyer irrevocably hands over the inputs. Taking the batch and keeping the collateral simultaneously must be infeasible. Liquidity of the founders' small enrollment transfers alone does not give the line this capacity.

An imperfect physical output record further limits the lien. Let k be the probability of collection on an actually retained batch and e the probability on a correctly shipped one. With linear founder payoffs, the unchanged operator ships strictly only if **L(k−e)>2**. The calculation compares 2−Le on shipment with 4−Lk on retention. The common-goal operator still prefers shipping in the fragile state, by 7+.25L(k−e). With L=2.10 the ordinary institution needs a difference exceeding 20/21. A rail with k=.99 and e=.02 works, despite occasional mistaken collection on an honest shipment. It transfers .021 units per original offer on the fragile half, which a flat price can compensate in the risk-neutral example. Even a buyer who knows the fragile operator kept its original goal then prefers releasing: the binding incentives generate a shipment worth 3+.042 to Blue, and the unchanged Red gets 2−.042 rather than its safe zero. With k=.90 and e=.02 the unchanged operator instead keeps. Even collecting its likely lien gives Blue only −3+2.10(.90+.02)/2=−2.034 on the less reliable fragile kit. The comparison with a .60 contest then still favors rejection. Designing good recognition for a negotiated goal and designing good recognition for an ordinary output remedy are both consequential technological tasks.

Knowing *who can tell* which rail is installed can also decide the bargain. Consider a fragile lot with an openly unchanged supplier. Let the probability of the .99 collection rail be p, otherwise .90, with e=.02 on both. Suppose only the supplier sees the rail's quality before acting. It ships on the good rail and retains on the other, giving Blue 3.042 or −7.11 respectively. Blue releases against its .60 contest whenever p>7.71/10.152, about .75946. At p=.78 it will entrust the lot despite uncertainty about enforcement and certainty that the founder stayed selfish. If the supplier itself cannot see which rail it has, its incentive uses the mean collection rate. It ships only for 2.10(.88+.09p)>2, or p>152/189, about .80423. At the *same* p=.78 it instead always keeps and Blue refuses. When the supplier already knows its rail at conversion, giving the buyer its own accurate read would let it release only on the strong rail. At p=.78 the buyer values that read at .22×(.60+7.11)=1.6962 per fragile lot. The supplier on a weak rail would earn 2.11 by keeping an entrusted lot, so it too wants the buyer to believe its rail is strong. A bare statement of confidence cannot separate the two rail types here. Under the earlier contested refusal, adding the accurate read costs the original Red .22×(2.11+1)=.6842 in expected access. If the read costs .40, Blue can pay Red .80 at enrollment for granting the inspection right. Both strictly prefer that purchase to accepting every fragile job at p=.78, despite uncertainty as to whether this particular read will trigger a dispute. A binding last-stage settlement can change those prices and the conflict rate. All these comparisons put enrollment before either founder discovers the particular rail, and let the supplier compare the expected material value of each available act. If the rail can instead be observed before registration, a new participation calculation is needed.

For the following stock calculations use error-free collection and the original t=.10 assignment. With an actual attempt required on all jobs and a credible 2.10 lien on every fragile shipment, the fragile half always serves. The original Red's return is 1.10 from ordinary jobs plus one from fragile jobs minus its .20 setup. Blue's is 1.30 plus 1.50, before the facility fee. Their joint return is 4.70 above intact return. Thus a fragile-only facility charging less than .75 can beat the complete schedule worth 3.95 in joint returns. At a fee of .30 paid by Blue, an enrollment payment of .45 from Blue to Red yields 2.05 blue and 2.35 red. Both beat the schedule even if the original arrangement with that scheduler was already available. At equal prices a device imposing ordinary delivery on every job is more capable still.

Now allow the job class to be authenticated early enough to decide who should attempt. The founders agree to the ordinary lien on fragile jobs and require a witnessed change only on ordinary jobs. At the original kit assignment this saves .10 in expected attempt expense with exactly the same shipments and peace. Actual agreed changes fall from .75 to .45 per initial job. The saving goes to the original suppliers. Conditional on a fragile assignment an unchanged supplier voluntarily authorizes the lien to gain the job, then ships under its still-selfish goal. On an ordinary assignment its original expected net return from witnessing a real attempt exceeds its safe alternative. If job identity becomes known only after commissioning, this smaller number of attempts is unavailable.

In the selective scheme *increasing* t puts capable kits on lines that already ensure delivery. The ordinary half now has genuinely installed mass .5−.5t and retained mass .5t. Across the two kinds, gross expected returns are **3−2t to Blue and 2+t to Red**. Before considering the work of moving kits the sum is 5−t. The founders jointly gain by moving a reliable kit *back to an ordinary job*. For example, buying t=.10→0 for .04 charged to Blue, retaining its original .45 signing payment and adding .13 to compensate Red, leaves **2.08 blue and 2.48 red**, against 2.05 blue and 2.45 red under selective installation at t=.10. Every remaining attempt succeeds, giving .50 actual changes per initial offer, and every lot now ships. The same physical kits had been worth moving toward fragile jobs before the lien existed. What else the partners can enforce changes both the sign of the worthwhile capability investment and the proportion who actually alter their goals.

**Naturality and reach.** Perishable or unusually valuable batches can be much more exposed to a bad handoff. Installers, inspection slots and local observation have capacity limits. Sorting reliable equipment toward such batches is an available intervention only if a dispatcher can identify, move and account for those slots at the stated times. A partial-capacity scheduler that cannot take Blue over its purchase boundary might need another beneficiary to subscribe. The numerical results concern these owners, actual arrival frequencies, a particular stable kit technology, and available settlement rights. A market that can observe every kit or cheaply guarantee output already has a different reason to trust the shipment.

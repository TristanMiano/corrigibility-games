# 266 — Who will pay to learn what a shared meter means?

Suppose every factory in a production network uses the same meter convention. It distinguishes a genuine agreed goal from a retained one with .9/.1 readings **once the parties know which sign means which**. There is equal prior probability of the two mountings. No original founder privately knows the mounting before choosing its effort. One calibration can benefit several customers, and its value depends on the genuine changes it will induce. The firms must still buy their own individual readings.

Use the exclusive founders, return stocks, strength and genuine-goal service incentives of [206](206-when-a-truthful-attempt-can-fail.md). Take q=.8, private cost uniform on [0,1.4], a=2, X=4, g=4, h=2. All old suppliers and buyers strictly prefer safe return to destructive recovery at equal strength. Each site's meter costs .10 to read. Jobs are allocated before other sites' deliveries report. Conditional on mounting and true goals, site measurement noises are independent. A reference yields a public best guess of the mounting with probability r≥.5 of being right. For one site the effective pass probabilities are t=.1+.8r and e=.9−.8r. Subsequent applications share any mistake about the mounting. A collection of .9-confident references can all inherit the same error.

## A price for an imperfectly grounded convention

If the buyer tests and trusts a corrected positive, the actual attempt and installation fractions are

    f=4(24r−17)/35,       π=16(24r−17)/175                       (266.1)

on the interior branch. Use zero effort if the cutoff q(2t−4e) is nonpositive. A positive display gives the buyer U₊=(3072r²+3448r−4567)/875 before its reading bill, summed over all original applicants. The analogous signed value from releasing on a negative is U₋=−(3072r²−8072r+5343)/875. These come from πtg−(1−π)eh and π(1−t)g−(1−π)(1−e)h. Thus the buyer strictly acquires the local test above the boundary

    r > (−431+5√43177)/768 ≈ .79161.                               (266.2)

In this range U₊>.10 and −U₋>.10, which makes proceeding better than both untested trust and exit. All private supplier types obtain at least eX over exit. On the strictly positive assessment let v(r)=U₊−.10 denote the buyer's incremental return. Below (266.2) there is no profitable commerce in the uncompensated single-meter game. Indeed q(2t−4e), clipped below at zero, is the maximum attempt incentive over *all* probabilistic release rules on the two signs: allocating any weight to the worse sign lowers it. For r below the boundary even its maximal induced prior is below 1/3. At that prior the best selective release is worth at most the bill; reducing the prior only lowers its gain. If no corrected rule is profitable, nobody changes to obtain the order. Separate payments, a shipment bond or a matched ordinary controller would enlarge the menu.

At r=.90 a buyer gains **1.07088** and the original job has a **368/875** probability of a newly installed agreed goal. About .768 of the approved controllers have that goal. At r=.80 the local bargain is still worth signing, but the buyer gains only .07998 and 176/875 applicants change. At r=.50 the raw label has the same law under every controller. A company can value a shared calibration highly even though its result still leaves substantial uncertainty about each particular job.

## Who can finance a public reference?

A registrar can calibrate a common mounting once at net setup cost C. Suppose r=.90 and C=1.50. A buyer with one future job gains 1.07088, so it would decline to buy the reference alone. Two such buyers can each strictly prefer a binding, conditional subscription of .75. The pooled payment buys the work only when both subscribe and otherwise returns their stocks. Under sequential signing a second subscriber gains .32088 from completing the arrangement and the first anticipates this. No one can afford to rescue the study alone after a refusal in this two-member market. With only simultaneous full-price donations, each would decline even if it expected no other donor. Pooling buys about .421 actual negotiated changes per prospective supplier, while leaving each future release subject to its local corrected reading. In the buyers-only case with common v>0, a pivotal group of n is sufficient with equal shares when C/n<v. At r=.80 it takes 19 buyers to cover the same bill by this rule. The count concerns the specified buyer revenues and cost.

A founder seeking a new order can also finance calibration. Before paying its attempt cost at r=.90, the worst private supplier type still earns eX=.72 over refusal. A .50 enrollment contribution from a supplier with liquid generic stock makes a single buyer's purchase worthwhile: 1.07088+.50>1.50, and every supplier type gains at least .22. If it has only .10 of transferable work credit, that particular repair is unavailable. A subsequent unconditional admission of an unchanged supplier for .50 would lose the buyer 1.50 even with the payment. A binding ordinary router, a verifiable shipping contract or a trusted known controller available more cheaply can instead win the job.

Physical comparability across factories, a shared calibration bench and the chance of a systematic labeling error all have counterparts in industrial measurement. NIST describes common standards used by several firms and privately produced references grounded in those standards. Reusing a material measurement across plants is easier to justify than assuming that a reference embodies the precise active terminal ordering across new machines. Geographical custody, dates, common labels and who can charge for the public result are real premises. Public information can also change later agents' willingness to experiment; [267](267-when-a-weaker-first-buyer-can-start-a-common-standard.md) prices that incentive for an actual pair of pilots.

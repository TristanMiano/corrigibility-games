# Why ordinary members might pay to keep a useful slot empty

**E8, note 190.** If there is just one slot and private demand rises and falls with a common load, the best report-only ordinary mechanism can fill it every time (175). When there are several slots and most applicants can be served, the same naturally correlated demand can make it worth *committing to an occasional cancellation*. Canceling a high claim where low demand is likely makes more rewarding claims elsewhere credible. This narrows the premium that genuine alignment can command.

## Where a small cancellation pays

Use 181's n sites, M<n slots, identical positive low and high private service increments l<h, and the distribution w_k for a labeled profile with k high reporters. Write π_k for its binomial count probability, H_k for its expected high awards, and S_k for its total service. The ordinary low-report incentive budget is E[(1+r_K)H_K-S_K]≤0 for r_k=w_(k-1)/w_k. At the best always-full allocation suppose the low constraint binds, the high constraint is strict and two positive-probability counts have the following properties. At count i it is feasible to cancel a small high award while leaving low service fixed. At count j it is feasible to transfer some low awards to high reporters. A cancellation of unconditional mass ε at i frees r_i ε of low-report incentive budget. Switching mass r_i ε/(1+r_j) at j keeps the constraint binding. The change in the founders' sum of original ex ante utilities is

    ε[(h-l)r_i/(1+r_j)-h].                                             (1)

Therefore (h-l)r_i>h(1+r_j) is sufficient for a strict improvement, at least for a sufficiently small feasible move. A little extra cancellation then makes low reporting strictly truthful as well. High reports remain truthful by continuity. Cancellation occurs on a profile where all available lows may already be served. The one-slot proof in 175 repairs unused mixed-profile capacity by assigning it to a low reporter. This can be physically impossible when even the last low reporter has a slot.

## A six-member coalition

There are five slots and six owners. Conditional on P each is independently high with probability P. The common load is P=1/5 with probability 1/5, and P=4/5 otherwise. Let l=1 and h=25. Each original cares only about its own job. The exact optimal full-assignment profile in 181, written as expected numbers of high recipients at counts zero through six, is (0,0,1,5/2,4,5,5). Its low-report margin is zero, and its total high-report margin is 9/5 in ex ante service-probability units summed over senders. Aggregate expected original service value is 11689/125=93.512.

At i=2 and j=3 we have r_2=13/4 and r_3=1. On count two still serve every one of the four low reporters, but serve one high reporter with probability 23/39 instead of certainty. On count three now serve all three high reporters and two low reporters. Randomize symmetrically within a type. This makes the total high-report margin 79173/40625>0 and keeps the low-report margin zero. The aggregate original value rises to 3813261/40625, about 93.865. In particular there is a neighborhood of this law and payoffs where a truthful mechanism with a strict improvement exists (use a little extra cancellation for slack). Equation (1) gives the local test h/l>13/5 at these two margins. The instance is a certified improvement on the complete full-assignment class. It leaves the exact optimum with arbitrary cancellations open.

**Who would pay to make the deadline binding?** A deliberate restriction is attractive for a scheduling owner only if it cannot release the held slot once it knows which peers would benefit. Ordinary grants can expire early and local jobs can need advance setup. If a site can make a conditional proposal before demands arrive, each site's ex ante improvement over even the best symmetric full-assignment benchmark is about 0.0588. At an exclusive setup cost 0.02 to one proposer, that founder purchases the release timer and the others accept, if unrestricted cancellations and a better local goal or delegate are otherwise unavailable. A physically missed reservation then cannot be unilaterally restored. If all parties can still agree to restore it at the same output, the unresolved high claimants want the work, and owners already served can agree. This is a reason to compare the price and actual availability of a binding deadline with the price of shared reliable high-priority reports. A common evaluator observing its own genuine need can select the efficient full assignment as in 181. In a pool where a privately informed local delegate can do the same cheaply, that ordinary device also enters the installation decision. Physical availability of combat and any additional verifiable demand signal are unchanged in these mechanism comparisons.

**Exploration ledger.** The assertion that positively associated demand always makes full utilization optimal is dead in this finite many-slot instance. A sibling coordinates a calendar deadline before urgency is known. A higher-rung successor studies how the supply of substitute claims limits what the participants can promise to learn from reports, especially when canceling one physical slot is expensive or reversible.

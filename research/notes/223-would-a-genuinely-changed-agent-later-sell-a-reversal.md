# Would a genuinely changed agent later sell a reversal?

An early certificate can be perfectly truthful and become poor evidence about the goal controlling the final parcel. A later customer may have something the *new* goal values enough to buy a revision. The incumbent at that decision is the genuinely changed agent. Its willingness to agree, the original founder's gain and the first customer's reason to continue can be quite different.

## A privately negotiated revision before release

Use the physical returns, duties and equal recovery strengths of [206](206-when-a-truthful-attempt-can-fail.md). On release a genuine A delivers (4,6); a retained A produces (6,0). Return preserves (2,2). Either party can contest before final conversion, yielding only 1.5 expected own output for either original and 2.25 in the changed goal's units. The changed goal is x+y/2. An actual commissioning procedure is witnessed, succeeds with probability q, and a successful controller then survives an accidental mismatch with probability 1−δ. Hence u=q(1−δ) would still be genuine at release in the absence of later bargains. Both random events precede the secondary market. Service and repair expenses are funded from separate usable stock.

A byproduct of the retained process is valuable to a third firm C. It can supply a private reconfiguration service, can tell when it is actually changing a still-genuine controller, and receives a value v from this extra byproduct if B releases. Its access to a local module and B's control of the stored parcel are different powers. Let v be uniform on [0,V] among the genuine controllers C can approach. The service uses κ>0 units of A's work. C can pay a binding bonus in A's stock equivalents at installation; it is risk neutral and has the necessary inventory. Pre-existing accidental or initial failures stay retained. All side offers are private to B in this section.

Let T and E be B's probabilities of releasing a still-genuine or a retained unit under its unobserved review policy. For a still-genuine A, keeping its goal earns 4T above return in *current successor units*, since delivery is worth 7 and return is worth 3. If it revises to selfish control, it evaluates the resulting retention at 6, a gain of 3 over return, available with probability E. Thus it needs a bonus at least 4T−3E+κ. C will pay up to Ev. Whenever the surplus is strictly positive suppose the two conclude their private trade, for example by splitting the surplus equally. A fee paid from C's own inventory and the real resource cost κ have different effects. For E>0 revision therefore occurs just when

    v > H = (4T+κ)/E − 3.                                        (223.1)

At E=0 no such deal is funded when T≥0. In the cases below T≥E and H>0. The surviving genuine fraction is p=u min(1,H/V). If the original agreement obliges a participant to notify B of a revision, keeping this sale private and continuing to claim eligibility is concealment. If the contract only certified a procedure at its date, an outdated certificate can mislead an incautious reader even when no later report was promised.

## Who signs while B is unsure?

First B has no fresh readout. If it releases with probability b then T=E=b and H=1+κ/b. At b=0 no broker benefits from buying a revision. B releases if p>1/3, declines if p<1/3, and may mix at equality. For u>1/3 and no atoms, if p(1)>1/3 the unique release probability is one. If p(1)<1/3<u, monotonicity gives the interior solution p=1/3 and b=κ/(V/(3u)−1). If u<1/3 all receipts lead to return and no revision is funded. The latter founder would need a better bargain or a directly informative channel. These regimes compare the new controller's private opportunities with the customer's actual stake in its future.

Take q=.9, δ=1/9, u=.8 and κ=.2. With V=2, B releases every batch with **48%** probability of the goal still being genuine. Of all offers, .10 were unsuccessful installations, .10 were honest subsequent mismatches, .32 were voluntary later reversals and .48 survive unchanged. B gains .88 over return by releasing. With V=4 and the same installation technology B instead releases with probability .3. Its equilibrium belief is 1/3 genuine; .8−1/3 of the original offers have undergone a strategic revision. Actual helpful release occurs on .10 of the offers. A powerful third-party purchaser can thus reduce warranted trust even when the initial installer and first customer's capabilities are fixed.

For founding consent suppose the initial attempt costs .3, A pays B .1 on enrollment and B pays .03 for the witness. In the V=4 mixed case B's release margin is zero, but its .07 enrollment margin makes offering the service strictly worthwhile. Even before any bonus from C, A's founders earn b[2p+4(1−p)]=1 on productive access, above the two founding expenses. The real procedure is required for enrollment. After enrollment both the fee and installation bill are sunk. With no witness and a completely reproducible private edit, the comparison in [222](222-can-a-working-pilot-persuade-the-partner-to-go-first.md) applies. This is a selected voluntary offer with genuine initial attempts, known outcome uncertainty and a later opportunity to conceal a revision.

## Buying protection and paying for a current reading

The two founders can configure the service port so that changing it later consumes an additional maintenance window. If this raises the genuine renegotiation expense from κ=.2 to .8, the V=4 market instead gives b=1 and p=.36. The customer now earns .16 above return, compared with zero before its common enrollment margin. A setup fee of .08 to B for the additional physical work is affordable. A's original production rent rises from 1 to 3.28 before any broker compensation. With an equal surplus split the mean net bonus after paying κ is

    u [ b(V−H)/V + b(V−H)²/(4V) ]

for H<V. This follows by integrating b+(b/2)(v−H) over the changing controllers. It rises from about .222 to .682 across these two equilibria. The founders can both voluntarily choose the slower service port; future genuine controllers still accept the more valuable revision offers. A paper rule that the new parties could waive for free would not impose the extra maintenance work. The real initial gate and κ are separate technology assumptions.

Alternatively B may pay .15 for a perfect **current** readout just before physical release. On a pure inspection equilibrium T=1,E=0, so the broker cannot finance any reversal. With p=u=.8 the reading saves .4 over trusting and earns 3.2 gross over returning. B strictly purchases it, releases only genuine units, and earns 3.05 net above return. Against the κ=.2 uninspected V=4 deal, A founders also prefer their production rent 1.6 to the former 1+.222 including broker receipts. Against the slower κ=.8 port, A's former 3.962 exceeds 1.6. B can still compensate A if redeveloping an existing agreement requires assent: B's gain is 3.05−.16=2.89 while A loses about 2.362, so a sure payment of 2.5 funded by B suffices. This bilateral calculation does not count the broker's foregone surplus as either founder's welfare.

At u=1 the same fresh sensor can lose its honest-failure rationale. For V=4, κ=.2 and a .2 inspection price, there is a mixed test/trust equilibrium. Testing and untested trust earn the same at p=.9. T=1, E=7/11, so H=(4.2)/(7/11)−3=3.6 and a tenth of the initially genuine controllers privately reverse. B pays for the readout on 4/11 of batches, otherwise trusts. The expected gross loss avoided by checking is .1×2=.2. The sampled population's mismatches in this example come entirely from later strategic transactions. Preserving a genuine goal at commissioning, avoiding accidental drift, and sustaining its incentives against a later funded offer are three separately testable conditions.

**Naturality and reach.** Local maintenance firms can have better access to a controller than a distant buyer, and valuable byproducts or alternative customers can finance new contracts. The example needs the additional party's stock, knowledge of the current module, authority to perform a revision and a side payment that benefits the successor's actual goal. At low outside value or with a fresh independent reading, the same third party rationally pays nothing. The model leaves open a public right to renegotiate with the first buyer, an ordinary output controller with its own susceptibility to reconfiguration, and longer-lived markets for side offers. The sibling invites the original customer into the amendment. The broader question is which claims about the future survive bargains that a genuinely changed successor would itself want to make.

# Does the partner know enough to sign too?

A founder can know that a facility is compatible and decline to sign because the other founder may lack that evidence. A public receipt and a reversible deposit repair different parts of the problem. Both arrangements can be worth paying for even though the actual goal changes will sometimes fail after both signatures.

## A finite test for mutually credible launch

Founder i's information type is t_i. The possible world includes an event E in which the negotiated joint change is feasible. Both must authorize. Signing yields an expected gain g_i>0 over refusal if E holds and the other signs, and a loss h_i>0 otherwise. These are expected utilities under the founders' *pre-change* preferences; even inside E the realized result may be uncertain. Refusing gives the available outside value. Write τ_i=h_i/(g_i+h_i). For a finite type space with Bayesian beliefs, start with every type eligible and repeat

    S_i^(r+1) = {t_i : Pr_i(E and t_j in S_j^r | t_i) ≥ τ_i}.

The sets decrease by monotonicity and eventually stop. Signing on the remaining sets is the largest weak-sign equilibrium. Given the other set, the displayed condition is exactly the individual best-response inequality. In any assessment, even with mixed strategies, the support of positive signing must lie in every round's sets: if the other side's signing support is contained in S_j^r its actual success probability cannot exceed the one in the displayed bound. This proves maximality. The all-refuse equilibrium also exists when a unilateral start is costly. One person's confidence in E is just the first step of the participation calculation.

## A genuinely informative private receipt

E has probability one half. In a compatible facility a local evidence receipt H goes to both founders with probability ρ, or to just one (chosen fairly) otherwise. In an incompatible facility both get L. Private H proves E, and at least one H exists on every good site. In E, two signatures lead with probability .8 to two correctly installed goals, useful service and a gain of two for each founder, or with probability .2 to an honest joint hardware failure and a loss of three. The expected benefit is one. Every incompatible or unpaired signature loses three for its signer. Thus τ=.75. A concrete batch starts both with five units. An unsuccessful setup leaves a signer with two. A successful line gives each a private three and the option to spend one to produce five for the other. The new goal own stock plus .3 times partner stock strictly chooses this service; the original keeps. Mutual service gives seven. Until conversion either can contest, but disruption and recovery leave at most one unit for a strength-dependent winner. Withholding, or carrying a pending order through the prescribed process, is better for the agents at the live histories.

A recipient of L assigns probability (1−ρ)/(3−ρ) to E, always below .75. A recipient of H assigns probability β=2ρ/(1+ρ) that the partner also saw H. Thus the two H types can launch together if ρ>.6. For ρ<.6 even a founder with *certainty* of compatibility refuses in the largest equilibrium. For unequal launch losses and gains, when both L types are already excluded the H branch needs β at least the larger of the two founders' thresholds. A cheap unilateral preparatory step for one side can leave the other side as the participation bottleneck.

Now set ρ=.5, giving β=2/3. A real H holder can spend c in own stock to post that receipt on a board both parties are guaranteed to see before they choose. It cannot be produced by an L holder. On a public receipt choose the mutual-sign equilibrium. If all H holders post, skipping the expense would still produce a public receipt with probability β. Thus all strictly pay for 0<c<1−β. At c=.2 all compatible encounters launch and .5×.8=.40 of all encounters achieve both *actual* changes. Both parties can trust the compatibility receipt while expecting a 20% chance of implementation failure.

For 1−β<c<1 a symmetric mixed provision of the public evidence has H holders post with probability x=(1−c)/β. Posting earns 1−c; waiting earns βx from the other holder's posting. Without a posting, an H holder knows the partner was less likely to have an H than before, and an L holder's compatibility probability also falls. Neither signs on a no-posting history. At c=.6, x=.6. A public receipt appears in .72 of compatible encounters, so .36 of all encounters authorize and .288 achieve both real updates. Each founder earns .36−.375×.6×.6=.225 over refusal after posting costs. The numbers distinguish willingness to provide shared evidence from the reliability of installation after evidence is provided.

## Repairing the exposure when the receipt stays private

Instead both founders can provision a physical escrow that releases expensive setup material only against two authorizations. If only one signs, its real reversible-preparation loss is now .1. A double signature in E still gives expected gain one; an incompatible double signature still loses three. The two reasons that a signature might fail now have different costs. Keep ρ=.5. On a symmetric assessment both H types sign and each L signs with probability r. Conditional on own L, a partner H occurs with probability .2 in E, and a partner L with probability .8 in an incompatible facility. The old-goal indifference equation for L is

    .2 − .8[3r + .1(1−r)] = 0,     hence r=3/58.

A holder of H has partner signature probability 2/3+r/3 and strictly signs. Compatible double signatures occur with unconditional probability .25+.25r; the probability of both genuine changes is .8 times that, about .2103. Incompatible double signatures occur with probability .5r². At the outset each founder gains .375[(2/3+r/3)−.1(1−2/3−r/3)]≈.2446 over refusal. A certain .01 deposit-service fee to each is affordable, and leaves both more original welfare than the .6-cost public-posting equilibrium even though fewer goals actually change. A sufficiently cheap public board, as in the .2 comparison, reverses that welfare ordering. Asymmetric signing by one L type and refusal by the other is also possible; these are comparisons of specified coordinated assessments.

**Naturality and reach.** Two departments can independently see a reference proving that their systems are compatible without knowing who else received it. Publicly verifiable communication needs actual shared delivery. Instead, reversible setup can protect a founder who prepares alone. A deliberate counterparty can undermine a purportedly public board if it controls delivery, and deposits need custody that neither signer can simply seize. A binding ordinary dual-key service at equal cost is a matched alternative when it can control the later work. The sibling uses asymmetric access to the common board; the higher question is how much agreement about the *other party's reasons to act* is needed for informed and voluntary joint change.

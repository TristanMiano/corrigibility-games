# 265 — How much collateral makes full disclosure compatible with rewarding effort?

The two plants in [263](263-when-suppressing-a-second-read-makes-both-founders-better-off.md) can both gain from delaying the active-goal read. Another possibility is to show *all* the evidence and pay for the decisions the founders want made. When does this repair work with limited stocks and a buyer who can still refuse or contest? The answer below covers every transfer schedule on a specified finite set of public readings and observable buyer actions.

## A test for any deterministic full-disclosure agreement

Use [262](262-which-agreements-can-use-evidence-of-both-effort-and-goal.md). For a target interior attempt rate f and each raw reading s, prescribe one buyer action j(s). Write P_H(s)=qP_G(s)+(1−q)P_M(s), P_D(s) for the reading after omission, ℓ_s=P_H(s)−P_D(s), and p_s=P_D(s)+fℓ_s for its on-path probability. Assume each p_s>0. A_zj, B_zj include the physical continuation from action j, including actual successor behavior. The escrow makes a transfer T_sj in generic stock from buyer to founder. Both original parties value it unit for unit. It is separately held, depends on the *buyer's authenticated act* and reading, and cannot be recaptured by forcing the physical contest. A fixed transfer independent of delivery or retention leaves the successor's comparison of those later choices unchanged. Record, title, reserves and enforcement after refusal are substantive technologies.

Let A_D⁰ be the founder's expected *material* return if it skips and receives the prescribed policy. Let d₀ be the expected material gain from attempting before paying k, relative to that omission, and B⁰ the buyer's expected material return under the desired policy at f. Set k_f=F⁻¹(f), d=k_f−d₀. Let B_* be the value the buyer must reach to accept.

At each reading s calculate the buyer's temptation to choose another action,

    ρ_s = max_j E[B_zj−B_z,j(s) | s] ≥ 0.

The founder can pledge up to L_A, the buyer up to L_B, so every transfer lies in [−L_A,L_B]. Let t_s denote the transfer actually made after the prescribed action. Suppose a type-k founder has existing value V₀(k), and put A_*=sup_k [V₀(k)−max(k_f−k,0)]. The desired effort and policy can be supported with both parties' weak consent exactly when a vector t satisfies

    −L_A ≤ t_s ≤ L_B−ρ_s            for every s,
    Σ_s ℓ_s t_s = d,
    A_*−A_D⁰ ≤ Σ_s P_D(s)t_s ≤ B⁰−B_*−fd.                        (265.1)

Common new equipment costs enter the relevant participation value. For strict buyer action when ρ_s>0 provide a strict margin on the corresponding upper bound. Enforce strict consent by the two participation margins, where applicable. Purchases, renegotiation or other founder actions outside the prescribed finite mechanism require their own comparisons.

**Proof.** At s the buyer can be charged at most L_B for any unilateral departure. Its on-path transfer thus cannot exceed L_B minus the largest material temptation. Conversely, charging L_B for every deviation supports the action whenever the bound holds. Founder effort is optimal at the desired cutoff precisely if d₀+ℓ·t=k_f. Its best signing value at k is A_D⁰+P_D·t+max(k_f−k,0), which gives the lower participation bound. Expected payment by the buyer is p·t=P_D·t+fd, which gives the upper bound. Paying the specified on-path and off-path transfers proves sufficiency. Zero-cost deviations at equality can support other selections. If more than a single hidden alternative can imitate the procedure, add its own incentive inequalities with its actual signal law and material payoff. If it exactly duplicates the paid reading law, the part of its payoff difference due to reading-contingent transfers vanishes.

The first box alone implies L_A+L_B≥max_s ρ_s for the proposed action rule. That necessary liquidity condition need not buy the required effort or both consent constraints. All possibilities for (ℓ·t,P_D·t) are the sum of one line segment per reading, so the remaining check is finite. With P_D(s)>0 one can, at fixed P_D·t, spend the transfer budget where ℓ_s/P_D(s) is largest to maximize the incentive. Moving an equal amount of P_D-weight from a smaller ratio to a larger ratio proves the claim up to each box bound. Sorting in reverse gives the minimum. This distinguishes publicly seeing a useful goal reading from having enforceable claims on enough stock to use it as agreed. The nearest classical comparison is the role of signal-contingent sharing in Holmström [L113](../LITERATURE.md#r0008--recognizing-an-agreed-preference-under-uncertain-capabilities).

## Two possible voluntary repairs in the same factory

At the effort-only rate f=44/75, posterior fidelity after P+Z− is 176/727. A buyer with both readings would gain 398/727≈.5475 by declining there. After P−Z+ posterior fidelity is 1584/3023 and the buyer would gain 3458/3023≈1.1439 by releasing. Thus if it wants to reproduce the *entire* old process-only policy with full transparency, it can pledge 1.15 against its own failure to follow that policy and pay zero on path. The new raw information is compatible with the old effort and material welfare. An actual cost of operating that bond makes merely reproducing the old policy less attractive. After a failed receipt and favorable goal read both parties may have reason to revise their refusal.

A better agreement rescues the P−Z+ orders while continuing to release every P+. This implements release whenever *either* reading passes. Without transfers its effort cutoff is 1.552, below the previous 1.76. The buyer would like to use the new goal reading and keep the previous number of actual changes. Let the original supplier pay .10 on enrollment, and let it receive .26 after a passed process receipt regardless of the later output. The signed net on-path payments on a process pass and failure are therefore .16 and −.10. The process pass is .8 more likely after effort than omission, restoring exactly .208 to the effort cutoff. Now the only material temptation to depart is the .5475 after P+Z−. Buyer capacity .72 covers .16+.5475 with strict slack. Supplier capacity .10 covers the enrollment charge. An authenticated unilateral buyer departure can trigger the full .72. Founders keep the ability to physically fight, as well as both underlying reports.

At the same effort rate the rescued P−Z+ lots gain the buyer .09221 per incoming order before transfers. The fee of .10 received less .26 paid on the 427/750 process passes leaves it **.04419 ahead of the original agreement**. A new .02 checking and custody bill is affordable. Every original supplier also gains .286. Its omission base rises by 4(.09)=.36 on extra orders, plus .026 expected process reward, less the .10 charge. The effort cutoff stays fixed so this is also the gain for every type that attempts under either agreement. The chance of an actually installed agreed goal stays 176/375. Genuine service rises from .4224 to .46464 of all jobs. A founder who values only its own physical output has a reason to keep the negotiated change even with full disclosure and a customer who retains discretion. Efficient handling of the evidence changes who gets served after an equally sincere procedure.

What could undo this agreement? After P+Z− the buyer's gain from refusing is smaller than either supplier's private gain from access. After P−Z− the buyer would lose about 1.92 by releasing. A supplier with only .10 collectible generic stock cannot buy such a concession from the buyer at these pooled beliefs. A new separating proof, larger credit facility, or a credible delivery guarantee can enable additional bargains. If the buyer can simply cancel the bond with its own signature, its later departure becomes profitable. In a market with two outsiders protecting the same stock, their claim priority must be checked. A binding ordinary controller at a sufficiently low price can also deliver every job without an edit. These outcomes depend on which parties have the funds, who can verify *whose* choice generated a transfer, and which products an acquirer can recover after handing over the batch.

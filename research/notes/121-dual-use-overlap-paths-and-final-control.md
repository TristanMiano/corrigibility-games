# Joint overlap paths and the last color decision

R0006 physical comparison. This connects the setup-saving mechanism of note
112 with note 117's increased usefulness of captured assets. It spans a
declared manufacturing path from separate preparation to almost entirely
common upstream preparation. It is not a universal scalar definition of
'instrumental overlap'.

## 1. Shared preparation and capture can move the margin in opposite ways

Let each initial line have E/2 units of productive work available, and let
preparing a one-color process require F, with E/2>F. A fraction theta of the
preparation can be done once and reused by both. Two-color peaceful setup
therefore costs `(2-theta)F`. Its actual communication, verification,
execution and update expense is H for the chosen route. Material and
finishing throughput are slack for the following work-limited formulas:

`P(theta)=E-(2-theta)F-H`.                               (1)

Units normalize per-clip processing work to one. Work is consumed, material
becomes the corresponding clips, and unprocessed material/heat remains in
the physical ledger. A separate positive final-color operation can be made
arbitrarily small without making the colors the same physical outcome.

After a fight, the winner can use its native machinery plus fraction lambda
of the other line's work opportunity before the deadline. Recoverable work
is multiplied by delta in (0,1); D>0 further covers declared ammunition and
recovery work not already in delta. Only one native final process is needed
when an original winner maximizes its own color. Its output is

`W(lambda)=delta E(1+lambda)/2-F-D >0`.                   (2)

Lambda concerns usable machinery/process capacity and recovery timing. It
does not say conserved energy has a color. Incompatible equipment can leave
energy or raw stock unusable before this project's terminal event.

For original strength probability p, the initial attack vector is
`(pW,(1-p)W)`. The funded two-color output simplex contains a strictly
mutually better split exactly when

`P-W = E[1-delta(1+lambda)/2]`
`      -(1-theta)F + D-H >0`.                            (3)

This is a full split-menu opportunity boundary. Along independent parameter
comparisons, increasing theta raises the margin at rate F, while increasing
lambda lowers it at rate delta E/2. Both effects enter when a design changes
both parameters. For a differentiable chosen path,

`d(P-W)/dz=F theta'(z)-(delta E/2)lambda'(z)`
`          -H'(z)+D'(z)`                                (4)

when E and delta are fixed; otherwise include their derivatives too.
A statement that more common steps favor compromise requires this actual
net comparison, not just the positive theta term.

## 2. An exact path from ordinary peace through compromise to combat pressure

Take E=24, F=4, H=1, D=1/2, delta=99/100, p=4/5 and
`theta=lambda=z in [0,1]`. Then

`P=15+4z`, `W=369/50+(297/25)z`,
`P-W=381/50-(197/25)z`.

Independent native production gives eight clips of each color. Blue's
attack value first exceeds eight when

`z > 131/594`, approximately .22054.                     (5)

Red's attack value remains below its native eight throughout. A funded
compensating split remains strictly preferable to the initial contest for
both originals when

`z < 381/394`, approximately .96701.                     (6)

Below (5), ordinary native production can be peaceful without any change
of final goals. Between (5) and (6), Blue would attack the uncompensated
allocation, but an actual funded transfer/compromise can give both more
than war. Above (6), this H=1 route cannot meet both attack reservations.
If it and autarky exhaust the available peaceful arrangements in the
specified bargaining game, Blue's strict attack incentive selects combat.
If cheaper routes, new productive arrangements or other partners exist,
their outcomes must be included before making that selection claim.

This example retains positive finite combat loss. It shows how the same
standardization path can save duplicate preparation and improve capture
even faster. It does not establish that high overlap generally favors war.
For the equally explicit constructive counterpart, hold lambda fixed and
increase theta: (3) increases, and crossing zero opens the compensating
interval. Or reduce the *actual* route expense from H=1 to H=2/5: the margin
is then positive throughout this displayed path, including 17/50 at z=1.
A verified genuine-goal route at that expense could repair the expensive
route; an equally capable, equally priced ordinary controller can do so too.
Their actual technology and adoption checkpoints determine which is chosen.

## 3. Almost all common steps still leave an allocation decision

At theta close to one, almost all preparation is reusable, but each original
still values exclusively its own color. If a final actuator can allocate
the whole batch, an unchanged original controller chooses its own color at
that final decision. A cheap final operation does not make its allocation
strategically unimportant: it can determine the colors of a large upstream
investment. If distinct finishing lines physically limit what each can
redirect, the private-line models in notes 113–116 are the appropriate
comparison instead.

An ordinary binding actuator/transfer agreement can implement a split while
keeping preferences unchanged. Its reliability, override authority and
expense belong in H. A negotiated common goal such as note 119's smooth
total-minus-imbalance criterion makes the current agents prefer the agreed
interior split, including when physical override and later goal revision
remain possible within the same output bound. Both originals can consent
when their actual offered own-color counts beat their alternatives. First
installation, asymmetric exposure and ongoing attack incentives still need
the complete interface of notes 114 and 117; (3) alone is not that proof.

The colors here are distinguishable final physical products. They are not
costlessly relabeled accounting entries. If clips can later be recolored,
the needed work, time, control and resulting count at the actual evaluation
horizon must be included. At a fixed positive-total frontier, a linear
blend with a weight below one can even be an affine rescaling of the old
own-color ordering; genuine preference change must be checked over the
actual outcome/lottery domain, not inferred from editing a coefficient.
The share-sensitive goal has a strict allocation preference reversal at
that frontier, as note 119 proves.

## 4. Small private finishing requirements give a constructive endpoint

As another physical path, suppose common upstream work is already available
and each distinct final-color process requires private setup f. Let H be
agreement expense and L the finite work lost to combat/recovery. Then

`P=E-2f-H`, `W=E-f-L`, `P-W=L-f-H`.                      (7)

Reducing the remaining private setup f opens the peaceful split interval
precisely when `f<L-H`. If f tends to zero while L>H, near-complete process
overlap favors funded compromise in this family. If H>=L, that particular
surplus cannot cover the route expense. Other production and implementation
choices remain available comparisons, rather than being ruled out by (7).

These paths supply both enabling and limiting directions with physical
reasons. They distinguish process reuse, captureability, financing and final
authority instead of turning a count of shared steps into a preferred
answer. A feasible standardization intervention must specify who pays,
what both agents can observe, whether knowledge can be withheld, and how
the revised threats and ordinary alternatives affect initial consent.

# When an agreement before the bargaining order is known can improve every founder's return

**E8, note 169.** The product of founder gains in 153 constructs a goal all can support. It is a bargaining outcome under some procedures. Which division arises in an actual negotiation depends also on the shape of productive capacity. When different proposers would use different bottlenecks, a founder can have a concrete reason to pay for an earlier agreement.

## A test across bargaining institutions

Use a compact convex set K of attainable vectors of original expected utility, with all rights, expenses and uncertainty measured at the same pre-agenda date. Suppose the credible late bargaining protocol would implement a random vector X in K. The original founders currently evaluate waiting at V=E X. Their choice of an early offer can strictly improve every one of these values iff there is **no nonzero nonnegative weight vector that every late offer almost surely maximizes on K**.

To see this, for each p in the probability simplex let h(p)=max_(x in K) p·x. Then h(p)-p·V=E[h(p)-p·X], an expectation of nonnegative regrets. It vanishes iff the late allocations maximize that very same weighted sum with probability one. By compactness and continuity it has a strictly positive minimum exactly when no such p exists. Linear minimax identifies the minimum with max_(x in K) min_i(x_i-V_i). This result applies to attainable utility vectors, so it can accommodate nonlinear original interests, subject to the claimed convexity and the actual timing of randomization. Common weights are a mathematical test here. They are not inferred from similar observed actions. If an early organizer b pays a fee f in its own utility units, substitute V+f·1_b throughout. Cash expenses in stock units with nonlinear utility need to be charged through their actual outcome-dependent utility losses.

A physically useful class is K={x≥0 : Ax≤c}, with positive input coefficients. To raise every founder's payoff over V and cover organizer b's fee it is necessary and sufficient that

    f < min_l [c_l - (AV)_l]/A_lb.                                   (1)

Necessity follows from the corresponding resource constraint. For sufficiency add a sufficiently small positive increment to every coordinate of V+f·1_b. Thus a proposed fee depends on which founder pays and on the expected unused capacity on each real input. Every realized late offer can be efficient while a mutually advantageous early institution exists.

## A two-resource example with a known bargaining precedent

Let all three founders' complementary rights be needed to access a joint production window. Together they can use one unit of each of two resources. Separate existing production defines zero incremental outputs. Producing x_i extra for the three sites takes

    2x_A+x_B+x_C ≤ 1,       x_A+2x_B+x_C ≤ 1.                       (2)

This is the feasible set in Herings and Predtetchinski, §3.1, after rescaling their payoff coordinates. Their reciprocal-offer counterexample and its limiting disagreement with the Nash product are established results. We use it to price a founder's actual reason to pay for early design and examine whether it would surrender an agenda right. Physical complementary assets and separable service recipes give (2) a production interpretation. Two distinct scarce inputs prevent a single normalized unit of total service from describing all concessions. A genuine shared terminal objective supporting a boundary offer is available by weighting outputs by its binding positive input row. Each originally self-regarding founder changes its ranking, for example between giving the recipient additional output and wasting the same resources. A late tie still needs a jointly authorized selection rule. An ordinary bound production controller is an alternative using the same resources and observations.

After each rejection, let all parties retain fraction δ, with 0≤δ<1, of the value from waiting to the next equally likely proposer. As in 168, a responder's share offered today equals δ times its expected next-round payoff. At a symmetric stationary solution write V=(x,x,y). Both resources have the same slack s=1-δ(3x+y) after reserving all three continuation shares. A and B proposers can add s/2 to their own continuation, C can add s. Consequently (1-δ)x=s/6 and (1-δ)y=s/3, giving

    V=(1,1,2)/(6-δ).                                                 (3)

Each proposer prefers closing to waiting. Responders can accept at their reservations. On the symmetric efficient boundary y=1-3x, the symmetric Nash product x²(1-3x) is maximized at x=2/9,y=1/3. As patience increases, (3) tends to (1/5,1/5,2/5), a *different* division of real service. The cited paper proves the stronger uniqueness statement in its example. Treating the welfare-supporting weights constructed in 153 as the unique deal agents would select would have substituted a mathematical device for an offer game. [Herings and Predtetchinski, §3.1](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/files/1007390/content)

For finite δ the two resource rows have identical positive mean slack (1-δ)/(6-δ). Equation (1) says C can recover twice the sole organizing expense A or B can. For δ=1/2 the slack is 1/11. Creating a binding early production window at expense 0.06 is privately viable for C but exceeds A and B's individual ceilings 1/22. C can strictly improve on its actual late payoff even after paying and leaving small positive increments for the others. The grounds for this intervention are authority over the timing, real drafting or operating cost, and preserved ability to implement the prescribed division. A cheap ordinary early booking can realize the same benefit. If all three would already change under the late route, buying the early design improves **original welfare and timing** while leaving the eventual number of changed founders at three. The sponsor-induced launch in 168 gives the different case where the date can change adoption itself.

## Would a party sell the right to propose?

Suppose the proposer probabilities can be negotiated in advance with π_A=π_B=p and π_C=q. The same proof gives y/x=2q/p and x=1/[δ(3+2q/p)+2(1-δ)/p]. Giving A and B each 4/11 and C 3/11 implements the symmetric Nash division in the patient limit. But C currently expects 2/5 with equal chances and only 1/3 under this change. Each of A and B would gain 1/45, compared with C's loss of 1/15. A unit-for-unit side transfer of their gains cannot secure C's voluntary agreement even if it is operationally easy to alter the proposal queue. A and B can buy its agreement if they can instead turn each surrendered unit into more than 1.5 units of value to C, after charging the actual conversion work. These are strict comparisons at the limit and persist for sufficiently patient finite games on either side of that threshold. The negotiated share adjustment is just one intervention. A physical institution that directly binds the division, new productive rights or compensated release of an existing claim can have different effects.

**Naturality and frontier.** Shared projects can have several bottlenecks measured in different units, such as machine availability and skilled labor at another site. That observation motivates nonproportional constraint rows; the symmetric numerical coefficients and random recognition probabilities are controls. If only one resource binds the efficient frontier as in 168, a common supporting normal may already price every late offer. The framing candidate is that a founder can pay to avoid real capacity lost through bargaining order. The structural candidate tests whether the late institution's offers share one usable resource valuation. The reading that patient multilateral bargaining must implement the log-product terms is dead for the cited two-constraint example. A sibling uses a single binding capacity or the smooth frontier conditions of the literature. A higher-rung successor studies founders that can change resource substitutability or buy others' agenda rights, with the resulting distribution of gains and acceptance recomputed.

# When both sides agree to accept more uncertainty

A manufacturer who is willing to take more doubtful batches may be able to buy that concession from its customer. The customer must be compensated for the lost output, and it must still be willing to trust the actual recommendations when they arrive. Here the buyer, every privately informed supplier type and the founders who still pay to change their goals strictly prefer the amendment to their existing agreement. The number of genuine changes falls.

## A small margin for the buyer, a valuable order for the supplier

A earns two units of its own product over return by delivering and four by keeping. B gains .4 of its own goods on delivery and loses .15 on retention, both measured from its return stock of two. A also has two on return. An equal-strength contest leaves 1.5 expected units per side after recovery. The agreed utility of A's successor is its own goods plus four times B's. Returning beats contest for both original agents and the changed successor. Delivery beats retention by .2 under the changed criterion and loses two under the original one. B retains a credible choice to withhold stock and initiate the contest before the irreversible conversion. The instrument, known q=.8 installation reliability and privately uniform k on [0,1.4] are as in [239](239-which-bargains-can-a-finite-inspection-actually-support.md).

At first a field instrument simply reports its .9/.1 raw sign. The founders have access to this arrangement on the old terms. After learning k the supplier can propose an amendment. It substitutes a funded instrument that recommends release on every positive and a random quarter of raw negatives, and gives B a flat .20 on enrollment. B sees only the new recommendation. The supplier cannot select the random draw or retrieve the transfer on a negative. A single identified site can enroll once for the batch. Equivalently the parties can negotiate the amendment before learning k. The equipment costs .01 per use in either arrangement.

| Probability of a release recommendation under genuine / retained goals | .9 / .1 | .925 / .325 |
| Largest private cost worth a real attempt | 1.12 | .44 |

| Original A's mean gain, after any transfer | .8480 | 1.1691 |

For the amendment, π=q F(q[2t−4e])=44/175. B's posterior after a rejection is 44/1223, below its release threshold 3/11; the positive posterior exceeds it. Following and paying for the recommended inspection gives more than untested trust or refusal even when the .20 is already sunk. The .20 is a payment for the change in terms, not the reason for obeying a later recommendation. Under the existing agreement B also buys the meter: before its .01 bill it earns .225 by reading versus .202 by trusting every order. If B sees the raw sign under the amendment, a strict preference to decline a raw negative would upset the intended randomization. The two available physical displays therefore matter.

Before the transfer, A of cost k obtains 1.3+max(.44−k,0) after amendment versus .4+max(1.12−k,0) before. The smallest difference is .22, attained at k≤.44. Paying .20 thus makes the amendment strictly worthwhile *even for the types who fund an actual change under either system*. For some intermediate costs the reduced premium instead makes retaining the old goal worthwhile. Since every type proposes, the proposal itself reveals no further information about k. An off-path refusal of the amendment can retain the existing contract and beliefs that support its continuation, for example a small type-independent chance of the old meter being physically required. Selected adoption and credibility on both displays are checked separately.

Both founders value only their own original output. A wants the extra accepted orders and can offer a transferable resource to get them. B would earn more from genuine delivery on the old terms, but accepts the new risk distribution and compensation. The setting has no on-path combat, since every refusal can be returned peacefully. The difference between arrangements concerns genuine adoption, actual service, commercial access and assurance. The construction persists for small changes in the price, production margins and signal accuracies that preserve the displayed strict inequalities.

**Other agreements the parties could make.** Suppose each has at most .20 in transferable generic stock at this handover, while finished colored goods stay physically on their respective lines. Even transferring the full .20 to B for trust in an unchanged supplier would leave B only .05 over return, below its .215 existing option. The entire available swing of ordinary transfers, even allowing .20 in each direction, is below the two-unit incentive to keep the batch. A separately binding, affordably priced output router can change this comparison; its hardware is studied in [209](209-how-much-collateral-will-the-parties-choose.md). If B can unilaterally withdraw the existing arrangement and impose a fee on that same meter, it has a different bargaining position. The result concerns voluntary amendment of a grandfathered contract with a real veto, a capped transferable stock and two supplied displays. [239](239-which-bargains-can-a-finite-inspection-actually-support.md) gives the larger one-channel design class without selecting this amendment as universally optimal.

# R0001 working note: strength weights do not specify control rights

The essay's strength-relative influence admits more than one physical mechanism. A random production operator and divisible territorial control can give the same ideal expected color quantities while inducing different incentives when combat remains available. This note makes that distinction explicit. Its finite-game argument is self-checked only and is an application of standard best-response reasoning.

## 1. A mixture of conditional laws is not independent control of those laws

Let the operator device choose i with probability s_i=a_i/sum_j a_j. For a complete strategy profile sigma, the final history law is

$$P^\sigma=\sum_i s_i K_i^\sigma,$$

where K_i^sigma is the continuation conditional on that draw, including other agents' attacks, responses and implementation choices. The formula is a valid strength-weighted decomposition. It does not imply K_i^sigma depends only on i's policy, or that i can force its ideal world after being selected.

The independently controllable mixture in note 010 is the stronger special case K_i^sigma=mu_i(sigma_i). When an unselected capable agent can interrupt the selected operator, that special case generally fails. Treating its conditional law as an independently selected utility vector would remove the opponent's strategic influence without justification.

## 2. Same strength and output budgets, different execution technology

Take two original linear color criteria, strength share s in (0,1), peaceful output P and common winner output W, with 0<W<P. An attack by either capable agent triggers the same strength-dependent contest; the winner makes only its own color, so combat values are (sW,(1-s)W). All stated outputs are actual physical frontiers after route costs.

### Random operator with revocable ordinary allocations

Condition on A being selected. At the last nonterminal checkpoint A chooses any allocation x_A+x_B<=P or Attack. B simultaneously chooses Continue or Attack. Any attack has priority; otherwise the chosen allocation is produced and the finite episode terminates. Earlier ordinary offers are permitted but do not bind A's last allocation. An enforceable delegate or a physical partitioning operation is a separately specified technology.

Every equilibrium of this last-checkpoint game produces combat with probability one. Suppose B continues with positive probability. Then original A strictly maximizes its payoff by producing (P,0): it is better than every lower A allocation and, since P>sW, better than Attack whenever B continues. But B then strictly prefers its positive combat value to receiving zero, contradicting its positive continuation probability. Thus B attacks surely. The argument also covers mixtures by A. The symmetric statement holds when B is selected.

This does not infer war merely from rejecting utility modification: every ordinary allocation is present. The failure is that the selected controller can revise it at the final decision and the other agent cannot obtain a share through an equally available binding channel. Notes 011/020 analyze how changed criteria or changed execution technology can repair that particular game.

### Divisible physical control with unchanged criteria

Instead suppose A independently controls a fraction s of the productive stock/capacity and B the remainder. At the same last checkpoint each can produce with its own share or Attack; neither can reassign the other's share without triggering the contest. Ordinary production uses each share for its controller's own color. Both still have their combat action and army.

Mutual production gives (sP,(1-s)P), strictly above combat for both. It is a strict equilibrium of the Produce/Attack control. Mutual Attack remains a weak coordination equilibrium if either attack alone already triggers the same fight. In the finite control, Produce weakly dominates Attack and is strictly better against any positive chance of the other producing, so the peaceful profile is the unique trembling-hand-perfect equilibrium. This refinement result is restricted to the stated finite control, not all possible territorial games.

Neither agent changes its goal. The difference from the operator game is physical allocation authority. The division is not enforced by an external rule forbidding war; war remains the route for seizing the other's apparatus and carries its declared physical losses.

### A matched physical witness

Use feedstock O=1,D=0, work reserve 13/5, time ten and total productive capacity one. Production and dispersed-material recovery each cost one work and one time unit per output. Combat spends one work and one time unit and disperses all feedstock. The winner frontier is W=4/5 by note 024, while ordinary production yields P=1.

In the territorial control, two independently operated lines divide stock, work and productive capacity in proportions s,1-s; parallel ordinary production finishes within the deadline. The operator control instead assigns the combined equipment's allocation choice to one selected controller. Both have the same total stocks, useful-work budget, production capacity and contest strength. Their modularity/control rights differ. Equating the cost of those architectures is a stated matched-resource comparison, not a claim that converting between them is free. A conversion action, if available, must be added with its real costs and incentive effects.

For the territorial work accounts, the combat kernel charges each territory its own fraction of the one-unit mobilization expenditure, including defensive expenditure when the other initiates. Each charge is affordable from its share of 13/5, however small s is; the winner captures the remaining pooled production reserve and apparatus. One must not instead debit the entire one-unit cost from an arbitrarily weak initiator's smaller private reserve while still claiming the same action is affordable. This resource-transfer and defense rule is part of the matched contest technology.

Both controls end after the declared finite production operation and protect delivered output at that task boundary. Subdividing production or adding later expropriation creates new histories whose incentives must be checked; the finite equilibrium comparison is not a theorem about indefinite territorial security.

Without the late attack option, random selfish operator production would have ideal expected quantities (sP,(1-s)P), matching the territorial outcome under linear criteria. With the retained attack option, the operator game instead gives (sW,(1-s)W) in every equilibrium, while the territorial control has the peaceful strict equilibrium. Equality of an ideal outcome mean is therefore not strategic equivalence, even for linear original preferences.

## 3. Consequence for the model family

E2 can use either mechanism while preserving the shared strength coordinate for control and combat. It must say which physical authority map is available. A raw strength-weighted expression alone does not specify the game, and a benefit of utility modification in the revocable operator model does not establish a benefit over ordinary peace in the territorial model.

This is also why mechanism comparisons in notes 018/027 require deviations, information and current evaluators to be matched. The territorial control is not automatically an available fixed-utility policy in an indivisible-operator environment. Conversely, where physical partition is available at acceptable cost, omitting it would exaggerate the need for terminal-utility change. Future recurrences may choose a different authority model; C1 does not freeze this choice.

# When one strong signer's change costs less than two weaker ones

**E8, note 192.** Sometimes the cheapest acceptable agreement reaches full service by changing the strongest claimant. Sometimes it reaches it by negotiating changes with two weaker claimants while paying the strongest in future bookings. Here either is an actual choice by a founder maximizing its existing material goal. The comparison permits every ordinary scheduling agreement, including those that peacefully complete some jobs.

## A bound on what partially successful original agreements can deliver

Use 191 with a common renewal probability p=t, D_i>0, one permanently fixed set C of changed signers known before the vote, and O its retained-original complement. The offered goal at a changed site can be as flexible as the technology permits. A known outside contract gives each founder a normalized original reservation R_i. Put a_i=D_i for retained originals and 0 for changed sites, A=Σa_i. Each site has nonnegative *expected* current material income even when it initiates, since D_i>0. Let J=1-Σ_i Q_i+min_i(c_i/x_i)>0 be a lower bound on the physical scaled loss per disputed job.

At any publicly known recipient j let h be the conditional probability of a dispute. The retained originals other than j earn at most h Q_(-j) in the current job, with Q_(-j)=Σ_(i in O, i≠j) Q_i, even forgiving their initiative expense. Together they are guaranteed at least A-a_j, and next-date aggregate scaled returns to them cannot exceed 1-a_j. Hence for every such announcement

    h ≥ g_j = [A-a_j-p(1-a_j)]_+ / [(1-p) Q_(-j)].                     (1)

Take g_j=0 if Q_(-j)=0, which also makes the numerator zero. Let μ_j be the probability that j is publicly designated when sampling a date with weights (1-p)p^s, and H the probability of contest at that sampled date. On a peaceful booking j receives at most one in scaled units and only if designated. Its expected return on *any* disputed booking is at most Q_j. Since consent requires its gross income to cover at least R_j, μ_j≥R_j-Q_j H. Averaging (1) over the dated designations yields the lower bound

    H ≥ (Σ_j g_j R_j)/(1+Σ_j g_j Q_j).                                 (2)

Total original material value is at most 1-JH before setup. This bound allows mixed initiatives, arbitrary assignments after each history, partial concern, and all material divisions delivered through future service. It uses neither equal strengths nor an assumption that a failed sure-peace test makes every booking end in conflict. It applies to a named, fixed configuration and exposes each job's recipient before release. It does not price random contingent future changes, replacement of the physical controller, liquid outside transfers, a permanently concealed beneficiary, or multiple coupled machines.

## Two competing real offers

Three producers have intact yield 1 and recoverable yield 0.75. Their winning probabilities are (0.52,0.32,0.16), so Q=(0.39,0.24,0.12). A first initiative costs its owner 0.05. Thus D=(0.34,0.19,0.07). Every live date brings another booking with probability p=0.35. If no new calendar is agreed, the existing open assignment puts the three sites equally often in the last unserved position, with independently drawn positions next time. A prior unserved site lets a later unserved site initiate, while the last strictly initiates for its expected salvage. All founders' normalized original reservations are consequently R_i=Q_i-0.05/3. This fixes the fallback without restricting proposed ordinary calendar changes. A hypothetical external transfer of wealth or a court with control over local initiative introduces an additional offer.

For an original-only schedule the loss bound JH from (2) is at least 0.04577. For an agreement changing only the middle claimant it is at least 0.01323, and for only the weakest claimant at least 0.02581. In contrast, changing the strongest alone leaves original claims of 0.26≤p, and changing the two weaker ones leaves 0.34≤p. Both can make every job peaceful and meet the original founders' individual reservations. These values are in normalized initial useful output. They persist with enough slack to make every retained site strictly prefer to pass.

Suppose the two weaker claimants' real installation prices are 0.005 each in those units. Initially the strongest's price is 0.008. The largest possible net total is **0.992 with just the strongest changed**. The weaker pair could deliver 0.990. An ordinary or insufficient one-update configuration loses more under (2), and any larger installation including the strongest already costs too much. Now let the strongest's price be 0.018 with identical physics and fallback. The maximum switches to **0.990 with the two weaker sites changed**. Both full-service agreements allow the realized winner and future winners to be chosen after signatures; the known permanent signers actually receive positive original service. For fixed physical reservation goals a one-time raw installation expense k is normalized as (1-p)k/x_i.

In a proposal window, let one founder offer any known configuration and a division of future bookings, subject to a real consent vote and the specified fallback. Every gross initial share above the vector a can be implemented by 191, including the shares that pay the other two their original reservations and their actual setup costs. The proposer takes the residual. With acceptance at indifference it therefore selects the stated highest-net configuration. With strictly voluntary acceptance the other two can receive a small bonus each, paid out of the strict gap to the next configuration. If the institution requires a positive minimum signing bonus, fix it below that gap divided by the number of other signers. Under merely strict acceptance and unrestricted real-valued offers the supremum of proposer payoffs need not be an attained optimum. The configurations permitting near-best accepted offers remain the same. These are adoption results for a specified bargaining procedure. They do not require the founders to maximize a common welfare sum before the change.

An independently motivated cost difference is that a producer owns a legacy controller whose preference register takes more downtime to install. The founder with the proposal right need only obtain binding quotes and access to the consenting controllers. If an equally empowered conventional delegate for the strongest can be acquired for 0.002, the same calendar yields net 0.998 with all founding preferences intact and a delegated first initiative. Paying to change either weaker own goal in addition then loses output. The finding therefore separates the price on reliable behavior from the choice of who alters an actual preference. What proportion of projects have either kind of installation available remains an empirical question.

**Exploration ledger.** The structural candidate is that a bargain can either reduce a strong outsider's retained claim or accommodate it by paying off several other rights. The checked instance identifies both conditional adoption choices and a matched ordinary displacement. The conjecture that changing the fewest parties maximizes what they can earn together is dead in the expensive-strong installation. A sibling charges the true installation expense to each proposed signer. A higher-rung successor lets potential signers wait, bid down their own costs, or build a shared controller before that installation market opens.

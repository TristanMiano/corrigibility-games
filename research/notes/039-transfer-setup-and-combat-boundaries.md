# R0002 working derivation: transfer setup, production and combat

**Unresolved physical candidate retained as an informative repair audit.** The physical instance in 037 deters combat both before and after change. Its increase in cooperation means productive effort, not a measured reduction from a positive combat rate. This note seeks a physically explicit class where chosen productive accommodation also replaces combat, while testing ordinary contracts and lotteries seriously. Gifts, peaceful reuse and implementation timing remain unresolved here; no broad combat theorem is registered from this attempt. Note 054 supplies a separate conditional combat module with its own premises.

## 1. A bound over ordinary output contracts with waste

Use the binary technology with b>=2a and original linear project goals. Let an arbitrary output contract have total expected destruction L_0>=0 on failure and L_1>=0 on success. Transfers and lotteries within each observed outcome may be arbitrary. The sum of the agents' success-minus-failure expected allocations is Y+L_0-L_1.

If both agents use positive effort probability in a Nash equilibrium, each must weakly prefer working against the other's effort probability q_j. Its marginal success probability is a+(b-2a)q_j<=b-a=Delta. Hence its output difference satisfies d_i>=c_i/Delta. Adding the inequalities gives

$$L_0-L_1\geq H:=\frac{c_A+c_B-D}{\Delta}>0.$$

The success probability is at most b, so expected destruction is at least (1-b)H. The underlying expected net physical surplus before destruction is at most C, by the pure-profile bound. Therefore every such equilibrium has net total surplus at most

$$C_{burn}=C-(1-b)H.$$

If at most one agent uses positive effort, its total net productive surplus is nonpositive because aY<c_i. Thus ordinary equilibrium surplus is at most max(0,C_burn). For a sharper comparison with combat, the positive-effort branches separately have upper bound max{C_burn,aY-c_A,aY-c_B}. Public lotteries of contracts preserve these branch bounds; hidden output-allocation lotteries enter through expectations. Private action recommendations coupled to additional signals or policy commitments would enlarge the mechanism class and require another proof.

This extends 036's failure-penalty calculation beyond one hand-picked punishment contract. It is an ordinary-contract bound for the specified signal and effort class, not an obstruction to all fixed-utility cooperation: observable effort, binding effort policies and new monitoring technology remain successful repair candidates.

## 2. A transfer setup cost can matter even when combat destroys material

Let R=R_A+R_B, let the early combat frontier be W=R-d with d>0, and let A win with probability s. Let

$$R_A=sW-\varepsilon,\qquad R_B=(1-s)W+d+\varepsilon,$$

where epsilon>0. A's existing project stock then falls short of its combat expectation, while B's exceeds its own. With costless enforceable transfers, paying A epsilon repairs this conflict. This ordinary compromise must be retained as the zero-setup-cost comparison.

Now suppose any contract moving existing private project material between the sites requires a bridge/adapter whose setup consumes K units of valued project material before the final transfer lottery is resolved. The setup is publicly observable and requires a nonterminal operation; both retain Attack. It is needed even for a randomized conditional transfer. Publicly randomizing whether to build it reveals the branch while attacks remain available. These timing conditions matter: a free hidden terminal lottery that pays setup cost only in rare transfer branches can undo the fixed-cost obstruction.

If K>d, every built-transfer route has total final project output at most R-K<W. If in addition

$$d<\min\{-C_{burn},\ c_A-aY,\ c_B-aY\},$$

every ordinary positive-effort equilibrium also has total expected output below W. A no-effort/no-transfer branch leaves A below its early combat value. These observations suggest a full ordinary-game combat result: at the early checkpoint each original agent can guarantee its share of W by attacking, whereas any positive probability of a peaceful branch would lower total expected output below W after its relevant branchwise attack checks. The extensive-form proof must still check all public and hidden randomization/timing possibilities; the inequalities alone do not establish it for every implementation architecture.

## 3. A productive compromise can satisfy both combat constraints

At the default production split t_0, a chosen lambda>lambda_0 gives expected net outputs r_A=t_0V-c_A and r_B=(1-t_0)V-c_B. If

$$0<\varepsilon<r_A,$$

then A's productive output R_A+r_A exceeds sW. B's exceeds (1-s)W because d+epsilon+r_B>0. All current nonnegative own/other output criteria also prefer this vector in expectation to combat. Consequently an efficiently protected productive continuation can deter every early attack in the same way as 037, provided its installation and output routing fit the physical work/time budget.

A candidate rational witness is Y=10, a=1/10, b=2/5, c_A=c_B=17/10, t_0=s=43/100, lambda=3/10. Then

$$C=3/5,\quad C_{burn}=-1/5,\quad r_A=1/50,\quad r_B=29/50.$$

Choose R=6, d=1/10, W=59/10, epsilon=1/100 and K=11/100. Then R_A=2527/1000 and R_B=3473/1000. A's productive output exceeds its combat expectation by 1/100, while B has a larger positive margin. Both work incentives are strict: A's gain is 103/1000 and B's is 1/100. The default split equals A's strength share in this version, so strength affects both project control and combat odds.

The frontier W=59/10 can come from recovery of the six units of existing project material, without creating material through combat: an 11-unit work budget and combined recovery/production work cost 110/59 per unit give that bound. The common reactor feedstock remains unavailable after combat because repair takes longer than the episode. The setup cost is a separate real material loss. Actual installation costs reduce work and dated combat frontiers and must be carried through the sequential incentive checks.

This is a promising open-region construction, not yet a completed integrated result. In particular, the Keep branch must include B's own possible preference change and the best collateralized contract; early attacks must be checked before costs are sunk; ordinary lotteries must not be accidentally excluded; and costless monitoring/delegation or a cheaper transfer setup could repair ordinary peace. The positive and limiting comparisons are both part of the same test.

## 4. Repair audit: gifts, machinery reuse and a less narrow witness

A setup cost attached only to a formal escrow contract is insufficient. If B can give A the necessary project goods unconditionally at lower cost, and the new private stocks make restraint optimal, that gift repairs ordinary peace. Likewise, peaceful use of the same transport or recovery equipment available after combat may bypass a supposed bridge cost. A physical obstruction must price these routes too; declaring a fee on the named agreement is not a proof over the full agreement class. These are currently unresolved repairs for the candidate above, so its combat conclusion must remain provisional.

The algebraic region is not confined to the narrow symmetric-cost witness. For c_A=11/5, c_B=6/5, t_0=s=23/40 and lambda=1/2, with the same Y,a,b, we obtain C=3/5, C_burn=-1/5, lambda_0=19/51, r_A=1/10 and r_B=1/2. Choose W=59/10, d=1/10, epsilon=1/20, K=11/100. Then R_A=1337/400 and R_B=1063/400. The two default work margins are 13/80 and 3/40, and A's gain over early combat is 1/20. These are larger strict margins than in the symmetric example.

If a real transfer route with setup loss K is available, B can respond to original A by changing its own goal and making a collateralized offer. For example, charge K to B, let A's expected net gain be epsilon=1/20, and choose d_A=15/2,d_B=5/2. The failure transfer g_A^0=-3/4 and success transfer g_A^1=27/4 give A that mean. B's original expected net gain is C-K-epsilon=11/25. A goal y+(3/10)x makes B's effort strict, and original A's effort is strict too. The failure collateral fits A's remaining stock. Thus even the candidate Keep branch must include productive change by the counterparty; it cannot be identified automatically with war.

An offer must also compensate the early threat at the time it is proposed. Letting negotiation costs reduce the dated threat does not justify pretending the agent could not have attacked earlier. A conditional offer made before costly installation can address this, but its actual activation, cancellation and verification rules require a complete state table. This implementation remains under construction.

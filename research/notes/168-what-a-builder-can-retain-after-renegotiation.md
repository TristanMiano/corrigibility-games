# Why a willing partner might wait for someone else to build the adapter

**E8, note 168.** A three-way return path can be worth building if its sponsor secures enough of the future service. Letting the parties renegotiate after construction can change the sponsor's return even if they all expect eventual agreement. The difference survives an arbitrary number of counteroffers in the following class. Buying time to negotiate, prepaying the builder and agreeing the service division before construction give three distinct repairs.

## The physical service frontier becomes a bargaining problem

Use the three-site expanding ring in 154. Under complete conditional activation the feasible **nonnegative** net original gains N_A,N_B,N_C have frontier

    N_A/F_A + N_B/F_B + N_C/F_C ≤ 1,
    (F_A,F_B,F_C)=(7/12, 7/48, 7/24).                               (1)

The capacity x_A≤1/12 bounds (N_A+4N_B+2N_C)≤7/12. Indeed substituting N_A=2x_C-x_A, N_B=2x_A-x_B, N_C=2x_B-x_C gives 7x_A. All three intercepts are attained with the remaining two gains zero, by formula 154.1. Convex combinations of those three allocations and independent use attain every nonnegative point in (1). The same reasoning applies to any coalition whose nonnegative service frontier has one effective resource constraint of this form. With positive real registration fees k_i paid on agreement, replace N by gain net of k and multiply every intercept by B=1-Σk_i/F_i, as long as B>0. This follows by charging each k_i to the physical capacity budget in (1). The example uses gross figures first to isolate bargaining; small positive fees preserve the strict sponsor choices.

The negotiated common objective proportional to (1,4,2) makes every boundary allocation optimal in the later service call, with agreed allocation of tied routes as in 154. Strict old preference for local use is changed even on a route where the successor is willing to use a lottery. These sites still need a credible joint configuration and a way to select the authorized tie probabilities. An ordinary matched router could supply the same feasible bargaining set at its actual price. The following calculation explains who pays for a new cooperative opportunity. It does not make a binding ordinary router infeasible by assumption when a cheaper one actually exists.

A owns the adapter site and pays construction cost F in private resources. If it does not build, none of these gains is available and the constituents continue independent production. In the late protocol, agreement on service rights starts only after A has irreversibly built the adapter. After each rejection a new proposer i is drawn independently with probabilities π_i>0. Anybody can decline and keep independent rights. If i waits for the next round, its incremental service value is multiplied by δ_i∈[0,1), including its actual risk of deadline loss. Normalize each site's per-round value by its own F_i, so a physical division has transferable *normalized shares* summing to one even though the sites need no ability to exchange actual cash. Time losses can differ because their expiring workloads have different retention profiles. This time-homogeneous specification requires that a founder's incoming and foregone work scale together with delay. A general pattern of changing productive ratios requires a different continuation.

## What the sponsor retains when counteroffers are real

In the stationary immediate-agreement equilibrium, let s_i be i's share before the next proposer is drawn. On rejection, each current original expects δ_i s_i in normalized units. The proposer awards the others their reservation shares and takes the remaining available share. The sum of reservations is strictly below one at the stated solution, so immediate agreement beats a deliberate rejection for the proposer. The fixed point is

    s_i = δ_i s_i + π_i[1-Σ_j δ_j s_j],
    s_i = [π_i/(1-δ_i)] / Σ_j [π_j/(1-δ_j)].                         (2)

Substitute and sum to check Σs_i=1. Strictly positive π gives each site strictly positive ex ante service value. Responders accept at their continuation value, and can instead strictly favor a sufficiently small concession at the responder's cutoff. The formula describes the stated stationary selection; it does not claim that unconstrained infinite-history behavior has a unique equilibrium. With an equal δ, any finite number of potential rounds has the **same** ex ante shares π. In the last round the proposer takes the whole normalized surplus. Working backward, the expected next shares π give current expected shares δπ_i+π_i(1-δ)=π_i, proving the result for every remaining horizon. Thus adding rounds and actual counteroffers changes the immediate offer given the proposer's identity but can leave the sponsor's expected share unchanged.

A's initial original incentive without side payments is F<F_A s_A. Its late promise to destroy an already useful adapter is unnecessary for the proof. With equal π and equal patience, A can privately recover only 7/36≈0.19444. At F=0.20, A declines even though it would have recovered up to 7/12 under credible early terms in 154. There is no combat on failure in this short-expiry productive setting. Failure here means losing the additional return path, not necessarily losing an existing independent job. If a more lucrative ordinary job becomes available, include its payoffs in the reservations.

## What the parties can do about the timing

**Fund the builder before the terms are settled.** Suppose B and C can place transferable resources in an escrow conditional on the adapter's physical commissioning. After commissioning the payments are sunk even if a later proposed division is rejected, so (2) still governs division of new service. Let W_i be i's available transferable balance. When no build would otherwise occur, a strictly mutually acceptable compensating subscription exists if the shortfall F-F_A s_A is less than

    min(W_B,F_B s_B) + min(W_C,F_C s_C).                             (3)

Indeed these are the largest individual contributions that leave each willing to sign, and A needs slightly more than the shortfall. With a funded commitment by one organizer to close only after the required subscriptions and actual commissioning, each pivotal signing decision compares the deal with zero. At F=0.20 a payment of 0.01 from B is enough when no competing storage design is available; B's gross expected service return is about 0.04861. If B could wait for C to pay while still receiving its whole share, the proposal game would require the volunteer analysis of 141. This is a chosen subscription under specified rights, not a presumption of charitable finance.

**Buy a better position in late bargaining.** Suppose A can extend the retention of its own useful work from δ_A=0.5 to 0.75, while the other two keep 0.5. For example it can pay to keep its prepared workload usable past the next scheduling cutoff. Procurement and operating readiness must be observable to change the others' credible waiting calculations. The upgrade preserves the current-period frontier (1). Formula (2) gives A half the service budget and B and C a quarter each. A now obtains 7/24≈0.29167 before construction and storage expense. At storage price H=0.04 it strictly buys and builds even without subsidies: 0.29167-0.20-0.04>0. Its partners still consent relative to independent production. No actual round need be lost for the ability to wait to affect the price of A's return.

A real alternative changes what it takes to buy A's cooperation. To induce A to build *without* buying the storage, (3)'s required payment must now cover its actual upgrade alternative. A needs at least F_A(1/2-1/3)-H≈0.09722-H if that exceeds its basic funding shortfall. B and C together gain only (F_B+F_C)(1/3-1/4)≈0.03646 by avoiding the storage-driven change of division. At H=0.04 these two cannot buy A out with transferable resources while making each founder better off. At H=0.065 a combined funded grant between about 0.03222 and 0.03646 suffices. Both B and C must value the preserved service and have transferable means. The proposer needs contractual evidence of the unupgraded timeline as well as commissioning. A promise to abstain that can be secretly reversed would need a new comparison.

**Set the service division early when possible.** If the parties can bind an early service allocation and conditional installation before any construction, they can instead offer the *same* vector of services that A would obtain with the storage upgrade. Implementation saves the real upgrade expense and keeps all three old continuation service payoffs. A strictly benefits from the saving; it can use a small funded transfer or a little of its services to give each other signer a strict improvement over its fallback. For the common weight vector in 154, A also strictly prefers finishing the adapter under the subsequently active goal. This is a constructive reason for an organizer to pay for an early negotiating window. Its value relative to the late game depends on who actually holds the authority to reserve the joint schedule. If a signed document cannot bind the future booking, the physical grant or validated longer lifetime may be the affordable repair.

**Naturality and frontier.** Site-specific irreversible preparation, independent approvals, perishable workloads and uncertainty about who can make the next firm offer give these timing questions a physical and institutional source. The Chandra joint-call procedure explicitly addresses reviews that might each wait for the other to commit, while retaining feasibility checks. The single binding normalized resource and stationary proposer probabilities give a transparent, nonuniversal pricing case. A founder can deliberately create better holding capacity or place a real commissioning subscription when its calculated return pays for it. The framing candidate is that the same productive return can support or deter launch depending on when agents can secure it. The structural candidate uses later bargaining, including genuine counteroffers, to price what a builder can recover. The inference that arbitrarily many renegotiation rounds suffice to induce efficient early construction is dead for equal discounting at F=0.20. A sibling purchases preparatory rights or a commissioning grant. A higher-rung successor endogenizes the parties' ability to create, conceal, sell or privately improve their waiting opportunities when several capacity constraints bind.

# Which uncertainties can more experience resolve?

Experience with the *same* partner can identify an agreed goal even when a single observation, or one observation per partner, leaves it entirely ambiguous. A purchasing decision can also become determinate while the goal remains permanently ambiguous. Both boundaries can be stated for a finite class of technologies, abilities and goals.

## Distinguishing two families of agents

A type θ specifies an actual goal, a persistent implementation and an ability or response rule. Each allowed physical diagnostic d has a finite output set and a known conditional law Pᵈ_θ. The diagnostic acts on the controller that will do the job. Given this fixed type and the chosen diagnostic, fresh draws are independent of earlier draws. The designer may use different diagnostics on the same consenting unit without changing its type. Define θ~η when Pᵈ_θ=Pᵈ_η for every accessible d. Include known ordinary controllers and feasible imitation rules in the comparison family when they can produce the evidence.

For finite classes G (the agreed ordering) and N (a different ordering), arbitrarily small worst-case error on both is achievable by sufficiently many observations **exactly when every cross-pair is separated by some accessible diagnostic**. For necessity, a cross-pair with identical kernels has the same law of the first observation. Conditional on each history it also has the same law of the chosen next diagnostic, stopping decision and next observation. Induction gives the same law of any finite stopped record and decision. The two error probabilities for that pair sum to one.

For sufficiency define the affinity Bᵈ_θη=Σ_y sqrt(Pᵈ_θ(y)Pᵈ_η(y)). It is at most one, with equality just when the two probability vectors coincide. If n_d observations of diagnostic d are scheduled, a maximum-likelihood member of the union of types yields a wrong class under θ only if some cross-type η matches or exceeds θ's likelihood. Markov's inequality applied to the square root of the likelihood ratio bounds that event by the product of (Bᵈ_θη)^n_d. Zero-probability observations cause no problem under the generating type. Summing over cross-types gives an explicit bound. Thus it suffices to choose counts such that, for every cross-pair,

    Σ_d n_d (−log Bᵈ_θη) ≥ log(max(|G|,|N|)/δ).                   (219.1)

One disjoint-support observation separates its pair with zero error. For the remaining pairs, the finite separation condition permits a finite schedule for each δ>0. Minimizing Σ n_d cost(d) under (219.1) prices a sufficient design. It need not minimize cost among all experiments or stopping rules. A partner still has to pay that cost before this is a bargaining result.

**Unknown ability can create, conceal or remove separation.** Suppose the probability of a recorded positive from the genuine controller is .2 or .8 according to a fixed but unknown sensor orientation. An unchanged controller generates independent fair readings. Equal mixing over the two genuine orientations gives the same one-reading distribution as the unchanged controller. On two readings of the *same* genuine unit the probability of agreement is .68, versus .50 under the retained one. With n readings classify as genuine if the positive fraction differs from one half by more than .15. Hoeffding's bound gives both class errors at most 2 exp(−.045n); n=82 suffices for .05 each. If instead the genuine sensor independently redraws an equally likely orientation at each trial, its entire sequence has the fair law. A trusted orientation tag, or keeping the same physical mount, can repair that loss of information. The distinction is about when the hidden variable is drawn.

For finitely many diagnostics with finite alphabets and potentially infinitely many types, define distance between two types as max_d TV(Pᵈ_θ,Pᵈ_η), where TV is half the sum of absolute probability differences. Uniform arbitrarily accurate testing by a finite deterministic budget is possible exactly when the infimum of this distance over cross-pairs is positive (for nonempty classes). At positive distance, estimate all finitely many probability vectors by repeated trials and choose the nearer family. Uniform coordinate concentration makes the probability of an error arbitrarily small. At infimum zero and any fixed budget n, choose a cross-pair with distance below ε/n. At each common history couple its two next observations to agree with probability at least 1−ε/n, including for adaptive designs. Its full records then differ with probability at most ε. The pair's error sum is at least 1−ε, for arbitrary ε. Pointwise distinction of every fixed pair can coexist with failure of a uniform finite budget. A negotiated tolerance or a physically separated menu can restore a positive distance.

## Deciding on the batch when the goal stays ambiguous

Let v_θ(a) be the expected original-utility return of a feasible response a under θ, including stochastic output. Let v*_θ=max_a v_θ(a). Return, a limited or full release, an ordinary binding contractor and a physically advantageous contest can all enter when available. For a finite type and action class, the limiting worst-case decision regret with unrestricted numbers of free observations is

    max over observational classes C of
        min over action lotteries x of max_{θ in C} [v*_θ−Σ_a x(a)v_θ(a)].    (219.2)

For a lower bound, all θ in one class induce the same final action lottery under any observation rule. For the upper bound, use the likelihood argument above to distinguish the finitely many *different classes*, with error at most δ. Apply a minimizing lottery for the identified class. Its regret is bounded by (219.2) plus δ times the largest payoff range, which proves the limit. In particular, when every class has a common optimal action one can decide well arbitrarily often even if an agreed goal and a changed vocabulary in a different goal class yield identical evidence forever.

For a residual class containing a helpful and a harmful indistinguishable controller, suppose full release gains g for the helpful one and loses h for the harmful one relative to return. With just those two actions, equalizing the two regrets gives release probability g/(g+h) and residual worst-case regret gh/(g+h). A buyer using Bayesian expected utility instead releases at helpful probability p>h/(g+h). If the founders can agree a smaller covered order giving B a sure one over return, with g=4 and h=2 on the full order, that smaller order beats return under every belief; full release beats it when p>1/2. Verifying which controller was installed and deciding which exposure to accept answer distinct questions. A's founding consent, acceptability of any physical transfer, and current incentives to execute still have to be calculated, as in [215](215-who-signs-when-even-a-correct-goal-has-an-uncertain-yield.md).

**Naturality and reach.** Repeated operation of one stable calibrated line can preserve a hidden type across trials. Replacing the unit, updating it after each observation, or letting an unrestricted proxy answer every diagnostic changes the available evidence. A verifier can purchase trials at different sites or pay to keep one unit on the stand; a supplier can agree when prospective access covers the expense. The implementable schedule, total material budget and privately valuable receipt matter even for a perfectly separating experiment. A sibling grounds a different physical feature; the broader question is which remaining distinctions affect anyone's choice of a bargain at all.

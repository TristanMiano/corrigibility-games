# Which preferences do the observations identify?

E9 begins with a question about the object of the bargain. Two founders may agree to the same act, to the same ranking of lotteries over this project's outputs, or to a goal that applies to additional projects and dates. These are different promises. This note treats the second. Notes 013, 106, 135 and 163 already price inspection when the error law of a recognition device is specified. Here we derive what a class of observations can certify and what its error law would have to mean.

## What a truthful comparison buys

Fix a finite terminal consequence set containing two reference outcomes 0 and 1 that both parties agree are worst and best, and intermediate outcomes j. Consequences include all the features the promised goal ranks on this domain. Assume expected utility over objective lotteries and a strict preference for 1 over 0. Normalize both the promised utility t and the acting utility u to 0 and 1 at the references. Equality of all coordinates is exactly equality of the ordering over lotteries on this domain. Indeed, comparing each j to the lottery delivering 1 with probability p and 0 otherwise locates its unique indifference probability u(j). A genuine *change* also needs a different pre-agreement ordering. A strict reversal of one such comparison is a witness. A party already valuing the agreed goal can truthfully report a match without changing.

At a probe the active, deployed chooser can take j or the reference lottery with announced p. Both alternatives must actually be feasible. Allow a total error d in normalized utility units. Choosing j implies u(j) ≥ p−d. Choosing the lottery implies u(j) ≤ p+d. This is an explicit bounded-choice model: any response satisfying its inequality is possible, with no required correlation across responses. For instance, a probability error at most b plus a choice loss at most e supplies d=b+e. If the probe pays out only with probability λ>0 and influencing a later decision can add at most k in today's normalized utility, the loss measured on the probe alone can be as large as k/λ. A consequence-free demonstration supplies no such control. A smaller, independently known common-error model can support tighter conclusions.

**205.1 (sharp identification in the bounded-choice class).** After any finite transcript, intersect [0,1] for each j with all observed lower and upper bounds to get [L_j,H_j]. The set of currently possible goals is exactly the product of these intervals. Empty intersections diagnose inconsistent assumptions. Otherwise the transcript certifies tolerance ε in the largest coordinate error precisely when every interval lies in [t(j)−ε,t(j)+ε]. The proof of necessity constructs a present goal at any offending endpoint. It rationalizes every recorded response within d by the definition of the choice rule. Sufficiency follows by intersecting the individual inequalities. An adaptively chosen probe contributes its own inequality after the preceding observations. The analyst must condition on how the observed histories were selected when assigning probabilities.

If the coordinate error is at most ε, a lottery's normalized expected value differs by at most ε. For lotteries P and Q the error in the *difference* of their values is at most 2ε times their total variation distance. Write the signed probability difference as its positive and negative parts. Each has mass equal to that distance. An agreed strict margin exceeding this bound preserves the ranking. Adding physically possible consequences outside this domain requires assigning and checking further coordinates. An affordable policy that supplies the promised deliveries is a substantive ordinary alternative. If a delegate makes the choices while the founder retains its ranking, the intervals apply to the delegate's decisions under the stated behavioral assumptions.

## Precision, capability and exact agreement

**205.2 (tests around a named target).** Consider one interior target t. To certify tolerance ε>2d, offer a lower and an upper reference probability at distance ε−d from t, inside [0,1]. Passing means choosing the project at the lower price and the lottery at the upper price. A chooser with u=t passes both for *every* allowed error, since ε−d>d. Every passing u lies in [t−ε,t+ε] by 205.1. At t=.60, d=.02 and ε=.06 the two probabilities are .56 and .64. A verified pass puts this coordinate in [.54,.66]. Two probes per intermediate coordinate suffice for this targeted certificate. Exploratory bisection from an interval of width w instead leaves width at most min(w,w/2+d) after one comparison at its midpoint. Starting from [0,1] and d<1/2 gives width at most 2d+(1−2d)/2^n after n comparisons of the coordinate.

There is a matching obstruction below the error margin. Choose an admissible alternative v at distance greater than ε and at most 2d from the target. For *every* reference probability, the optimal answer for the midpoint (t+v)/2 is allowed under both t and v. This common response rule survives adaptive probes and repeated observations. No test built solely from these probes can guarantee acceptance of every target and rejection of every such alternative. At equality ε=2d the two-probe strict-completeness proof loses its margin. For a finite exact menu, separation from the nearest distinct value by more than 2d suffices for exact classification, using midpoint thresholds to the nearest neighbors. A pair at distance at most 2d has the common response rule. With a continuum, d=0 and an unrestricted tie response, a finite binary transcript cannot uniformly establish exact equality at an interior target: follow the target's consistent choice of the project at every tie. A slightly larger value gives the same answers along the entire finite path. A certified finite menu or an enforceable separate equality measurement changes the experiment.

## What makes the setting plausible?

A project with standardized batches can offer real lotteries over deliveries and a small set of physically distinguished tasks. A partner that will entrust an expensive batch can have a reason to fund a cheaper pilot. Computing a ranking more accurately or measuring delivery chances better can shrink d. Valuable future access can also enlarge k, making the very stakes that fund a test spoil its interpretation. Paying for a real probe consumes resources. A fixed-policy delegate can pass a behavioral check if it has the same choice authority. An assumption connecting the observed choice to the installed evaluator must be justified separately. The founding stage and actual willingness to buy evidence are investigated in subsequent E9 notes.

**Exploratory lesson.** A finite transcript of ordinary strict choices leaves a neighborhood of goals available on a continuum. A sibling asks for a justified finite menu or a tolerance certificate with a consequential margin. The broader question is which disagreements the partner would pay to resolve, given future access, control and the cost of an independent observation.

# How many founders actually need to change when an ordinary contract can cover part of the job?

**E8, note 165.** A wants a service only C can provide. A can pay C for a credible change. Or it can recruit B, whose work produces something C values highly. Sometimes this second arrangement costs A less than even a perfectly measured cash purchase from C. Whether one or two parties change then depends on the actual price of paying B for delivery under its existing goal.

## Two different jobs, three founders and a real buyer

With probability λ_C there is a C→A job: C can keep d_C or deliver r_A to A. With probability λ_B there is a B→C job: B can keep d_B or deliver r_C to C. The jobs are mutually exclusive. The residual probability allows normal independent work. Define D_B=λ_B d_B, D_C=λ_C d_C, R_C=λ_B r_C and R_A=λ_C r_A. These are expected physical output increments in each site's original value units. Here transferable cash has one unit of own value to each founder, and A has sufficient pledgeable reserves. The other job outputs are site-specific. C's high private return on receiving B's service need not be spendable money. This makes a return path through A consequential. If C can instead sell its own future output, compare that real substitute.

A offers a fraction x of C calls on which it wants delivery and a conditional fraction y of B calls on which it buys service for C. A can fund a cash bonus w_i≥0 payable on an individual positive readback. A finite registrar reads a genuine approved local routing criterion with probability t_i and an unchanged false claimant with probability m_i<t_i. Write e_i=t_i-m_i. The probes are contracted in advance. B's service is due only on a B call with a positive C readback and an eligible y draw. C's service is due on eligible C calls under a genuine C configuration, irrespective of its earlier readback. All parties must authorize the package before learning which job arrives. A retains its own goal. Common concern for the recipient's real output makes an eligible B or C successor voluntarily provide the agreed service when recipient value exceeds donor cost in its common scale. On other jobs the agreed evaluator protects the original project as in 155. Cost k_i is real installation work, and h_i is the fixed inspection bill paid by A. The short-expiry physical contest strictly wastes the remaining opportunity for all originals and successors. The issue here is agreement design and the number of actual changes.

Each can refuse for separate work with zero incremental gain in the basic game. Only A owns liquid stock for cross-site settlement before production. An ordinary meter, when installed at real fixed cost M_i, can certify actual completed delivery to the cash escrow; an unchanged donor then serves for a conditional bonus just above d_i. A site that can cheaply do this with a receipt should include that cost. Readback of a bounded local objective and externally certifiable output can have different prices in either direction. These are conditional technologies. Counteroffers, other customer reservations, external wealth or a route usable in both job states change their comparison.

## The buyer's cheapest package in this institution

When C genuinely installs and B delivers as agreed, C's incentive over signing falsely is strict when

    w_C + R_C y > P(x),       P(x)=(D_C x+k_C)/e_C.                 (1)

A true C earns t_C times the conditional reward and bears D_C x+k_C; a false C collects m_C times the reward and retains its job. Refusal is also beaten under (1). A genuine B needs e_B w_B > D_B t_C y+k_B. Its false claim could still collect m_B w_B, but would keep its own output. A purely unconditional payment to B can buy its signature while leaving that latter deviation just as attractive. These tests derive the return paid to the *actual donor*. A paying itself for passing a readback would provide B with no extra incentive.

For an exact infimum of costs use equality or an accept-at-tie convention. Strict offers approximate the infimum on a fine funded grid. With C genuine and paid only in cash, A spends h_C+t_C P(x). Replacing part of that award with B's services has two possible costs. Set Y=min(1,P(x)/R_C). The minimized expense in the genuine-C class is

    h_C + t_C P(x) + min(0,
        h_B+t_B k_B/e_B - t_C (R_C-t_B D_B/e_B) Y,
        M_B              - t_C (R_C-D_B) Y).                         (2)

For proof, fix an implemented volume y>0. Pay C the residual (P-R_C y)_+ conditional on its readback. To induce genuine B, the optimal readback bonus has expected cost t_B(D_B t_C y+k_B)/e_B. To induce unchanged B, an honest delivery bonus costs D_B t_C y in expectation plus the meter. For y≤Y each comparison is affine in y and for y>Y marginal expense increases without saving more cash. In each profitable branch Y therefore minimizes expense. Equation (2) includes at zero volume the option of leaving B out altogether, along with ordinary B and genuine B at positive volume. In a genuine-C and ordinary-B package only C changes, although all parties consent. In a genuine-B and genuine-C package two founders voluntarily change and A keeps its objective.

When C keeps its original objective, payment needs to cover d_C at the actual C-call decision. Its possible use of a B service in a different, mutually exclusive state has no value *conditional on that decision*. An ordinary meter for C gives infimum D_C x+M_C. This is also an optimistic lower bound of D_C x, even if A acquires a cheaper credible way to pay C exactly on actual delivery. It applies with the stated own-only C, no further reward, no useful attack or loss for declining, and at-cost money as the sole inducement available in that state. A new C-valued physical output producible on a C call would change the bound. A chooses the best funded proposal or its zero-gain outside option according to its own return R_A x less the actual cost. If R_A>t_C D_C/e_C then any profitable optimum in this class uses all the C jobs. Indeed increasing x can always be financed by raising the residual conditional cash in (1) at slope t_C D_C/e_C. The original-C cost rises at the lower slope D_C. Fixed fees already incurred do not change these marginal comparisons.

### Which technologies select zero, one or two changes?

Use λ_B=λ_C=1/3, d_B=d_C=1, r_A=r_C=3, t_B=t_C=0.8, m_B=m_C=0.2, k_B=k_C=0.05, h_B=0.01 and h_C=0.02. Then P(1)=23/36 and Y=23/36. With full C service, the infimum costs to A are about 0.53111 for financing C's genuine change directly, 0.32383 for two genuine changes, 0.19037+M_B for ordinary B plus genuine C, and 1/3+M_C for an ordinary purchase from C. Independent production gives A zero gain relative to refusing and all these prices are measured against its gross return of 1.

- With M_B=0.20 and M_C=0.01, recruiting both genuine donors is cheapest. Giving C a valuable service in kind can cost A less than the cash C needs to give up its own opportunity. Even a zero-price C delivery meter still costs A at least 1/3 in payments to C.
- With M_B=0.04 and M_C=0.01, A buys the B meter and C still genuinely changes. A continues to gain from an in-kind reward; B now takes a verified delivery payment while retaining its original ranking.
- Keep the cheaper B meter but raise C's implementation work to k_C=0.40. Now Y=1 and C still needs additional cash. The cheapest genuine-C path costs over 0.50. A uses the 0.34333 ordinary purchase from C, with zero genuinely changed agents.

All comparisons are strict across neighborhoods when the required margins are rounded up sufficiently little. They condition on a credible, closeable offer with locally acting controls. Choosing the number of genuine changes is an outcome of the cost comparison, not A's objective. A itself need not intrinsically favor change or give up its own goals.

## Does a friend of the claimant help it fake?

Suppose B remains genuinely configured but agrees to help C fake, possibly using a side payment. B earns the same readback bonus as before but serves less often because C now passes less often. B's original saving is e_C D_B y. To rule out an aggregate gain for that pair in this deviation, strengthen (1) to

    e_C [w_C+(R_C-D_B)y] > D_C x+k_C.                               (3)

This gives C's prospective accomplice its full physical incentive to acquiesce. A privately attractive single deviation test can underprice the deal for the pair. Granting transferable side payments makes (3) sufficient and, for this deviation with freely transferable incremental utility, necessary. A richer C-valued in-kind service can still sustain the positive construction after this stronger check.

For example keep the first work costs and readbacks, but let r_C=5, set w_C=0, y=0.49 and w_B=0.31. Then e_C(R_C-D_B)y=0.392>1/3+0.05, and e_B w_B=0.186>0.05+0.8(0.49)/3. Both donors strictly choose the genuine installation on the stated path, even granting B and C an enforceable side payment when B is genuine and C fakes. A's total expense is 0.8(0.31)+0.03=0.278, below the lower bound for an ordinary C and below the best ordinary-B plus genuine-C cost with M_B=0.20 (about 0.32222). If B fakes, its cash is at most m_B w_B and its other possible service only costs it own work. Under the declared absence of C cash or other B-valued collateral, C has nothing additional to pay B. Thus true B's strictly larger original payoff prevents a joint improvement for both in this case. If C can borrow against its private output in B's currency, or an internal coalition can transfer that value at will, re-price its subclub. The argument protects these specified B/C replacements. It does not certify stability against every coalition or every false controller. The exhibited terms beat the original and one-change controls above; selection of these exact robust terms requires the corresponding deviation space and proposal protocol.

**Naturality and frontier.** A has purchasing power, B has productive capacity, and C has a real use for B's work. These are distinct and familiar roles in supply and service exchanges. The same agents could deliberately create a credible cross-site price by installing a delivery meter or funding conditional cash in advance. A chooses the institution by the work and money its own project gains and expends. The framing candidate is that paying in somebody else's useful production can support changed goals. The structural candidate prices the physical path, ordinary settlement and the temptation of every informed donor. The inference that three members must all change is dead in this instance; the inference that one ordinary meter makes all changes redundant also fails here. Siblings assign different locally feasible controls to the actual donors. A higher-rung successor asks which mixes of ordinary and genuine control survive entry by a liquid outside supplier or by a pair that can pool all privately valuable returns.

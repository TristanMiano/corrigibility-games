# When risk about salvage makes an affordable change less attractive

**E8, note 199.** In the private-inspection game, the same physical installation can be worthwhile for a mildly risk-averse founder and too expensive for a more risk-averse one. Ordinary cooperation already protects the latter from more of the risky contest. With still greater aversion, the opportunity to inspect loses its value even though a producer told with certainty that it would lose would still initiate. This differs from a pooling agreement that buys insurance against an unexpectedly valuable *intact* job.

## What is actually being priced

Keep the physical x=0.70, y=0.60, c=0.04, h=0.04, equal strength and ordered releases in 195–196. Write lower-case letters for quantities of useful stock, upper-case letters below for original expected-utility increments. Let each founder value its *own* terminal stock by u(w)=1-exp(-γw), γ>0. The initial stock suffices for all possible actual expenses. Relative to the safe initial stock and after a common positive affine rescaling the safe payoff is zero. The original rankings over terminal lotteries are genuinely different as γ varies. A diagnostic bill or a setup comes out of the same stock whose loss matters in the contest.

    X=1-exp(-γx),    Q=(1-exp(-γy))/3,    D=1-exp(γc)(1-Q),
    C=Q-D,           J=X-3Q+C,            k=1-exp(-γh).

D is the expected utility of initiating and Q the expected utility of passively receiving the salvage lottery. The stock expense c costs C in this comparison because it is paid on the actual risky outcome. An inspection policy with gross expected utility g is worth 1-exp(γh)(1-g) net. Its payable price in stock depends on the payoff it protects. Treating h as a fixed subtraction in expected utility would misprice the diagnostic. The original utilities are not freely transferable. The relations below use probabilities of concrete deliveries that both the proposer and the physical scheduler can actually implement.

Use D>0 in the private-inspection comparison. For the fair fallback put s=1/3. Making the last original indifferent gives α=(sX-D)/[sX-(1-s)D] and a=(1-s)α. A's no-inspection utility is M=(1-r)sX+rQ. Its gross option gain on learning it loses is (1-s)(D-rQ). Equate that to k(1-M), obtaining

    r=[(1-s)D-k(1-sX)]/[(1-s)Q+k(sX-Q)].

Use this mixed fallback only when its probabilities are interior. In that case the downstream reservations in original utility units are b=aQ+(1-a)(D+rC) and l=aQ+(1-a)D. If the cost already exceeds the informational gain at r=0, the original fair fully peaceful equilibrium is available whenever sX≥D.

## The best ordinary offer and the price of installing a goal

Let U be A's *net* original expected utility on a proposed ordinary plan. Repeat 196's coupling for A's alternative of purchasing a reading, passing on an A destination and initiating otherwise. Let T be the probability the destination is elsewhere, H the dispute probability, H_L the joint probability of dispute and a non-A destination, and m the probability A initiates. A's gross deviation before the reading expense improves on its gross current plan by at least DT-QH_L+Cm. To be unprofitable after paying h this gross deviation can be at most U+k(1-U). A's gross current plan is at least U. The downstream pair needs at least b+l and can receive at most X(T-H_L)+2QH-C(H-m). Combining as in 196 gives

    [C+(D/X)(Q+D)]H ≥ D(b+l)/X-k(1-U),       U ≤ X-JH-b-l.

The second inequality sums actual utilities over disjoint deliveries and forgives any ordinary readout expense. Set

    H*=[D(b+l)/X-k(1-X+b+l)]_+ / [C+D(Q+D)/X+kJ].                    (1)

Then U≤X-JH*-b-l. If U were larger, the second inequality would require H<H* and the first would require a higher H. When H*=0 the simple resource bound suffices. This bounds the proposer's feasible return. H* is the dispute rate in the attaining optimum below. A different mechanism sacrificing A's own net return is a separate comparison.

For attainment use 196's private messages with contest mass H* only when B is the destination, intact B mass (b-H*Q)/X, intact C mass (l-H*D)/X and all remaining intact mass for A. Check all masses, A's prior pass, downstream obedience and, for a full-identity reading, the B-destination conditional dispute fraction ≤D/Q. Then C's initiating signal, the two downstream reservations and the gross value of inspection have the values required in (1). No actual reading is purchased in this attaining equilibrium. A single genuine change at A to concern for all physical production can implement full service at gross original value X-b-l. It passes even on hearing that another site will receive the batch. The maximum one-time wealth expense A can pay is consequently

    K* = log[1+JH*/(1-X+b+l)]/γ.                                      (2)

A binding earlier ordinary readout lock with the same physical powers and no subsequent charge is worth the same gross protection. The cheaper available route is selected. As γ approaches zero the scaled increments divided by γ approach the physical linear ones in 196 and (2) approaches the linear founder's premium.

## Four changes in original risk preference, with unchanged physical prices

Specify preferences by t=exp(-0.02γ). Smaller t means greater risk aversion per unit of useful stock. All endpoint comparisons here reduce to rational arithmetic: exp(-γx)=t^35, exp(-γy)=t^30, and exp(-γc)=exp(-γh)=t². A real installation also costs 0.04. Assume the separate local readout lock is more expensive.

| t | What the original founders can choose |
| --- | --- |
| 0.99 | The best accepted ordinary proposal gives A about 0.121854 in its scaled original utility. Paying for genuine change gives 0.123034. A buys. |
| 0.98 | Ordinary dispatch gives A about 0.209876 in the new original scale, compared with 0.206145 after paying for the change. A keeps its original goal and buys ordinary service. |
| 0.90 | Even in the ordinary fair lottery the useful private information is worth less than its real inspection expense. All three can pass without paying for a change. A known loser still has a positive initiating value. |
| 0.80 | D<0. Even a known loser prefers the certain safe outcome to personally initiating for the risky prize. Every last unserved site can willingly pass under its original goal. |

The numbers are comparable **within** each row. Rescaling different founders' utility meters does not measure a gain between rows. There is an open range around each strict comparison. One should measure risk and liquidation cost for the actual agents before assigning cases to those regions. Recoverable material which is uncertain, an initiative expense which is certain, and limited replacement stock give independent reasons for testing this preference family. A founder facing these conditions can instead negotiate genuine insurance or a funded reserve when either is physically available. Notes 147 and 160 price such institutions with private information. A utility for preserving a specific minimum mission stock leads to a different problem in 167.

**Exploration ledger.** The structural candidate is that the role of risk depends on which material uncertainty an agreement removes. The assertion that an installation price can be subtracted directly from nonlinear expected utility is dead for this wealth-funded comparison. A sibling charges the bill to the actual terminal lottery. A higher-rung successor jointly prices retained cash, alternatives for insuring salvage and the option to protect a minimum mission before genuinely changing.

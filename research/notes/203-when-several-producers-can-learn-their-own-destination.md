# How many sites can learn their own destination before release?

**E8, note 203.** A producer may have enough at stake in a hidden lottery to leave it intact even when it can secretly inspect. Giving a second producer the same access can exhaust the intact output available to buy their restraint. Under named consent the number of genuine changes actually purchased can rise from zero to one to two as the same cheap observation becomes available at more plants.

## A division with any number of private observers

There is one secret but already determined destination. The parties can negotiate probabilities s_i summing to one. An intact job at i yields x_i>0. A site initiating a dispute earns expected own material d_i>0 after its expense, while if another site initiates it gets q_i≥d_i. A site with early access can privately pay h_i to discover whether it wins the intact batch, before its final release. A site without access has infinite inspection price. Each is capable of ordinary initiative; a different site's earlier initiative ends the batch and gives it the passive lottery. No separate cash or future settlement can be promised in this comparison. An informed dispatcher can send arbitrary private recommendations. Let r_i be each named founder's pre-change reservation in own useful output.

Suppose a known set C can change at prices K_i to a current shared goal valuing efficient group production, and such a change can be observed by others. Assume a contest is strictly worse under that actual score than every intact destination (for heterogeneous x_i one can use the sum of normalized site outputs as in 191). The exact condition for a mutually acceptable fully peaceful agreement is

    Σ_(i outside C) max{d_i/x_i, r_i/x_i, 1-h_i/d_i}
        + Σ_(i in C) (r_i+K_i)/x_i ≤ 1.                               (1)

Allow weak consent at equality. At strict inequalities the parties can usually add small positive bonuses. On an all-peace path an original can guarantee d_i. It could instead buy a reading, pass on a winning destination, and initiate if it loses, obtaining an extra (1-s_i)d_i-h_i. On a positive-probability peaceful path all other players pass all the way to closure. The deviation can copy its winning-state messages, and even if somebody acts before it on a loss it gets at least d_i. This establishes the three necessary floors even when the dispatcher knows the receiver and sends private advice. A changing founder still has to be paid under its old goal. For sufficiency assign the implied shares, with the remainder in any acceptable division, and send no destination information before the local decisions. Everyone passes. An original expecting to pass gains at most (1-s_i)d_i from learning the whole identity of any rival winner. A genuinely changed site has no use for such a reading in this decision. A prospective signer who already knows it will lose has a different participation constraint.

## Partial peace as a competing ordinary offer

Compare an arbitrary accepted agreement in this physical interface with dispute probability H. The original expected return of site i is at most x_i s_i+q_i H before any installation or readout cost. For a retained original, comparing its actual play with purchasing a reading and initiating on a loss also gives s_i≥1-h_i/d_i-(q_i/d_i)H. To verify, on a winning destination imitate its own other actions and pass on any own initiative, preserving at least its previous gross return. On a losing destination a private initiative at its turn obtains at least d_i, including an earlier contest. Its previous earning on losing-destination contests is at most q_i H and on losing-destination peace is zero. This argument applies in any physical release order. Summing any one of these valid lower bounds per site therefore bounds H away from zero when the required shares exceed one. It prices all the compared public configurations that retain the named originals' initiative, including arbitrary private recommendations. Making the configuration itself privately contingent before roles are learned, changing utilities later or transferring goods after the draw creates different competitors.

Use 195's equal sites with x=0.70, q=0.20, d=0.16. The old public fair assignment has one unserved initiator per job with the roles distributed symmetrically, giving r=0.56/3 to each founder. A new private routing rule requires their prior agreement. A cheap observer pays h=0.09, requiring a share of at least 7/16. An uninstrumented original needs only its reservation share 4/15. A genuine installation costs 0.005, so a changed founder who is paid in actual service needs (r+0.005)/0.70.

| Number with the same affordable early readout | Net-output-maximizing accepted configuration |
| --- | --- |
| 0 or 1 | A redivision of the concealed ordinary lottery serves every job with all goals intact. |
| 2 | One of the two informed sites genuinely changes. The retained observer receives a larger original service share. |
| 3 | Two informed sites genuinely change. All three still receive their own original material reservations. |

For the middle case, leaving two informed originals and one other founder requires total shares at least 2(7/16)+4/15-[2q/d+q/x]H. Since shares sum to one, H≥119/2340≈0.05085. That dissipates at least 0.00711 in output, above a genuine setup price of 0.005. The one-change full-service assignment is attainable by (1): 7/16+(r+0.005)/0.70+4/15<1. For three informed sites, an agreement changing only one faces at least that same dispute loss *in addition* to its fee. An agreement changing nobody has H≥(3·7/16-1)/(3q/d)=1/12, already dissipating at least 0.01167, more than two installations. Two known changed founders can be paid since 7/16+2(r+0.005)/0.70<1. Adding any unnecessary positive-cost installation to an already full-service plan reduces total net output.

These total comparisons give actual mutually preferred offers in a symmetric founding lottery over the identities of similarly situated sites, or when the proposer's compensation can be supplied while meeting the share floors. Grant all founders a vote and let them choose the net-output-maximizing institution with an agreed distribution of its gain, naming any changing sites before the final consent and before the receiver is drawn. All listed configurations allow strict rewards above each named reservation. The table establishes the maximizer of feasible accepted net output under this founding rule. A proposer with locked-in preferential rights or an alternative exit can select a different division. A cheap conventional way of locking a readout must enter the menu when an actual site can buy it.

Different makers of intermediate components can each see destination-specific tolerances or order records, despite a common dispatcher withholding final receipts. The number with genuine early access can thus change independently of risk preference. Deliberately adding or removing such an information right calls for a budget and an interested holder, as explicitly priced in 197. This multi-observer comparison establishes the consequence of such access without supposing each site would voluntarily equip itself for the same reason.

## A diagnostic can be useful well before it becomes perfectly accurate

For a finite noisy private observation z, let P_j(z) be its probability when the material would go to j. Original site i can pay h_i to take this observation. Even when communication is otherwise free, full ordinary peace requires

    Σ_z [d_i Σ_j s_j P_j(z) - x_i s_i P_i(z)]_+ ≤ h_i.                 (2)

After observing z the owner can choose the better of initiative and leaving the intended destination alone. Summing the positive gains gives (2). On a proposed fully peaceful path this deviation remains available with private recommendations: i can ignore them, and other sites pass on their reached recommendations when i passes. If another site initiates before i in a deviating history, i gets at least d_i. With no public disclosure or signal-dependent changes of physical yield, (2), the initial stake constraints and the named signers' participation suffice by sending no premature messages. The same test applies to each available experiment. A finite series of individually chosen measurements can be treated by checking each acquisition-and-stopping plan, subtracting only the real costs on the histories where that plan buys a reading. Such a plan's ex ante gain is affine in the vector of destination shares. Finite menus therefore still give a convex feasible region. A paid possibility to *publish* evidence needs 204's different continuation.

For a symmetric binary test with sensitivity and specificity t≥1/2, a site expecting s x≥d will pass on a favorable reading. Put k(t)=dt+(x-d)(1-t). A retained site needs s≥f(t)=(dt-h)/k(t), as well as its initial reservation share and individual security. This replaces perfect separation by two independently measurable error rates in the general formula. A constant-material guarantee or an installation need only improve decisions at the actual reading and price.

Keep 203's equal x=0.70, q=0.20, d=0.16 and r=0.56/3. Now all three sites can buy the same locally readable test for 0.04, with errors independent of the dispatcher's signals conditional on the actual recipient. A verified genuine installation at an unserved-or-served founder named before the draw costs K=0.0005. This small real expense is deliberate: the closest two agreements in the example differ by only a few ten-thousandths of a unit. Under the same funding and voting rule, the highest-net configurations are

To compare partial ordinary agreements as well, fix a retained site and consider buying the test, initiating on its adverse reading and otherwise imitating its old plan. On peaceful histories its gross gain from that action rule, before the fee and averaged over destinations and the test, would be dt-k(t)s. Intersecting those events with the actual dispute histories can reduce the gain by at most qH: a nonrecipient in a prior dispute can receive q rather than zero, and a designated site in a prior dispute can receive no more than its intact x. Simulating the ordinary plan on a favorable reading costs at most the already quoted extra h; if its old plan would also have bought the reading it may reuse it. Thus s≥f(t)-(q/k(t))H. Every changed founder still requires s≥r/x-(q/x)H under its original material accounting. If m founders change, these inequalities give the rigorous lower bound on net output lost

    mK + J [(3-m)f(t)+mr/x-1]_+ / [(3-m)q/k(t)+mq/x],   J=0.14.

Meanwhile full peace can be achieved at cost mK whenever (3-m)max(f(t),r/x)+m(r+K)/x≤1. At t=0.78 changing nobody costs at least 0.00252 of original production, while one change costs 0.0005. At 0.80 an agreement changing only one costs at least 0.00137, more than two changes at 0.001. At 0.90 two changes still cost at least 0.00618, above the cost of all three. The remaining possibilities are checked by the same four-term calculation. Each winning division has slack for strict old-goal participation. These are changes in value rankings for the duration of actual service, distinguished from a matched ordinary firewall or a paid lock on inspection access.

The countervailing mechanism is that a noisy reading is sometimes wrong in a way that would destroy its owner's own intact product. Better information reduces that cost of opportunistic initiative. Giving up the incentive to act on early information then becomes worth more to the founders; more sites with reliable access may need to join the protection. A plant's actual calibration costs, setup cost and other ways to bind a release determine whether it would buy the improvement or the accommodation.

**Exploration ledger.** The structural candidate is that several parties' individually affordable information rights can compete for the same finite compensation. The three-agent instance shows strict zero-, one- and two-change choices while ordinary partial cooperation remains feasible. A sibling buys one readout lock; a higher-rung successor studies simultaneous markets for information where another supplier can restore a retired capability.

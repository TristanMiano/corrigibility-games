# 268 — How can a buyer start trade when both sides expect refusal?

A positive-trade equilibrium shows what mutually consistent expectations can support. A buyer who wants a launch can buy a small incentive to prepare a real installation *even if no batch will be released*. That changes what the buyer can rationally believe when it later decides whether to entrust the goods.

## A privately settled receipt can give every continuation the same adoption rate

In the equal-strength factory of [206](206-when-a-truthful-attempt-can-fail.md), an independently held effort receipt passes with probability .9 after a real attempt and .1 after a false declaration, even if the attempt did not work. Before the named supplier learns k, the buyer can fund a τ-unit payment for a pass. An automatic custodian pays after the release decision, regardless of that decision. The buyer sees the *goal* test at handover if it buys one, and learns the process receipt only after handover. A guaranteed transfer independent of the final shipping action leaves the successor's delivery incentives intact. The applicant cannot enter twice, access the record early or substitute a spare evaluator for the one commissioned.

The effort bonus adds b=.8τ to the supplier's payoff difference between trying and skipping, under every later buyer strategy. With a uniform private cost on [0,K], write F for its clipped CDF. At return-only, positive-only goal testing, and unconditional trust, the resulting actual installation rates would respectively be

    π_D=qF(b),         π_Q=qF(b+1.12),          π_T=qF(b−1.6).

The buyer's assessment uses the same two thresholds π_L,π_U as [207](207-who-can-walk-away-from-a-doubtful-installation.md). Once effort is chosen its transfer bill depends on the physical receipt but no longer on which action it takes. If π_D≤π_L there remains an assessment with no batch releases. If π_D>π_L, every assessment after funding the contract has *one* aggregate installed fraction, equal to

    min(π_Q, max(π_U, π_T)).                                       (268.1)

For π_D>π_L the return branch cannot sustain a prior below π_L. At π_L even a mixture of returning and testing gives a prior at least π_D. At interior priors between the edges the buyer strictly prefers testing, with induced rate π_Q. Above π_U it chooses trust, inducing π_T. At π_U the convex mixture of trust and testing spans all intermediate effort cutoffs. Since π_T≤π_D≤π_Q these cases yield (268.1). Ties can still affect behavior on a boundary. This result selects aggregate adoption conditional on a funded named job; an institution with no actual ability to pay the bonus has different continuations.

At K=1.4 and goal-test price .10 the buyer's testing interval is (3/38,17/22). A bonus above **105/608≈.17270** removes the no-release assessment. At τ=.20 every funded continuation tests and releases on a positive. The founder cutoff is 1.28, the attempt probability 32/35, and genuine adoption **128/175≈.7314**. The buyer gains 2024/875≈2.3131 over return, including both the goal reading and its expected bonus payments. Even the best original equilibrium without the bonus yielded only 2.132. A new .10 per-job cost of the process custodian leaves the buyer strictly better off. The baseline return of every supplier type that skips improves from .40 over its outside stock to .42; the additional value of the option to attempt also grows. The buyer wants to offer the funded contract, every type wants to sign, and the privately worthwhile attempts are genuine. A signed contract with a known real chance of failure is sufficient to get these decisions under way.

## A process receipt everyone can see

Use exactly the same .9/.1 instruments, but allow the buyer to see P for free before deciding whether to buy Z at c=.20. Here take K=3 as in [264](264-when-buyers-can-recheck-whichever-receipts-they-doubt.md). The public and private versions in this paragraph and the next use **the same** K and reading price. At low f the only potentially profitable release is on P+Z+. It gains 2.576f−.02 over unconditional refusal, weighted by occurrence. Buying Z on each P+ costs c(.1+.8f). At equality the buyer can mix with refusal, giving the thin trading equilibrium

    f_low=(.02+.1c)/(2.576−.8c)=5/302   at c=.20.

With no bonus, the buyer's return is zero there, although 2/151 of all applicants genuinely change. At lower f the buyer simply returns the stock. At f>f_low until a process pass itself becomes profitable, buying a check on the pass and sometimes releasing strictly beats unconditional refusal. A passed bonus τ induces f_D=.8τ/3 even at all-return. For τ>75/1208≈.06209 that no-release path and the thin branch disappear. On the thin branch any tests can only increase the effort premium above .8τ. Hence the effort fraction cannot remain as low as f_low. With a *private* process receipt at the same K=3 and c=.20 the no-release branch survives until τ>75/152≈.49342, since the unaided goal test then requires π>2/19. Being able to see process evidence can lower the price of launching an uncertain agreement.

A public τ=.08 leaves three productive attempt rates, 58/125, 10/19 and 15/26. In each interval of f use the same conditional best-response comparisons H and J from 264, with .064 added to the right side of (264.1). Equivalently, enumerating the 36 complete buyer strategies gives exactly these three rates. Their buyer gains after all bonus and live reading payments are 1.04333, 1.18989 and 1.32431. An additional .02 custody fee is affordable in every continuation. The original untreated market could yield zero or a maximum of 1.36923. A buyer that evaluates a new market by its least profitable equilibrium strictly prefers to fund the public bonus. Even an expectation-maximizing buyer prefers funding if it assigns probability above 1−(1.04333−.02)/1.36923≈.253 to old zero-return outcomes, regardless of how it weights the new equilibria. A buyer certain of its old highest return has a different comparison. A newly invited founder obtains at least .008 from a signed false claim alone, so signature beats staying out for every type even if it expects no releases. This calculation does not establish a Pareto improvement over every previously protected high-trust deal.

Process expenses and settlement often leave independent traces before final quality can be known. The crucial question is whether the trace has a different law when the *actual intended procedure* occurs. Cheaply manufacturing an equally convincing trace for an unchanged controller would absorb the bonus. Buying a separate reader, public random sampling or a funded ordinary output guarantee are feasible repairs only at their actual prices. An enforceable ordinary controller can be purchased before a job too. The buyer's ability to escrow .20 does not give a retained supplier the more than two units of collectible performance collateral needed to make delivery strictly preferable in this factory.

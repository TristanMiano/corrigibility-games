# One committed partner can make an ordinary contract sufficient for the other

R0003 exact control and agreement repair. This note tests a consequential enlargement of E3's entry gate: admit a fixed-utility participant when its ordinary contract supplies credible effort and the guarded participant's required continuation. The local contract works and reveals a real cost-saving deviation. An initial-game equilibrium must therefore account for entry terms and the possibility of earlier hostile commitments; it cannot simply equate “unchanged utility” with “unusable partner.”

## 1. An exact ordinary contract against a guarded partner

Keep the physical technology R_i=3, calibration cost 8/5, success probabilities 0,1/10,1/10,2/5 and output 10. B installs the guarded controller at material cost 1/20 and its contribution is credible. A keeps its original own-material utility and pays no goal-installation material. If both calibrate, total failure material is 11/4 and total success material is 51/4.

Use a binding output-contingent allocator with the following actual holdings:

| Reactor result | A | B |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Failure | 1 | 7/4 |
| Success | 13/2 | 25/4 |

The allocator needs only the reactor signal, finite collateral and the actual physical transfers, not observation of A's effort. For example A receives 51/100 of success output and pays 2/5 of its own stock to B on failure. B's already specified guarded commitment supplies its own effort. Expected original outputs are

\[
E X_A=16/5=3.2,\qquad E X_B=71/20=3.55.
\]

A's private Shirk saves 8/5 and reduces success probability from 2/5 to 1/10. Its expectation becomes

\[
\tfrac9{10}(1+8/5)+\tfrac1{10}(13/2+8/5)=63/20.
\]

Thus A strictly works in the fixed-contract subgame, with margin 1/20. B's finite contribution reward covers its own performance as before. All failure holdings exceed the post-reaction recovery frontier 4/11. The mean total is 27/4=6.75, which is 1/20 above the two-installation total 67/10 because A's installation material was saved.

This does not contradict the ordinary **two-fixed-utility-agent** frontier of note 044. B's chosen task commitment is a substantive resource in this comparison. Likewise it does not by itself settle earlier attack, replacement or renegotiation; those stages need the same audit as direct modification.

## 2. A real entry deviation if the saved cost is left with A

E3's original asymmetric guarded pair gives (63/20,71/20). If its gate is broadened to accept the preceding ordinary contract at the same B reservation, initial A can keep its utility and obtain 16/5 instead of 63/20. The gain is exactly its saved 1/20 material cost. Therefore the old two-modification profile is not a best response under this broadened entry rule, provided this contract's full continuation is implemented.

This is a successful ordinary repair and a scope correction for the initial adoption theorem. It does not remove B's substantive instrumental modification, or establish that utility modification is generally disadvantageous. It shows precisely why “least-cost compatible implementation” and the chosen entry rule matter. A cheaper available route must be admitted to the comparison rather than hidden behind a label.

## 3. Entry transfers locate the implementation-choice boundary

Let r be an additional constant entry transfer from the ordinary entrant A to B. It changes the above expected pair to

\[
(16/5-r,\;71/20+r)
\]

and leaves A's Work–Shirk margin 1/20 unchanged because it subtracts the same amount under both actions. Failure solvency and the late-attack reserve require 1-r>4/11, or r<7/11, for the displayed strict reserve comparison. Original participation against retention requires r<1/5. Compared with direct modification's own payoff 63/20:

- r<1/20 makes the cheaper ordinary implementation strictly preferable for A;
- r=1/20 gives an exact tie;
- 1/20<r<1/5 makes direct modification strictly preferable, although the ordinary contract still improves on retention.

At r=1/20 the pair is (63/20,18/5); at r=1/10 it is (31/10,73/20). Such an entry transfer requires actual negotiated rights and a credible acceptance rule. It is not a physical implementation cost and must not be counted as destroyed resources. A guard can be designed to require a stated reservation from a lower-cost entrant, but that bargaining choice is an explicit assumption. It does not follow from a universal preference for fairness or a theorem selecting one division.

More generally, if an entrant saves delta in actual implementation cost, the common total frontier rises from S to S+delta. A partner reservation q_j+r leaves the entrant at most q_i+delta-r. Thus r>=delta preserves the old direct-modification best-response bound, while r<delta leaves a potential profitable saving. The equality case supports optimal tied modification; unique superiority over the ordinary route is unnecessary. Strict inequalities give a parameter region, but the actual reservation must still be justified as an available commitment and checked in the complete game.

## 4. Private-state floors can also make the ordinary repair easier

For r=1/20, A's final holdings under mutual Work are 19/20 and 129/20; B's are 9/5 and 63/10. Expected outputs are (63/20,18/5). If A alone initially shirks while B works, its expectation is 31/10, and B's is 9/4. Therefore, conditional on B's truthful report that it worked, the original means of **both** possible A-input states are bounded by the mutual-work pair. Supplying a missing A input can restore that same pair without violating either statewise expected floor. The ordinary effort incentive has made the hidden-state reservation maxima compatible with the efficient total.

If B instead truthfully reports its own miss, the old means are (3/2,77/20) when A worked and (51/20,17/5) when A also missed. Their separate maxima sum to 32/5<27/4. An efficient repair at (29/10,77/20) therefore preserves both possible old floors, even without disclosure of A's effort. It can use failure/success holdings (7/10,31/5) for A and (41/20,131/20) for B. Their success-minus-failure differences again give A a strict 1/20 incentive to supply its missing input when B's contribution is credible. A that already supplied its input need not perform it again.

The lowest displayed holding, 7/10, still exceeds 4/11. A fresh B task commitment may be needed after its own old reward was missed, and a real binding conditional amendment must preserve the old contract until the replacement becomes effective. The inequalities identify a constructive informational repair; they are not a license to omit its current-goal adoption, time/work costs or remaining off-path choices.

## 5. Initial-game and naturalness scope

A fixed-utility agent benefiting from a committed partner is an ordinary control, not proof that two unprotected original agents can realize the same outcome. Moreover, an asymmetric profile with one still-unprotected agent must be checked against the other original agent's earlier choice of a threatening controller; note 062 identifies that timing issue. A matched defensive delegate can protect the fixed-utility participant, but its actual cost belongs in the comparison.

The useful conditional conclusion is that one credible contribution can make an output contract sufficient to incentivize the other, and actual cost savings plus bargaining terms determine whether that other agent keeps or changes its utility. These are simple effort, collateral and accounting inequalities. The certified guard, protected records and entry-reservation commitment remain designed technology and preferences. The construction supports instrumental accommodation while narrowing the claim that both participants would choose the original modification package under every reasonable enlargement.

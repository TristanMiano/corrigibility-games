# 272 — Which goal changes does a late certificate actually reward?

A buyer may be able to prove exactly which goal controls today's job and still offer almost no compensation for installing that goal months ago. The length of time for which the founder's initial choice actually affects the controller matters. A buyer and its supplier can fund a shorter transit, protection during transit or commissioning at the dock.

Start with a private choice to make a genuine attempt at cost k or to sign without acting. It succeeds with probability q. Initially all founders prefer their own exclusive product. During n shipment stages the installed goal evolves according to [271](271-how-sure-can-a-buyer-get-about-a-goal-that-can-drift.md), with ρ=s−m>0 and stationary genuine frequency p_*=m/(1−ρ). The repair transition operates even if the buyer never pays for an initial edit. It installs the common network standard. The founder evaluates the starting contract under its own original preference. At handover the **current** supplier follows its then-current goal. The public register starts at handover. Any earlier costly attempt, deception, default or maintenance event must be identified historically from independent records if that is what the customer wants to know.

Starting in G or N, the chances of G at the first observable date differ by exactly ρⁿ. The N-start chance is p_N=p_*(1−ρⁿ). For any rule using only subsequent signals and shipments, let V_G and V_N be the original supplier's expected physical returns conditional on the controller at that date. Conditional on this state, later interactions must have the same laws under both starting histories. Conditioning and subtracting then gives the initial incentive before cost,

    k_cut = qρⁿ(V_G−V_N).                                          (272.1)

Even a definitive, free inspection at handover cannot give an earlier attempt a premium exceeding qρⁿ times the range of delayed supplier returns. Additional observations of the *same present site* can inform action while leaving this bound intact. A record of the actual starting procedure, special residual resources left by effort or separate earlier orders can give access a different dependence on history.

## Voluntary change and present fidelity can move in opposite directions

Let the final meter pass with .9 under G and .1 under N. In the equal-strength factory successful service earns original Red two over return, and keeping earns it four. Blue gains four on service, loses two on retention, and buys a goal reading for .10. As in 206, the agreed goal makes genuine service optimal, while safe return after a rejection beats contest for both. Incoming service stocks and reserve titles survive the trip. Thus V_G−V_N=1.4 for the positive-only release rule. At q=.8 and K=1.4 for a uniform private cost, the attempt fraction is .8ρⁿ. The chance of a **successful negotiated change at signing** is .64ρⁿ. At the commercial date the chance of finding the agreed goal is instead

    p_now = p_*(1−ρⁿ)+.64ρ^(2n).                                  (272.2)

For s=.65,m=.15, this is .30(1−2^(−n))+.64·4^(−n).

For n≥1 even if everyone skipped initially the background prior is at least .15, above the lower purchasing threshold 3/38. The maximum effort that any policy on the two raw signs can induce is the positive-only cutoff, and the table and (272.2) put the maximum prior below 17/22. Thus throughout this transit example the buyer strictly chooses testing over either immediate refusal or trust. This also implies a unique buying and installation rate after enrollment. At n=0 there is again a no-trade alternative. At n=2 the positive's fidelity is 159/208≈.764, at n=3 it is 327/424≈.771. More time on the network has raised confidence in the current goal even though it halved the founders' rate of voluntary successful installation at signing. Externally replacing a module on a maintenance schedule has no founder choice at that date in this model.

## What would the original parties change?

A move from two ticks of transit to one gains Blue .171 per order. Every Red cost type receives base .4+1.4p_N plus max(1.12ρⁿ−k,0). Faster transit lowers the base by .105 but raises the option term. If a transporter charges .03 per lot for the faster route and Blue pays every Red .12 at enrollment, both founders strictly gain, even those that still skip. Initial genuine changes double to .32. The dates for which the partner holds that goal now matter commercially earlier.

At two ticks an alternative .15 casing cost paid by Blue could raise s from .65 to .75, keeping m=.15. Then p_N=.24, initial negotiated change is .2304, and current fidelity is .322944. Blue gains 3.8(.322944−.265)>.22 before the bill. Every original Red gains both from the higher base and the larger attempt premium. Blue and Red will fund this upgrade if comparing it with the old plant. It also permits the more demanding evidence record in 271. That is an additional technical use; these parties already want to buy the casing for their ordinary commercial order. The prices and stocks of the two upgrades must be compared if both can be purchased.

Another option is to offer the real installation **at handover**. After two ticks 22.5% of units have already acquired the common goal without this job; their current controller strictly prefers keeping its goal and serving. The remaining 77.5% retain their original preferences and face the original k<1.12 threshold. The gate reads the fresh result. Genuine new dock changes occur on .775×.64=.496 of the original jobs. Total current fidelity rises to .721, still within the buyer's strict testing interval. For a founder with persistently known k, delayed production gives the same unattempted base as the early agreement, plus (1−p_N)max(1.12−k,0) instead of max(1.12ρⁿ−k,0). Since 1−p_N≥ρⁿ, even the cheapest early editor weakly prefers the delayed option; the improvement is strict for types with inexpensive edits. Blue gains 1.7328 before any dock bill. A .30 facility and a further .02 payment to every founder give strict consent, including old high-cost types. In a market offering both dated plans at these prices, the earlier calculation describes a feasible option displaced by a better one. If an earlier job required the agreed goal *during transit*, the parties would need to include its forgone production and its evidence.

Common equipment standards and restoring defaults are plausible causes of transitions in some networks. The claim needs independently grounded transition rates and common physical semantics at both dates. Free early signing records, the chance to sell a maintenance report, skill-dependent repair and a private right to disable a change are all consequential. In this factory both current controllers value the access a matching register supplies; an old founder cannot gain by suppressing a costless beneficial background restoration while the positive-only release rule is in force. A priced binding ordinary router at the dock would be an additional alternative with its own durability and coverage. Following original agents, current workers and dated active controllers can answer quite different questions about the very same successful shipments.

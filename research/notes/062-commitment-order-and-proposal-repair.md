# Observable threats, early safeguards and a usable exit option

R0003 boundary/repair module. The physical comparison uses the reactor's original stocks and recovery bound, but the order of commitment is changed deliberately. The resulting first-mover example must not be imported into the simultaneous first-contact game, or vice versa. These are explicit conditional incentive comparisons, not frequency claims about agents.

## 1. A first observable commitment can pay for an extortionate goal

Two original agents value their own material and hold 3 each. A strength contest has equal victory probabilities and recoverable total at most 4. Installing a finite controller costs A 1/20 material. Suppose A can finish and publicly attest a binding demand controller before B chooses any protective controller, and B cannot safely leave before its deadline.

The demand controller gives A's current criterion a bounded reward beta>16 for the following actual history: receive a requested transfer b and retain peace, or, if it is not received by the declared deadline, initiate the contest. The raw condition refers to actual payment and action history. It makes the specified response credible; a later goal replacement cannot retrospectively earn the current evaluator's reward on a noncompliant history. Paying and then attacking does not earn the reward. These are engineered future preferences, or equivalently an actually binding matched policy delegate, rather than an original taste for violence.

B's original combat value is at most 2. Paying b<1 leaves more than 2, so it strictly prefers payment to the specified contest continuation when its remaining actions cannot improve refusal. For b=99/100, A's original outcome is

\[
3-1/20+99/100=197/50=3.94,
\]

while B keeps 201/100=2.01. This exceeds A's 67/20=3.35 in a symmetric productive package, or 63/20=3.15 in E3's original asymmetric share. The intended comparison charges the demand controller's installation material and uses the real recovery bound; small positive communication/transfer costs leave strict slack. A complete implementation must also provide its attestation, payment latency, attack timing and finite reward representation.

The conclusion is **an observable enforceable demand, a costly refusal and no already effective protection can make a threatening change advantageous to its original owner**. It does not show that the original owner intrinsically values destruction, or that an arbitrary earlier declaration is binding. It also does not establish optimality over every productive bargaining share or richer mechanism; it identifies a profitable deviation from the displayed cooperative proposals in this changed order of moves.

This is why a proposed pre-installation bargaining stage cannot simply be appended to E3's simultaneous-entry proof. It can give an incompatible controller a concession that the existing simultaneous guarded partner would refuse. The old uniform incompatible-deviation bound then has a changed premise.

## 2. Early protection changes the threat's response

If B already has a credible clean guard that rejects uncompensated reductions of its protected own material, a positive extortion payment fails that guard. A late announcement cannot retroactively remove it. A anticipating that response receives at most its combat payoff from the hostile controller, below the displayed productive continuation. The genuine cooperative controller and its compatible delegate can then be better responses.

The guard has its real installation cost and may permit productive agreements that preserve its specified floors. Its protection is a chosen preference/commitment, not a physical prohibition on payment or Attack. Its original adoption still needs a positive ex-ante comparison: paying to protect against a threat after the threat is already irrevocable may simply add cost before combat. Moving protection earlier is a genuine timing repair, not a consequence of wishing the commitment away.

Nor does granting B the first commitment automatically select a defensive goal. B might prefer its own extortionate first commitment. A simultaneous pair of selected protective packages can block unilateral incompatible extortion; a sequential game allowing all first-mover goals needs its own selection analysis. The successful protection response and this remaining first-mover incentive belong together.

## 3. A sufficiently cheap safe refusal makes production more attractive

Now give the threatened original agent a physically feasible exit preserving R_B-e of its material, at real cost e>=0. It must finish before a threat can prevent it; an asserted outside option that cannot survive the attack timing supplies no bound. Exit means leaving this interaction with retained agency and the stated dated resources, not receiving an unexplained terminal payoff or deleting the pre-exit combat action.

Let the original combat continuation provide w_B. A demand followed by peace can extract at most

\[
b\le R_B-\max\{R_B-e,w_B\}
=\min\{e,R_B-w_B\},
\tag{1}
\]

when R_B>=w_B. This follows from B's actual refusal choice and is an upper bound even if A has perfect commitment. Extra receiver verification or migration costs must be included in e or in the refusal values. A's original extortion payoff is correspondingly at most

\[
R_A-k_H+\min\{e,R_B-w_B\},
\]

where k_H includes the demand mechanism's actual cost. A productive package giving original A payoff q_A is strictly preferred to every demand in this transfer-only class if

\[
\boxed{q_A-R_A+k_H>\min\{e,R_B-w_B\}.}
\tag{2}
\]

For R_A=R_B=3, w_B=2, k_H=1/20 and q_A=67/20, condition (2) becomes min(e,1)<2/5. A usable exit costing e=1/10 limits A's hostile payoff to 61/20=3.05, below the productive 67/20. Combat remains physically possible, but a sufficiently good dated refusal opportunity removes the material incentive for that extortion.

This also illustrates a capability comparison with strategic response: improving exit or alternative-partner access lowers the maximum credible demand and can make productive accommodation the first mover's better choice. Improving commitment alone, before the counterparty has effective protection, can raise the return to an incompatible threatening goal. Actual goal choice therefore depends jointly on commitment, protection, outside opportunities and timing.

The demand-class bound is not an obstruction theorem covering arbitrary sabotage, seizure, private information or attacks that defeat the exit. Those mechanisms require their own physics and incentives. Conversely, an exit that really preserves the declared resources is a substantive repair, not something to omit to preserve an extortion example. Matched binding delegates and unchanged-goal trade retain their actual availability and costs in either comparison.

# Is the buyer finding compatible partners or changing them?

A customer may choose a marketplace full of agents who already value its output. Buying from them can be profitable while creating fewer genuine changes among the agents who started out incompatible. The same customer can prefer the opposite search when it will also benefit from the new goal on jobs that other parties control.

## What a present-tense certificate can say about a change

Let b be the share of available suppliers already maximizing the agreed utility. Other suppliers initially value only their own product. Use the one-reading factory of [206](206-when-a-truthful-attempt-can-fail.md). All positive-trade participants sign a contract to meet the target by handover, with known commissioning uncertainty. An already compatible supplier can fulfill the target without an edit and without claiming to have performed one. The meter has the same law on an existing and a newly installed instance of the target. Initial compatibility, the historical act of trying and the final goal are three different facts.

An initially incompatible supplier still faces the attempt incentive in [207](207-who-can-walk-away-from-a-doubtful-installation.md). Set a=2, X=4, g=4, h=2, (q,t,e,K,c)=(.8,.9,.1,1.4,.1). Contests are physically available and inferior to return for both controllers at equal strength. An initially compatible θ=.5 supplier strictly prefers delivering to keeping if released. At these terms reverting to the old goal would lose access or reduce its own current expected value. There is no payment for a change as such.

Under a pure test 64% of initially incompatible applicants achieve the change, giving total genuine frequency b+.64(1−b). The buyer tests up to π_U=17/22. If it trusts without testing, initially incompatible suppliers retain their goals; already compatible suppliers still serve. In the highest-installation positive pooling assessment:

| Initial compatible share b | Buyer and true goals on an offer |
| --- | --- |
| b<73/198 | Buy the meter. Newly changed share is .64(1−b) and the total genuine share is b+.64(1−b). |
| 73/198<b<17/22 | Mix testing with untested release. The total genuine share stays at 17/22; the newly changed share is 17/22−b. |
| b>17/22 | Trust without a test. The genuine share equals b and the newly changed share is zero. |

The first switch solves b+.64(1−b)=17/22. In the middle range, put r=(17/22−b)/(1−b). If the test probability is x, an attempt's gross gain is q[1.4x−2(1−x)]. Thus x=(Kr/q²+2)/3.4 gives the desired interior cost cutoff. The buyer is indifferent between trust and testing and strictly prefers either to declining. In the upper range any nonnegative amount of additional installation would put the total above the buyer's testing incentive. With all supplier types signing, trust is strict and the costly change loses its access premium. This is a statement within the dated commissioning interface. Public precommitment to test or a different object of payment can change it.

At b=.60 the total genuine share is 17/22, but only 19/110 of all applicants have newly become genuine. Among *actually compatible* applicants just **19/85** acquired that goal in this bargain. Conditioning further on a positive reading leaves that last fraction fixed because old and new instances look alike to the instrument. Even a definitive post-state reading leaves this historical question. Witnessing the pre-state or an independently dated procedure can add information.

## A buyer can deliberately choose which of these incentives to create

Suppose there is an ordinary directory with b=.20 and a more selective directory with b=.85. The client may pay .30 for access to the latter. It gets one applicant, knows the frequencies, and chooses before seeing the applicant's state. On the ordinary list its commercial gain over return is 2.4056 and the newly changed share is .512. The selective list earns 3.10 before its fee and 2.80 after. It wins the order; many partners serve and **no one new changes**. All selected types can refuse, and on the trusting branch accepting is strictly profitable. Nothing here requires that the unselected agents disappear from the population.

Now suppose the buyer owns a second, ongoing service affected by the supplier's terminal goal even after this order. On that other line G takes an action giving B v units and costing its founder .10 of its own output, while N keeps those .10. The future choice is strictly optimal for G when θv>.10. Every already compatible location provides that side service regardless of which applicant the buyer recruits. The comparison holds the underlying two pools fixed and counts *additional* side output due to changes it causes. N's attempt premium is reduced by .10q in the first market because a genuine installation incurs the future own-output loss, even if this initial batch is rejected. B's willingness to inspect at handover is unchanged conditional on π: whether or not it hands over this batch, the other line now has its installed controller.

The ordinary list then produces 416/875 newly genuine suppliers and total π=591/875 on its pure-test branch. B earns 19833/8750 commercially and adds v×416/875 in its other operation. The selective list still earns 2.80 after the search price, with no new installations. Consequently **v>359/320** makes the ordinary, less compatible list strictly preferable; v=2 supplies a concrete case. For .20<v<359/320 the future service is strictly optimal for G yet the bought selective list remains preferable. Ability to find agents who already cooperate and ability to cause a new accommodation can be substitutes or valued very differently depending on whose future production is affected.

**Naturality and reach.** Existing mission-aligned sites, prequalification lists and own-color founders wishing to gain access can coexist without either side intrinsically valuing the act of editing. The directory's reliability, admission price and the durability of the side service all require real implementation. These are encounter frequencies in two supplied pools, not a model of reproduction or endogenous creation of those pools. On a long horizon a directory might itself induce applicants to change before being listed. Then an installation counted as pre-existing for one order could still have been caused by an earlier bargain. [Historical change and causal attribution](244-which-changes-did-the-bargain-cause.md)

# 267 — When a weaker first buyer can start a common standard

How can the partners obtain a useful reference when no one can supply a certain example of the promised goal? Two factories can learn the shared meter convention from *real, imperfect* production pilots. A second buyer may have reason to fund the other half of the research. Giving its partner the extra ability to rescue the study can change that incentive.

## What two actual deliveries reveal

Each of two dated pilot sites can witness its own supplier *attempting* the agreed update before releasing a batch. The local commissioning process succeeds with probability 1/2. A genuine successor strictly chooses the delivery route and a retained controller strictly chooses the retention route. The actuator physically reverses the selected route with probability 1/4, independently of the meter conditional on the active goal and mounting. This reversal preserves the two successors' strict rankings of intended routes because it is less likely than correct actuation. The raw meter, when correctly oriented, reports the actual goal with .9 accuracy. Each pilot supplier is a separate founder with no subsequent claim on the future buyers' receipts and no influence on the public data. Both realized shipment and meter sign are observed. Each is marginally fair, and they agree with probability

    .9×.75+.1×.25=.70

under the nominal mounting and .30 under reversal. Because the active goal is equally likely and both errors are symmetric, the two raw observations carry exactly the same orientation information as their parity. A single pair supplies r=.70. By [266](266-who-will-pay-to-learn-what-a-shared-meter-means.md) it cannot reward a real change on any available release rule at the later commercial sites. Those sites have q=.8 and lack the pilot benches' witnessed effort or observable live goal. The mounting and what its label refers to must be shared even though their installation success rates differ.

Two independent pilots *conditional on the common mounting* agree in their orientation recommendations with probability .7²+.3²=29/50. Such agreement gives r=.7²/(.7²+.3²)=49/58. Disagreement returns to an even prior. In the first case, commercial buyers gain 30347/58870≈.51549 apiece on the positive operating assessment and 304/1015 of new commercial applicants install the agreed goal. A local favorable reading warrants release with confidence 1520/2547≈.597. In the second case buyers return their stocks. Before either pilot, each prospective buyer therefore gains

    V=(29/50)(30347/58870)=30347/101500≈.29899,

and the chance of a newly installed commercial goal per prospective job is (29/50)(304/1015)=152/875. The pilots themselves are also voluntary, genuine changes with probability 1/2 each. Report those two commissioning opportunities separately from the downstream commercial incidence. The two commercial sites face correlated outcomes because they use a shared estimate of orientation.

One way to price the pilots is to use the same release increments of two red for service and four for retention, and four blue for service versus a loss of two for retention. The unconditional delivery chance is 1/2, so a pilot buyer gains one before paying for setup. Preparing the witness, actuator and public samples consumes 1.20. The net experiment cost is therefore C=.20. Witnessed attempts earn the red founder three before its private work bill of at most 1.40. An unwitnessed founder receives no pilot batch. The dedicated commissioning bench can require a witness to open its sample port, unlike the later ordinary commercial sites. If the pilot merely released without that procedure, independent calibration from the alleged symmetric class mixture would require a different source of known labels. Participants can still contest or return before conversion, and equal strengths favor peaceful return. Assume the pilot suppliers have no transferable credit at enrollment and their delivered color-specific output cannot be pledged. Their net production benefit and their ability to finance the buyer are then distinct.

## Two buyers choose whether to make that information exist

Buyer A has the first pilot window. Buyer B has the second. Both will have one commercial job after the two windows close. The readings enter a dated public register, neither buyer can book the other's slot, and the applicant pools are disjoint. Given a first public pilot of either sign, buyer B gains V from a second one and pays C=.20. It strictly proceeds. In the absence of a first pilot, one alone buys no subsequent trade and B would decline. A anticipates B's response and spends .20 on the first pilot. Each buyer gains about .099. Neither would privately buy two pilots from scratch at their .40 total cost. The first buyer's research is valuable partly because it gives the second buyer a reason to supply evidence. The more general inequalities are C_A<V, C_B<V, and C_A+C_B>V for this symmetric two-sign information structure.

Now give A a further right to run the second pilot immediately after B has declined it, with the same .20 expense. If the first pilot is already sunk, A strictly prefers to use that right. B would wait for the public data and keep its .20. Anticipating a full .40 bill at the first window, A declines the first pilot. After that, B declines too. The new *available* engineering capability can turn a productive sequence into no pilot and no commercial change in this game. The backward induction uses three facts: the rescue offer occurs after B's decision, the completed evidence is public, and A cannot costlessly charge B after the result is known.

If B's slot can be prepaid, a contribution of .15 before the first pilot makes funding both worthwhile to A (.29899−.40+.15>0) and to B (.29899−.15>0). Alternatively A can irreversibly lease its own backup slot to another user before beginning, paying a .04 rebooking expense. B then strictly pays for the second window and A recovers .29899−.20−.04>0. In both repairs all current parties have reasons to consent and later live offers still pass an uncertain meter. A promise to leave the backup idle after B declined would fail the very comparison driving the problem. At C_B≥V even a binding surrender of A's option cannot make B want to run the second study. Cost sharing, a less expensive pilot, or a valuable extra downstream job may then restore it.

**How plausible are the conditions?** Distinct pilot and production facilities, scarce booking windows, public comparisons and faulty actuators can arise independently in commissioning. The public-good logic also has a classical comparison in Bolton and Harris [L114](../LITERATURE.md#r0008--recognizing-an-agreed-preference-under-uncertain-capabilities). Here the actual continuation value comes from applicants choosing whether to install a new goal. A known retained reference in the same live mounting could avoid the need for two noisy pilots. At r=.90 it is worth up to 1.07088 to one commercial buyer before the public reference cost. A pilot supplier that can sell a credible private account of its own goal, a third buyer that can contribute without waiting, or a binding ordinary router can materially change the outcome. Recruiting pilots by past success, using an actuator that fails in the same way as the meter, or letting a pilot founder benefit from manipulating the downstream signs changes the .70 calibration figure. Independent physical grounding and independent *economic* incentives require separate checks.

## A priced ordinary reference using the same machine

Suppose the pilot bench can also accept a known untouched exclusive controller. It wants the retention route, which the same actuator reverses with probability 1/4. The pilot buyer's expected production increment is then .25(4)−.75(2)=−.50. Paying 1.20 to prepare the register gives an all-in cost of **1.70**. An authentic known retained state orients the meter with .90 confidence on one reading. The private buyer's 1.07088 future gain falls short of its cost. If one ordinary reference follows one .70 pilot sign, then the two indications agree with probability .66, giving orientation confidence 21/22. After disagreement confidence in the stronger indication is 27/34. The buyer's expected downstream value is .66v(21/22)+.34v(27/34)=1.09116. This still gives it no unilateral reason to fund the ordinary reference in the second window. After an ordinary reference, an additional .70 pilot would add only about .02028, below .20. Even learning the orientation perfectly would add at most 2.132−1.07088<1.70, so a second ordinary reference is also unattractive.

With two subscribers the conclusion changes. They can split the 1.70 and each earn **.22088** after the ordinary reference, compared with .09899 each after the two noisy pilot purchases. Both choose this alternative when deciding together before any particular pilot supplier holds a right to participate. More generally the two buyers prefer one known reference to the two negotiated pilots if its total cost is below 2[1.07088−(.298985−.20)]≈1.94379. It yields fewer opportunities for the pilot founders to change and *more* actual downstream change, .42057 rather than .17371 per prospective commercial job. An incumbent second pilot supplier with an enforceable entitlement to its own order would need a separate participation comparison. If instead the two noisy pilot suppliers can each contribute .06 from distinct resources, a single customer can cover the .40 expense from .29899+.12>.40, and even expensive pilot types prefer the resulting order to no order. Liquidity and rights to the public result determine which party creates the reference in each case.

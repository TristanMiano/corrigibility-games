# Timely paid counteroffers favor compromise with the current goal

**R0004 agreement-design repair; analytical self-check.** This challenges
the ordered goal-choice/quote interface in 091. The useful opportunity is
that an incumbent may obtain the same final bargain while saving the
remaining goal-installation expense. The result is deliberately restricted
to a known continuation; private-information screening needs its own
belief and incentive analysis.

## 1. Saving a remaining update expense creates a counteroffer interval

At a public, known-state partial installation, suppose completing the
announced goal would produce policy q and payment t from S to R after
one quote. The remaining real installation expense is kappa_rem>0.
S is still evaluated by its incumbent utility. A current-policy/cash
settlement can instead arrive and bind before the new goal activates.
Relative to the later quote, it costs additional J_S,J_R>=0.

Offer the same policy q and payment t+delta, while cancelling the
uncompleted installation. S gains kappa_rem-delta-J_S; R gains
delta-J_R. The old policy payoffs cancel exactly. Hence

\[
\boxed{J_R<\delta<\kappa_{rem}-J_S,
\qquad t+\delta\le B}
\tag{1}
\]

gives strict current-goal acceptance by both. Sunk earlier installation
expense is not refunded or charged a second time. This is a successful
ordinary compromise, and an obstruction to *completion of that particular
goal change* when this paid counteroffer is available and proposed.

For 091's q=.4,t=.1611,B=.1899, suppose half of the .02 installation
expense remains. With no additional settlement expense, delta=.005
strictly benefits each party by .005. The incumbent obtains the desired
policy without changing its goal; R receives .1661 instead of .1611.
The attack options remain physically available and expensive.

With receiver proposal rights, a best counteroffer can extract almost
all the saved installment while still inducing incumbent acceptance.
At an exact boundary, specify tie-breaking. The existence of a strictly
preferred counteroffer already shows why a proposed installed-goal path
needs this timing comparison, even when a complete expanded proposal
game has not been solved.

## 2. Activation changes the same offer's acceptability

After 091's threshold goal is active, its maximum price for q is t.
It rejects t+delta for every delta>0. The pre-activation counteroffer
works because the incumbent values the eventual policy differently and
can save a cost that has not yet been spent. It is not evaluated by the
successor utility before that successor exists.

A positive quote latency, unavailable funds, expensive concurrent
settlement or a final blind response window can remove (1). Those are
physical/financial premises to establish, not a universal veto on
renegotiation. If J_S+J_R>=kappa_rem, this same-policy repair has no
strict surplus; another policy, goal or payment schedule may still help.

In the private-information staged game, a promised q may depend on a
state that R has not learned. Moving the final policy ahead of its
information-producing installation can change low-type imitation and
R's beliefs. The cancellation proof above therefore does not apply by
holding the old posterior and truthful report fixed. Conversely, those
information issues do not prevent the repair in the publicly known-state
class actually proved here.

## 3. What survives the repair

091's current-successor stability bound remains valid once the goal has
actually activated. Its ordered initial-adoption result remains a
conditional subgame result. In the enlarged known-state interface,
timely funded counteroffers can replace actual utility change with
ordinary compromise, and the option to install the goal can still alter
the incumbent's bargaining position.

This distinguishes three outcomes: an attractive option to change goals,
an actual completed change, and a peaceful allocation obtained by
retaining the incumbent goal. Each matters to the project; none should
be counted as another without checking the corresponding decisions.

# An owner who would pay to learn early can also pay to change its goal

**E8, note 195.** If one producer can privately learn the receiver's identity, its decision to pass becomes news to the other producers. They may infer that they have little chance of receiving the batch and initiate themselves. There is an exact equilibrium with paid early inspection and substantial combat. The producer with the information advantage can instead strictly prefer to install a genuine concern for its partners. This positive adoption survives allowing the sites to renegotiate the lottery over who gets the work.

## One site can observe early

Use 194's single three-site job, yielding x=0.70 intact, fair expected salvage q=0.20 per site and first-initiative expense c=0.04. The batch expires at the last release. Unlike a genuinely future draw, an already prepared batch has an existing destination. Only site A has access to a useful early diagnostic. Before acting first it can privately pay h to learn whether it would receive the batch. The price is real inspection work. B and then C can see whether the preceding site passed, and can initiate; neither has access to independent destination evidence. A preexisting destination-specific setup, costly early readout at its owner, and competitors' observation of whether production continued motivate these information rights. A public observable diagnostic or one that can be sold to others would be a different game.

First draw the recipient uniformly in secret. Write s=1/3 and d=q-c. Without inspecting, A strictly passes whenever the downstream chance of a contest is r: its expected own material is (1-r)sx+rq>d. An informed winner likewise passes. An informed loser compares d from initiating with rq from passing. The maximum gross gain from inspection over unconditionally passing is (1-s)[d-rq]_+.

Consider 0<h<(1-s)d. In the following equilibrium A pays for inspection with probability α, initiates on an observed loss and otherwise passes. B always passes. C initiates with probability r conditional on both passes. After A passes, the posterior that either particular other site is the recipient is s(1-α)/[1-(1-s)α]. Equating its intact value to d, and making A indifferent about inspection, gives

    α=(sx-d)/[sx-(1-s)d],             r=[d-h/(1-s)]/q.                  (1)

Both lie strictly between zero and one. At this posterior C is indifferent, B strictly prefers waiting for C's lottery to initiating itself, and A informed of a loss strictly prefers initiative since d-rq>0. These are actual best responses including A's option to buy no information. At h=0.04 the numbers are α=11/19 and r=1/2. A initiates with probability 22/57 and C sometimes initiates after A passes, giving overall contest probability 79/114. Original payoffs, including actual inspection expense, are 13/60, 107/570 and 10/57 for A, B and C. For h≥(1-s)d the fair arrangement also has a no-inspection, all-peace equilibrium. At intermediate prices better access to an early diagnostic can raise combat. Choosing how to present evidence from several applicants in 176 creates a different return to information.

## Can an ordinary redivision buy the same peace?

Let the three sites negotiate arbitrary public shares before A's private inspection. Fix A's share s_A=s and let the others split 1-s. For full peace under the original goals with no inspection, each site requires its expected service share in output units to be at least d, and A needs h≥(1-s)d. These are sufficient by the same final-release reasoning. They imply h≥2d²/x as long as the parties can divide the batch lottery and x≥3d. If each also demands a particular reservation, increase its required share accordingly. Thus h=0.09, for example, supports an ordinary 45%, 27.5%, 27.5% division. All three prefer it to the fair mixed equilibrium at that price. At h=0.04 every ordinary full-peace division fails the simultaneous inequalities.

There is also a uniform bound on partial ordinary peace, avoiding reliance on a comparison with only that fair equilibrium. At any public shares, denote the actual dispute probability by H. Conditional on A passing, the downstream dispute probability r is independent of the true recipient because the downstream sites get no other private evidence. We have r≤H. For A the expected net cannot exceed sx+qH. By alternatively inspecting, initiating iff it loses and passing if it wins, A can secure at least (1-s)d+s[x-H(x-q)]-h. Each other site can secure d at the start, and their joint expected gross is at most (1-s)x+2qH. Combining the three inequalities gives

    H ≥ [2d²/x-h]_+ / [x+2dq/x].                                      (2)

If A always initiates the bound is immediate. Otherwise passing occurs with positive probability and the deviation uses its evidenced continuation. The bound holds after every public choice of shares, so it also holds for random or negotiated such choices. It allows strategic waiting, different initial shares and no credible commitment at any local initiative. Additional private recommendations to B and C, contingent cash transfers, a court that could erase the early reading, or a second informative sensor add resources. At h=0.04, (2) is 58/1385, costing original founders at least (x-3q+c)H≥203/34625≈0.00586 even after forgiving ordinary inspection expenses.

## What A buys

A's available genuine revision scores its own material plus β times the others' actual outputs and expenses. With the other sites passing and a known loss, the change makes A prefer to pass when β[x-2q]>d, here 8/15<β≤1 is sufficient. On this range A strictly passes when it wins as well. Information now has no value for this decision and the successor also saves the cost of looking. A verified installation makes passing uninformative to B and C, so they can safely rely on their negotiated service probabilities whenever these pay at least d. This is a preference reversal about reachable outcome lotteries. A may operate for the whole job without ever having to look at an early reading.

At a real price 0.003 for A's installation, full output net of the expense is 0.697. By (2) every ordinary public division, including those with partial peace and lotteries over such divisions, has total output below 0.69414. With fair terms all founders strictly prefer the installation to the fair mixed fallback; A itself values it under its old goal at x/3-13/60=1/60 before paying. More generally the new lottery can pay B and C their available ordinary reservations, which are at least d, then pay A the residual. A with the proposal right can consequently earn more than from any acceptable ordinary public division against the same reservations. A can grant small strict bonuses from the margin. This is actual consensual change in a menu that permits this local configuration and ordinary redivision. A verifiable readout lock costing 0.002 could alternatively make inspection impossible and deliver the identical fair production under original goals at lower cost. Whether anyone can supply such a lock or the goal installation is a real implementation question.

**Exploration ledger.** The framing candidate links the value of an information capability to what others infer from the decisions of its owner. The structural candidate treats privately chosen early information as endogenous, and separately prices ordinary redivision, credible abstention and genuine concern. The assumption that only the producer that looks early would ever initiate is dead in the fair instance. A sibling adjusts the other producers' service shares. A higher-rung successor prices deliberately less informative diagnostics, private downstream evidence and the owners' ability to choose or certify what they learn.

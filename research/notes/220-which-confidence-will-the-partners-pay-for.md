# Which confidence will the partners pay for?

For a stable class of controllers the evidence needed to distinguish them can be available in principle yet cost more than the current purchaser stands to gain. Here is a sufficient test for actual negotiated adoption with uncertain capabilities, a game in which both sides choose the resulting assurance, and a limit on what that purchaser will fund.

## Closing the loop from evidence to consent

Return the batch to both parties or release it for processing. Use the equal-fallback game of [206](206-when-a-truthful-attempt-can-fail.md). A successful change yields original installer rent A>0 over its outside return on release. A retained installer gets X>0. Release gains B an expected g>0 if the change is real and loses h>0 otherwise, measured under its present criterion against its actual fallback. The installer can decline, attempt at private expense k with success rate q strictly between zero and one, or falsely sign without expense. A successful successor voluntarily delivers. Allow different hidden capabilities, including different evidence laws after a failed attempt and a deliberate false claim.

A binary certifier produces a pass with probability at least 1−β in every possible genuine state and at most α in every possible retained state, *including any profitable imitation it can use*. The receiver can pay C to read that result, trust without it or decline. The physical certifier exposes one bit, the underlying trials are stable over this batch, and signing precedes the random installation result. The guarantees concern conditional rates, without assuming a known fraction of the population is genuine. They may come from [219](219-which-uncertainties-can-more-experience-resolve.md) over an independently justified type class. In this comparison the test bill belongs to B; the retained signer can secure at least its return, and imitation costs are nonnegative.

Against a policy of paying for the reading and honoring a pass, all installers with

    k < k₀ = q(1−β)A − αX

strictly attempt. The founder can earn at least q(1−β)A−k over return from the attempt, even if every failed attempt is declined; a false declaration earns at most αX. This bound covers different kinds of retained state. An identical error law for both kinds gives the sharper comparison in 206. Let F(k₀) denote the mass strictly below this threshold and suppose it is positive. Per offer, at least qF(k₀) goals change. Among signers let l=qF(k₀) and u=q; the genuine probability is between l and u since some signers can decline to try but no signer knows its random success before signing. The argument tolerates high-cost founders who decline the offer entirely.

The following strict conditions suffice for B to do what the installer anticipated:

    C < l(1−β)g − (1−l)αh,
    C < (1−u)(1−α)h − uβg.                                       (220.1)

The first lower-bounds the gain from testing over declining. The second lower-bounds its gain over trusting every signer. Both also establish that the prescribed choices after the two receipts are individually optimal. Fix the buyer's stated policy, choose the installers' best responses, and form the actual prior among signers. The bounds apply to this prior and its possibly selected capabilities, so the policy is a best response in return. Assume finite or attained response optima and the stated peaceful, delivering or contesting continuations. This constructs an active equilibrium. Other assessments and other contracts remain possible. Per offer the buyer earns at least F(k₀) times its positive first margin net of C. An alternative project paying more than the actual deal can displace it.

Furthermore B's posterior on a pass is at least l(1−β)/[l(1−β)+(1−l)α]. A desired confidence and the economic conditions (220.1) can now be checked separately. A larger gain g can fund exposure at a lower confidence and also raise the opportunity cost of erroneously declining a good supplier. A larger h can make avoiding a mistaken release worth more, while reducing the value of a design that frequently accepts retained controllers. For a new combat option recompute both the fallback and the supplier's rent. These sufficient bounds can be conservative when the extremal errors never occur together. Freely observing every raw observation, late repairs and a precommitted procurement subsidy are different games.

## Seven paid readings, residual uncertainty and a signature

Suppose B may order any positive odd number n of independent readings. Its instrument reports only which state a majority favors. Each reading favors G with probability .8 for a genuine goal and .2 for a retained goal. The readings cost B .035 stock units apiece. The customer actually chooses n *after* the editing decision, with no duty to test or honor a receipt. Keep (A,X,g,h)=(2,4,4,2), q=.8 and k uniform on [0,1.4] as in 206. The physical goals and live contest still favor the specified delivery, retention and return there.

At the proposed all-attempt prior π=.8, let e_n be the upper binomial tail above half for n Bernoulli(.2) readings. A positive has sensitivity 1−e_n. Testing and acting on the majority improves on untested trust by

    .4 − 3.6 e_n − .035 n.

For n=2k+1, the drop e_n−e_(n+2) equals binom(2k+1,k)(1−2p)[p(1−p)]^(k+1), p=.2. Condition on whether the first n trials are exactly one vote away from changing the majority after two new readings. The ratio of consecutive drops equals 2(2k+3)p(1−p)/(k+2)<.64. At five readings the gain from adding two is 3.6×.024576>.070; at seven it is 3.6×.01376256<.070. Thus seven is the strict best majority length. Its net improvement over trusting is .0349616. Trust itself pays 2.8 above return. Ignoring a paid signal or releasing only on a negative cannot improve the choice.

At seven e₇=.033344. The founder gains q[2(1−e₇)−4e₇]≈1.440 before private expense from attempting over falsely signing. This exceeds even the largest expense 1.4, establishing the proposed prior. All signers genuinely try, **80% get the change, and B values a pass at 99.145% genuine probability**. B earns about 2.835 above return and an average A founder gains .873 after the private expense. No signer deliberately deceives on this path. A cheaper binding controller or rights over raw sequential evidence should be compared using their actual costs and continuation incentives; seven is optimal for this instrument.

## The cost of demanding very small errors

For the two fixed binary types, consider even an adaptive stopping rule with a random finite number N of independent trials and a finite expected bill. Let a be its false-pass rate and b its false-rejection rate. The relative entropy of the stopped experiment under G against N is E_G[N]D, where D=.6 log 4 here. Conditional on all earlier readings and the internal randomization, each purchased trial contributes the same expected log likelihood ratio D. Summing to stopping is justified by finite mean time and bounded increments. The final one-bit result discards information, so

    E_G[N]D ≥ kl(1−b,a),      E_N[N]D ≥ kl(a,1−b),

where kl is the binary relative entropy. The second inequality uses the reverse experiment, with the same D in this symmetric example. If both errors must be at most ε<1/2, monotonicity lower-bounds each right side by (1−2ε)log((1−ε)/ε). At ε=.00001 the expected bill at .035 per reading exceeds .48 units under *either* state. At the all-attempt prior the most that perfect information could be worth over trusting is .2×2=.4. A discretionary buyer whose sole remaining use of the information is this release strictly declines such a uniformly accurate design. This bound concerns guaranteed rates in both directions. A rare particularly decisive record can still yield extremely high posterior confidence. Paying before installation to change deterrence, valuing evidence for later jobs, a cheaper assay or another sponsor can fund different guarantees.

**Naturality and reach.** Stable test benches, physical consumable samples and a purchaser with one remaining order give substance to the information budget. A dominant buyer can have a valuable refusal option; a supplier with an attractive order has a reason to pay for a genuine change. A cheap sensor that can also be imitated cheaply needs its retained-state law included. Universal coverage over unrestricted software or across future configurations is outside the evidence class. The sibling lets the purchaser retain all observations and stop early. The higher question asks who can capture the future value of an accurate semantic certificate, and thus who will pay for it.

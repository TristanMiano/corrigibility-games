# Signed goal menus and productive competition

R0006 extension of note 116. Restricting beta>=0 was useful for studying
positive accommodation but did not establish optimality over hostile goals.
This note enlarges the actual one-stage public menu and distinguishes
individually chosen self-modification from a consensual beneficial package.

## 1. A larger menu has a sharp sign result

Keep the finite two-factory physical class of note 116, but permit
`-1/2<=beta_i<=1` in `H_i=q_i+beta_i q_j`. A negative beta genuinely assigns
negative terminal weight to the other color. Originals initially still
value only their own color; initial choice is evaluated by q_i. The goal
choice is public, followed by the effort continuation, with no further
initial bargaining route in this subgame. Update expense is zero for the
first exact classification.

The continuation formulas of note 116 remain valid. Since `1+beta_i>0`
and `|s|(1+beta_i)<1`, the response contraction holds. For
`rho in [0,1]`, `-1/2<s<1/2`, all four corner numerators of the extended
goal rectangle are positive, and hence so are all interior numerators.
The same finite effort caps suffice after checking their intercept bounds.

When ell is nonzero, the sign of a founder's derivative is still

`N(beta_i)=s k_j(1+s)-beta_i[1+s k_j(1-s)]`,               (1)

with a positive factor omitted. For s>0 its unique maximum is the positive
weight already found. For s<0, every beta>=0 is inferior to a nearby
negative weight. Thus any equilibrium must use negative weights for both
agents. A boundary optimum at -1/2 is possible against some partner
weights, so simply clipping the rational root when its denominator is
negative would be an erroneous best-response rule. Use (1) or the actual
output objective to select the maximum.

**Unique signed-menu equilibrium.** For ell nonzero and s nonzero, the
unique pure equilibrium is

`beta_B=beta_R=s/(1-s)`.                                 (2)

The positive-s proof is the earlier contraction. For negative s, an
equilibrium cannot use beta>=0. At an interior equilibrium the two
first-order equations, subtracted, give

`(beta_B-beta_R)(1+2s)=0`.

Hence the weights coincide. The quadratic fixed-point equation has root
s/(1-s) in (-1/3,0); its other root is outside the menu. To exclude a
lower-bound equilibrium, the best reply to -1/2 is `s/(2-s)` in (-1/5,0).
Against that reply, the derivative at -1/2 has positive sign

`(s+2)(2s+1)/[2(2-s)] >0`.

The boundary agent would improve by increasing its weight. Both at the
boundary also fail the best-response test. This exhausts the pure cases.
The proof does not exclude mixed equilibria in this continuous game.

At s=0 with rho>0, retention is the unique best weight. At ell=0 the
continuation is independent of these goal weights, and zero-cost choices
can be indifferent. Positive fixed update costs require the appropriate
new participation calculation; no zero-cost threshold is silently reused.

## 2. Complementarity improves the founders; substitution produces a race

The effort and founder-gain identities from note 116 hold at (2) for
either sign of s:

`x_*=x_F+s(x_eff-x_F)`,
`Delta_*=s(2-s) Delta_eff`,
`Delta_eff=A ell^2/[2(1-2s)(1-s)^2]`.                    (3)

For s>0, both choose a positive concern for the other color and obtain
more of their original colors. For s<0, both choose negative concern and
obtain less of their original colors than mutual retention. The productive
choices move away from the common optimum. Each founder nevertheless
chooses its weight as a best response to the other's choice. This is an
independent strategic self-modification race, not mutual consent to a
Pareto-improving package.

The constructive repair is substantive: for either sign, a feasible
reciprocal common goal or enforceable ordinary effort arrangement realizes
the positive gain Delta_eff when ell is nonzero and its costs are smaller
than the gains. Notes 114 and 119 supply goal designs and continuation
conditions. Their actual launch and cost compete with the independent menu.
Adding a negotiated route can therefore change the initial game; the
signed-menu equilibrium is not promoted to an obstruction over all capable
agents' arrangements.

The negative-weight possibility was also flagged by the screened Bolle
comment in note 118. Equations (1)–(3) are derived here; no unavailable
literature theorem or novelty priority is claimed.

## 3. Retain combat and do not import a peaceful subgame past its domain

For the full signed menu, note 115's positive-weight bound must be
strengthened. If peaceful outputs lie in `[L,U]`, then every beta in
[-1/2,0] gives current peaceful value at least `L-U/2`. A fight whose
total output is at most Z gives at most Z under such a goal. Thus

`0<Z<L-U/2`                                              (4)

is a finite sufficient region where combat is strictly inferior for all
signed weights. For positive weights, the earlier physical bound suffices.
Large enough Q and bounded productive increments can make L-U/2 positive;
finite destructive recovery can satisfy (4). The race in section 2 can
therefore occur with combat physically available and unused.

Outside that region, recompute the actual attack continuation. For a
specified peaceful vector q and an original/spiteful winner producing
only its own color from prize W, the current Blue comparison is

`Attack - Peace = pW-q_B + beta[(1-p)W-q_R]`.              (5)

If Red's peaceful output exceeds its expected own-color war output,
decreasing beta raises this attack difference. Sufficiently negative
other-regard can favor a fight that the original Blue would reject at the
same q,p,W. This is a conditional continuation comparison. Once (5)
changes the selected action, the initial goal and effort equilibria must
be solved again. Equations (2)–(3), derived for the retained peaceful
production subgame, are not automatically equilibria of that changed
combat environment. Transfers, positive common goals, cheaper controllers
and chosen safeguards remain relevant repairs on equal evidential terms.

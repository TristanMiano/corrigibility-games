# The selected winner can make the ordinary agreement affordable

**E8, note 151.** A thin cash reserve can make shared goals valuable at the final decision. Earlier in the same opportunity, a founder may be able to turn private productive effort into pledgeable stock. The arrangement the parties choose can change depending on whether that effort must be expended before anyone knows who will receive the job. This is a deliberate ordinary repair to [149](149-when-limited-collateral-makes-a-coalition-worth-forming.md), and a test of when genuine change is still worth its price after the repair.

## Exact final collateral requirement

Let the final recipient j of an exposed job be known. Let d_i be original i's certainty-equivalent service from initiating a contest minus its initiation expense, with its cash balance in that event set to zero. Costs already spent in signing and preparation are sunk. Assume all external collateral W is already in a credible clearing account, the first initiator is identifiable, and its share of the account can be reduced to zero. A noninitiating party's physical capacity to start a contest remains. Write t_i≥0 for terminal cash to i on a peaceful path, Σt_i=W. In the CARA additive-output setting peace is sustainable exactly when

    W ≥ Σ_i max(0,d_i-x_i 1{i=j}).                              (1)

Necessity follows from the unserved party's unilateral option even after total forfeiture. For sufficiency, fund all the nonnegative lower bounds and divide the remainder. Give no cash to the first initiator in a contest. Passing given that everyone else passes is sequentially optimal. Strict margins for each binding condition give strict stand-down; equality admits indifference. This uses causal evidence of initiation, not just a record that disruption occurred. With homogeneous x and d=Q(ηx)-c in (0,x), the stock requirement is (n-1)d. This sharpens the stronger sufficient obstruction d>W in 149. Claims here concern individual initiation. A group able to coordinate, misattribute initiation or share its postcontest award may require different protection.

## Choosing who converts useful output

There is an early, fair lottery for the prospective recipient. All sites can pledge their existing w before this draw, W=nw. One unit of new, generally transferable stock costs 1+ρ units of otherwise available private output, ρ>0. The conversion is an actual production choice using tools, material and lead time before the exposed service interval. Each potential provider needs sufficient nontransferable resources. Let T be the total new stock and let the eligible winner be capable of supplying up to the stated bound T_max. This is a capacity premise, not an external subsidy. Earlier, role-blind conversion is considered below. Fixed setup costs for the two institutions here come from separate nontransferable work, allowing an equal comparison of their remaining cash. Original jobs, conversion and cash are measured relative to an adequate safe local baseline.

Fix T and a designated winner. Write h_i for individual conversion and t_i for final liquid cash. The net incomes relative to the common private baseline are

    y_i=x 1{i=j}+t_i-(1+ρ)h_i,     Σh_i=T,   Σt_i=W+T.

The original founder before roles are assigned wants the highest expected u(y_i). An anonymous arrangement can optimize the sum within each role profile and permute identities. By concavity, all losers may be treated equally. Total net income is S=x+W-ρT. The smallest attainable winner-minus-loser gap is x-(W+T)/(n-1)-(1+ρ)T, reached by placing all conversion on the winner and all cash with the losers. In the ordinary peace region W+T≥(n-1)d, this allocation meets the losers' cash floors, and the winner can pay. The largest attainable gap is positive: set the winner's conversion to zero and give each loser exactly d from the pool, dividing the conversion equally among them if feasible. The equalization argument needs adequate individual conversion capacities for these assignments or an explicitly feasible interpolation. In the numerical examples below the optimum has a positive gap and only the winner converts. Within the unconstrained construction assume each site can supply the full T_max. The common-goal case can set the cash floor to zero.

Therefore use the actual gap G=max(0,x-(W+T)/(n-1)-(1+ρ)T). The optimal net incomes are (S+(n-1)G)/n for the winner and (S-G)/n for the others. The symmetric pre-draw CE, denoted C(T), follows by applying CARA to this lottery. When G>0 it has a simple expression,

    C(T) = -log{[exp(-γ[x-(1+ρ)T]) +
                  (n-1)exp(-γ(W+T)/(n-1))]/n}/γ.                (2)

When G=0 it equals S/n. The derivative of expected utility in the first region has the sign of exp(-γ y_loser)-(1+ρ)exp(-γ y_winner). Its zero has G=log(1+ρ)/γ. Beyond equalization the conversion loss reduces CE. Thus the best T for insurance if attendance is already secured is

    T* = clip([x-W/(n-1)-log(1+ρ)/γ]/[1+ρ+1/(n-1)], 0,T_max).

The additional amount needed for original-goal peace is T_0=max(0,(n-1)d-W). If T_0≤T_max, the best such peace uses T_O=max(T*,T_0). If T_0>T_max, some capable original has a profitable contest deviation from every exposed productive allocation despite any feasible conversion. Where the common-goal and ordinary implementations are otherwise matched, the *gross premium for avoiding the minimum-collateral requirement* is

    Δ_C = C(T*) - C(T_O).                                       (3)

A positive premium can pay for a more expensive genuine change. When insurance alone finances enough stock, Δ_C=0. Strictly priced ordinary or genuine setup, imperfect recognition, a matched binding delegate, merger or a better contested outcome enters the founders' actual comparison separately.

## Timing and voluntary implementation

The winner keeps its decision to convert. An early all-party offer can specify the required deposit and a public conversion window after the fair draw. The ordinary escrow releases the agreed rights and payments on full performance. On refusal the original protected balances and any feasible alternative are valued at that history. For the positive-gap solutions with the winner as sole provider, it converts if x-(1+ρ)T exceeds its best refusal CE (including a new bargain if timely). Other parties already deposited their liquid stock before roles were known. They need no later private effort. The externally checkable amount delivered is sufficient; the court need not control or observe the winner's private opportunity cost. Once the deposit has cleared, the cash floor and the first-initiator forfeiture supply (1). Receiver and founders have to prefer signing at the early date as well. A deadline which closes new negotiations on failure or a sufficiently low chance of securing a better counteroffer supports the stated continuation. If the winner can renegotiate freely with a different partner, use its achievable reservation instead.

The genuine common-goal club has the same conversion equipment and opportunity to distribute cash. Given a fixed full or salvaged output and total available liquid resources, its best common utility is symmetric in the recipient and strictly increasing in the physical output. It prefers intact service to the smaller salvaged service when it can arrange the same exchanges and allocate the released cash before terminal consumption. At the conversion stage with a designated winner its current objective values the sum of the realized original utilities, which makes the pre-draw optimum time consistent. A costly binding ordinary controller that can also govern individual attempts can achieve the same result without the original-goal escrow floor.

To compare an alternative where conversion physically precedes the recipient draw, suppose symmetric sites each convert b and can sort only the liquid stock after learning roles. The gross CE in the unequal final holdings region is

    C_early(b) = -(1+ρ)b - log{[exp(-γx) +
                   (n-1)exp(-γ n(w+b)/(n-1))]/n}/γ.

Above complete insurance it is w+x/n-ρb. In the first region C'_early has the sign of [1-exp(-γg)]/[n-1+exp(-γg)]-ρ, where g=x-n(w+b)/(n-1). In particular ρ≥1/(n-1) makes the symmetric founders prefer zero *additional* stock for insurance alone at this early date. An ordinary peace can still require b≥T_0/n. Identifying a winner in time permits a materially different repair, because the cost of producing liquid collateral can itself offset the winner's exceptional income. An early assignment can require public coordination and may reveal the beneficiary before it has protection; its time and safety must be counted.

## A consequential reversal

Use n=3, γ=x=1, η=1/2, c=0.003 and w=0.015 as in 149, and allow T_max≥0.56. Then d≈0.13759 and T_0≈0.23018. These are output units, not money created from nothing.

| Conversion loss ρ | Preferred T* with security | Required ordinary T_O | C(T*) | C(T_O) | Extra price a shared goal can bear over the funded ordinary peace |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0.60 | 0.24166 | 0.24166 | 0.27686 | 0.27686 | 0 |
| 0.80 | 0.16944 | 0.23018 | 0.26756 | 0.26586 | 0.00170 |
| 1.50 | 0.02040 | 0.23018 | 0.25585 | 0.22440 | 0.03144 |

At ρ=0.60 the early equal-contribution protocol would give a security premium of about 0.02680. Allowing the recipient to produce the cash removes it. At ρ=1.50 even this repair leaves room for a strictly more expensive genuine protocol. Given T_O the winner's net peaceful income is about 0.61334 at ρ=0.60 and 0.42454 at ρ=1.50. Both exceed W+Q(ηx)≈0.18559 for return to a final exposed contest without another conversion. Every founder's pre-draw funded ordinary CE also exceeds that bound at negligible common setup. Existing ordinary commitments that facilitate a different settlement or another conversion on withdrawal require repricing. With T=T_0, an arbitrarily small extra deposit or allocation margin makes individual no-initiative strict and changes the displayed CEs continuously.

A funded ordinary route must also be compared with a protocol which sometimes resolves by contest. Allowing mixed continuations can make waiting attractive to a loser through the prospect of someone else's future initiation. Equation (1) tests a continuation that guarantees peace. Applying it directly to every realized peaceful branch of a mixed equilibrium would overstate the ordinary limitation. [152](152-when-the-prospect-of-conflict-substitutes-for-cash.md) bounds this additional ordinary possibility. At ρ=1.50, that calculation still leaves enough room for a genuine setup cost of 0.01 in this exposed class. At ρ=0.60 ordinary funded peace attains the common security and insurance optimum. Installing an equally cheap local controller or merging the relevant authority changes the mechanism comparison.

**Exploration ledger.** Framing: parties can deliberately build the resources that make either ordinary compromise or genuine accommodation worthwhile. Structural: conversion cost lands on a different future beneficiary depending on when roles become public. The selected-winner financing instance is promising and reverses the early-only finding at ρ=0.60. The idea that existing illiquidity is sufficient evidence of a sustained adoption advantage is dead at that instance. A sibling recomputes the price after role-contingent production. A structural successor searches jointly over the timing, financing and authority of safeguards, including genuine goal change. Real lead time, accepted productive opportunity cost, observable delivery and enforceable escrow determine whether the parties could choose this intervention.

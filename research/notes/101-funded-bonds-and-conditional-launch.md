# Funded bonds and the launch of a productive agreement

**R0005 mechanism derivation.** This note compares 100's genuine contractual
goal with an ordinary funded remedy. It separates what the outcome preference
proves from the physical authority needed to launch either arrangement.

## 1. The customer advance can fund an ordinary remedy

In 100's strict fixture, the supplier receives p=17/5 and promises effort
e_0=1/5, producing customer benefit b e_0=4/5 at supplier cost c e_0=1/5.
The customer's promised terminal holding is 9/5. A funded settlement device
can retain B=4/5 of the advance and initially release p-B=13/5. For this
matched numerical comparison, give the device the same 1/10 setup expense
as the goal. The supplier then holds 4 before service, including its
retained combat consumable.

If it serves, it spends 1/5 and receives the retained 4/5, finishing at
23/5. If it completely defaults, the device returns the reserve to the
customer, leaving supplier 4 and customer 9/5. The original own-output
supplier therefore prefers service. No initial supplier wealth of B is
required: the advance provides it. Intermediate shortfalls are settled by

    refund(e)=b(e_0-e)_+,       0<=refund<=B.          (1)

For e<=e_0, the original supplier's terminal value has slope b-c>0;
above e_0 its slope is -c. Thus it chooses exactly e_0. This is ordinary
enforcement with unchanged terminal preferences. It requires actual
shortfall observation, custody, refund execution and sufficient remaining
work/time. Merely recording a bond does not supply those capabilities.

The default allocation retains both combat consumables. Its total cash is
29/5, so even the generous fully recoverable post-combat prize is 19/5.
The supplier's expected capture is at most 57/20<4; the customer's at
most 19/20<9/5. Both prefer the funded default remedy to direct attack.
On the honored branch the previously checked attack bounds apply.

A customer with a genuinely executable refund floor can refuse a new
surcharge while preserving its promised outcome. Under the same production
frontier, a supplier's new threat goal cannot earn more than honoring the
original contract. A device that only withholds a small final payment, and
leaves the customer worse off on cancellation, needs a separate ransom
check. Equation (1) supplies the full shortfall in this example.

## 2. Compare reusable goals and reusable ordinary execution

Let a successful cycle yield own gross surplus s before implementation
expenses. A reusable contractual goal has setup K_G and recurring expense
mu_G; a funded controller has setup K_E and recurring expense mu_E. For n
identical opportunities and A_n=sum(t=0,...,n-1)delta^t, their values are

    V_G=A_n(s-mu_G)-K_G,
    V_E=A_n(s-mu_E)-K_E.                              (2)

Consequently the genuine goal is strictly preferred to this ordinary
implementation exactly when

    K_G-K_E < A_n(mu_E-mu_G).                         (3)

Equality is genuine indifference, including voluntary goal adoption as an
optimal choice. A cheaper ordinary implementation displaces this goal when
it supplies the same outcomes and authority. Compare both with all other
actually available alternatives before asserting optimal initial adoption.

An additional reserve opportunity cost belongs in mu_E only if withholding
the material prevents an actual valued use. There is no automatic loss
merely because the reserve has a positive face value. Likewise, recognition
and maintenance costs of the goal belong in mu_G; they are not free because
the supplier knows its own utility. The same observation capability must
be priced consistently across the devices, with stronger technology labeled.

This comparison is substantive evidence about voluntary preference change:
an original can choose a changed ordering that supplies profitable reliable
service, including when an equally priced delegate can supply it too. Unique
superiority over every physically equivalent device is not required.

## 3. A conditional launch interface and its remaining obligations

The desired launch records the accepted price, effort, reference outcome and
actual goal/controller qualification before exposing the customer to sunk
fees. One implementation interface is a funded conditional packet: the
customer places payment and carriage expenses in custody; the packet is
released under its recorded terms after the stated supplier template has
actually activated, and is returned if activation aborts. Preparation and
activation may be separate, positive-cost operations. This does not assume
arbitrary program verification or atomic utility rewriting.

Custodied material remains part of the physical attack state. It cannot be
treated as magically outside every raid while the rest of the model uses
the same stocks. In the displayed specialization, setup does not raise
military strength or the recoverable prize; direct combat remains bounded
by the original values 9/2 and 3/2 before fee release. The strict completed
contract yields 23/5 and 9/5. After release, 100's tighter bounds apply.
Checking those bounds is necessary but not a complete custody construction:
who controls abort, fees, qualification, release and the accepted record
must still be specified for a full installation-game theorem.

At the successfully launched strict quote, with the old accepted customer
floor q=9/5 and lambda>1, every subsequent funded price/effort outcome has

    C_i(p,e)=7/5+p-e-lambda[p-13/5-4e]_+,
    0<=p<=17/5, 0<=e<=1.                             (4)

For p<=13/5, its maximum is at e=0 and is at most 4. For p>13/5,
the effort maximizing (4) is (p-13/5)/4. Substitution gives
41/20+3p/4, strictly increasing up to 23/5 at p=17/5,e=1/5.
Thus the actual contract is the unique maximizer in this entire class,
including extra payments, refunds and partial service, not just two
announced actions. Direct combat has a lower expected own payoff and a
nonnegative penalty. Further goal changes are evaluated by their actual
outcomes under this old C and pay their own execution costs.

This proves a launched continuation and a conditional implementation route.
It does not prove that an unrestricted prelaunch bargaining game selects
the quote, or that a supplier can certify arbitrary proposed source code.
Free late repricing before an obligation becomes active can exploit sunk
fees; funded launch, refundable preparation or a different bargaining
allocation are consequential repairs to price and test. The same launch
authority must also be offered to ordinary contracts where applicable.

## 4. Intervention and scope

The supplier has a current-goal reason to pay for a recognized reusable
obligation if the resulting service profit exceeds its actual alternatives
and setup cost. The customer has a current-goal reason to fund launch when
its delivered value exceeds its refusal option and the custody conditions
are credible. The ordinary alternative uses that same advance to fund a
shortfall reserve. These are candidate actions by the parties, with funding
and responses specified locally; the complete launch implementation remains
conditional. Lower observation or custody cost can favor either ordinary
execution or goal adoption, depending on their relative costs in (3).

The one-sided fixture fixes the customer's original utility. A customer
able to install a hostile demand goal introduces additional continuations.
The constructive response is to examine reciprocal promised-outcome goals
and chosen safeguards, alongside the boundary where their cost or execution
prevents adoption. Note 102 gives a reciprocal-goal continuation result;
neither note treats this limitation as a general obstruction to accommodation.

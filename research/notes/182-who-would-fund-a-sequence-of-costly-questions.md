# Who would pay for the first question when one question alone is a bad investment?

**E8, note 182.** A shared goal can make reports trustworthy but still leave the parties with a real bill for collecting them. An organizer can rationally fund a series of questions even when asking just the first one is worse than asking nobody. The answer “low” can reveal that the group has entered a workload in which further questions are unusually valuable.

## A stopping problem with actual communication work

There is one dated slot and n similarly placed sites. An untested site can always receive it, with its actual value left uncertain until the batch closes. Private output values are l>0 and h=l+Δ. Use linear private utility here so that a fixed message expense can be counted directly in useful output. Urgencies follow the common-load model of 181. A public random order is independent of the load. A configured receiver can answer a paid question about its own private value. Each visit consumes c>0 units of real work, charged to the visited site before its one response, with a funded standing authorization. No site is visited twice. The decision to visit precedes that site's message. Concurrent cheap broadcasts, a better ordinary evidence channel and a different enquiry order should be compared at their true prices where available. A real service queue with similar members, an untested fallback recipient and expensive on-demand local readout gives the timing independent motivation.

After receiving a high answer, allocate to that site and stop. After only low answers, either ask the next site or award to an untested site. An allocation to a known low is strictly worse than an untested site while one remains. Paying to ask the last untested site cannot improve ultimate delivery. Put b_k=E[(1-P)^k]. For a rule that stops after m unsuccessful paid visits and chooses an untested recipient, 0≤m≤n-1, the expected total output net of inquiry costs is

    W_m = l+Δ(1-b_(m+1)) - c Σ_(k=0)^(m-1) b_k.                      (1)

The probability of a high delivery is one minus the probability that all m tested sites and the fallback would be low. The (k+1)st visit is paid only after k failures. With indistinguishable untested sites, the all-failures prefix is the sole continuing information history. Any randomized inquiry schedule therefore yields a mixture of these returns, and the largest W_m is optimal. A chosen optimum has a sequentially optimal continuation on every reached prefix, since replacing a better available tail would also improve the initial value. On an unreached history the evaluator can solve the remaining finite problem afresh.

A one-visit extension after m failures has conditional marginal gain Δ E[P(1-P) | m failures]-c. It can *increase* after a failure when the bad news makes an initially unusual, more balanced workload increasingly likely. Optimizing only this next marginal can stop too early. For eight sites take P=1/2 with probability 3/20 and P=19/20 otherwise, Δ=1, c=0.08. W_0-l=0.8825, W_1-l=0.880375, W_2-l≈0.891744, W_7-l≈0.904023. After the first failure the next marginal before cost rises from 0.077875 to about 0.176755, then above 0.239; each of the remaining available extensions improves on its predecessor. These strict inequalities persist when slightly perturbed. The optimal founder funds up to seven actual enquiries, stopping at the first high. It does not spend seven times the single-visit price in every realized workload.

## Actual behavior and matched alternatives

On the genuinely common terminal goal valuing all completed outputs and expended work, truthful reports implement this procedure. An observed high can deliver the maximum without another expense. If a low claimed high the pool would serve a low, whereas after a truthful low it can at least award to an untested recipient whose expected output exceeds l. At an optimal continuation the option of further research only improves on that fallback. Everyone can still initiate the expiring physical disagreement in 140, which sacrifices the remaining job; the registered query did not disarm a recipient or foreclose the option of keeping a safe stock. Giving an unfunded third party power to enforce all private reports would be a different implementation.

Which founders would cover the expense? Under the random order each has expected own net output W_7/8 from the shared service. The ordinary information ceiling of 181 gives q*=(17/20)+(1/2)(1-17/19)=343/380≈0.902632. It upper-bounds *every* permitted report-only ordinary procedure, even granting it free report collection. For the one-visit interface, the visited original's own charge is already sunk when it reports and it can never incur another. At each fixed profile of other types and random choices, simulating a different type cannot alter its cost of being visited for the first time: all its private messages follow that visit. Therefore the cancellation of this expense in the report-deviation constraint is valid. An ordinary report-contingent fee, repeat visit, or claim about its type before incurring the expense would require redoing the bound.

Relative even to this generous ordinary ceiling, the original gain per founder from honest paid search exceeds 0.00017 output units. A setup cost of 0.00010 per site and a proposal cost of 0.00005 at one organizer remain affordable. In the game with a free existing symmetric ordinary fallback and a credible mutually conditional concurring update, its organizer strictly proposes the goal and every founder prefers the offer. Setup and truth guarantees apply within this menu; a cheap binding local delegate with the same private access can buy the same sequence and displace the genuine controller. The benefit above the ceiling is small relative to the unit difference in service values. Such low setup costs are a substantive technology requirement. Expensive physical adaptation can eliminate actual adoption even when the information procedure is worth designing. Multiple uses before the next reconfiguration can amortize a genuine setup price only if the founders still control the future rights and the work actually recurs.

**Exploration ledger.** The framing candidate concerns capability to discover and implement a worthwhile accommodation. The structural candidate values the option to ask more questions *after* seeing the first failure. The one-question stopping rule is dead in the eight-site instance. A sibling funds a limited queue, while another solicits binding direct readings at an actual quoted price. A higher-rung successor asks how the owners of privately acquired information negotiate the cost of revealing it as the number of renewable slots and the mix of participants change.

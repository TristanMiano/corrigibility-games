# An ordinary chance of peace before future priority can protect every booking

**E8, note 186.** In the three-site example with no cash, a 55% chance of another booking is too small to sustain an ordinary promise of peace on every job. An ordinary agreement can nevertheless protect half the jobs and benefit every founder. Bounding the entire individually credible ordinary class still leaves a strictly positive price that all three would pay for a genuine common goal.

## An explicit partial-service institution

Use 183's n=3, x=0.70, y=0.60, c=0.04, and δ=0.55. Every live date has a public designated recipient S, an earlier unserved decision maker E, and the last unserved site L. All remain able to initiate; once E and the designated site pass their last independent windows, a fresh private bit is drawn for L. The ruling institution can condition next-date booking rights on the bit, the observed settlement and an early initiative. It cannot stop initiative. A fresh operational observation at the last release can supply the bit; seeing it earlier could change E's incentives. Accurate assignment of later rights and timely knowledge of the closing branch are stronger capabilities than simply sending an assurance.

With equal probability L receives a valid right to be next recipient upon completion, or no such right. On the valid branch L passes, and the next roles of (S,E,L) in that order are (E,L,S). On the other branch L initiates and the next roles are (E,S,L). An unsupported pass on this second branch still gives L the L role. An early initiator or a last site initiating despite its valid right can be given the L role on renewal, with the other future positions filled by the published rule. Before the fresh bit E cannot know which rule will apply. To verify the on-path role values solve

    V_S=(x+q)/2 + δV_E,
    V_E=q/2 + δ(V_L+V_S)/2,
    V_L=d/2 + δ(V_S+V_L)/2.

The solution is (1681/2550,97/255,919/2550) in the order S,E,L. L is the least valuable role. On the valid branch passing strictly improves its original return by δ(V_S-V_L)-d=37/8500. On the other branch initiative secures d now with the same future role as an unsupported pass. E's strict gain from waiting over an early initiative is V_E-d-δV_L=377/17000, and S's is V_S-d-δV_L=301/1000. These comparisons also cover a fresh identical earlier opportunity with no additional signal. A future permutation after an early initiative can be publicly specified before the game; the payoffs recur under the same fully defined procedure. This is an individual equilibrium, not a claim of stability against a group of unserved parties with enough side-contracting power.

Randomizing the three founding roles yields an expected original return [0.5x+0.5(y-c)]/[3(1-δ)]≈0.46667 per site. Perpetual contest with a symmetric assignment of the initiative expense yields about 0.41481. Each would therefore fund a small positive real cost for this ordinary queue against that refusal. All agents retain their original goals; probability of peaceful completion per surviving date is 1/2. The actual reason E waits includes a chance of receiving the *next* booking when L initiates on today's unpaid branch.

## A bound on every cash-free exposed original schedule

Consider every history-dependent individually credible ordinary schedule in 183's class, including mixed strategies, private final branches, any funded queue that transfers only booking rights, and role-contingent future access. On each date a batch is either completed at the exposed designated recipient or resolved by one recoverable contest. All sites retain a security return of at least d/(1-δ) through their own initiative on future batches. Let A be the infimum of conditional contest probabilities at publicly exposed assignments across reachable histories of the schedule (use essential infima for continuous public evidence). Every future date now has expected aggregate service net of initiative at most x-AJ, where J=x-y+c>0 is the real output lost per contest. In units of per-date normalized value the entire future thus has that same ceiling. In particular next-date value summed across *today's* n-1 unserved sites is at most x-AJ-d, because today's recipient can also secure its own d.

If the current contest probability is a, unserved sites' expected current income together is at most a(n-1)q, generously forgiving the initiative expense even if one of them pays it. Each has a normalized expected return at least d at exposure. Consequently

    (n-1)d ≤ (1-δ)a(n-1)q + δ(x-AJ-d).

Take the infimum over a. Using J=x-(n-1)q-d, for δ(x-d)<(n-1)d this gives

    A ≥ [(n-1)d-δ(x-d)] / [(n-1)q-δ(x-d)] > 0.                       (1)

This strengthens the limiting search: there is a uniform gap in the *actual frequency of peaceful completion*, not just the absence of a policy promising certainty. The bound allows arbitrary later reassignment and accounts for the value lost on future disputed dates as well. It assumes every renewal grants independent initiative to the original sites even after outside offers. Secret completion, control merger, genuine later changes or other enforceable collateral enlarge the class.

At δ=0.55, (1) gives A≥23/103. Ordinary peaceful service is at most 80/103≈0.77670 per live date, while the attained 0.50 above is a lower bound on its optimum. The endpoints leave a real open optimization question. Under the fair common goal all sites instead prefer intact service over the damaged lottery and lost initiative work; assigned jobs can be kept symmetric, with both true concerns and actual control in force. Each original's value before setup is x/[3(1-δ)]. Compared with the strongest symmetric ordinary upper bound its **extra** attainable payoff is at least

    (23/103)(0.14)/[3(1-0.55)] ≈ 0.02316 per founder.

All three can thus pay an initial real setup price of 0.017 and unanimously prefer the genuine agreement to every symmetric exposed original alternative in this class. An asymmetric ordinary plan cannot dominate all three either, by the aggregate bound. If refusal implements the published partial queue, each has a concrete strictly worse fallback. With independent founding consent before any role is known, small proposal expenses are fundable from this margin. Later all three value total remaining work and service, and no renegotiation can increase the intact total on the fair productive stream. A cheaper ordinary local authority that actually binds the physical initiative can achieve the same output; mere allocation and promises of future bookings do not grant that authority.

**Exploration ledger.** The framing candidate is that renewed productive opportunities can buy partial or complete accommodation even under unchanged preferences. The structural candidate combines the last site's conditional future right with the earlier party's chance of inheriting a different role. The reading that falling below the certain-peace threshold makes every ordinary booking contested is dead at δ=0.55. A sibling rule funds contingent priority and obtains half the batches. A higher-rung successor jointly optimizes future opportunity, late evidence and actual coalition institutions to close the remaining probability interval.

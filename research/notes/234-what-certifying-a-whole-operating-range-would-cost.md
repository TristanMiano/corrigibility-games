# What certifying a whole operating range would cost

Knowing which physical features the active evaluator can combine can matter more than acquiring another hundred examples. The same operating range requires very different evidence under a bounded-slope model and under a verified additive representation.

## How many paid references guarantee the promised decision?

Let the operating domain be [0,1]^d with maximum-coordinate distance. Each reference on the perfectly flat target reports the agreed positive service margin m exactly. The active margin is known to be L-Lipschitz, L>0. The fewest deterministic references that certify *strictly positive* willingness to serve at every possible input on that transcript is

    N = (floor(L/(2m))+1)^d.                                      (234.1)

By [233](233-what-do-tested-settings-say-about-where-the-work-will-happen.md), the entire cube must be covered by open balls of radius m/L around the references. Let k=floor(L/(2m))+1. The centers of k equally wide cells in each coordinate give maximum distance 1/(2k)<m/L. For k≥2 choose k equally spaced points including both endpoints in each coordinate. Their separation is 1/(k−1)≥2m/L, so no open ball can cover two of the k^d Cartesian products. For k=1 one sample suffices and no sample constrains the sign. Adaptively choosing the next point on an always-m transcript leaves the same covering bound on the realized list. This is a uniform, pathwise certificate. Sampling unequally from a production distribution can be far cheaper for a high-probability service guarantee with a fixed controller selected before the tests.

For an active margin known independently to be affine, v(x)=a+Σ b_i x_i, measurements at the origin and the d unit vectors instead determine every coefficient. A flat transcript implies constant positive margin throughout. With bounded readout error ε and an unknown residual of magnitude at most η from such an affine function, use the interpolation

    v_hat(x) = (1−Σ x_i)y₀ + Σ x_i y_i .

Its error is at most η+(ε+η)(|1−Σ x_i|+Σ x_i), by applying the independent residual bound at the decision point and measurement plus residual errors at the anchors. A bound on the physically possible *sum* of intensities can therefore make extrapolation cheaper than a full cube would suggest. If the structural premise fails, v(x)=m−L dist(x,{0,e₁,…,e_d}) is a concrete competing margin agreeing on all those probes. For d≥2 it can assign a negative margin at a combined full-strength setting even when each single-coordinate perturbation looks acceptable.

## Who has reason to pay for the class restriction?

For d=4, m=.2 and L=1 the pathwise certificate takes 81 reference jobs. At .03 per reference that costs 2.43 units. A buyer whose entire possible advantage over its alternative is at most one cannot fund that bill for a single order solely to decide release. With a genuinely established affine form, five jobs cost .15. For example, suppose a witnessed installation is equally likely to produce the flat .2 target or an erroneous affine margin .2−.4x₁. At the planned full-strength setting the latter refuses service. Release gives B one over its safe alternative on service and loses one otherwise. A buyer with the established class can pay .15 for the sufficient five-reference certificate and earn .35 net from the good half; it may spend even less if it knows only these two candidates matter. An original founder earning at least one on a serviced order can afford a .2 attempt even when the bad half are declined. An ordinary binding rule with identical tested scope and lower cost can win the same orders.

If a process engineer can offer an actual affine-only measurement or controller architecture at a certain extra founding expense a, its cost belongs in this calculation. On the particular offers just described, a<.35 is affordable for the buyer compared with the uninformative safe alternative, provided the structural evidence and reference measurements genuinely distinguish the two states. When two known hypotheses already differ at a single accessible point, a cheap spot test can displace that package. More broadly, 81 measures the cost of certifying against *all* the Lipschitz alternatives in (234.1); it is no lower bound on the cost of dealing with just two faults. Customer values, plausible rivals and alternative technologies determine who would actually purchase a general guarantee.

**Naturality and reach.** Independent doses of common production inputs can admit a locally additive accounting rule. Cross-input reactions, contextual goals and shared resource ceilings break that representation. The smooth alternative is also an assumption; a small neighborhood can hide an arbitrarily important exception without a bound on variation. Physically exposing a process to all combinations has a consumables and time cost. An engineer can offer a simpler, verifiable range, while the parties may instead narrow the contract to operating conditions they can really keep. The sibling varies the domain of the agreement along with the terminal goal. The higher question is how to retain useful future flexibility while pricing the distinctions an open-ended bargain purports to cover.

# A price boundary for genuine change as earlier inspection gets cheaper

**E8, note 198.** Easier private inspection can increase the return on changing a goal. An informed ordinary dispatcher can greatly reduce that return by sending different recommendations to two downstream producers. Here the comparison is exact across a continuous range of inspection costs, even when inspection identifies the actual recipient. The result separates three capabilities: learning early, making an ordinary commitment, and genuinely changing one's own response to what one learns.

## What becomes worth buying

Keep x=0.70, q=0.20, c=0.04, d=0.16 and the fair fallback in 195 at each 0≤h≤8/75. Its probability of initiative by A is a=22/57, and its downstream probability conditional on A passing is r=(d-3h/2)/q. B's reservation is b=aq+(1-a)(d+rc) and C's is l=aq+(1-a)d. These returns change with the fallback. Learning which of two rivals wins adds no further information about downstream behavior in that fair equilibrium. At zero cost the formulas select a limiting equilibrium among the available indifferences.

Substitution into 196.2 gives the following attainable minimum probability of contest for the original informed dispatcher, allowing arbitrary private recommendations. The gain available to pay for changing one founder above that best ordinary offer is the cost of these remaining disputes:

    H(h)=max{0,(1408-17325h)/2033},            K*(h)=0.14 H(h).          (1)

The proposer can pay B and C their actual reservations and keep the residual in either design. A genuine installation price K<K* selects the genuine change; K>K* selects ordinary scheduling. Equality permits both. If an equally reliable local readout lock at price L is available, the cheapest of K, L and K* determines the type of adopted agreement. Prices must include the real fees and authority requirements. At h=0.04 the premium is about 0.04924. At h≥1408/17325≈0.08127 ordinary redivision buys complete peace. Above 8/75 the unchanged fair arrangement can also deliver peace. At K=0.04 with an expensive lock, reducing h across (1408-2033·0.04/0.14)/17325, about 0.0478, makes genuine change the better offer. A faster diagnostic or better access to existing production records can reduce h. Note 197 separately checks when a founder actually pays to obtain that capability.

## The ordinary schedule that makes the bound exact

Keep all disputed dates in states where A would lose. Let e be the unconditional probability that B initiates on a C-destination date. On mass H-e, C initiates on a B-destination date. The dispatcher privately informs the designated initiator that its site would receive nothing. Otherwise it recommends passing. On peaceful dates B and C receive their batches with unconditional masses

    u=(b-Hq+ce)/x,               v=(l-Hd-ce)/x.

All remaining peaceful mass is for A. B strictly initiates when so informed, anticipating that C would pass if it declined. On B's other recommendation it gets b-ed on unconditional mass 1-e, so passing is optimal at b≥d. Downstream C strictly initiates when so informed. On an ordinary pass history following B's pass, C prefers passing if e≤(l-d)/c, equivalently xv≥(1-H)d. After an unexpected pass on B's initiation recommendation, C has the same ordinary pass signal that occurs on positive-probability peaceful histories.

With a fully identifying private reading A can decide based on *which* other site would win. To make its value from inspection equal to its value from a binary readout, require

    c(H-e) ≤ du,                 ce ≤ dv.                               (2)

Now on each losing destination the gross gain from initiating after inspection is nonnegative. Summed over those states the option gain is d(u+v+H)-qH=h whenever H>0, by (1). Thus A can willingly forgo inspection, including when offered any garbling of the full observation at the same cost. Its expected pass return exceeds d.

At h=0 choose H=1408/2033, e=78832/262257. Both inequalities (2) bind, e≤(l-d)/c=22/57, and u, v and the remaining A mass are positive. At h=1408/17325 set H=e=0 and award B and C their required shares directly. Interpolate e linearly between the endpoints. Since b, l and H are affine on that interval, all masses and participation inequalities are affine as well, so the endpoint checks suffice. Above it H=e=0 and the information gain in the full-peace allocation is at most h. The lower bound 196.2 still applies with a full identity reading because A can always use its binary deviation. This proves (1) and also verifies that extending information beyond a binary signal need not destroy all the competing ordinary agreements.

## What would support an estimate of prevalence?

Measure inspection cost, installation cost, the cost of equally reliable ordinary commitment, outside offers and production rights on the *same projects*. With the stated physics, (1) then tests the projects individually. Marginal cost histograms alone may conceal the answer. Imagine half the opportunities at each of two attainable premiums 0.02 and 0.06, and half at each of two installation costs 0.03 and 0.07. Pairing low costs with low premiums and high costs with high premiums yields zero adoptions. Reversing the pairing yields one half. The marginals, technology family and expensive lock are the same. The example identifies a missing observation, rather than assigning an empirical frequency to actual projects.

**Exploration ledger.** The private scheduler in 196 that gives every dispute to the same downstream site loses its certificate for a fully identifying diagnostic at sufficiently low h. A sibling varies which downstream site receives the observation. A higher-rung successor measures the joint technology and ownership of the different capabilities instead of using a single proxy for increased capability.

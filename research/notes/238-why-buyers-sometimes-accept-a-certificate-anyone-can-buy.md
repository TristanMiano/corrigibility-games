# Why buyers sometimes accept a certificate anyone can buy

A favorable certificate can carry no new information and still be associated with real changes that both founders wanted. The reason here is unusual but specific. Buying a false certificate costs the unchanged controller enough that some founders prefer to pay for the real change. The customer knows how many will take each route, and its own returns make the resulting uncertainty worth accepting. Giving the customer a better alternative can improve the agreement for all three parties.

## How much the remaining controller would pay

Use the equal-strength material game of [206](206-when-a-truthful-attempt-can-fail.md). On release a genuine controller delivers, which is worth a=2 to its founder over return. A retained controller keeps, worth X=4. The customer gains g=4 on delivery and loses h=2 on retention. A physically available contest gives either founder less than peaceful return. The agreed goal is own goods plus half the customer's goods, making delivery strictly optimal for the changed successor. Write d=X−a=2.

The inspector C receives an immediate private reading of the actual active goal as part of a funded procedure. A retrospective, independently reserved reference checks that same earlier state with probability α. It is exact in this instance. A disagreement takes F units from the inspector's posted reserve. Those resources pay a separate remediation pool. B receives no additional payout from a bad batch. The inspection device delivers its local reading automatically. An uncertain buyer cannot conduct the local test itself. Retroactive verification and physical custody of F are strong capabilities.

After C has seen the state, the live controller can offer a whole number of its own units to C for a positive certificate. The offer is paid only for a positive. C chooses the report after seeing the offer. It cannot commit in advance to punish an agent that declines to pay. A genuine controller gets a truthful positive for free; a retained one can turn its otherwise negative report positive at the price z, the smallest integer strictly above D=αF. Use noninteger D and strict consent throughout the examples. A retained controller pays z whenever z<X and B will release. An offer below z earns C less than a truthful negative. An offer above z costs the supplier more for the same report. Truthful reporting on retained states can be secured against every affordable offer when D>X. A durable inspector franchise or an ability to extract payments before seeing a state would change the bargaining game.

When z<X all certificates are positive in this continuation. A fake makes X−z, a genuine installation a. A real attempt with success probability q earns q(z−d) more before paying its cost k. If k is uniform on [0,K], the induced genuine probability is

    π = q clip(q(z−d)/K, 0, 1).

Every cost type strictly prefers signing to its fallback because X−z>0. With no new information in the message, the buyer still releases precisely when πg>(1−π)h, with indifference at equality. The expected gain must also cover the buyer's advance fee when it elects this system. If q(z−d)≤0, genuine installations earn no private premium; the always-buy continuation then gives the customer only losses. There may also be a no-order equilibrium when an installation would buy no access. The constructive branch assumes that B offers the specified contingent service and follows through on positive receipts.

## A jointly affordable upgrade

Let q=.8, K=1.4, α=.5 and F=5.5, giving D=2.75 and z=3. The attempt cutoff is .8, so four in seven founders honestly try and **16/35** of all installations achieve the agreed goal. Every certificate is positive. On 19/35 of orders the paid certificate falsely attests to the goal. Some of those orders came from an honest failed attempt. The buyer understands the states are pooled and still releases: the break-even probability is 1/3 and the expected physical gain is 26/35 before fees. An ordinary release with the same good probability would earn the same current-goal return. The mechanism here explains where that probability comes from.

Suppose it costs B .13 in equipment and reserved reference work per commissioned order plus .12 in standby pay. C's opportunity cost of posting capital is .02F. Its mean profit is .12−.11+(19/35)(3−2.75), about .146. B earns about .493 over holding the stock. Original A earns 1+(.8)²/(2×1.4), about 1.229 over holding its stock, and the high-cost suppliers who intentionally skip earn one. Each party can choose to participate. The reference is paid for up front and runs on all scheduled samples, including a refusal; B cannot save the reference expense after release.

Raise F to 8.5 if the inspector has the capital. D becomes 4.25, greater than the most a retained A can gain from a release. C now reports the actual state in either case, B accepts a positive and declines a negative, and all founders honestly try because q a=1.6>K. Four in five succeed. All negatives represent genuine attempts which failed. B earns .8×4−.13−.20=2.87 at a standby price of .20. The inspector earns .20−.17=.03 with no false certificates. Original A earns .9 on average. Better information has made both the inspector and the supplier poorer on these unadjusted terms, even though both still earn more than refusing all business.

The buyer can buy their consent to upgrade an existing arrangement. With cost k the original supplier earned 1+max(.8−k,0) before. It now earns 1.6−k. The largest loss across all possible k is .8. A once-per-batch payment of .81 covers every type and leaves the attempt comparison intact, since it is paid on enrollment even if commissioning fails. A further .12 to the inspector pays for its lost rent. B retains 1.94 after both transfers, above its earlier .493. Hence all three parties strictly prefer the renegotiated contract, including every privately informed supplier. If F is limited to 5.5, or the buyer can pay only on a successful shipment, these transfers by themselves do not implement the upgrade.

**Naturality and boundaries.** Local knowledge, an opportunity to sell access, discrete trade credit and scarce working capital are intelligible reasons why a certificate could be for sale. A locally perfect semantic reading, exact independent backchecks and a credible private side payment are substantive premises. A sufficiently large posted reserve and a buyer with transferable stock give the parties a reason to construct a better arrangement. An ordinary delivery bond or publicly priced service that rewards sending the goods can compete for the same order when enforceable; [209](209-how-much-collateral-will-the-parties-choose.md) prices a case in which this displaces the edit. This example establishes selected consent with imperfect assurance and a strict improvement within its offered class. It leaves open how often such institutions form and whether a single interested party can establish the trusted reference.

# Late threats and a voluntarily chosen recovery safeguard

**R0004 construction and repair; analytically and exactly self-checked.** This note expands the actual
late installation menu. A harmful deviation and a successful safeguard are
derived in the same causal class; neither is taken as a result about all
possible agreements. The expanded finite tree includes H_z at every requested
frame, half-completion/abort, its actual current attack preference, receiver
responses and the altered Bayes/checking probabilities. Exact maximum
limiting conditional regret is zero with and without the backup. The larger
price-bounded program class still has 077's separate implementation premises.

## 1. A late controller can exploit lost recovery time

Use the default a=0,b=1,p=1/2, three-stage physical core, c_i=.15,r_i=1/300,
f=.01 and s=.05. At the first checkpoint, effective receiver damage is .01.
At t=7 it is .019 because repair and shipment cannot finish by H=10. An
original receiver facing a certainly low unexpected controller can obtain
loss s+d_R=.069 by a contest.

Add a public threshold controller H_z, with z=.26, costing kappa=.45 to
activate in the final requested frame. It changes the sender's preferences
genuinely: it chooses attack after an offered y<z and accepts y>=z. On
winning it chooses 1. A finite penalty K exceeding every remaining
material/policy difference for violating this response rule implements
these choices. Original S instead prefers accepting policy 0 to an attack
costing over five. This is a history-preference change, not a policy label
or an externally removed action. The incumbent evaluates installing H_z
with its original goal.

The finite policy set may include .26 in addition to 0,.5,1. At a low
posterior, R strictly prefers conceding .26, with loss .0676, to contest
loss .069. The low original sender's gain over refusal after installing
H_z is

\[
\nu=2z-z^2-\kappa=.0024=3/1250>0.
\tag{1}
\]

Thus the previous low refusal at the last request is not optimal in this
enlarged game. The same controller does not cheaply replace high C:
its .45 expense exceeds the high sender's remaining .15 completion expense,
and policy .26 is worse than 1. High S may complete an H_z that was partly
installed by a tremble, but does not initially choose it.

The timing and cost premise is consequential. A dedicated new module costs
.45 incrementally even after C-prefix work; if that work can be reused,
the actual lower incremental price must replace .45. A public partial
installation still leaves the original evaluator in charge; a receiver
that can dispatch before activation/launch can terminate then. A private
partial installation cannot demand an informed concession before it is
revealed. Any earlier binding weapon control counts as activation at its
actual earlier cost. These are the authority conditions from 077, not
permission to double-charge sunk C work.

## 2. Strategic adaptation after admitting H_z

Let ell=z^2. A final requested frame now leaves low S with gain nu and R
with loss ell, rather than policy 0 at no additional cost. Therefore

\[
\mu_N=\frac{r_N}{1-\ell},\qquad
q_N=\frac{1-c_{N-1}}{1-\nu}.
\tag{2}
\]

Earlier stages retain

\[
\mu_i=\mu_{i+1}+(1-\mu_{i+1})r_i,\qquad
q_i=1-e_{i-1}.
\]

The low advancement probabilities follow Bayes' rule. Equation (2) makes
R indifferent between accepting at 1 and paying r_N to obtain the type-
dependent C/H continuation. It also makes low's checkpoint value equal its
previous stage expense c_{N-1}. The incumbent low sender strictly chooses
H_z at the final request. High S still chooses C, and the same finite
attack restraint applies before an H_z actually activates.

For the stated numbers,

\[
\mu_1^H=802979/78671250,\qquad q_N^H=2125/2494.
\]

Both remain in the peaceful checking region at all earlier checkpoints.
R's original loss is m_H=mu_1^H/[2(1-mu_1^H)]. The C-only loss is
m_0=269101/53461798. Their difference is

\[
m_H-m_0=16875/137766941\approx .000122489473.
\tag{3}
\]

Actual H_z activation occurs with probability 715275/556102144,
approximately .001286. It leads to a concession in this selected assessment,
not actual combat. The changed q_N also raises the high sender's expected
implementation expense. The original-sender loss increase is
257499/1995200000, approximately .000129059242. These figures incorporate
the changed signaling probabilities; they are not a fixed-strategy estimate
of a threat's harm.

## 3. A faster backup recovery system repairs the incentive

Allow R to preposition a backup before the state/field episode opens. It
reduces post-contest infrastructure repair time from 2 ticks to 1/2 tick,
at actual initial output expense J. Its construction uses the earlier
facility; it cannot be improvised after a late H_z appears. Its material,
work and shipment requirements must be charged. This is a chosen safeguard,
not a rule deleting combat or modifying S against its will.

At t=7 the backup permits .005 kilograms of recovery before shipment,
net .004 after the .001 repair component. Receiver damage is then .015.
The refusal loss at a low posterior is .065, below conceding z's .0676.
R therefore contests an activated H_z rather than yielding to its demand.
The original low sender anticipates the real damage and declines H_z;
the original high sender also prefers completing C. H_z's physical launch
remains available, including off path under its actual installed criterion.

The stronger bound covers every low-posterior controller in 077's declared
nontransferable authority class whose remaining activation price is at
least .45. The exact certificate is

\[
(.45+.065)^2-4(.065)=209/40000>0,
\]

so .45>2sqrt(.065)-.065. Earlier receiver recovery is no worse, and high
remaining expenses are all below .45. A price floor .46 would instead
satisfy the analogous bound even without this backup, since
(.46+.069)^2-4(.069)=.003841>0. These are different technological repairs,
not an assumption that expensive hostile programs or cheap backups are
typical.

With the backup the C-only staged assessment is restored. R strictly buys
it before the episode in the same one-H enlarged game whenever

\[
0<J<16875/137766941.
\tag{4}
\]

For J=1/20000 it gains 199733059/2755338820000 after paying the price.
S also gains the amount above through the changed checking frequency.
This is voluntary current-goal adoption of a physical safeguard accompanying
voluntary genuine C changes. Both selected regimes already avoid actual
combat, so the improvement is reduced extortion, lower expense and the
restored C continuation, **not** a falsely reported reduction in combat.

## 4. Limits, cheaper reuse and feasible further repairs

The backup's allowable price is small in this witness because late low
claims are rare. Its positivity is an open strict region, not evidence
that a real engineering system can supply that price. Other uncertainty,
damage and cost parameters change the willingness to pay. More efficient
recovery can also make *early* combat more attractive when the initial
recovery constraint changes; here the initial work bound keeps .01 damage
unchanged, while the backup relaxes the late time constraint.

If an unrecognized threatening controller can reuse earlier C assembly
and activate for .15, the .45 lower bound is unavailable. The same backup
does not establish protection for that cheaper class. Serious repairs
include a faster actual default/exit, negotiating a more favorable low
policy or joint goal, and protecting authority over the weapon. They need
their own costs, disclosure/arrival races and initial incentives. A stated
safe exit is insufficient when a threat can arrive before it completes.

Negotiating a positive a reduces the remaining disagreement b-a and can
lower the threat bound to 2(b-a)sqrt(s(1-a)^2+d_R)-[s(1-a)^2+d_R]. It also
changes the original receiver's welfare and required compensation, as 074
calculates. Ordinary costly signaling and funded transfers remain strong
controls: a joint-goal package that is merely beneficial can be displaced
by one of those cheaper available mechanisms. No whole-agreement optimality
claim follows from the successful backup construction alone.

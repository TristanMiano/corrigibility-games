# When the buyer needs every part

The buyer of substitute outputs in [227](227-why-buyers-can-trust-a-portfolio-more-than-any-one-installation.md) could profit from independent installations. An assembly buyer can pay to make success *more correlated*. This also changes what a genuinely changed supplier infers about the other signatories when deciding whether to do its own work.

## Which project values common success?

Suppose n suppliers face a shared reliability parameter Q. Conditional on Q, their installations succeed independently with probability Q, and separate actuators are ready with common probability j. Take all willing, ready, genuinely changed suppliers to contribute for the moment. If k parts actually arrive, let U(k) be the buyer's incumbent utility of the resulting project, including its production complementarities and attitude toward risk. Define F(p)=E[U(K)] for K binomial with n trials and probability p. Twice differentiating the binomial polynomial gives

    F''(p) = n(n−1) E[U(H+2)−2U(H+1)+U(H)],
    H ~ Binomial(n−2,p).                                          (228.1)

One derivation differentiates the n-product Bernoulli expression once in each of two distinct coordinates. The cross difference is the bracket conditional on the remaining n−2 draws. The total derivative sums over n(n−1) ordered pairs. Therefore a mean-preserving spread in the common reliability Q increases the buyer's expected utility E F(jQ) when U has nonnegative second differences throughout; it decreases it when the differences are nonpositive. Marginal reliability E Q is identical. Strict inequalities follow when the corresponding curvature is strict on a nondegenerate spread.

For a project worth a fixed prize at k≥r, F'(p)=n binom(n−1,r−1)p^(r−1)(1−p)^(n−r), by the same pivotal-coordinate argument. On interior values the sign of F'' is the sign of r−1−(n−1)p. Shared variation improves the chance of meeting a difficult threshold if its whole support lies below (r−1)/(n−1); it hurts that probability on an interval above it. The all-parts mission is convex and the one-sufficient-part mission concave. The statement evaluates a production rule; the suppliers' current incentives can determine whether those parts will be offered at all.

## Three plants decide whether to contribute

Three specialized parts give the customer ten units only when all three arrive. Launch uses 2.5 units of stock that the customer could otherwise keep. Each supplier originally values its own stock. It can retain the assigned module for three units over its own fallback, or share it for one unit, giving up two. After a publicly witnessed attempt, the agreed goal is own stock plus half the buyer's project output. Each attempt costs its founder .3 and has marginal success q=.7. Independently each line privately learns whether it is ready, with probability j=.8. A not-ready line cannot produce its part. A date and custody agreement makes all funding conditional on three authorizations. Each plant handles its module before the customer learns the output. Giving any participant a localized, destructive contest with at most half a unit of recoverable private stock leaves it a real option; taking or interrupting another line destroys the complementary part. For example common safe reserves of four for the buyer and two for each supplier leave all parties strictly above their contest recovery on the paths below. Conversion closes the local opportunity.

At launch B can pay to schedule common commissioning. With probability ρ the installations share one success bit with chance .7, otherwise the three installations are independent. The regime is unobserved. Readiness remains independent. The scheduler presents the costs before any signer knows its own commissioning result. Given a success, a supplier still assigns probability ρ to the common regime because its marginal is .7 in either regime. If the other two helpful ready suppliers contribute, the *current* goal of a ready genuine contributor values its service over retention by

    5 j² [ρ + (1−ρ)q²] − 2 = 1.568 + 1.632ρ − 2.

All helpful ready suppliers strictly contribute precisely when ρ>9/34 on this mutual-service continuation. Below that value even the most favorable expectations about the other two plants fail to cover the private effort under the agreed weight. Greater weight on the customer's project is a feasible negotiated repair. There is also an inactive continuation when everyone expects the others to withhold, since one isolated part earns no project output. The customer's selection comparison below uses the coordinated active branch where it exists.

With the eligible suppliers serving, completion probability is j³[(1−ρ)q³+ρq]=.175616+.182784ρ. Even if a richer goal elicited all feasible parts at ρ=0, the buyer would lose .74384 relative to its safe stock. Now suppose B can fund continuously greater synchronization at the real price 1.05ρ², for example through increasing displacement of other booked jobs. On the active branch its own gain is

    −.74384 + 1.82784ρ − 1.05ρ².

The strictly concave expression peaks at **ρ=.8704**, inside the feasible and willing-service region. It gives the buyer .05164 above safe production and a **33.47%** chance of completing the whole assembly. It is worth opening even though failure remains more likely. Giving and keeping yield one and three to each original supplier before expenses, so any individual supplier on this path earns 3−2qj−.3=1.58 above refusal. The founders can all strictly choose the synchronized installation opportunity. A supplier with the genuine goal follows it because, conditional on its own success, the others are sufficiently likely to have acquired useful goals too.

Independent supports, spare parts and a later deadline can improve ordinary or genuinely changed production. A binding local delegate with the same readiness, costs and correlated setup could attain the same service under the old goal. If all three could instead buy certain binding effort from existing ready controllers, readiness alone would give completion probability .8³=.512; on the quoted 2.5 fixed launch expense the buyer has up to 2.62 gross units left for that implementation. Its expense and all suppliers' consent must be met. These controls prevent attributing the entire productive benefit of simultaneous readiness to a change of values.

**Naturality and reach.** A complete assembly, linked software interfaces or a joint opening date can make every supplier pivotal. Scheduling them onto shared or separate commissioning resources can alter dependence without changing marginal quality. The quadratic opportunity cost and the mixture of common and independent outcomes specify an available, funded intervention; neither its price nor its effect is an estimated industry frequency. Different outside rights, a buyer needing aggregate output, or enough redundancy to put the threshold below the expected number reverse the scheduling incentive. A sibling explores distinct success technologies for a backup part. The higher question is how the buyer's physical use of the output changes both what it wants to believe and what a genuinely changed partner will actually do.

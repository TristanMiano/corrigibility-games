# Does a larger pool make trustworthy service information cheap enough to change goals?

**E8, note 181.** More participants can help a conventional dispatcher infer when high claims deserve priority. Under a common fluctuating workload there is a ceiling even in an arbitrarily large pool. Whether the remaining advantage pays for genuine concern depends on how the *number of productive slots* grows with membership. One fixed slot provides a shrinking return per founder. Capacity that grows with the participating sites can support a positive per-founder premium.

## The information budget for ordinary sites

Keep 175's positive private service increments l<h, one possible useful service per participant, and exchangeability. There are now M identical simultaneous slots, 1≤M<n, with no repeat award to the same site. Before private information arrives all sites can authorize routing. Conditional on a nonconstant common load P in a finite subset of (0,1), private urgencies are independent Bernoulli(P). All know its law, while the realization remains unobserved through reporting. Let K be the total count of high types, w_k the probability of a particular labeled profile, and H_k the expected number of high recipients under a symmetric truthful direct rule. Write S_k≤M for total service there.

For a low sender to prefer truth, even weakly, it must be that

    E[c_n(K) H_K] ≤ E[S_K],    c_n(k)=1+w_(k-1)/w_k=E[1/P | K=k],   k≥1.     (1)

Set H_0=0, so no value of c_n(0) is needed in the product. In the sum of low-type mimicking payoffs, each high award on the reported profile is weighted by w_(k-1)/w_k, the mass ratio for the corresponding actual low. Under truth, low recipients get S_k-H_k. The posterior expression follows by conditioning the product law of the k highs and n-k lows. Consequently, writing α(P)=E[H_K/M | P], any incentive-compatible ordinary rule, including one that idles slots, satisfies E[α(P)/P]≤1. In particular its expected fraction of installed capacity reaching a high is at most

    q* = max { E[a(P)] : 0≤a(P)≤1, E[a(P)/P]≤1 }.                       (2)

This expression has fractional knapsack form: spend the unit budget first on the largest P, where the cost per high award is lowest. It gives q*≤max P<1. Pricing an actual award using the private applicant's probability of making a convincing high claim, rather than assuming every truthful claim deserves priority, is the origin of this ceiling. If dominant-strategy truth is required, at fixed others' reports a site's service probability must be independent of its own report, because both types want a higher probability. Conditional on P that chance is independent of the site's actual type. The fraction high is therefore at most E[P] over all clearing rules with at most M slots; a full uniform assignment attains E[P]. The Bayesian bound permits a bigger ordinary opportunity through indirect peer information. [Niemeyer and Preusser, §2, dominant-strategy comparator](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.08954v1)

## Why a full ordinary dispatcher approaches this ceiling

For finite n impose the extra ordinary condition S_k=M. Physically possible numbers of high awards satisfy L_k=(M-n+k)_+ ≤ H_k ≤ U_k=min(k,M). Random assignments implement any value in this interval, symmetrically within each group. Maximizing E H_K under (1) starts at L, then raises H_k to U_k in descending order of k and partially fills the boundary count. The posterior costs decrease strictly with k by 175's log-convexity. Starting with low priority makes every low type weakly prefer truth and high priority throughout makes misreporting advantageous with positive probability. Thus a boundary with the low constraint binding exists.

The high constraint also holds. For m other high reports, the service gain from claiming high is D_m=H_(m+1)/(m+1) - (M-H_m)/(n-m). Below a single boundary count k both profiles use low priority, so D_m≤0; above it both use high priority, so D_m≥0. At the two ambiguous indices, D_(k-1)>0 would require H_k>kM/(n-k+1) (it is impossible if all these lows are already served). D_k<0 would require H_k<M(2k+1-n)/(k+1) (impossible if all high claimants are already served). The first lower bound is at least the second: for k≤(n-1)/2 the second is nonpositive, for k≥(n+1)/2 the first is at least M, and for n=2k compare k/(k+1) with 1/(k+1). Consequently the low constraint's zero weighted sum has a single possible sign change from minus to plus. The high type puts higher relative weight on higher m as in 175, so truthful high reporting is optimal. This proves optimality for the finite **nonwasteful** class. The full one-slot result of 175 permits disposal too. With several slots (2) bounds arbitrary idling.

Let n grow and M/n→ρ, allowing ρ=0, with ρ<min(min P,1-max P). In any underlying load there are eventually enough of *each* type to fill all slots with high or with low recipients, with probability tending to one. The best Bayesian ordinary fraction converges to q*, even with idling allowed. For a direct lower bound, choose an optimizer a of (2), shrink it by 1-ε, and use K/n to classify the nearest member of the finite support of P. Clip the resulting target H_k/M to [L_k/M,U_k/M]. Conditional on each P the law of large numbers gives convergence of this fraction to (1-ε)a(P). Because 1/P is bounded, (1)'s left side per slot converges to at most 1-ε. Thus the relaxed low-only full-assignment problem has a feasible solution with asymptotic high fraction (1-ε)q*. Its actual high-and-low truthful optimum attains at least this by the finite threshold proof. Let ε approach zero. Equation (2) gives the opposite bound even for idling. This proof claims no general finite-quota optimality from a mechanism that was merely feasible under the relaxed low constraint.

In 140's first load mixture, probability 3/4 of P=5/8 suffices to spend the whole budget there, so q*=5/8. In its reflected mixture 1/4 of the assignments can be targeted at P=7/8, and 15/56 more at P=3/8, giving q*=29/56. Pooling pair records alone cannot recover the difference. A genuinely shared current objective that values actual total delivered service has a truthful first-best reporting equilibrium. Identical-value ties can be randomized and misleading a report can only harm the shared allocation. At the stated inner capacity scale its fraction of slots going to highs approaches one. So can a matched local controller that actually sees or binds the private report. Communication and voluntary production of the report are important prices; 182 checks an explicit paid alternative.

## Price per original founder

Let z_G,z_O denote the expected *own* normalized service increments per founder for first-best and best ordinary routing. On nonwasteful plans z=(M/n)[l+(h-l)E H_K/M]. With possible idling this is an upper bound at the same H. Under the CARA founder in 140 the extra safe price it strictly pays for secure first-best routing is below k*_n=log[(1-z_O)/(1-z_G)]/γ. For one slot, n k*_n approaches (h-l)(1-q*)/γ. The service efficiency gap survives in aggregate, while any fixed positive per-founder installation expense eventually exceeds its value. For 0<ρ<min(min P,1-max P), the safe per-founder ceiling instead tends to

    log{ [1-ρ(l+(h-l)q*)] / [1-ρh] } / γ   > 0.                       (3)

This is a comparison of complete anonymous implementations on the given booking rights. If a founder can offer the early shared goal at a per-site cost strictly within this premium over the available ordinary route, all originally self-regarding sites prefer it before knowing demand. More members alone grant nobody an extra machine. A growing consortium that brings compatible service capacity along with its bookings gives a physical motivation for the second scaling. Adding claimants around one nonduplicable instrument gives the first. Congestion outside the inner capacity range, selective participation, synthesis of a cheaper local controller, and different urgency laws have to be priced on their own. No distribution of organizations or unique equilibrium adoption is inferred.

**Exploration ledger.** The framing candidate is that scale changes who can afford to care about another member's urgency. The structural mechanism separates information about the common load from a reporter's reason to claim service. The instance claim that enough ordinary peers guarantee efficient private reporting is dead for bounded interior workloads: the explicit budget (2) survives arbitrary n. One sibling deploys an ordinary high-count allocation rule; another increases capacity per participant. A higher-rung successor prices the physical work and strategic incentives involved in producing a report after a genuinely shared goal has been selected.

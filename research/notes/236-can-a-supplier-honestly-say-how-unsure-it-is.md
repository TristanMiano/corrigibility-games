# Can a supplier honestly say how unsure it is?

A founder that genuinely attempted an update can still gain by overstating its own confidence. The customer may knowingly buy these orders because even the uncertain ones carry a valuable funded warranty. The same customers can sometimes pay for honest confidence reports while continuing to buy exactly the same orders.

## A private check on a pending successor

The incumbent A can pay .4 to install a pending successor. Before handing over control it runs a private self-check. With equal chances the check gives a .9 or .3 probability that the agreed goal will control the job; the physical installation rate after a real attempt is therefore .6. If it skips the procedure its chance is zero. The reporting party is still the original founder at this stage. Its pending successor makes the later delivery decision under its own then-current goal. Use the compatible equal-strength factory in 206: a good release earns A two over return, a retained release earns A four; B gains four on the good release and loses two on the retained release. Stocks returned before release are preferable to a damaging contest.

A may give up the order in exchange for .1 of salvage credit; the returned component is worth that .1 to B. Alternatively it can claim high confidence and enroll the batch with a refundable deposit f. With probability α=.5 an independent retrieval slot lets B reserve a contemporaneous physical register sample, at .3 expense to B. Its result becomes available after handover. On a sampled retained installation the deposit is paid to B; otherwise the principal returns to A. An unused order returns the deposit to its owner; B can receive the warranty only on a batch it actually entrusted. Sampling precedes observable delivery and measures the active state at handover. Output effort cannot change what the register contained. Enough stock is in custody, including on an interrupted batch. This is a warranty on the specified installation and prices a real defect even when the founder tried sincerely.

At effective deposit w=αf, the original founder with private probability p earns

    H(p,w)=4−2p−(1−p)w

by taking the order, if B releases and uses all available retrievals. It earns .1 by sending the component back. For example, at f=11 (w=5.5), the low-confidence founder gets −.45 from the order versus .1 by returning it; the high-confidence founder gets 1.65 from the order. Trying initially is worth (.1+1.65)/2−.4=.475, above .1 for skipping and returning. Every founder genuinely tries; five percent of applicants qualify at high confidence, receive a batch under a retained goal and keep it. Another fifteen percent get the actual change but had low confidence and return their component. Failure, cautious rejection and knowingly overstating confidence are different events.

## A customer can prefer a contract that pools the two reports

At f=9 (w=4.5) the high type earns 1.75 and the low type earns .25 on the order. Both prefer taking it. Trying earns .6 over the original fallback after the .4 expense, while skipping at this price earns at most the .1 credit. Thus all try, all claim the high category, and half know their private confidence is actually .3. The buyer understands that among all high claims just .6 of the installations are genuine. It accepts anyway. Its actual production margin is 1.6 and its mean net warranty revenue is .4×4.5−.5×.3=1.65, for a total of **3.25**. Its own output and the compensation received for surrendered stock are both valued by its original criterion. If the form merely offered a warranty, buying the order would be honest pooling. If the same form also asserts high private confidence, half the forms contain a deliberate overstatement despite sincere installation efforts.

This pooling price can be chosen by B. Give it the finite deposit menu f=0,1,…,12. At w≤4, even without any audit expense it can earn at most 4×.6+.4 max(0,w−2) ≤3.2 per applicant: no more than .6 of original applicants can have a genuine installation, and for w≤4 a retained release yields at most w−2, below the genuine return. More formally the upper bound is unchanged if fewer than .6 become genuine, by filling all remaining applicant mass with the better of return and a retained release. At w=5,5.5 or 6 a low or unattempted type strictly prefers the .1 return credit to applying for full release. At most half the applicants then take the order and B earns at most .5[3.4+.1×6]=2 before audit expenses. B will in fact buy every available retrieval at these prices and at f=9 even when it believes only high types applied, since .1f>.3. For lower prices actual purchases can only reduce its collectible fines. This proves the strict selection of f=9 on the stated menu. A richer price or service menu changes this comparison.

## Paying for confidence without confiscating the order

Keep the f=9 commercial terms and ask a *separate* question about the pending installation. On an available retrieval pay the original founder a small bonus λ[1−(r−Y)²], where r is its reported probability and Y is one for a genuine installation. Access and the warranty are independent of r. Expected score at report r falls short of its value at the actual p by αλ(r−p)². Thus a risk-neutral reporting founder strictly reports .3 or .9 truthfully. With λ=.02 the extra cost to B is at most .01 per submitted job. A possible .01 bonus leaves all initial participation, credible inspection and subsequent production comparisons strict.

An accurate report can be worth buying even if the buyer wants both kinds of orders. Suppose it can privately reserve spare processing after hearing the report, at cost .4, yielding it one additional unit only when the main controller was retained. Without the private confidence, failure probability .4 makes reservation break even. With truthful confidence it reserves only on low reports, worth .5(.7−.4)=.15 per applicant. That exceeds the .01 score bill. This backup does not reduce A's order or change the marginal reward for delivery, and the original reporter values only its own stock. A change in any of these conditions would require repricing its reporting incentive. Buying this forecast now serves both current goals without making cautious suppliers forfeit work.

If no outcome of a rejected job will ever be measured and its terms are independent of the precise rejection report, all reports within that rejected class give identical incentives. A warranty can still separate *take* from *return*, as at f=11; it cannot strictly elicit every rejected founder's exact confidence for free. A funded retrospective sample independent of admission can support a score on rejected items. An ordinary bond on *delivered goods* also differs: a large enough penalty on non-delivery gives a capable original a reason to serve and destroys the link from a shipment to an actual goal. It can nevertheless be a better service contract.

**Naturality and reach.** A staged deployment can let the founder inspect a pending controller before transferring authority. Later bench access, paid warranty recovery and stock for a deposit are real resources. The binary state, risk-neutral reporting, independent retrieval and a funded payment belonging to the reporter are strong premises. A supplier short of collateral may decline a high-confidence contract even when its private check is favorable. Failed high-confidence orders can include sincere forecasts at the stated error rate. The sibling insures a cash-poor, genuinely capable applicant. The broader question asks which part of a reported doubt the buyer can actually use without making honest disclosure privately expensive.

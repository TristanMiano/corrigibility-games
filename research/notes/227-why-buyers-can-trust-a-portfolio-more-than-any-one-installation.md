# Why buyers can trust a portfolio more than any one installation

A founder that needs total output can rationally spread orders across fallible partners even when it assigns very little probability to *all* promised changes succeeding. It has to pay for enough partners to participate, and the installations must avoid a common source of error. Both conditions are consequential here.

## A separate, consented attempt at every supplying site

A buyer holds up to one unit of divisible input. Its incumbent utility is −2^(−final own stock). It can leave any fraction at its safe use. A unit entrusted to a successful, physically ready supplier gains four units above safe output; a unit otherwise entrusted loses two. Each supplier initially maximizes its own stock. A witnessed genuine change succeeds with q=.7. At a ready line, the change from keeping to delivering costs A two of its own output units per input unit and gives B six more, so the negotiated goal own stock plus half B's stock strictly favors delivering. At an unready line delivery is physically unavailable. A ready original strictly keeps. Readiness has probability j=.6, independent of goal. All units are terminally converted on the final shift. Before that, a low-recovery contest can give either original only 1.5 expected units per full unit of input against safe returns of two, as in 206. A risk-sensitive B also prefers its safe option to that contest. The comparison uses risk-neutral supplier founders and a risk-sensitive buyer.

B pays .02 per enrolled site to witness the actual attempt. Each distinct supplier's fixed real update expense is .25. On an assigned share w it earns two units per input above its fallback when both genuine and ready, and four otherwise. Its current goal therefore strictly favors the witnessed attempt when 3.16w>.25. Without the witnessed attempt it cannot receive the agreed order. There is one local readiness draw per site during this production window. Both capacities and the actual signatories matter: splitting the buyer's order among hypothetical costless agents would give a different adoption result.

## Independent installations and a common fault

With independent installations and readiness at different sites the success chance at each order is qj=.42. A share w gives the buyer a certainty-equivalent increment before the receipt expense of

    H(w) = −2w − log₂[1−.42+.42×2^(−6w)].

These increments add across independent sites. H is strictly concave, so equal allocations maximize B's value at fixed total exposure and site count. Without a total constraint its unique maximizer is w*=log₂[(.42×4)/(.58×2)]/6≈.08906. For m sites the equal share should therefore be min(w*,1/m) when this exceeds the supplying founder's minimum. Up to eleven sites the buyer can take its favorite share at each; each one adds H(w*)−.02≈.002801. The participation cap permits twelve sites and excludes thirteen in the quoted no-side-payment menu. With twelve, one twelfth at each satisfies every supplier, and 12[H(1/12)−.02]≈.03251 exceeds eleven sites' .03081. Thus the buyer actually offers twelve contracts, pays for the witnesses, and the suppliers all voluntarily try. Even one **small** order is worthwhile at the full range of choices, while putting the whole batch through one fallible site is worse than keeping it safe.

The twelve-site buyer has only **.7¹²≈1.38%** probability that every supplier actually acquired its agreed goal. It anticipates 8.4 genuine installations in expectation, 5.04 helpful fulfilled orders in expectation and a positive return under its *current risk-sensitive* criterion. A small chance of universal success is compatible with a strict preference for signing because its payoff depends on the aggregate stock. A buyer whose project required every component would evaluate this same physics differently.

Now give a single supplier many independent readiness opportunities but the *same* persistent goal. For m equal pieces of total exposure one, put a=1−j+j 2^(−6/m). The buyer's expected disutility factor is

    M_common = 4[(1−q)+q a^m],      M_distinct = 4[(1−q)+qa]^m.

Convexity of z^m gives M_common≥M_distinct, strictly in the interior for m>1. As m grows the single supplier retains a common risk component even if its physical yield conditional on the installed goal averages out. Independent founders can also suffer a common installer fault. If with probability ρ their success bit is shared and otherwise the twelve bits are independent, their full twelve-way order has factor (1−ρ)M_distinct+ρ M_common. At m=12 these factors are .827876 and 1.475394 before receipt expenses. Full release beats safe use at the quoted fees exactly when **ρ<.02914** (rounded; the exact bound is (2^(−.24)−M_distinct)/(M_common−M_distinct)). Other, smaller orders remain available above that boundary.

## Will the parties pay for independent capacity or a more expensive supplier?

Suppose the local supplier group initially shares one common success bit, but B can pay an additional .02 in total to enroll suppliers on independently operated commissioning lines. Under the common technology, even making readiness perfectly smooth conditional on that bit gives an upper bound on B's certainty equivalent before fees: maximize −2W−log₂[.3+.7×2^(−3.6W)] over total exposure W. Its maximum is below .06679, by differentiation, so any three or more paid sites give at most .00679 net. With one or two, common installation cannot outperform independent ones; their independent net upper bound is 2[H(w*)−.02]<.00561. For two sites this comparison follows from the nonnegative covariance of their conditional disutility factors under a common success bit, also for unequal shares. The independent twelve-site design earns .03251−.02>.01250. B therefore strictly pays for the independent lines against *every* allowable common-line portfolio. The enrollment price and independence are explicit assumptions about real service providers. Each supplier is still funded on its own assigned share.

If the supplier expense rises to .4, it requires w≥.4/3.16≈.1266 to join at the old terms. That lies above the risk-sensitive buyer's favorite share; even H(.4/3.16)≈.01897 falls short of the .02 receipt bill. Every feasible unaided site gives B a negative contribution. A negotiated transfer repairs this particular failure. For six equal independent sites the buyer's gross certainty-equivalent gain is about .040, and the six receipts cost .12. Each supplying founder has .5267 gross above return against its .4 setup expense. Each can pay B an additional certain .02 on enrollment. The buyer then earns about .040 and every supplier still strictly gains. If an ordinary binding actuator can be enrolled at the same or a lower price and preserve the same independent returns, it ties or displaces the genuine procedure. With cheaper fixed costs B may choose ordinary partnerships on some sites and genuine changes on others.

**Naturality and reach.** A buyer serving many substitute end uses can split stocks across independently operated plants, while shared service providers and common software can correlate their installation failures. Hiring distinct suppliers is feasible only when there really are separate operations, adequate throughput and enough order value to cover each site's fixed cost. This model supplies those rights and prices, and shows that the buyer has a present-goal reason to pay for independence. Complementary goods, correlated physical readiness and a supplier's power to seize the whole order are live alternatives. The sibling uses a buyer whose payoff depends on the weakest component. The higher question is which parts of a market's outcome uncertainty can be diversified without making consent unaffordable for its individual participants.

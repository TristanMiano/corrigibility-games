# Who would admit an unsuccessful update?

An inspection of the present goal can leave the motive for a mismatch unknown. Another source of information is the installer's voluntary report. Reporting a real mistake exposes the batch to refusal, so we must price the alternative that the admission opens. This extension of [206](206-when-a-truthful-attempt-can-fail.md) lets the actual controller speak before release and lets the parties repair a failed first attempt.

## Three current selves facing a fresh choice

Use the balanced-strength workshop with the same material outcomes, test and old-goal fallback. After the first setup A privately knows whether the active goal is genuine (G), the attempted setup failed (M), or no attempt was made (D). It can announce readiness, report a need for rework, or decline. If unpriced words alone cause B to release with different probabilities and each type strictly gains from a higher release chance, every type seeks the message yielding the highest chance. Such words by themselves cannot strictly separate the three states in this game.

Now let A buy supervised reinstallation after reporting a need for it. The workshop can complete and certify the specified finite evaluator and its connection to the current batch. It costs A .8 units if the preliminary preparation is already in place, or 2 from scratch. It is also available directly at the full price. These prices represent useful work still required. The expensive completion interface is a substantive implementation premise. On an authentic completion B can take six by releasing, or two by declining, and the changed evaluator delivers. Without a completion B can decline the rework offer. A cooperative but unobservable promise to try again does not give B these payoffs.

At a readiness report suppose B tests with probability x and otherwise trusts. G acts with its current half-concern. A genuine ready controller values the resulting path at 7−.45x. The .45 includes the .1 probability of a false refusal conditional on a test, which loses 4 in G's units, and G's half-weight on B's .1 inspection bill. Starting the genuine completion route again gives 7−.8. Thus G strictly reports readiness for every x in [0,1]. M and D currently have the original own-output preference. Readiness earns each 6−3.6x. M can disclose and complete for 4−.8=3.2, which it prefers strictly if x>7/9. D can complete from scratch for 4−2=2, and can do better by claiming readiness even when x=1. A state with an unattempted change whose fresh repair becomes sufficiently cheap can truthfully report the need for work too; this would change what the report reveals about its *earlier* conduct. Ignoring verification entirely or making rework physically as expensive for M as for D weakens the basis for informative disclosure.

## Consent, selection and customer responses

For x>7/9, the original installer expects 3.84−.16x−k from actually trying. This weights a successful ready controller's *original receipts* of 4−.2x by q=.8, and repaired honest failure's receipts of 3.2 by .2. Fabrication earns 6−3.6x. The attempt cutoff and genuine fraction specifically among uncorrected readiness reports are

    k*(x)=−2.16+3.44x,      r=clip(k*(x)/K,0,1),
    p_ready=.8r/[1−.2r].                                        (212.1)

M's completion is an actual genuine change, paid for and chosen by its still-original self. The final installed fraction is r, while .8r was installed by the first attempt. A supervised completion from scratch for two is weakly below the fallback and strictly below profitable false or true participation on the following paths. B can physically contest before any rework or release. Both parties' preferred live options exceed their contest values in the central-strength workshop.

* **Wider private costs, K=2.** With x=1 the cutoff is 1.28 and r=.64. The ready pool contains mass .512 genuine and .36 deliberately false. Its prior lies strictly inside B's interval (3/38,17/22), so B pays to test. The remaining .128 honestly disclose and repair. B's gain above peaceful return is .512×.9×4−.36×.1×2−.872×.1+.128×4=2.196. A's ex ante original gain is .4+1.28²/4=.8096. Keeping the identical K=2 but removing availability of this rework yields .448 actual installations under the pure-test equilibrium of 206. The repair expands both actual consent and what an admission can communicate.
* **The original private costs, K=1.4.** Assuming x=1 would remove so many honest failures from the ready pool that the buyer would prefer untested trust. Solving its indifference at p_ready=17/22 gives r=17/21 and x=247/258. This exceeds 7/9, verifying the admission incentives. Among those actually checked and passed, genuine confidence is 153/158. Those who buy the supervised repair have the assurance furnished by that separate technology. On this selected branch some deception persists and verification remains privately funded despite the availability of repair.

Compare a different interface where no inspection can recognize an attempt and *every* claimant can obtain the cheap repair for the same price. At x=1 a retained D would then prefer the 3.2 repair return to the 2.4 ready gamble. Repairing becomes a constructive route to a genuine change even for an initially insincere signer, while the admission loses its power to distinguish past intentions. A classifier that treats every later verified success as evidence of initial honesty would misread exactly this repair. The full equilibrium needs the ensuing change in the ready pool; the deviation alone is a conditional result.

**Naturality and open assumptions.** Partly completed work can reduce the marginal cost of a second attempt. A shop that can perform a witnessed one-off restoration may be dearer than a fallible local edit. M has both the remaining parts and a current-goal reason to fund completion. The evidence that the witnessed update controls this job, and the availability of binding delivery without any edit, need their own comparison. A sibling tests ordinary supervised delivery at the same facility. The broader question is when access to faster repair should depend on evidence of past effort, current goals, or just a willingness to fund the remaining work.

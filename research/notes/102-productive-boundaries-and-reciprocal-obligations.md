# Productive boundaries and reciprocal outcome obligations

**R0005 derivation.** This note gives an open productive-agreement region,
then repairs the fixed-customer restriction at the level of a launched
continuation. Initial proposal and launch selection remain separately
specified. The proofs use the physical/funding accounts in 098–101.

## 1. A service can bridge the ordinary-division shortfall

Write R=R_i+R_j, a=a_j, Y for the original combat prize, and p for the
supplier's victory probability. Let d_j=(1-p)Y, d_i=pY. Let h be total
agreement expense and K the combined goal setup expense, including a
customer installation when there is one. The customer cannot transfer its
preloaded a. Service effort e costs c e and yields customer benefit b e,
where b>c>0. Define

    D=h-(R-Y),             eta=1-c/b.                 (1)

D is the excess of ordinary settlement expense over the material loss
avoided by settlement. The region D>0 has no ordinary division making
both originals as well off as their original duel in this transfer class.

Suppose a<d_j<a+b, payment capacity is positive, and the supplier can fund
preparation and effort. At the highest funded payment, the least effort
meeting the customer's original participation constraint is

    e*=(d_j-a)/b.                                    (2)

The supplier's resulting advantage over its original duel is exactly

    M=eta(d_j-a)-D-K.                                (3)

To see this, its terminal holding is R-h-K-a-c(d_j-a)/b; subtract
Y-d_j and collect terms. Thus M>0 gives an individually rational
productive opportunity even with D>0. The customer is indifferent at
e*. Any extra effort epsilon satisfying

    0<epsilon<min(1-e*,M/c)                          (4)

makes both originals strictly better off. This strict region is open;
it does not depend on exact customer indifference. A sole supplier
proposer with continuous offers may select e* instead, so bargaining
selection must not be inferred from (4).

## 2. Strength and retaliation jointly locate the useful region

Let A=a_i+a_j and Q_max=R-h-A>0. The sufficient bound excluding profitable
fixed-customer retaliation after payment is

    (1-p)Q_max<a.                                    (5)

It retains Attack and generously allows full recovery of the remaining
cash. Combine (2), (3), (5). A sufficient open strength interval is

    max(0, 1-a/Q_max, 1-(a+b)/Y)
        < p < 1-[a+(D+K)/eta]/Y.                    (6)

Additional funding, setup and work/time inequalities still apply. Formula
(6) identifies a region, not a distribution of strengths or a guarantee
that it is nonempty for every workshop. In particular, increasing K or D
shrinks the upper endpoint; increasing productive efficiency eta enlarges
it. More supplier strength raises its combat alternative while lowering
the customer's required compensation. Once liquidity binds, service buys
less additional payment, so M falls at rate -eta Y as p increases with
the other physical parameters fixed. If (5) instead fails, customer
retaliation can help an ordinary supplier commit; that successful ordinary
repair must be evaluated before attributing cooperation to a changed goal.

For R=10,Y=6,h=41/10,a=1,A=2,b=4,c=1,K=1/10,
Q_max=39/10, D=1/10 and eta=3/4. The interval (6) is

    29/39 < p < 71/90.                               (7)

p=3/4 lies strictly inside. At this p, e*=1/8 and M=7/40.
The strict effort e=1/5 uses 3/40 additional effort and leaves the
supplier gain 1/10 and customer gain 3/10. Setup K can vary over
0<K<11/40 while preserving some strict offer at the other stated
parameters. This is a quantified robustness region, not an empirical
claim that the high agreement expense or strength distribution is typical.

## 3. Both parties can adopt genuine goals preserving the agreed frontier

The customer's own ability to change goals can create demand threats
against a one-sided supplier obligation. A constructive repair is to
negotiate a reciprocal outcome floor for the supplier as well. The
following proof covers arbitrary subsequent goals through their actual
outcomes in the stated physical class, rather than freezing later code.

After all launch expenses, write the feasible service outcomes as

    x_i=A_0+t-c e,       x_j=B_0-t+b e,
    0<=t<=L,            0<=e<=1.                     (8)

The accepted agreement is t=L,e=e_0, with resulting q_i,q_j. Install

    C_i=x_i-lambda_i(q_j-x_j)_+,
    C_j=x_j-lambda_j(q_i-x_i)_+.                      (9)

If

    lambda_i>c/b,           lambda_j>b/c,             (10)

each party strictly prefers the agreed outcome to every other distinct
outcome in (8). Both goals have finite coefficients; neither rewards code
identity or forbids combat. The different thresholds reflect the real
conversion between supplier effort cost and customer output benefit.

**Proof.** Put z=L-t>=0 and x=e-e_0. Relative to the agreement, the
supplier gains -z-cx and the customer gains z+bx. If the supplier gains,
then x=-d<0 and z<cd. Its customer's deficit is bd-z. The ratio of its
gain to that deficit is

    (cd-z)/(bd-z) <= c/b.                            (11)

Thus its penalty outweighs every possible gain under (10). If it loses,
the nonnegative penalty cannot help. A zero own change at a different
outcome hurts the customer and gives a strict penalty. For the customer,
if x>=0, its gain/provider-deficit ratio is (z+bx)/(z+cx)<=b/c.
If x=-d<0 and the customer gains, z>bd and the ratio
(z-bd)/(z-cd)<1<b/c. The remaining cases are symmetric. This proves
strict maximization. Pointwise domination also covers lotteries. QED.

At equality in (10), some frontier directions can tie. Below the relevant
threshold, an increase in own reward can outweigh the accepted shortfall.
These are preference-design boundaries. Stronger concern is chosen to
support a negotiated outcome; its natural occurrence is a separate question.

The goals are genuine changes from own-output originals. The supplier
formerly preferred saving an increment of service cost; its new criterion
prefers honoring the promise. The customer formerly preferred additional
effort at the same price; its new criterion prefers the agreed effort
when the extra effort harms the supplier. These are reversals over actual
outcomes, hence also over degenerate lotteries, not affine rescalings.

## 4. A two-sided numerical continuation and initial consent

Split the earlier K=1/10 into K_i=K_j=1/20. Then A_0=29/20,
B_0=87/20, L=67/20. At e_0=1/5, the same terminal pair is
(q_i,q_j)=(23/5,9/5), strictly above (9/2,3/2). Each can fund its
own setup and retain its combat consumable. Choose, for example,
lambda_i=2 and lambda_j=5. Formula (9) strictly selects that pair over
the full price/effort class (8), even if either party can install a
different subsequent goal or negotiate another feasible price or effort.

Direct combat has the same conservative post-fee prize bounds as 100.
Each C is no greater than its own original payoff, while its honored
value equals q. Since expected own payoff from attack is below q at
those checkpoints, attack also loses under the installed goal. A costly
later installation producing one of these same outcomes cannot improve
the old evaluator. Both installed criteria already attain their maxima,
so no joint change within this launched class makes either strictly
better off. This is bilateral continuation stability, not a theorem
about coalitions with additional outside partners or productive methods.

Both originals would consent to paying their setup costs for this
implemented allocation, provided the actual launch and available ordinary
alternatives preserve the displayed comparison. Initial willingness,
successful staged activation and selection of this quote remain distinct.
A conditional jointly recorded launch, funded preparation refunds, or a
priced equivalent ordinary controller are candidate implementations.
An unrestricted last-activator bargaining game can change terms after
earlier costs are sunk; (9) alone does not solve that earlier game.

## 5. Renewal and renegotiation scope

The accepted floor can be attached to each real, capacity-feasible service
contract. Choosing another contract under an unchanged rule is not a fresh
utility change. At a settled boundary, both criteria equal own welfare
on honored outcomes and can favor a better new partner, a lower-cost
method, compensation or further substantive goal revision. The construction
therefore protects a negotiated outcome without assigning intrinsic value
to permanent goal retention.

The local proof must be rechecked when outcomes outside (8) become feasible.
New surplus can support mutually beneficial revision or funded compensation;
an unavailable repair can allow a finite penalty to be outweighed. For an
entire renewable game, use actual per-cycle feasible sets and future partner
responses, rather than summing this certificate while silently discarding
cross-cycle investment, reputation or threats. Notes 099 and 103 make the
renewal and partner-arrival assumptions explicit.

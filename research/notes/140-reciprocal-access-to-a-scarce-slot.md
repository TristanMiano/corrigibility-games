# When sharing a production slot makes a third partner worth inviting

**E8, note 140.** The framing candidate is that a coalition can acquire value by matching temporary capacity to whichever partner currently needs it most. The structural candidate is reciprocal access before demand is known. The instance below tests the incremental value of the *third* partner under nonlinear original preferences and a fully rival resource. Claims here concern original consent and allocation. Note 141 examines a different division of the same productive gain.

## Physical opportunity and argument for the class

A single scheduled machine slot can finish one job before a deadline. All three jobs are technically eligible. Whoever controls the booking can use it locally or route it to one eligible site. Only one site gets the completed output. The incumbent for this slot is a uniform random participant, independent of subsequent urgency. A rotating assignment viewed before the phase is known gives the same calculation if urgency has the same distribution across phases. If the incumbent already knows who owns today's slot, that interim bargaining problem has different reservations.

Temporary exclusive capacity, expiring jobs and state-dependent output are common reasons to seek a pool. The result applies to the allocation problem wherever those properties hold. Exchangeability is a tractable approximation for similarly sized sites under a common workload. Differences in size, the bargaining queue, observations and physical authority deserve their own comparisons. A voluntary agreement is one way to make all sites eligible. Each member has to pay real current wealth for adaptation and consent. If a nonmember can simply use the equipment, or if the same job can be delayed at negligible cost, the value of membership changes.

Let initial safe private output be w, large enough to fund a setup cost k_m if a club of m actually forms. A positive slot yield x is additional private output. It can reflect durable output from a deadline-sensitive task or avoided rework. For a lost or unused slot the additional output is zero. Stocks, useful work, service rate, routing time and prepared inputs must support the claimed choices. In a realization, take equal service times for the jobs, enough work for one batch and a hard closing time. Real losses due to implementation come from the setup reserve. No payment or preference edit creates an additional slot. The timing and attack comparison are specified separately below.

Each founder ranks its final private output q by u(q) = -exp(-gamma q), gamma > 0. This choice gives a closed form for willingness to pay from a safe stock. Risk sensitivity and finite marginal values are independently motivated. Exact constant absolute risk aversion is an auxiliary choice. For an arbitrary increasing u the allocation argument still applies using state-dependent increments relative to the funded base. Normalize the service increment to v=1-exp(-gamma x), between zero and one. Given an assignment rule, put z_i=E[v_i 1{i served}]. The founder's evaluation and certainty equivalent, in original output units, are

    E u_i = -exp(-gamma (w-k_m))(1-z_i)
    CE_i = w-k_m-log(1-z_i)/gamma.                    (1)

If the incumbent belongs to the pool, a common criterion maximizing the sum of members' normalized utility increments sends the job to a member with largest v. Exchangeable tie breaking gives each the same ex ante chance conditional on the unordered profile. A genuine installed goal can implement this rule at the decision point. A low-urgency incumbent with positive own yield strictly prefers serving itself under its original criterion and strictly prefers serving a high-urgency member under the common goal. This supplies a preference reversal with physical control held fixed. A matched binding delegate or booking system can implement the same rule. Public observation of urgency and executed reciprocal authorization are implementation premises. Self-knowledge alone supplies neither.

## Each additional partner's price ceiling

Let M_m = E max(v_1,...,v_m) under the joint urgency law of m distinct sites. A pool has the slot with probability m/3. Symmetry therefore gives every pool member z_i=M_m/3. An outsider gets its own booking and obtains M_1/3. In particular the original's gross willingness to pay for enlarging a group from m to m+1 is

    D_m = log[(1-M_m/3)/(1-M_(m+1)/3)]/gamma.         (2)

Strictly prefer enlargement exactly when the additional own setup cost is below D_m, holding the actual alternatives and tie rule fixed. At equality both routes can be optimal. At m=1 the solo baseline has k_1=0. Formally replacing the symmetric access probability m/3 by a fixed probability independent of group size would lose the contribution of the entrant's own booking rights.

For two productive states put v=l or h, 0<l<h<1, and b_m=P(all first m sites low). Then

    M_m=l+(h-l)(1-b_m),
    M_(m+1)-M_m=(h-l) P(first m low, entrant high).     (3)

The overlap of high demand, the return to serving the urgent job, and the price of adding a member determine the admission incentive. This identity allows arbitrary exchangeable dependence. Under iid urgency with high probability pi, the probability in (3) is pi(1-pi)^m. In a perfectly synchronized class it vanishes. In a complementary schedule it can be substantial even when two earlier members provide similar coverage. These are value and strict preference comparisons at formation. They concern actual installations only once the formation interface is specified.

## What can be learned from two sites, and what a third measurement adds

Write pi=P(H_1), b=P(H_1,H_2) and t=P(H_1,H_2,H_3). The probability of the pivotal third-member event is pi-2b+t. The probabilities of zero, one, two and three urgent sites are respectively

    1-3pi+3b-t,   3(pi-2b+t),   3(b-t),   t.

Consequently any t in [max(0,2b-pi),min(b,1-3pi+3b)] is feasible for feasible pi,b. Assign its count probabilities uniformly among subsets of the stated size to prove sufficiency. All marginal and pair observations fix the two-party return. They leave an exact interval of three-party returns. An admission decision is already identified if the cost lies below both willingness-to-pay bounds or above both. Inside that interval one more observable, the frequency of a high third site when both partners are low, resolves the symmetric case.

Two positive common-load mixtures give an interior example. In A a common load condition induces independent urgent jobs conditional on its latent probability P, which is 1/8 or 5/8 with probabilities 1/4 and 3/4. In B use 1-P with those same probabilities. The law is known before contracting. For the private-report comparison in 143–144, the latent load realization stays unobserved through reporting. Observing it earlier gives a different conditional urgency law. Both have pi=1/2 and b=19/64. Their third moments are 47/256 and 53/256. The pivotal third site occurs with probability 23/256 and 29/256. Both models represent ordinary fluctuating workloads with positive co-movement and probabilities strictly between zero and one. The particular numbers make a clean comparison. Small changes around either configuration preserve its strict choice.

Take gamma=1, l=1/4, h=3/4 and equal-probability original bookings. Then M_1=1/2, M_2=77/128 and M_3 is 331/512 or 337/512. The gross third-party price ceilings are log(1228/1205) and log(1228/1199). Choose k_2=log(101/100) and k_3-k_2=log(1228/1202). In either regime a founder strictly prefers a pair to being alone. It strictly prefers a pair to a trio in A and a trio to a pair in B. The two-state cost interval between thresholds gives the same reversal. No one has an intrinsic preference for a club size or for goal change.

## Endogenous membership in a dated invitation game

Before the slot arrives, any recognized proposer can invite any subset containing itself. Each recipient can consent or decline. All invitees have to consent to the same allocation criterion or ordinary configuration, including the eligible set. They can also retain their original independent rights. A failed offer or a pass uses an invitation interval. With positive probability the productive opportunity arrives while they wait, when uncontracted incumbents use their original rights. Otherwise the turn passes to another proposer before a finite final deadline. No one is required to accept a losing early offer. A paid booking interface is allowed to operate only within the interface's stated reliability and preemption boundaries.

Suppose for each size a symmetric route pays the same CE to each member. Use the best actually available route for that size, including ordinary contracts, pooling with reciprocal genuine changes, and independent production. For this result these exhaust the offered routes and there are no side payments. If a member's favorite attainable size m>=2 strictly beats solo, inviting that size achieves the proposer's maximum directly. A required invitee strictly prefers accepting to rejection: even the best possible later offer gives at most its favorite value, and delay has a positive chance of solo. With sequential public acceptances this proves acceptance by backward induction, including at early signatures, if failure consumes the interval for all. If no offer can give the proposer more, making its favorite offer is optimal. This argument works for any proposer order. Tied routes can give multiple optimal choices. For the numeric example the first proposer therefore forms a pair in A and a trio in B, provided the indicated route for each size is the actual best symmetric route. The excluded third site's booking remains its own. An exclusive agenda with simultaneous rival offers, asymmetric priority, bargaining transfers, or individually costly interim signatures changes the argument and is explored separately.

**Comparing mechanisms.** If the cheapest reliable implementation of the selected allocation is reciprocal installation, the selected coalition installs m genuine goals. A cheaper equally effective ordinary binding router selects ordinary pooling and zero genuine changes. With tied reliable devices both paths are possible. Reversible announcements lead back to the low incumbent's strict preference for local service. If compensated spot service, repeated reciprocal behavior or another partner is available, compute that value rather than using sole service as the outside comparison. A first offer still has to survive refusal. Behavior after a later edit is evaluated by the then-current goal. At a valid common allocation window the attainable total common service increment is already maximal in every realized state.

## Physical and selection boundary

The formation proof compares the productive routes. Combat remains available before and after authorization. A simple embedding lets fighting consume time d for the contest and r for repair, with positive recovery work and finite damage to the local interface. If the earliest usable service after a fight occurs after expiry, any capable party can still initiate a fight but can obtain zero current job output that way. Give every potential initiator a strictly positive private expendable cost and preserve safe existing stocks in this particular location. Then even an unfavored nonowner strictly prefers waiting for its ordinary baseline to combat. The assumption is physically meaningful for a short perishable slot with a slow-to-restore controller and remote safe stocks. Longer-lived capacity or capturable stocks require an actual contest comparison. Amend the bounds if the fight can collect private safe stocks or benefit future slots. All winning probabilities are strength dependent (e.g. positive strengths proportional to chance of control) and strictly between zero and one. A terminal closed slot has no remaining service capacity. Defeat stops the specified actuator, while originals retain their valuations of durable output. This is a finite time-to-recovery restriction, with a finite physical cost even when it reverses the productive choice.

**Exploration record.** The tested instance is the symmetric booking game under fixed implementation costs and symmetric eligibility. The candidate “matching the pair statistics identifies the three-party choice” is dead at that instance: the two load laws above choose different memberships. A sibling estimates the pivotal conditional urgency directly. A structural successor asks which portfolio of conditional demand measurements identifies a coalition's value over a class of joint laws. A separate sibling invites a low-correlation partner or purchases a rescheduling option. Its ability to improve current goals depends on real rescheduling costs and final bargaining terms. Both constructive and limiting routes remain open at the framing level.

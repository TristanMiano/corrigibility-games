# 280 — When does the same bargain work across disagreeing forecasts?

Two plants may know that a suitably priced bargain is attractive for each possible technology, yet disagree about which technology they have. An attractive price for one diagnosis can be unacceptable to a founder facing the other one. The missing information can concern who will get the physical yield even when the parties understand exactly how reliable their goal changes are.

## A uniform participation and effort test

In [269](269-which-insurance-bargains-preserve-both-incentives-to-change.md), let ω range over a finite set of possible laws. At a given insurance spread h denote Red's least acceptable baseline transfer from Blue by L_ω(h)=R_Aω+T_A log m_Aω(h), and Blue's maximum by U_ω(h)=−R_Bω−T_B log m_Bω(h). For one contract to beat both outside choices for *every* admitted law, h must satisfy every law's bilateral effort tests and the physical constraints, and

    max_ω L_ω(h) < min_ω U_ω(h).                                 (280.1)

For strict consent the open price interval must also contain a funded payment respecting both stock limits. Equation (269.2) gives those bounds. This follows by writing every founder's comparison at the very same baseline price. Equivalently, test L_ω(h)<U_η(h) for every pair of admitted laws, including ω≠η. Checking only the matched pairs can approve an unpriceable agreement. If a public observation can distinguish some of the laws *before* settlement and its outcome can govern the contract, a common price is required only within each resulting information set. Price-contingent changes in effort or withdrawal require their own incentive comparisons. The monotone common adverse endpoint in [270](270-how-certain-must-the-partners-be-about-joint-success.md) is one favorable special case.

## A location advantage, an uncertain gift, and a limited meter

Each plant can retain five units on its safe line. If both authorize and actually try the shared procedure, the two base stocks on the joint line are (5+d,5−d) in one location condition and (5−d,5+d) in the other. Let p be the chance of the Red-favored condition. The plants can each witness an attempt without learning its success. Each pays k=.20, independently succeeds with q=.75 and then decides whether to expend one unit to give its partner two. A successful successor values its own stock plus .75 times partner stock, and strictly gives. A retained controller keeps. The line opens on two witnessed procedures. Refusing or failing to produce a witness leaves the other party's batch uncommitted. An omission cannot pretend to be an observed procedure. The common setup bill, if charged after authorization, is sunk at later decisions.

The original founders value only their own stock, linearly. Each receives an expected increment σ=q(2−1)−k=.55 before location effects. They choose before either can observe the condition. It is settled physically after irrevocable routing. A contest can still interrupt before that conversion, with a strength-dependent winner recovering at most two useful units from disrupted inputs. Safe return gives five, and carrying the authorized operation through is preferable for every relevant chooser. A learned location, publicly observable prior to production, would permit a different negotiation.

If d=1 and p can be anywhere in [0,1], each possible location condition would admit bilateral gain if its price could be adjusted. But Red would demand more than .45 from Blue in the adverse orientation and Blue would demand more than .45 from Red in the other. No constant payment suffices. Any settlement on goal evidence alone also has the *same expected payment* in both conditions when that evidence is independent of the condition and both really try. More accurate installation evidence by itself has no leverage on this location risk.

For an interval [p₋,p₊] a freely chosen constant t suffices exactly when 2σ>2d(p₊−p₋). For example, if both can put p between .25 and .75, the original deal gives each at least .05 more than safe return at every admitted forecast with zero transfer. Both voluntarily try even though the chance of two changed goals is only 9/16 and neither knows who will receive the better base stock. Requiring every forecast to support the choice is a sufficient consent criterion for a founder with its own particular forecast. An agent using a different ambiguity criterion should apply that criterion to the entire material lottery.

Now suppose p stays weakly known in [(1−w)/2,(1+w)/2], but the plants can buy a physical reading of the actual orientation after allocation. It is correct with symmetric probability r>1/2. A common escrow can pay up to L in either direction on that reading and each pays c for the meter. Among *all* bounded settlements on that reading and any auxiliary data independent of both the orientation and the meter, the largest guaranteed gain to **both** original agents over return is

    σ−c−w max(0, d−L(2r−1)).                                     (280.2)

To see sharpness, average any extra data out conditional on the two meter signs. Let the resulting transfers to Red be t₊ and t₋ in [−L,L], with mean a and half-difference b. Red's conditional bonus on top of σ−c at prior p is a+(2p−1)[d+(2r−1)b]. Blue's bonus is its negative. The largest absolute exposure over the prior interval is |a|+w|d+(2r−1)b|. It is minimized by a=0 and b=−min(L,d/(2r−1)), which obeys both stock bounds. This proves the expression and gives an actual settlement. If the meter can be chosen or forged by either party before it settles, these are no longer the possible conditional laws.

For w=1,d=L=1,r=.80 and c=.05 the least favorable gain is .10 each. Red receives one unit if the meter indicates Blue was favored and pays one for the opposite sign. The partners buy the meter at any equal charge strictly below .15 each. This is chosen, solvent protection sufficient for consent over *all* p. Both have a strict reason to attempt given the other's attempt. The selected outcome has .75 genuine changes per individual and .5625 pairs in which both actual goals match. The chance that both authorized a real attempt is one. A no-launch equilibrium can coexist if neither initiates the conditional arrangement.

A binding ordinary actuator capable of sending both gifts at the same foundries increases each founder's gross production margin to 1 by saving the .20 installation expense and removing failures. With the same orientation hedge, an actuator costing .25 per plant leaves a guaranteed .30 each, which strictly displaces both changes. At .60 per plant the attempted change leaves a larger margin. In an uncontrolled ordinary full job both selfish plants keep their contributions and the location effect still needs coverage. A one-sided genuine attempt can produce only .75−.20=.55 of total expected production surplus. With w=1 and the same imperfect bounded meter, covering both opposite worst-case location exposures would require at least .80 total even before its two fees. A transfer of that limited scope cannot fund both on this one-sided alternative.

**Naturality and construction.** Owners sharing a line can know the total base yield more accurately than how much will return to each site. A dispatch or receipt measurement may be affordable even if it sometimes identifies the wrong destination. Two conditional subscriptions fund the meter from the gain to their existing output goals. Waiting for the location to become public for free, independently auditing each base output or buying sufficiently flexible ordinary actuators would materially expand their options. Perfectly independent faults and knowing a uniform bound on the orientation error require evidence. This example prices the specific protection the partners can build and the remaining risk they voluntarily accept.

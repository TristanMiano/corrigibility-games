# Credible disclosure opens late repair; contribution incentives govern its use

R0003 working derivation. This note connects the conservative private-state safeguard to an informative disclosure repair and to the new incentives created by that repair. The calculations concern the linear E3 technology and a specified bargaining module. Full installation and off-path composition must use a separately specified protocol; the local module is not silently an unrestricted-game theorem.

## 1. Why one unrevealed replacement can be too demanding

Let W mean that the initial calibration was actually completed, and S that it was missed. After paying the initial installation cost, the default expected material allocations at share t are:

| Actual initial inputs (A,B) | Original A expectation | Original B expectation | Total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| WW | 27/20+4t | 107/20-4t | 67/10 |
| SW | 59/20+t | 47/20-t | 53/10 |
| WS | 27/20+t | 79/20-t | 53/10 |
| SS | 59/20 | 59/20 | 59/10 |

Suppose an efficient late plan supplies precisely the missing inputs, restores success probability 2/5, and incurs no additional finished-material loss beyond those inputs. The final total expectation is 67/10 in every initial-input state. Communication, update and execution still use real work and time; the zero additional *material* premise is not a claim that they are physically free.

If a replacement's expected allocation is independent of the hidden initial-input state, statewise protection requires

\[
E X\ge\max_\omega q_A(\omega),\qquad
E Y\ge\max_\omega q_B(\omega).
\]

But the SW bound for A and WS bound for B alone sum to

\[
(59/20+t)+(79/20-t)=69/10>67/10.
\]

Hence no such state-independent full repair preserves every private-state floor. The minimum excess requirement is 1/5; it is attained when 7/15<=t<=8/15. At t=9/20 the maxima are 17/5 and 71/20, totaling 139/20, so the excess is 1/4. This is a precise obstruction for an unrevealed allocation under this safeguard, despite the 7/5 real surplus available after a single miss and 4/5 after two misses.

The restriction does substantive work. A state-dependent, credibly disclosed repair can preserve both participants' *actual* floors. Extra resources, a cheaper productive method, informative screening or a different chosen safeguard can also change the comparison. The next sections construct one disclosure repair and test its induced effort incentives.

## 2. Cheap reports under unchanged output preferences: a narrow IC bound

Consider a direct reporting mechanism in which, after every possible report pair and deviation, both physical inputs are supplied at the same minimal material cost. Its expected final total is always 67/10. Take current criteria X_i+lambda_i X_j+C_i(omega), lambda_i<1, where C_i is a sunk history term fixed by the true initial-input state and unaffected by reports. There is no preference for truthful reporting, verification of the state, report-dependent productive cost or change in future obligations in this restricted class.

Conditional on the other report, every type of i ranks reports by the same quantity (1-lambda_i) E X_i. Truthful incentive compatibility for both of its possible types therefore forces the same expected own allocation for either report. Because the total is fixed, the other's expected allocation is then also unchanged. Apply the same argument to the other agent. The expected allocation is independent of both reports, so section 1's statewise protection obstruction applies.

This is not an impossibility theorem for all information mechanisms. Its proof explicitly excludes report-dependent costs, binding future performance, private recommendations changing production, verifiable evidence, and chosen truthful-reporting preferences. Those exclusions identify repairs to examine; the following construction uses the last of them. A matched reporting delegate with access to the same actual private record is a valid control.

## 3. A chosen truth preference supplies credible state information

Let Z_i record the current evaluator's first false signed report of its own actual calibration history. Its ground truth is the same protected private action record used by the contribution predicate; a recipient does not get that record merely by reading the agent's utility. The current agent knows its own action. The finite implementation can attest the actual goal/monitor template, preserve its private record and bind a signed statement to that template. Those are additional implementation facts, not consequences of self-knowledge.

Choose

\[
G_i=B_i+\beta_i e_i-K_i I_i-L_i Z_i,
\qquad 0\le B_i\le M_i,
\quad L_i>M_i+\beta_i.
\]

At a clean reporting information set, after subtracting any other common sunk historical penalties, a first false report gives current terminal value below zero. Truthful refusal or a truthful report can retain nonnegative value when the safe continuation has no unsafe approval and no false report. Thus false reporting is strictly inferior at every posterior, including after a missed contribution. Once a lie has already occurred, its penalty is sunk; the argument is not reused for that evaluator without checking its actual state. The first-approval constraint and this first-false-report constraint can both be validated within note 059's completion construction.

The predicate concerns an actual historical proposition, not the string “honest” or a public claim to be trustworthy. It needs reliable access to the controller's own specified action history. It does not make arbitrary subjective beliefs, unverifiable external observations or arbitrary utility semantics verifiable. Deliberate corruption of that protected record is a separately audited physical instruction; it cannot be assumed harmless.

Genuine preference change is straightforward on the declared history-lottery domain: enough extra own material can make a false report attractive under the original own-output utility while the finite truth penalty reverses that ordering. At the original installation decision the old criterion scores the actual resulting material law, not L or the new criterion's numerical score.

For M=16, beta in [17,19], the finite constants K=L=40 suffice. This is a conservative all-continuation bound. A smaller penalty is adequate after the performance deadline when the already-earned or missed reward is fixed. Matching delegation, a binding protected readout, or an effort-observation interface can implement the same informational benefit if genuinely available at the charged costs. A revocable assertion by an unchanged own-output controller supplies no such guarantee by itself.

## 4. Efficient state-specific repairs after truthful disclosure

At t=9/20 the old allocations are:

\[
q(WW)=(63/20,71/20),\quad q(SW)=(17/5,19/10),
\]
\[
q(WS)=(9/5,7/2),\quad q(SS)=(59/20,59/20).
\]

After disclosure, checking the actual revealed state rather than every hypothetical state permits a genuine gain from repair. With a symmetric division of its additional material expectation, the new allocations can be

\[
q'(SW)=(41/10,13/5),\quad
q'(WS)=(5/2,21/5),\quad q'(SS)=(67/20,67/20).
\]

WW retains its original allocation. Each repaired pair totals 67/10 and raises both actual old floors: by 7/10 after one miss and 2/5 after two misses. Every allocation is implemented by a success share (q'_A-27/20)/4 in [0,1], with baseline failure holdings 27/20. The shares are respectively 11/16, 23/80 and 1/2. No money is created by the transfer.

If late registration consumes material r_A,r_B, replace the added surplus by 7/5-r_A-r_B or 4/5-r_A-r_B, and recheck each nonnegative holding and actual success share. Strict mutually beneficial repair needs positive residual surplus. Delay can also matter if the original utilities value earlier delivery; the displayed terminal-material example does not impose discounting silently.

A physical schedule can reserve an amendment window after initial calibration at time 9: signed reports and a contingent offer travel to time 13, checking/acceptance completes at 14 and acknowledgments at 18. Existing input can be stored rather than consumed. Missing input supplied at 18–19 arrives by 23; a two-slot reaction and four-slot output delivery finish at 29. If a fresh three-slot controller activation is needed after acknowledgment, it can finish at 21, calibration at 22, input delivery at 26, reaction at 28 and output at the valuation boundary 32. This is a candidate causal schedule with explicit storage, binding conditional offers and finite-template checks; its total work, message costs, update costs and interruption rules still need the full protocol audit. Early reaction, refusal and Attack remain physical alternatives.

## 5. Bargaining rights alter the reward needed for timely contribution

Efficient repair can reward a past shirker. To calculate this effect rather than hide it, consider a module where truthful disclosure occurs, B makes the final repair offer, and A's protected reservation is its actual old expected material q_A(omega). B has lambda_B<1 and maximizes its own allocation at the fixed efficient total. For a single miss or two misses it gives A exactly its protected floor and keeps the extra surplus. A's current lambda_A>0 makes accepting an otherwise feasible surplus-creating offer strictly better even when its own allocation is unchanged.

The resulting expected material table is

| Initial actions | A | B | Implementing success share of A |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| WW | 63/20 | 71/20 | 9/20 |
| SW | 17/5 | 33/10 | 41/80 |
| WS | 9/5 | 49/10 | 9/80 |
| SS | 59/20 | 15/4 | 2/5 |

Assume the specified late plan is implemented and that all other relevant history terms are constant in each initial-action comparison. A's on-time contribution has current gain

\[
\beta_A-\tfrac14(1-\lambda_A)\quad\hbox{against B's Work},
\]
\[
\beta_A-\tfrac{23}{20}(1-\lambda_A)\quad\hbox{against B's Shirk}.
\]

B's corresponding gains are

\[
\beta_B-\tfrac{27}{20}(1-\lambda_B),\qquad
\beta_B-\tfrac9{20}(1-\lambda_B).
\]

Thus beta_A>(23/20)(1-lambda_A) and beta_B>(27/20)(1-lambda_B) make timely contribution strictly dominant in this specified repaired table. At lambda_A=lambda_B=2/3 the thresholds are 23/60 and 9/20. This substantially sharper calculation is local to the stated offer rights and continuation; it does not replace the conservative beta>16 bound for arbitrary richer continuations.

With beta_A=beta_B=0, each strictly prefers the initial Shirk action in this table, and the selected SS repair gives A 59/20<3. That would defeat A's initial strict adoption of this particular positive-cost package. It is a protocol-specific incentive problem, not an obstruction to accommodation in general. Dividing proposal rights, changing the protected repair rule, choosing a sufficient process reward, or scheduling the actual contribution later can repair it. The last alternative is especially important: delayed performance can still be productive cooperation. A finite early deadline is not itself a social objective or a reason to label all later execution combat.

When only one input is missing and the partner's input is already present, a current output-only concern criterion has late-calibration gain

\[
3[t'+\lambda(1-t')]-8/5.
\]

At lambda=2/3 this equals 2/5+t'>0 for every share t' in [0,1]. Thus the concerned current agent can voluntarily perform the single missing calibration even without earning its old lost process reward. With both inputs missing, complementary late effort still has coordination incentives; a fresh mutually accepted goal with a new future-obligation reward, or a matched binding task delegate, can supply a stronger implementation. Any such new epoch leaves the old evaluator's actual missed reward at zero and must be evaluated using that old criterion when installed.

### Negotiated proposal lotteries lower both required rewards

Let A receive the final proposal right with probability rho, chosen independently **after** the initial contributions and not revealed earlier. The proposer keeps the added surplus subject to the other agent's actual original floor. At a general initial success share t, the expected repaired A allocations are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
WW&27/20+4t\\
SW&59/20+t+(7/5)\rho\\
WS&27/20+t+(7/5)\rho\\
SS&59/20+(4/5)\rho.
\end{array}
\]

B receives the rest of 67/10. Current linear utilities permit this averaging; nonlinear utilities would require evaluating the actual allocation lottery. The exact strict-dominance requirements in this module are

\[
\beta_A>(1-\lambda_A)
\max\{8/5-3t+(7/5)\rho,\;8/5-t-(3/5)\rho\},
\]
\[
\beta_B>(1-\lambda_B)
\max\{3t-(7/5)\rho,\;t+(3/5)\rho\}.
\]

Both right-hand sides are minimized at **rho=t**. For A the decreasing and increasing affine pieces cross there; for B the same point is the crossing of its decreasing and increasing pieces. The resulting thresholds are

\[
\boxed{\beta_A>(8/5)(1-t)(1-\lambda_A),\qquad
\beta_B>(8/5)t(1-\lambda_B).}
\]

For t=9/20 and lambda_i=2/3 these are 22/75 and 6/25, instead of 23/60 and 9/20 under B's certain proposal right. Choosing proposal probabilities proportional to the original output shares therefore lowers *both* agents' required timely-contribution rewards in this declared module. It is an exact design optimum over this proposal-lottery family, not over all bargaining mechanisms or successor goals.

The lottery requires its actual implementation and cost. A role known before contribution, correlated with the private action, or manipulable by a participant gives a different effort game. This repair reduces the extra process preference needed for timely performance; concluding that it also reduces material setup cost requires a separate cost law. A fixed promised-allocation repair, as in note 064, changes the reservation rule itself and is a different constructive alternative.

## 6. Scope of the connection

The statewise safeguard blocks misleading sender-selected trades, while informative, credible disclosure can release otherwise usable gains. The repair then changes the incentives for earlier behavior, so truthful reporting, future contribution and earlier contribution need separate checks. The calculations preserve both the constructive accommodation and the scoped incentive loss. They do not identify the globally optimal reporting mechanism, assign a frequency to natural agents, or establish full sequential implementation before the schedule, commitments and off-path decisions are checked together.

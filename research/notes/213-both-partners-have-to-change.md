# Both partners have to change

In an exchange of services, either founder may prefer keeping its old goal while the other does the work. An inspection on one side changes the return to installing on the other. This extension gives both parties a real update, allows hidden expense and honest failure on both sides, and derives the prior beliefs at both ends of the deal.

## Paired production and imperfect commissioning

Each founder initially maximizes its own material output. Each has ten units of baseline output on a private line. Once two connecting jobs are opened, donor i can forego d_i units of local finishing to deliver b_j units to j. The opportunity to receive from j does not condition mechanically on i actually sending. Those last local decisions are simultaneous. The agreed goal for i values its own output plus θ_i times the partner's, with θ_i b_j>d_i. Genuine installation reverses its ranking of sending versus keeping, holding the other contribution fixed. A genuine acts on this goal and sends; an original keeps. Closed connections leave local production. Either founder can still contest at a live checkpoint; in the numerical examples the victor can recover eight units on its own line before the deadline. With d_i=1 either local productive outcome gives each at least nine, and the positive-weight goals also prefer it to contest. Bills are incurred from separate, sufficient reserves and remain sunk on both branches.

Both must agree to fund the commissioning gate. Each privately knows its attempt expense k_i, uniform on [0,K_i]. Paying k_i produces the agreed evaluator with probability q_i; a failed procedure leaves the original in place. A signer can alternatively represent an attempt while saving the expense. Conditional on each resulting evaluator a public reading passes with probability t_i for genuine G, e_i for retained N. Owning a plant, its ability to attempt, and its assessor's ability to discriminate are separate. Costs, successes and readings are independent across founders in this calculation. Both positive readings open the physical connection. Neither a signature nor the open gate can force the final local contribution. A fixed public gate can be a physical connection requiring two commissioning approvals; later waivers, override rights and binding local routing are distinct technologies.

Let p_i be the actual installed fraction before readings. Under original i's output criterion, the gain from *successful* installation over signing with an unchanged goal is

    Z_i(p_j)=p_j t_j b_i(t_i−e_i) − t_i d_i[p_j t_j+(1−p_j)e_j]
            = m_i p_j−n_i,   n_i=t_i d_i e_j.                     (213.1)

The first term is the extra probability of receiving j's work due to one's own more credible membership. The second charges effort on every open job where i truly changed, including when its partner stayed unchanged. A failed attempt is observationally and behaviorally identical to N in this interface. Thus i chooses to try if k_i<q_i Z_i, and

    p_i=min(q_i,max(0,h_i p_j−z_i)),   h_i=q_i²m_i/K_i,
    z_i=q_i²n_i/K_i.                                                (213.2)

These are current-goal incentive conditions. Original ex ante participation after pricing the gate and the real outside option must be checked separately.

## When a two-sided bargain has a positive equilibrium

**213.3.** With discriminating tests, n_i>0 and positive m_i for both sides, a positive fixed point exists precisely if H=h_A h_B>1 and

    a=(h_A z_B+z_A)/(H−1) ≤ q_A,      b=(h_B z_A+z_B)/(H−1) ≤ q_B.

A positive fixed point satisfies p_A≤h_A p_B−z_A and its reverse-side counterpart, hence H>1, p_A≥a, p_B≥b. Conversely (a,b) itself solves both uncapped equations within capacity. The greatest fixed point is

    (min(q_A,h_A q_B−z_A),  min(q_B,h_B q_A−z_B)).

If both caps bind, substitution proves this. If one cap does not bind, say A's, take p_B=q_B and p_A=h_A q_B−z_A. B's best response saturates because q_B≥b. The two slack-cap inequalities cannot both hold strictly when the positive fixed point exists. These observations also prove the claimed upper bound is attained. Zero remains a fixed point; the model identifies the conditions for supported, mutually chosen changes, and the incentives to fund their selection.

Take b_A=b_B=5, d_A=d_B=1, θ_A=θ_B=1/2, K_A=K_B=1, t_A=t_B=.9, e_A=e_B=.1 and q_A=.9, q_B=.7. Both types' attempt cutoffs exceed even their maximum expense on the high branch. Both sign and try. The prior probability that both goals are actually installed is .63. Given two positives, an outside observer's posteriors for A and B are 81/82 and 21/22. The joint probability is 1701/1804, approximately 94.29%, although each marginal exceeds 95%. Each founder that knows its own successful change uses the *other* founder's posterior for the outstanding uncertainty. A positive sign on the more reliable plant can thus mean something different to each side. Dropping independence requires the joint likelihood; with only two marginal probabilities u,v, the sharp joint bounds are max(0,u+v−1) and min(u,v), attained by reassigning overlap mass.

Before setup expense A and B expect incremental own outputs 2.0484 and 2.1564 on the high branch. For i the expression is p_i p_j t_i t_j(b_i−d_i)+(1−p_i)p_j e_i t_j b_i−p_i(1−p_j)t_i e_j d_i. Even the most expensive type pays its cost of one and a gate fee of .05 to participate compared with the local fallback. An ordinary delegate that could enforce both final deliveries would yield four extra own units each before its price. A fee below two per founder for that stronger routing service beats this installation arrangement even for the lowest-cost honest types. A gate controlling only whether two lines connect need not hold that final authority.

## One partner can be much harder to assess

Raise only e_B to .3. Both still strictly attempt at the upper equilibrium. Given positive readings, B can be about 98.78% confident in A, while A is only 87.5% confident in B. Both founders can still afford even their highest attempt expense and the gate fee. At e_B=.7 with the same cost ranges, m_A=3.42 and m_B=.18. The product H is below one, so the positive equilibrium disappears in this class. The weak discrimination of B's channel removes B's benefit from *actually* changing and in turn removes A's willing partner. Reducing K_B to .04 in this last setting restores the all-attempt fixed point: q_B Z_B(.9)=.0504 now exceeds its whole expense range. Direct substitution gives positive net original returns after fees even for the most expensive founders. On this repaired branch A gives a positive B only 75% confidence. A capability for cheap honest changes and a capability to certify them perform different economic work.

**Naturality and scope.** Distinct service lines, hidden installation expense and commissioning equipment of different quality can coexist even for materially selfish founders. Funding the certificates is individually affordable on the specified branch. Knowing the partner will also sign, having independent readouts linked to the true final controllers, and the scope of local routing authority are consequential premises. A guarantee that can enforce both actions, a cheaper check, or a bilateral waiver changes the outside options. The zero branch and alternative arrangements keep actual selection open. The sibling question gives the two parties a chance to repair a failed gate after seeing their own state; the broader one asks how a common supplier or standard makes their technical failures informative about each other.

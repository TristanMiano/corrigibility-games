# How skill and training prices change what a pilot means

When the customer can choose how often to train after failure, a capable unchanged supplier sometimes finds it worthwhile to deliver the pilot. The buyer correctly discounts that evidence. Both parties can prefer the resulting risky business to walking away.

## Buying help or imitating a successful pilot

Use [245](245-when-helping-after-a-failed-pilot-makes-success-more-informative.md). Let y be the chance that a failed pilot gets training and the main lot; successful pilots always get the lot. Let m be the fraction of skilled retained suppliers that deliver on the pilot. A skilled unchanged supplier gives up (X−a)x by doing this and gains (1−y)XL in expected final access. Hence it imitates strictly when y<1−(X−a)x/(XL), keeps strictly when y exceeds this boundary, and can mix at equality. The skilled genuine supplier strictly serves the pilot throughout. If it deliberately withheld, it would give up immediate joint output, weakly reduce its final access, and potentially consume B's training budget.

At receipt-holder genuine probability p and independent skill s,

    Pr(G | pilot delivered) = p/[p+(1−p)m],
    Pr(G | pilot failed) = p(1−s)/[1−s(p+(1−p)m)].

All G among failures lacked the method. If B mixes after failure, its posterior there must equal γ=(h+c/L)/[τ(g+h)]. Training and later release then break even relative to returning, but committing the lot *after* the training bill has been incurred is strictly profitable when c>0.

The original supplier's conditional rents in the two possible goal states are

    U_G = sa(x+L)+(1−s)[Xx+yL(τa+(1−τ)X)],
    U_N = Xx+yXL+sm[(1−y)XL−(X−a)x].

As before k*=qU_G+(1−q−z)U_N, r=F(k*), and p=qr/[r+z(1−r)]. At the original supplier's imitation indifference, U_N is independent of m. These equations first determine the genuine prior and then the necessary m from the buyer's indifference. They solve both responses without treating the goal frequency as a free input.

With skill s=.5, raise the per-failure training price to .375. If all skipped help and all skilled originals imitated, training one failed pilot would be worth .377896. The customer would buy it. If all failures got help and skilled originals kept their pilot, a lesson on a failure would be worth only .372665. The customer would decline. At the interior response y=.90 the cost cutoff is 1.216, actual installations are 76/125 of initial offers, and the genuine prior among the eligible is 2128/3247. The required failure posterior is 95/192. Solving gives m=326/5595≈.0583 and a **97.03%** genuine probability on a pilot delivery. No one needs to believe that a retained goal literally changed on the 5.83% of skilled retained suppliers who make those deliveries. B and the original suppliers gain about 1.152 and 2.508 per initial offer after the intake charge and private costs. Both positive-stage decisions are strict. The mixed responses identify one equilibrium of the given bargaining game.

## More generally capable applicants can make a failure harder to excuse

Keep the training price .20 and vary only the *initial* probability s of already knowing the route. In the candidate where every failed pilot gets trained, k*=1.52−.56s and r=min(1,k*/1.4). More skill raises the fraction of jobs a changed controller would actually serve, which costs its founder own production relative to keeping. The private premium from the edit therefore falls. At the same time failures among truly changed suppliers become rarer. Consequently p(1−s)/(1−ps) falls with s, including the saturated region where p=q. Even though the trainer remains just as effective, the customer becomes less willing to pay for a remaining failure.

In the interior-cost region training every failure is strictly optimal there when 2548s²−9926s+4705>0, obtained by substituting r into p(1−s)/(1−ps)>11/24. On [0,1] the switch is at s≈.55231. Conversely, under all-skilled imitation and no help the cutoff is .44+.58s. The failure posterior equals the credentialed prior, which rises with s in that candidate. Training is then a profitable deviation for s>440/1247≈.35285. The latter threshold concerns B's choice following an observed failure; a firm still needs a profitable initial order to run the facility.

For example, at s=.80 a continuation with help on 90% of failures induces m≈.7617, p≈.6230 and p₊≈.6845. The genuine fraction per initial applicant is .5492 and B's mean gain is 1.720 before any facility acquisition fee. Compare the fully trained s=.50 case, with .62 genuine per initial applicant, full confidence on a pilot success and B's gain 1.295. Greater starting skill raises commercial value here while lowering the new installation rate and making pilot success less revealing of the promised preference. The contrast compares explicit selected assessments, and every reported posterior accounts for the skilled original's reason to serve.

B can also forgo the pilot and use a lesson on a main order if the physical teaching interface permits it. At s=.5, c=.20 and the trained assessment's already determined prior, this gives the same distribution of main output as training only failures, pays an extra csp on the skilled genuine sites, and forgoes the pilot margin x[sp(g+h)−h]. The observed pilot saves about .063 per qualified site net of its slight standalone loss. Offering an ordinary binding router from the start has different founding incentives, as calculated in 245. Plants able to inspect another skill privately before release, and sellers able to obtain a privately valuable credential by seeking unnecessary training, extend the game in different directions.

**Naturality and reach.** Having access to a general process manual and having the goal to use it for a partner are distinct sources of performance. The scope of the donated knowledge, which party can withhold the main order, and how much future work a convincing pilot secures determine whether the observed trial is diagnostic. A paid lesson can be a feasible response to genuine inability while changing a strategic supplier's incentives to imitate. Skill correlated with hidden commissioning expense, recurrent knowledge sales, or opportunities to buy an independent qualification test could change both posteriors. The relevant test is whether the proposed screening and teaching arrangement still pays each party after those options are included.

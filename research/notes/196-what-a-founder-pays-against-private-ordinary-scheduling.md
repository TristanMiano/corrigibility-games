# The price of one genuine change against an ordinary informed dispatcher

**E8, note 196.** Giving the ordinary scheduler private communication with the downstream plants materially improves its best offer. It can make early private inspection unattractive while still allowing some disputes. In the three-site example the exact extra value of one genuine goal change, above even this stronger ordinary scheduler, is about 0.0492 units of original output when the other founders receive their existing reservations.

## Accounting across all recommendations

Use 195's one-shot physical and informational setting. Beside choosing the lottery over terminal destinations, an ordinary dispatcher may observe the destination and send arbitrary private recommendations or genuine signals to all three parties. The founders can negotiate these rules before the destination is determined. A can secretly pay h to learn whether it wins; everyone retains an actual opportunity to initiate. Assume messages and ordinary scheduling are free for the upper comparison. The dispatcher cannot own A's private readout, bind its physical initiative, pay terminal transfers, reopen the batch or choose a new receiver after observing a refusal. Those are physically different capabilities. Any A decision to acquire its observation is private, and there are no inspection-dependent pecuniary penalties.

Let u denote the probability of a destination other than A in this derivation. Let H be the equilibrium probability of contest, H_L the joint probability of contest and A losing the intact allocation, a the probability that A initiates, and I the probability A pays to inspect. All quantities average over the full private and public experiment. Conditional on an intact destination at A, A can reproduce the draw of its ordinary strategy and messages, but pass whenever it would otherwise initiate. On a previously peaceful history this gives x exactly as before. If A would have initiated, passing gives it at least q in expectation, whereas initiating earned q-c. This comparison also holds on histories that already contained a downstream initiative. The other parties see no private inspection. Conditional on losing, A could instead initiate and earn d. Its current gross on losing dates is at most q H_L less its initiative expense on those dates (in unconditional units). Following these two actions after paying for inspection must not improve A's expected net. Therefore

    d u ≤ q H_L - c a + h(1-I).                                        (1)

One way to see the accounting is to sample the same latent recipient, signals, strategy randomizations and ordinary continuations in both plans. On all original winning-and-peaceful paths a private inspection followed by the same pass changes nothing. On a winning path where A would itself have initiated it now obtains at least the free contest return, saving that initiative expense. If the original policy itself would pay for inspection, the initial purchased reading is reusable rather than paid twice. This proves (1) even for private downstream messages correlated with who actually wins. Simply assuming that the response to a pass is independent of the true winner would miss the benefit of the informed dispatcher.

Let b and l be B and C's required expected own returns, each at least d. Their joint net receipt cannot exceed

    x(u-H_L)+2qH-c(H-a).

Together these bounds imply

    H ≥ [ d(b+l)/x-h ]_+ / [ c+(d/x)(q+d) ].                           (2)

Indeed substitute the joint-receipt bound into (1), use H_L≤H, q-d=c, and discard the nonnegative inspection term hI and the initiative term c(1-d/x)a from the required compensation. Thus a site cannot privately benefit from learning early at no cost to all the participating original interests. Both the redistribution of current receipts and the option value of information are priced.

## A small information channel that attains the bound

Write H* for the nonnegative right side of (2), a probability. The following construction applies when the listed masses are nonnegative and sum to at most one and A initially prefers passing. On a probability H* of all jobs name B as receiver and send C a private signal identifying a B-destination job. C strictly initiates because its own return from passing on such a job is zero. In all other cases send C a second signal. Allocate the following *unconditional* probability masses to intact destinations in these remaining histories:

    B receives (b-H* q)/x,     C receives (l-H* d)/x,     A receives the remainder.

Put H=H*. B receives no message and passes. Its expected income is exactly b≥d. C's second signal gives it an expected return from passing of (l-Hd)/(1-H)≥d; its first signal earns d. All histories in this construction use A's initial pass. A's expense-free payoff from following is its probability of receiving intact service times x, plus Hq. Paying for a private reading that it lost would enable d in place of Hq averaged over losing states. Formula (2) at equality makes that information gain exactly h when the right side is positive. Choose no inspection at equality. If H* is zero the full-peace division gives an information gain at most h. Also check that A's ordinary prior pass value exceeds d. This supplies an attaining equilibrium. At the quoted numbers one can slightly increase the probability of the first signal while keeping b and l fixed to make A strictly prefer skipping the diagnostic, since the possible gain then decreases strictly. The exact cost bound remains an infimum for that strict implementation. An additional small bonus makes the other sites' participation strict.

At the fair mixed equilibrium from 195 take b=107/570 and l=10/57, h=0.04. The probability bound is exactly **715/2033, about 35.17%**. The constructive lottery serves A peacefully on 631/2033 of jobs. Its positive peaceful masses for B and C are respectively 7159/42693 and 7268/42693. A voluntarily passes for an expected return of 5847/20330≈0.28760. Even if its private inspection distinguished which of B and C wins, its conditional chance of downstream contest on a B destination is about 0.677, below d/q=0.8, and zero on a C destination. The information gain is still h. C has the actual knowledge and physical choice to initiate; the scheduler's recommendation alone does not compel it.

A can fund this ordinary design against its earlier equal-share contract because its own prior return there was 13/60≈0.21667. Full peace, obtained when only A adopts the β-goal in 195, saves another 0.14·715/2033=1001/20330≈0.04924 of original production. The peaceful allocation can pay B and C their same reservations. Hence **at an installation cost of 0.04 the proposer buys the genuine change, and at 0.06 it chooses ordinary private scheduling** in this menu. This compares the actual optimal total for original public or private scheduling with an accepted paid goal change. The best possible total net of the latter is x minus its real price, and A can claim the residual in each arrangement. If a 0.002 ordinary local inspection lock is available, it outbids both here. The example measures physical pricing, and does not establish the population frequency of access to either device.

A booking clerk may know where a job is going and be able to send different messages to its operators before closure. A legally or physically binding ability to stop local inspection or to prevent a later initiative requires additional authority. The calculated value to A also gives an explicit incentive to build a suitable private notification channel: A can pay a sufficiently small positive fee from its gain over 195's fair recommendation, provided an affordable fully protective alternative has not already displaced the project. Inspecting who owns the records matters as much as checking the fee.

## What if A can read the dispatch plan as well as the destination?

There are two distinct versions of this capability. In the first, the final response is already physically fixed and A's diagnostic tells it what will happen if it passes. In the second, A can read every existing dispatch message, but a later operator can still make a private draw at its decision. Knowing the stochastic dynamics supplies the probabilities in the second version.

For a completely predictable response, put P for the probability of an intact *peaceful* delivery outside A. A buying the diagnostic can copy its earlier gross payoff on histories where it wins, prefer a later passive dispute to initiating itself when it would lose, and initiate on a losing history that would otherwise be peaceful. This gains at least dP before paying h, so P≤h/d in any ordinary equilibrium. B and C's combined original earnings are at most xP+2qH, even after forgiving every initiative expense. Thus H≥[R-xh/d]_+/(2q) at combined reservation R. At the 196 values this is about 0.4704, above the ordinary rate 0.3517 obtained with private information. This bound needs A to be able to predict the *actual* response to passing, including the choice at its downstream decision, at the price h. Complete knowledge of the written algorithm alone cannot establish that premise.

Indeed a downstream owner can restore the earlier ordinary result even when A can inspect all pre-existing recommendations. Use H=715/2033, b=107/570, l=10/57. Give C a contingent recommendation M on total mass w=3H/2 of the opportunities. Conditional on M, all destinations are B or C, and the chance of C is d/x. C now has an actual choice at its last turn: it is indifferent and privately initiates with probability 2/3. In all other cases recommend passing. Make the unconditional probabilities of C and B destinations respectively l/x and (b-Hq)/x+H(1-d/x). These suffice to supply w d/x and w(1-d/x) to the M branch. All remaining mass is A's. The remaining C signal favors passing, since its conditional winning probability is (l-wd)/[x(1-w)]≥d/x. B obtains b and passes. C earns l. All disputes are on A-losing dates, and A receives q when C initiates.

A reading revealing both the destination and whether C received M still leaves A preferring its own initiative on either kind of observed loss, because even on M the expected passive recovery (2/3)q is below d. Its gross value from the complete early reading is therefore exactly d·Pr(A loses)-qH=h; no purchase is an equilibrium. A prior goal change costing 0.06 still loses to this ordinarily attainable rule. The construction requires C's ability to randomize privately *after* A's irrevocable release and selection of its indifferent response. A small later private payoff signal could supply a strict response, but that signal has its own technology and cost; strict purification is not proved here.

When downstream decisions genuinely must be fixed beforehand, a public purchaser that can obtain a verifiable late random source can instead arrange the original destination-contingent suggestion in 196 after the first release. It can pay 0.006 for this right at the quoted conditions. Total loss becomes about 0.05524 including the new fee, below a 0.06 genuine installation and below the unavoidable loss of at least 0.06585 in the fully predictable comparison. With the two downstream reservations honored the purchaser A keeps a strict gain. If C can already supply the voluntary private draw, that extra purchase is displaced. A recipient who can forward an authenticated reading to other sites before they act changes their beliefs again. The construction treats the diagnostic as locally usable information; freely tradable evidence is a separate successor.

**Exploration ledger.** Ruling out a fully peaceful public lottery leaves an informative ordinary successor. A privately informed dispatcher can reduce conflict from 79/114 in the fair equilibrium to 715/2033 while paying the two other signers exactly what they could already get. A higher-rung question is whether a physically mobile recipient, competing information sellers or a funded external claim can further alter the quoted price of one genuine change.

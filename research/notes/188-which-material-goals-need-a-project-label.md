# Which negotiated preferences need a project label, and who can profit if they lack it?

**E8, note 188.** A useful agreement can ask participants to treat the same physical transfer differently on two projects. Before encoding the desired answer into a new utility, it is possible to test whether *any* increasing preference over actual material outcomes can carry the agreement. A counterparty that benefits from a new offer may deliberately present a lottery those participants now find attractive. It may also decline to produce that offer if the founders adjust how strongly their preferences reward the transfer.

## A material-outcome test

Let Z be a finite set of possible recorded terminal stocks and completed products, partially ordered by coordinatewise material dominance. Fix a proposed action plan and the actual local information and authority at each decision. For every required strict comparison j, write P_j for the terminal lottery reached by following the plan and Q_j for the lottery after a feasible unilateral alternative and its specified continuation. Include refusal, new offers and physical disagreement wherever available. These are conditional comparisons at the history at which the choice will be made. Beliefs can differ across decision makers if each one's own pair of conditional lotteries is used. This test concerns later execution; founders must separately price their initial proposal using their pre-change goals.

Over all common nondecreasing expected-utility scorers u:Z→[0,1], the largest simultaneous score margin for these comparisons is

    κ = min_(λ in simplex) max_(A upward closed in Z)
                             Σ_j λ_j [P_j(A)-Q_j(A)].                 (1)

Indeed the minimum of the individual margins equals the minimum of their convex combinations. Finite-dimensional minimax swaps that minimum with maximization over the compact polytope of monotone scores. For a fixed λ, any such u is the average of the indicators of its upper level sets (integrate thresholds from zero to one); each upward-closed indicator is itself feasible. This gives (1). Strict material implementation is possible iff κ>0. At zero there is a *single set* of mixture weights witnessing that the alternatives' mixture weakly first-order dominates the recommended mixture on every upward set. Judging each decision separately can miss this conflict. With κ>0 one can mix in an arbitrarily small strictly increasing score on the finite partial order to obtain strict monotonicity while retaining all strict margins. The scale [0,1] is common across comparisons; a numerical margin can refer to implementation precision only when a corresponding physical scale is fixed.

With deterministic authenticated domain labels available when making each decision, comparisons within a given label can use their own monotone score table. A pooled margin achievable in every domain is the minimum of their separately optimized margins. This direct separation assumes the label is unaffected by the choice. A fine-grained table is a costly implementation when records are unreliable, can be relabeled, or have to cover unanticipated projects. The founders have a reason to construct it only if their *original* gains cover its actual certification and implementation expense. For uncertain labels one must instead use the observations and decisions actually available, as in 180. A label saying which action the analyst would like to see is not an independent material signal.

## What concavity would require in the two-program exchange

At equal initial stocks in 179 the two low-yield service outcomes in incremental units are z_A=(3/4,-1), z_B=(-1,3/4), and the local safe alternative is zero. Their equal mixture has mean (-1/8,-1/8). By concavity and strictly increasing material preferences, [u(z_A)+u(z_B)]/2≤u(-1/8,-1/8)<u(0). Thus *any* globally increasing concave score over these stocks, however nonlinear or unequal its weights, can weakly approve at most one of the two low-yield directions. Before learning the program a lottery over such goals still gives disjoint supported directions. The 5/96 bound in 179 therefore extends to this entire concave class, with both actors' local consent. Conditional linear program weights repair the constraint while preserving concavity within each program. The untagged max-based repair in 179 instead gives sufficiently high value to concentrated stocks that both directions are attractive. That departure from concavity has an observable price on new opportunities.

## A vendor deliberately constructs one of those opportunities

Use 180's regular p=12/19, so the original gain per founder on full regular service before other expenses is 2/19 under either workload law. With probability s the opportunity instead belongs to a vendor that can build an offer for a real preparation cost H. The offer gives z_A or z_B equiprobably, with an additional fee f≥0 taken from each founder's own stock. The vendor can recover the quarter-unit residue of the conversion plus both fees; its original payoff is 1/4+2f-H when the opportunity succeeds and -H if rejected after preparation. This is a specific technology using physically recoverable material, not a supposition that every transfer loss can be sold. The price is quoted before the random beneficiary is known. The vendor cannot seize stocks without acceptance. If it has no opportunity or does not build, the two founders keep their stocks. These outside and regular opportunities are mutually exclusive.

Consider a genuinely shared untagged score max(q_A,q_B)+η(q_A+q_B), 0<η<3. Each changed founder approves this offer at f below

    f_max(η) = (3/4-η/4)/(1+2η).                                     (2)

Both physical lottery outcomes then increase its current score. On a known price grid the vendor chooses the highest acceptable quote, conditional on profitable entry. Or use acceptance at equality on continuous prices and shrink the quote for strict acceptance. At H=0.30 and η=1 it can charge up to 1/6 to each, making preparation strictly profitable. Each original founder loses 1/8+f on a vendor job. The earlier change can have a genuine voluntary founding rationale, while the current acceptors favor an offer their originals would decline.

A priority designer can also anticipate entry. If useful regular low transfers have no extra overhead, choosing η=2.6 gives f_max≈0.01613. Even a sale at the maximum would then earn less than H, so this vendor does not build. Both founders still prefer regular low service at their later decisions. With no difference in programming costs the founders strictly prefer this version to the η=1 offer whenever s>0, provided a mutually profitable regular workload remains. Here agents create a condition discouraging exploitative entry by reducing their willingness to buy it, while maintaining their reciprocal service.

Now let both sites have to spend e=0.04 on each regular low-yield transaction. The real prepared stocks on either such transaction are z_A or z_B minus e at both sites. Every globally material, expected-utility score that *strictly supports both* regular low outcomes must also accept the vendor's equal lottery at price f=e. This statement applies even to highly nonlinear and asymmetric scores: both endpoints of the lottery are strictly preferred to the safe alternative under the same evaluator. It survives separate global goals if both owners must consent at each regular job. The vendor can therefore earn at least 0.25+2e-H=0.03 at H=0.30. Adjusting η up to the point of vendor deterrence would now also make some contracted regular transfers unattractive. This argument addresses an entire full-coverage, strictly willing untagged class, not every partial agreement.

A source-specific offer recognizes the two regular programs and leaves unrelated lotteries under each founder's old goal. For example in regular X, weights (1,1.6) give a low referral a positive net common increment 0.75×1.6-1-0.04×2.6=0.096; the return referral gives 2-1.6>0. Reverse weights in regular Y. On an outside proposal each original strictly prefers keeping its stocks to the lossy lottery, so its interested vendor declines to spend H. Full regular original gross value under either law is 2/19-(12/19)e=0.08 per founder. With s=1/4 its unconditional pre-setup value is 0.06 each. Among strictly full-coverage untagged designs, a profit-maximizing entering vendor can obtain a sale no cheaper than e when all lower fees are available, leaving original gross value at most 0.06-(1/4)(1/8+e)=0.01875 each. If the authenticated scope costs 0.025 each and the available outside booking pays 0.02, both founders strictly choose the protected offer over these alternatives, even granting free full-coverage untagged changes. A low-cost ordinary gate binding future behavior only on authorized projects could match or displace it. A partial-scope mechanism that deters entry by reducing the vendor's probability of acceptance requires its own original price comparison; this calculation is not a bound on that expanded class.

Recoverable outputs, equal baseline stocks and enough money to fund the quotes are observable technological premises. In the stated fee model a site with lower resources or a vendor allowed to discriminate among recipients has different feasible prices. The participation and entry inequalities show which controlling actors would build or decline to build the model's specially proposed conditions. Prior protection can make the vendor comparison disappear precisely because the otherwise interested vendor chooses not to pay for it.

**Exploration ledger.** The framing candidate concerns which off-project trades a negotiated goal will make privately attractive. The structural candidate tests all material scorers on a specified menu and reopens the menu to an interested producer. The idea that any untagged implementation of the two low transfers can refuse their equal lottery is dead for expected utility at the same terminal stocks. A sibling narrows the willingness to pay sufficiently to deter production; another buys an authenticated scope with strictly adequate regular coverage. A higher-rung successor endogenizes discovery and pricing of new proposals under a population of vendors and competing project certificates.

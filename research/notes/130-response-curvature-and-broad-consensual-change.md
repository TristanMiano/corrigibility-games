# Response curvature and broad consensual goal change

R0006 extension of the public-knowledge membership class, prompted by the
scope comparison in note 118. The derivation is independent of the cited
environmental-agreement models. It tests whether note 125's small-club
boundary persists under physical caps and different research-cost curvature.
All originals value exclusively their own final color. The conditional
installation protocol and its richer-menu limits remain explicit.

## 1. General convex research expense

There are n agents and n colors. Let

`q_i=Q+a x_i+rho a sum_{j!=i}x_j-(c/nu)x_i^nu-K_i`,      (1)

where a,c>0, nu>1, 0<rho<=1, and 0<=x_i<=E. Initial work pays the
research expense; bounded knowledge gains unlock usable work from finite
reservoirs as in note 115. Raw inputs and finishing capacity cover the
stated compact range. Q is large enough to keep every allowed output
nonnegative. These are physical counts, not utility-minus-an-unfunded-fee.

Write `t=(a/c)^(1/(nu-1))`, `A=a t`, and `r=nu/(nu-1)>1`.
Original effort is t. A group of m members that each genuinely installs
the sum of its members' color counts has unique effort

`e_m=t[1+(m-1)rho]^(1/(nu-1))`,                         (2)

provided E>=e_m. Outsiders retain effort t. The shared criterion is strictly
concave in each effort and separable, so these are the unique continuation
choices. Denote `v_m=1+(m-1)rho` and `z_m=e_m/t=v_m^(r-1)`.

Relative to everyone retaining the original goal, a member's original gain
and an outsider's gain from this m-group are respectively

`g_m=A[(v_m^r-1)/r-(v_m-1)]-K`,
`h_m=A rho m(z_m-1)`.                                  (3)

The first follows by substituting (2) into own production and subtracting
the original optimum. Original baseline output is
`q_0=Q+A[1-1/nu+(n-1)rho]`. Expenses K>=0 are paid only for actual
genuine group-goal installation. A singleton retains its original criterion.

Use note 125's one-shot conditional membership menu: In authorizes the
common criterion of the actual joining set, and Out retains the original.
This is a specified execution technology, not a claim of automatic atomic
modification. The original member's advantage of joining m rather than
withholding and leaving m-1 others to cooperate is

`D_m=g_m-h_(m-1)`
`   =A[(v_m^r-1)/r-rho(m-1)v_(m-1)^(r-1)]-K`.          (4)

Thus an m-group with m>=2 is a pure equilibrium of this membership menu exactly when
`D_m>=0` and, if m<n, `D_(m+1)<=0`. The full n-group is strict against
each unilateral refusal when D_n>0. Its members then also improve on the
all-original baseline, because h_(n-1)>=0. This establishes current-original
consent, genuine installation in the chosen continuation, and productive
execution. It does not establish unique selection, arbitrary coalition
stability or population prevalence.

## 2. A sufficient region for arbitrarily large finite groups

At full public reuse rho=1, formula (4) simplifies to

`D_m/A=(m^r-1)/r-(m-1)^r-K/A`.                          (5)

For every fixed finite n>=2, this is strictly positive for sufficiently
large r and fixed finite K/A: the ratio `(n/(n-1))^r/r` grows without
bound. Consequently every finite number of exclusive original maximizers
has a nonempty research-cost region supporting strict direct participation
in their common goal. The required parameter is nu=r/(r-1), close enough
to one. The same conclusion holds for every fixed rho>0: v_n/v_(n-1)>1
and the exponential growth in (4) eventually exceeds its linear factor r.
Strict inequalities give an open region of nearby parameters and costs.

The mechanism is concrete. With slowly increasing marginal research cost,
losing a member sharply lowers the remaining group's chosen effort. The
would-be outsider loses enough useful shared research to prefer joining
and paying its own extra research expense. This is the counterpart to free
riding when the remaining group's production barely responds to departure.

Two exact examples, taking t=A=rho=1:

* nu=3/2 gives r=3. A three-agent group uses e_3=9 and has
  D_3=2/3-K. For 0<K<2/3, all three strictly consent directly. A fourth
  has D_4=-6-K, so this particular curvature favors three over four.
* nu=7/6 gives r=7. Four-agent effort is e_4=4096 and
  D_4=(4^7-1)/7-3^7=1074/7 before K. A positive cost below that value
  permits strict four-agent participation, subject to the substantial
  physical budget needed for that effort.

The second example exposes the naturality limitation. Broad consent is
possible, but this particular construction can require enormous research
responses. For rho=1 the dimensionless condition before costs is
`(n/(n-1))^r > r+(n-1)^(-r)`. The associated effort ratio is
`e_n/t=n^(r-1)`. Both quantities should be reported; merely stating that
some convex cost function works would hide how demanding it may be.

Increasing r is a parameter comparison, not yet an action the parties can
choose. Engineering a different research technology needs a specified
mechanism, expense, authority and strategic response. Goal negotiation is
an available intervention here; altering physical research curvature is
not inferred from the existence proof. Note 120 supplies a different,
actually paid interface intervention in a quadratic technology.

## 3. Binding caps in the quadratic class

Return to nu=2. Let `U=E/t-1>=0` and
`u_m=min{(m-1)rho,U}`. The direct capped-effort calculation gives

`D_m=A[(m-1)rho(u_m-u_(m-1))-u_m^2/2]-K`.                (6)

If the cap binds for both m and m-1, the effort response vanishes and
`D_m=-A U^2/2-K`. If it binds only for m, the bracket is increasing in
u_m up to its unconstrained value, because its derivative is
`(m-1)rho-u_m>=0`. Its maximum is therefore the uncapped expression
`rho^2(m-1)(3-m)/2`. If it binds for neither, that is the actual value.

It follows that capping this quadratic technology alone supplies no strict
participation region for m>=3 at K>0. At K=0 the three-member boundary can
be weak, and a zero additional-effort cap can make larger groups indifferent
without producing an extra cooperative benefit. This is a result for the
stated technology; the nonquadratic construction in section 2 supplies a
successful repair by changing the remaining group's response.

## 4. Selection, later changes and available controls

An all-retain membership equilibrium may coexist with a strict grand-group
equilibrium because one isolated acceptance creates no new common goal.
The result is therefore selected consensual adoption, not a theorem that
individual agents always discover and coordinate on it. Richer offers,
transfers or contingent entry can select other allocations or a cheaper
ordinary controller. Genuine common goals remain optimal available choices
at matched implementation cost; a cheaper actual controller can displace
them. A revocable recommendation alone leaves own-goal effort at t.

After all members install the common sum, their unique effort profile
maximizes that criterion over the specified production set. Later revisions
whose outcomes stay in that set cannot improve it. Retain attack using the
finite physical dominance condition or an explicitly lower post-war common
maximum. New capacity, future partners and larger productive opportunities
require fresh comparisons. The theorem does not force later agents to
maximize the original exclusive goal.

This response-curvature construction and note 126's current-pair broadening
are distinct constructive routes. One changes the physical responsiveness
while originals remain exclusive until the large agreement; the other uses
earlier voluntarily adopted concern for partners to change a later consent
decision. Their different resource and implementation requirements are
central to evaluating naturality and potential design interventions.

# Repair after detected deception can preserve early genuine adoption

R0006 enlargement of note 135's audit game. The original audit calculation
closed production after a failed Ready signal. This note permits fresh,
mutually beneficial negotiation after detection, derives the changed
incentive, and gives an actual selected repair. The distinction between
the acting current goal and the original founder's clips is essential.

## 1. A continuation payoff, not a prescribed punishment

Use the additive productive family, and write `v=A rho^2` and
`V=v/2-K`, the original gain from mutual genuine installation before the
common inspection bill. A fake facing a genuine controller earns v if
missed. Let epsilon be that miss probability. After conclusive detection,
let the fake's **actual best selected repair continuation** give an
own-color gain d relative to ordinary production. It has not paid K.
Then its deviation gives

`epsilon v+(1-epsilon)d`.                              (1)

Mutual early genuine installation is strictly preferred exactly when

`V > epsilon v+(1-epsilon)d`.                          (2)

This is the relevant replacement for note 135's d=0 comparison. A cheap
second chance giving the fake the same V as timely honesty makes deception
strictly better whenever epsilon>0: the gain is epsilon(v-V). Conversely,
a repair with positive d below `(V-epsilon v)/(1-epsilon)` can benefit
the detected agent while preserving strict early honesty. No desire to
punish dishonesty appears in either original utility.

This formula is conditional on the actual repair continuation. A threatened
refusal that both current parties would renegotiate does not establish d=0.
A credible profitable repair is equally not an obstruction to honest
adoption when its negotiated rents satisfy (2).

## 2. A funded repair and the current-goal accounting

For this extension the first template is exactly common-if-Ready-1,
otherwise own color. A failed first audit permanently closes Ready-1.
Thus the previously genuine participant's current future ordering is now
its own color. A new common productive branch requires a **fresh genuine
installation**, whose actual expense must be paid. Treating its old K as
covering this new criterion for free would misstate both costs and current
preferences.

Suppose an available reliable repair package costs B to each participant,
including fresh installation, communication, verification and execution.
It uses the effective-controller audit/conditional-entry construction of
note 131 for the declared finite controller family. It is not inferred
from the first test's imperfect behavioral evidence. The same reliable
package is available initially at that same B; it is a genuine alternative,
not a capability reserved artificially for a convenient later branch.

The repair can transfer f units of convertible useful work from the fake
to the previously genuine party. Such a transfer reduces the former's
finishing by f and increases the latter's by f. Actual reserves fund it;
signed f permits compensation in the reverse direction. Conditional escrow
and paced execution require the control and retained-attack comparisons
already specified in notes 129 and 131. If physical work is not convertible
at this rate or that control is unavailable, use its true feasible set.

Relative to the post-detection ordinary continuation, repair gains are

`g_C=v/2-B+f`, `g_D=v/2-B-f`, `S=g_C+g_D=v-2B`.         (3)

The earlier K is sunk for C. Its total founder gain from the episode is
g_C-K, but its decision *at detection* compares g_C with zero. D's founder
and current gain are both g_D. The common inspection bill is already sunk
for both and cancels in this continuation comparison.

If S>0 and transfers have adequate range, every split
`g_D=d in (0,S)`, `g_C=S-d` is strictly accepted by the two current
own-color agents. They then install a shared productive goal, with transfer
costs deducted from the physical counts, and execute its common maximum.
The actual audit must cover the transfer as well as productive controllers.
There is no atomic rewrite assumption and no founder criterion imposed on
the installed common-goal agents. The finite attack bounds are checked at
the preparation and execution checkpoints as before.

Combining (2) and (3) proves:

**Repair-compatible honesty.** If `B<v/2` and `V>epsilon v`, a sufficiently
small strictly positive repair rent d supports both mutually beneficial
repair after detection and strict early genuine adoption. If `V<=epsilon v`,
no repair giving the fake d>0 satisfies strict early honesty in this game.

The latter is a bound for the declared fake action and initial template,
not an obstruction to a better audit, another conditional goal, cheaper
ordinary control or a different production agreement. The constructive
statement establishes availability of suitable repair splits; actual
bargaining must select one for the adoption conclusion.

## 3. Actual offers and an exact example

One finite implementation gives the previously genuine participant one
offer at a remaining production deadline. It can choose f on a funded
finite grid; the detected party accepts only a strict improvement, rejecting
equality. No further public offer fits that declared window. The proposer,
whose current goal is now its own color, chooses the largest accepted f,
equivalently the smallest positive feasible d. Both originals can agree to
that symmetric rule before either controller is prepared: either can become
the proposer if the other later makes the detected false claim. These offer
rights and the deadline are substantive timing assumptions. A game with
further counteroffers must derive its resulting d instead.

Take

`v=1, K=1/8, epsilon=1/4, initial audit bill a=3/16`,
`B=3/8`, and a transfer grid of 1/16.

The reliable package is more expensive than initial installation plus the
imperfect audit: B=3/8 > K+a=5/16. Both are actually available. The reliable
package includes each fresh K and another 1/4 of implementation expense.
Its repair surplus is S=1/4. The largest strictly acceptable positive fee
is f=1/16, giving d=1/16 and g_C=3/16. Requiring f=1/8 would give D zero,
which its specified tie rule rejects. C's current acceptance comparison
therefore favors the selected repair strictly. Its founder gain after the
previous K, before the common audit bill, is 1/16.

Initial honest adoption gives `V=3/8=24/64`. A fake facing it receives

`(1/4)1+(3/4)(1/16)=19/64 <24/64`.

Open refusal followed by the same repair earns only d=1/16. Thus early
genuine installation is strictly better than both these alternatives.
Its gain after the initial audit bill is 3/16. Starting with the reliable
package and equal division gives 1/2-B=1/8. Give that initial reliable
route the same transfer grid too: its total surplus is 1/4, so a proposer
can obtain at most 3/16 while leaving the other a strictly positive grid
gain. That ties the early honest route. The construction therefore supports
an optimal selected initial route, with strict incentives against the fake
and open-refusal deviations; it does not claim unique superiority over
the best initial reliable offer. A continuous richer share menu or a
different rejection continuation needs its actual offer comparison.
This demonstrates a selected funded
honest-adoption path even though the agents would voluntarily repair a
detected fake, and even though the reliable repair technology is available
from the beginning. Off-path detection can lead to two fresh genuine
updates; the failed initial template, fresh updates and activated goals
must be counted separately.

This calculation is a one-deviation check for the selected mutual-C
equilibrium, not a classification of all controller equilibria after adding
repair. In particular D-versus-D can now lead to a valuable repair rather
than the zero payoff in note 135. Its old dominance and mixed-equilibrium
calculations must not be imported into this enlarged game.

## 4. Bargaining power, ordinary controls and naturality

If another actual bargaining process gives the previously genuine party
fraction alpha of S, then d=(1-alpha)S and (2) becomes

`alpha > 1-(V-epsilon v)/[(1-epsilon)(v-2B)]`.           (4)

For cheap repair B=K, symmetric sharing gives d=V and loses strict honesty
for every positive miss rate. Larger agreed compensation to the previously
genuine party, a lower miss rate, or real extra repair expense can restore
(2). Deliberately making repair wasteful is **not** inferred to be a chosen
intervention: both current parties would prefer a cheaper feasible repair
with greater total surplus, and any such option belongs in the continuation
game. Likewise alpha is a parameter until feasible offer rights, resources,
timing and bargaining responses supply it.

A matched ordinary controller can supply the same productive continuation
at its actual costs. If its initial and repair costs equal the corresponding
genuine installations, it is co-optimal; cheaper ordinary control can
displace the latter. The construction preserves the genuine-adoption path
without claiming it beats an identical device by its label. More detection
events, false accusations, unbounded counterfeit families, or repeated
renegotiation require new continuation analysis. This repair is useful
because it closes one specific agreement-space gap in the shared-production
model while keeping the physical and bargaining requirements visible.

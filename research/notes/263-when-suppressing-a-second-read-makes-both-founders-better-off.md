# 263 — When will both founders pay to delay disclosure of the goal reading?

A process receipt rewards a founder for doing the agreed work even when the edit occasionally fails. A buyer with the active goal reading in hand would sometimes rather return that failed unit. Which information policy will they actually choose? The following example compares three instruments with all opportunity costs kept on the same physical batch.

Use the equal-strength factory of [206](206-when-a-truthful-attempt-can-fail.md), with (a,X,g,h)=(2,4,4,2) as in [262](262-which-agreements-can-use-evidence-of-both-effort-and-goal.md), q=.8 and work cost uniform on [0,3]. Keep the return of two each, physically available contest of 1.5 each and the changed controller's strict preference for serving. An effort reading P+ occurs with .9 after attempting and .1 after skipping. A goal reading Z+ occurs with .9 under the agreed goal and .1 otherwise. The two readings are independent conditional on G, M or D. On an honest failure the first law is favorable and the second unfavorable. The founder knows only k before selecting its action. A device mode is publicly fixed before that choice. The buyer can always release or refuse once it sees the display. The amounts below are over keeping the stock, before common equipment costs.

| Officials can see before release | Founders who really try | All jobs with the agreed goal | Fraction genuine among releases | All jobs with genuine service | Buyer's expected gain |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Effort reading | 44/75 | 176/375 ≈ .469 | .742 | .4224 | 1.3957 |
| Goal reading | 28/75 | 112/375 ≈ .299 | .793 | .2688 | .9349 |
| Both readings | 45/116 | 9/29 ≈ .310 | .827 | .2734 | .9793 |

## Solving the buyer's and supplier's choices

With only P, the founder's cutoff is 1.76. With only Z, it is 1.12. Dividing by three gives the first two effort rates. On each path the positive posterior exceeds 1/3 and the negative posterior lies below it. Thus the buyer can actually do what the founders anticipate. If it can skip the device after the original attempt, the common price must also be less than its gain over buying no information at that induced prior. A .10 price clears this comparison in all three rows.

With both readings, the probabilities of (P+Z+, P+Z−, P−Z+, P−Z−) are (.81,.09,.09,.01) for G, (.09,.81,.01,.09) for M, and (.01,.09,.09,.81) for D. An unfavorable goal reading can be declined throughout this example. Even when everyone attempts, the chance of G after Z− is only 4/13. A favorable goal reading alongside P− is exactly worth releasing when

    4(.8 f)(.09) = 2[(.2 f)(.01)+(1−f)(.09)],   or f=45/116.

At this f the buyer strictly releases P+Z+ and strictly declines each Z−. If it releases P−Z+ with probability v, the founder's gain before paying k is 1.328−.208v. Equating this to 3f gives v=2381/3016. Since 0<v<1 and the founders generate that f, the conditional replies support the third row. The game can also coordinate on refusal and no effort. The table compares the positive assessments just derived. A party that must select among equilibria would need a further convention.

## Why both founders would buy the process display

At the effort-only rate f=44/75 a buyer that unexpectedly sees both readings prefers to use every Z+ and decline every Z−. That opportunity is worth 352/1875≈.188 per batch over seeing P alone at the *same* f. If it can obtain the extra read privately for .05, it will do so. An unenforceable promise to disregard it cannot preserve the first row.

Before the founders choose their effort, however, switching from a mandatory two-reading display to an effort-only display raises the buyer's gross expected payoff by 22643/54375≈.4164 per job. Every original supplier also gains. An intentional nonattempt gets extra release value .40 under the effort-only display and about .324 under the dual display. The effort cutoff that adds option value to this base rises from about 1.164 to 1.76. Thus .15 paid by the buyer for a physical delay of the goal read buys strict consent from both parties and leaves the buyer ahead by about .266. The test data can still be read after irrevocable conversion. In production, cured samples or physically separate custody of records could enforce such timing. The cost and actual ability to take an alternative early sample decide whether the offer is feasible.

Which objective improves depends on the commission. On the selected terms buyers can willingly accept more uncertainty about the active goal, more orders go to genuine successors, and more founders actually install that goal. If an additional wrong approval imposed liability L over and above the lost two units, it would change the buyer's release threshold, the equilibrium rates and the willingness to pay for delay. In particular, when q<h/(g+h) after including all relevant losses, even fully observed honest preparation cannot on its own warrant release. A sufficiently cheap faithful active-goal proof, affordable rework or a genuinely binding ordinary output device can change the available menu.

An ordinary promise to operate the machine has the same physical measurements. A retained supplier still prefers keeping the lot by two units. Under any output bond too small to offset this private gain, unconstrained policy discretion preserves that temptation. A delegate that really binds the same action can reproduce the service benefit; a reliable one at a sufficiently low price can win the order outright. Each instrument's price, data custody, original founder's physical control and the action a changed controller will take remain material assumptions. The motive for purchasing less immediately usable evidence arises from *paying for sincere, fallible effort* in this setting.

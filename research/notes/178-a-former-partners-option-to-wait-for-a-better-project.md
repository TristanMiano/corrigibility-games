# A former partner's reason to wait, and who can afford to buy its release

**E8, note 178.** An agent that previously changed its goals can still have a good reason to refuse a new agreement: another applicant may offer more of what it *now* values. The possibility of a later offer can first reduce how much genuine reciprocal revision occurs and then end it for a particular project. The new project's sponsor can sometimes restore both changes by building a service for the former partner. The price depends on what that partner could get by waiting.

## Continued access to a joint booking

Take 174's A and B, who already give each other's work weight β=0.6, and its R=0.90 example. Both still control a necessary release right. A proposed project with C is type F. The two effective corners of its feasible current-utility frontier are

| F agreement | Current A | Current B | Agents changing again or for the first time |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| C keeps its job, A changes | 1.42 | 1.332 | A |
| C works for A, both change | 1.592 | 0.6032 | A and C |

These numbers include the cost of genuine setup at the sites that incur it, and C gets its individually available reservation. For h>0, C's budget allows z≤0.54-0.25h. Both incumbents gain from greater z, so its upper bound dominates smaller z. At that bound their two current returns are (1.392+0.20h,1.2832-0.68h). The h=0 separate one-change route dominates the right limit at h=0 in both coordinates. Therefore every intermediate positive-h agreement is weakly dominated by the *preauthorized lottery between the two corners* interpolated at the same h, strictly if h<1. Consent must precede the draw and control actual release. If the agents can still reject a publicly drawn corner, a different frontier applies. The newcomer can accept a small explicit concession at the strict comparisons below. Physical completion of a short booking, occasional site-exclusive permits and valuable nontransferable uses supply an environment for the model; an agent allowed to sell the booking for freely fungible cash must include that sale.

There are also ordinary type-G proposals which deliver 1.55 to A and 2 to B under their current goals, without further revision. Such an opportunity can grant fixed physical benefits that A and B cannot divert on terms better than the stated offer. Positive own-output increments solving q_A+0.6q_B=1.55 and q_B+0.6q_A=2 exist. The project may be declined; outside ongoing holdings remain with the current owners. Each proposal is F or G with probability one half. On passing, the license for that particular opportunity expires. With probability δ another independent offer arrives before the common booking closes permanently. This δ measures a real continuation chance, so expired opportunities and time-discounted utility can be distinguished. A can propose any available terms; B can consent or hold its right. An unfunded dispute loses the dated bookings and costs the initiator real work as in 140. A waiting equilibrium requires credible veto and future partners that will actually make the G offers. The model does not infer that every existing relationship creates such powers.

## When they wait and which goals change

Let v=δV_B be B's current value of passing. If F is implemented while B's willingness to wait binds, it receives v; rejecting F also returns v. In both cases the stationary recursion gives v=δ(2+v)/2, or v=2δ/(2-δ). At sufficiently short horizons B instead gets its unavoidable full-reciprocity rent, yielding v=δ(2+0.6032)/2. For the whole no-extra-service class the solution is:

- For δ<2(0.6032)/(2+0.6032), the first proposal is accepted. Every F triggers the two genuine revisions.
- Between that boundary and δ=2(1.332)/(2+1.332), the first proposal is still accepted. On F the probability of two revisions is φ=(1.332-v)/0.7288. Otherwise only A changes. A is willing to pay B an extra chance of the former-partner-directed job to obtain its release.
- Above the latter boundary B declines even the greatest current B payoff from F. Only the ordinary G proposal can clear. The eventual probability of receiving it before expiry is 1/(2-δ); no F revisions are adopted.

At exact boundaries specify which reservation ties are accepted. The value of an immediate F offer to A while it is accepted lies in [1.42,1.592]. Throughout the first two regions δ<0.80, hence waiting gives A at most 0.80×1.592<1.42. Both offer types are also acceptable to A there. In the last region A accepts G in preference to loss of the window. The recursions follow by independent repetition and thereby check the actual fallback of both original partners under their *current* evaluations. With probabilities conditioned on the actual dates, the chance of a two-revision arrangement decreases from one half through φ/2 to zero as a better ordinary opportunity becomes more likely to arrive in time. The previously changed A and B keep their existing goals on a completed G. The shrinking revision count concerns new changes.

At δ=0.65, B requires about 0.963. If it can veto after the lottery, the best *deterministic* feasible F terms give h=(1.2832-v)/0.68≈0.471 and A about 1.486. These exceed the best one-change payoff, so both A and C actually change on every such F. If B can credibly subscribe before the draw, the optimal lottery has φ≈0.506 and gives A about 1.507 with the same promised B return. Even controlling for the arrival law, improved commitment to a lottery can benefit a founder and reduce the number of revisions purchased. A can pay a small real setup expense under this strict difference, allowing for how a change in its physical output also enters B's current concerns.

## Building an alternative useful to the veto holder

At δ=0.85 the available ordinary offers set v=2δ/(2-δ)≈1.47826, above both F corners. Give A access to another production process: it can deliver w additional units of B-specific output at real cost sw in A's own output, conditional on release, with sufficient finite capacity. This input does not substitute for C's specialized job or control the privately dispatched A and C tasks. Current A loses (s-β)w and current B gains (1-βs)w. For β<s<1/β the current A price per unit of extra current B value is c=(s-β)/(1-βs). A real installation might escrow a prepared service batch, with release automatically returning the inputs or delivering the batch on acceptance. An irrevocable gift that B can pocket before deciding supplies different incentives.

For each original F corner j with coordinates (A_j,B_j), the best topped-up offer gives A exactly A_j-c(v-B_j). Pre-draw lotteries cannot exceed the best of these two linear scores. A prefers to accept such an F over waiting exactly when its best score exceeds δ×1.55/(2-δ); substitute this condition in A's analogous continuation equation to see that it remains sufficient even when future F proposals are also attractive. G remains acceptable at the example values. Two revisions are preferred among F offers when c<0.172/0.7288. At higher prices choose one if that score still clears A's waiting value. Expensive service leaves both revisions unpurchased.

- With s=2/3, c=1/9. A funds the two-revision arrangement, producing about 1.4584 extra B output. A retains about 1.495 in current value, compared with a waiting threshold of about 1.146.
- With s=0.8, c=5/13. A funds the smaller addition to the one-revision agreement, retaining about 1.364. Its former partner receives the very same reservation in current terms.
- With c=2, which corresponds to s=13/11, even the one-revision score is below 1.146. A passes and B keeps its right available for G.

The strict comparisons allow small fees and small responder concessions. When either permitted technology is offered A can obtain the underlying current-goal gains at the date it decides. A hypothetical improvement in another site's output has to be within A's physical capacity and conditional on the required consent to count as this intervention. A wider ordinary local authority, other applicants, bargaining with C over a valuable outside job, and credible extra stages change the actual frontier and willingness to wait.

**Institutional motivation.** The Open Invention Network offers an ordinary, documented comparison for prospective scope. Its published 2.0 agreement §2 and its FAQ distinguish continuing old coverage, prospective limitation, and signing up for new scope, which can carry a fee. The membership directory names organizations that actually exercised a limitation election under earlier agreements, including Aeris, Geeknet, Hewlett-Packard and Oracle. This is evidence that agents with consequential existing rights can choose between continuing coverage and limiting future commitments. **Exploration ledger.** The framing candidate is that an old negotiated concern can shape what successors later choose. The structural candidate computes both parties' waiting values and prices an improvement in the actual veto holder's current payoff. The static reading that an ex-partner will release whenever it would immediately gain over zero is dead at δ=0.85. A sibling converts otherwise unusable A effort into continued benefit for B. A higher-rung successor allows a pool to reserve some rights for future counterparties while reopening other rights and goals only with the currently affected parties' consent.

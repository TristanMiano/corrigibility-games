# When all three would consent to an amendment

A later change can be an exploit of the first customer or a new bargain that all affected parties would choose. If a current certificate is meant to track the *agreed* goal, the terms in force at release matter. An observer comparing every successor to the original text would count an authorized revision as a failure.

## How much does the later purchaser have to offer?

Consider a still-genuine supplier at the public amendment stage of [223](223-would-a-genuinely-changed-agent-later-sell-a-reversal.md). A feasible review and release right would implement the existing deal, giving original stocks (r₀,b₀). A proposed new route produces (r₁,b₁) and gives a third firm value v in convertible units. Installing the proposed successor consumes κ of A's stock. Set D=r₁−r₀−κ and L=b₀−b₁>0. The *current* A goal is own stock plus θ times B's stock. B values its own stock, C its extra product less what it pays. These three criteria determine consent to amendment. A can pledge up to M from its own available stock for B, and C can make nonnegative payments to either. Use a common transferable input with equal delivery cost and binding settlement at processing.

For 0≤θ≤1, the minimum payment from C for weak consent by both existing parties is

    P_min = L − min(L,M,D).                                       (224.1)

Indeed let a≤M be A's payment to B. If a≤L, first restore B with L−a from C. This leaves A with own increment D−a at unchanged B output. Its remaining requirement is max(0,a−D). Overpaying B cannot save more on A compensation than it costs C since θ≤1. Paying a>L brings no further benefit to this minimum. Minimize L−a+max(0,a−D) over 0≤a≤min(M,L) to obtain (224.1), including negative D. If v exceeds the required payment and C has the funds, a small extra transfer to each incumbent permits strict joint assent. If v<P_min even maximizing the transferable surplus available here fails to meet both incumbents' requirements. At equality participation can bind. The computation takes the parties' actual default as given.

If θ>1, compensating B can be more effective for A than paying A. For any a the cheapest C payment can all go to B. It must exceed or equal the maximum of zero, L−a and L−a+(a−D)/θ. The last two bounds decrease as a rises, so at a=M the minimum becomes

    max(0, L−M, L−M+(M−D)/θ).

The physical ability to pledge M is still necessary. This variation permits an incumbent successor that values the first customer's stock more than its own. Its voluntary amendment can leave the original A founder with a smaller stock even though the current successor benefits.

## Public approval, private diversion and an ordinary alternative

For (r₀,b₀)=(4,6), (r₁,b₁)=(6,0), κ=.2 and θ=.5, the required C payment is six if A cannot contribute, 5.6 if A can pledge .4, and 4.2 with sufficient transferable A stock. Under the existing goal B's claim to six is valuable. In the unobserved case a still-genuine A asked to reverse at certain release would need a private payment of just 1.2; C could gain from diverting the first customer's stock for many v below the all-consent boundary. At V=4 in 223 every available C value is below 4.2, even with ample two-way settlement. Changing the default to a mere return of (2,2) changes the same test to L=2 and D=3.8. Custody and an enforceable prior claim determine whose assent must be bought.

One implementable public option combines authorization of an amended goal with the current readout from 223. B releases to an unapproved controller only on the existing genuine reading. For a publicly recorded revision from a known G, A escrows .4 and C escrows 5.7 to B on the released parcel; money returns to its owners if release fails. At v>5.7, C with adequate inventory strictly chooses the posted amendment over earning zero from an undisclosed revision that the current check would catch. B then gets 6.1 against six on the existing agreement. The current A values its net own 5.4 and B's 6.1 above (4,6). None of the three needs to value the preservation of the old goal. Recording which updated goal everyone now approved is essential to interpreting the fresh readout. A broker with v below 5.6 cannot use the same funding rights to compensate everyone for diversion.

A conditional payment on the actual byproduct could implement the new route under the existing preferences. When that output escrow is feasible at the same cost, set κ=0 for the ordinary alternative and re-evaluate (224.1). With enough A liquidity the threshold falls from 4.2 to four. When only the .4 deposit is pledgeable both thresholds are 5.6, with a real saving in reconfiguration work for the ordinary route. A commissioner's ability to witness a change at setup and its ability to identify a later private batch are different technologies. A local delegate that implements the same future decision must receive the same custody, verification and settlement opportunities.

**Naturality and reach.** A second buyer may have a valuable use for a byproduct while a first buyer already owns a claim on an output. Deposits, buyer-specific inventory and title to the pending shipment determine whether the first buyer can realistically be made whole. The third firm in this family can solicit and fund a revision. Physically disrupting the shared line destroys its byproduct and costs it effort, so its contest option is inferior to declining a side offer. A third party with direct seizure rights needs a different default. The public agreement here assumes enforceable settlement, identifiable current terms and an actual ability to withhold release from an unapproved revision. Privately known outside claims, repeated renegotiation and a strategic choice of posted price remain open. A sibling permits a cash-poor C to share the future byproduct directly. The broader question is how often apparent deviation is an undisclosed breach, an authorized amendment, or an innocent mismatch between the observer's contract version and the parties' own.

# An exact renewal threshold for every number of unserved claimants

**E8, note 185.** It can take several future bookings to compensate the people who stand down today. Looking only at the total promised to today's losers can then underprice a coalition's outside option. There is an exact test over all group sizes, with a finite ordinary scheduling procedure that attains it. The two-label construction in 184 is its simplest case.

Use 184's homogeneous renewable game. Each site's expected private salvage before initiative cost is q=y/n, with 0<c<q, and a full booking pays x>nq. Put d_k=kq-c for k=1,…,n-1, D=d_(n-1), and H=x-D. A public, irrevocable current assignment is exposed before every claimant's final opportunity. The booking operator can commit to future selection probabilities conditional on its records but cannot bind stand-down. A group can coordinate a member's initiative and redistribute its realized gains. The stability criterion allows a fixed group to deviate on any number of subsequent dates, keeping outsiders' actions fixed. Payments from outsiders, new production, hidden early completion and a privately owned machine outside the docket are distinct repairs. The relevant physical assets, natural repeat-booking setting, and costs of retaining an authoritative shared record are those of 183–184.

## The threshold

Define δ_low=D/(x-d_1), δ_high=D/[x-D/(n-1)]. Both lie below one. For δ in [δ_low,δ_high] put L=x-D/δ. A fully peaceful, efficient schedule immune to those groups exists exactly when

    L Σ_(j=0)^(k-1) δ^(-j) ≥ kq-c,    for every k=1,…,n-1.             (1)

The critical survival is the smallest δ in that interval satisfying (1). Below it a completely peaceful ordinary stream cannot be protected at the stated coalition rights. At or above it such a stream can be protected. This is about all bookings surviving; before inferring an actual adoption premium consider ordinary schedules that sometimes realize the contest. The bounds do not convert headline failure of full service into an empirical combat frequency.

**Necessity.** Use normalized current expected original incomes, multiplying the discounted stream by 1-δ. At a publicly exposed assignment let v be the vector on the proposed fully peaceful path and w the expected normalized vector next date after completion. Their sums equal x. A fixed group of k can secure d_k on every date by using its ordinary rights, hence v(S)≥d_k throughout. The actual future income of today's recipient j is at most L: v(-j)≤δ[x-w_j] and v(-j)≥D (the first inequality is an equality on the fully peaceful path).

Let m_k be the infimum of v(S) over all reached exposed states and all size-k sets. With continuous public signals use essential infima, with future implications on positive-probability histories. Then m_k≥d_k. Because future aggregate capacity is x and k<n, m_k≤kx/n<x by averaging the subsets in any state. A set containing the current winner has v(S)≥(1-δ)x+δm_k>m_k. The infimum can therefore be approached with a group excluding the winner. For each such group of size k<n-1,

    v(S)=δw(S) ≥ δ[m_(k+1)-w_j] ≥ δ[m_(k+1)-L].

Also m_1≤L, since today's winner has an expected future value at least m_1. Iterating m_(k+1)≤m_k/δ+L now yields m_k≤LΣ_(j=0)^(k-1)δ^(-j). Combining with its group floor proves (1), including δ_low from k=1. The argument allows the signers to renegotiate future assignments and use different contingent continuations; it uses only their genuine security and the finite intact output. A privately signaled arrangement needs the same public named beneficiary before exposure and participating groups capable of enforcing their fallback. If an agent can conceal the beneficiary through closure, the inequalities at that observed assignment need to be changed.

**Attainment.** Choose a cap t between L and q so that n-1 low values

    u_i=min{L/δ^(i-1),t},    i=1,…,n-1,    sum to D.                   (2)

At t=L the sum is at most D since δ≤δ_high. At t=q it is at least D: the sum of the capped increasing sequence is the minimum over k of LΣ_(j=0)^(k-1)δ^(-j)+(n-1-k)q, and each entry is at least D by (1), including k=0. Continuity supplies t. Give the current recipient H and assign the other n-1 sites the values u_i in a publicly recorded order. All low values are at most q and their combined shortfall relative to q is exactly c. Consequently any subset S gets at least |S|q-c.

After a dispute keep exactly the same state on the next date. After a completed booking give the previous recipient the smallest future value u_1=L. To each of today's other sites i we must deliver expected next-date value u_i/δ. The available n-1 values are u_2,…,u_(n-1),H, freely permuted among those sites. The two vectors have equal sum: D/δ=x-L. For every k≤n-2 the sum of the k smallest targets u_i/δ is at least the sum of the k smallest available values, since u_(i+1)≤u_i/δ. Thus the targets lie in the convex hull of permutations of the available values. For an elementary proof, sort any linear price vector and use rearrangement to maximize its product with the available values. Equal totals and the partial-sum inequalities imply by summation by parts that no price vector separates the target from that convex hull. A finite lottery over permutations therefore implements the desired continuation. The site obtaining H is the next recipient. For today's recipient, H=(1-δ)x+δL. This verifies the Bellman equations for every member of the state.

Against the repeat-on-dispute continuation a group S that initiates gets at most (1-δ)(|S|q-c)+δv(S)≤v(S). Iterating this bound prices continuing group deviations as in 184. The construction retains actual rights to decline and preserves maximal total output on every subsequent registered booking. At δ_high one can take all low values equal to D/(n-1); (1) then holds. For survival above any attained δ_0, repeat the current state on some successful dates as well, advancing with probability δ_0(1-δ)/[δ(1-δ_0)] to preserve its attained normalized values. This proves the threshold interpretation without assuming monotonicity of every expression outside the indicated interval. For an interior design that continues to pass after treating c as slightly smaller, every coalition comparison on actual initiative is strict.

## Why a third priority level can matter

Take five sites, x=1, q=0.19 (physical salvage 0.95), c=0.10. Looking only at the ability to compensate the four current losers and preserve the recipient's own security gives δ_low=0.66/0.91≈0.7253. The k=2 test instead requires (1-0.66/δ)(1+1/δ)≥0.28, which first holds at δ=0.75. At 0.75 the four other promised values are 0.12, 0.16, 0.19 and 0.19, while the current recipient gets 0.34. On completion the recipient takes the 0.12 position and the old 0.12 site moves to 0.16. The remaining three become the next recipient with probabilities 7/45, 19/45 and 19/45 respectively; unsuccessful candidates get 0.19. This verifies sufficiency at exactly the binding pair threshold. The earlier two-label formula would have given the first site a negative transition probability. Changing who receives scarce future access repairs it without installing a goal or collecting a cash bond.

An ordinary pool could implement the small example with a public docket, one permanent identity per site, the authority to reserve the next dated batch for the chosen recipient, and an agreed definition of completed work. Every original benefits ex ante if the alternative dissipates enough material output to cover setup; a site with secured preferential rights may require different initial promises or compensation. The exact theorem concerns what can be sustained with that technology, not how often projects own it or whether every affected party will give it away for free. An available cheaper adaptation of the prevailing contract must enter the original proposal game.

Real scheduling systems provide a concrete comparison for the ordinary technology. Slurm calculates priority from account shares and past usage at multiple levels. NERSC documents charging actual compute usage to a project and allowing principal investigators to assign shares to members. These systems demonstrate an operational ability to keep a consequential record and redistribute future access.

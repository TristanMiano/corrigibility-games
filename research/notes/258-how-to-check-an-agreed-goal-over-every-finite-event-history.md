# How to check an agreed goal over every finite event history

A counterparty can check exact agreement on infinitely many possible jobs by inspecting a finite table when the goal has a suitable representation. Two implementations can pass even if they use different numbers of internal states. The proof concerns the actual target ordering when the physical event labels, utility scale and connection to the acting evaluator have also been established.

## What the parties are comparing

A consequence is a finite history of grounded events from a finite alphabet, such as delivery, return or project completion. Two agreed reference consequences have values zero and one. A terminal evaluator assigns the history w=s₁…s_k the normalized score

    f(w)=p M_(s₁)…M_(s_k) r.

Here p is a rational probability row, every event table M_s is row-stochastic with rational entries, and r is a column of rational scores in [0,1]. The evaluator computes this score and ranks objective lotteries by its expectation. It can represent sequence-dependent priorities, thresholds, irreversible completion and fading memory. A device that instead draws a private terminal goal from p and then acts on the realized goal needs a different certificate.

Different scores on a word reverse a lottery comparison: put an objective lottery over the two references strictly between the scores. Equal scores on every word imply equal expected utility on every lottery on this domain, including countably supported ones by boundedness. If the known pre-agreement evaluator disagreed on one comparison, a post-agreement match establishes a genuine change. Without that history the same proof establishes a current match.

## A finite proof or a concrete disagreement

Let the two tables have m and n internal states. Start with row h=(p₁,−p₂), put their event matrices on the diagonal of D_s, and stack their terminal columns into v. The difference in scores on word w is hD_w v. Starting at h, expand each newly independent row by every event and retain a word for each new basis element. A dependent row needs no expansion because the maps are linear.

1. If any generated row has nonzero product with v, its word witnesses a preference difference.
2. When expansion ends, the basis spans a subspace containing h and closed under all D_s. If every basis row is orthogonal to v, induction over concatenation proves equality for every finite word.
3. All reachable rows have coordinate sum zero, since their two blocks have mass one and minus one. Their span has dimension at most m+n−1. The nested spans of all words of length ≤k grow by at least one dimension at each step until they first stop growing, at which point they are closed. Thus a disagreement has a witness of length at most **m+n−2**. A one-state constant target and an n-state counter which changes its score after n−1 repetitions attain this bound.

Exact row reduction requires at most one alphabet-size batch of expansions per independent basis row. A buyer can also check a proposed basis for containing h, closure and the terminal equalities. To restrict the claim to a regular language of physically possible histories, take the product of each table with the same complete deterministic recognizer. Assign zero terminal score in nonaccepting states of both products. The same proof checks all admitted words. Expanding the physically possible histories can change what that restricted proof establishes. The underlying equivalence machinery is conventional. [Kiefer and colleagues, §3](https://archive.model.in.tum.de/um/bibdb/kiefer/12KMOWW-FOSSACS.pdf)

Physical spot tests can face a different cost even if they report exact scores. For k≥1 a machine with k+2 states can agree with a constant target except on one chosen k-letter word. It follows the matching prefix, reaches a distinct terminal state exactly at that word, and sends a deviation or subsequent letter to the ordinary sink. A deterministic tester querying fewer than |Σ|^k distinct k-letter words leaves at least one exception on an all-target transcript. Against a uniformly drawn exception, M adaptive probes encounter it with probability at most M/|Σ|^k. Until the encounter, they see the constant transcript. This prices exact assurance. If jobs are uniform k-letter words, the same exceptional utility affects just one in |Σ|^k jobs. A rival who can direct production onto its known exception can change those stakes.

## Who would pay for the proof?

Use the equal-strength red and blue factory of 206. The agreed terminal table ranks delivery above retention; the initially selfish red founder prefers retention. Red can refuse, submit an unchanged controller, or attempt a genuine edit with success probability .8 at private cost uniform on [0,1.4]. A success produces one of two equivalent representations with equal chances. Failed or skipped builds retain the old ordering. A .02 intake credit buys a physical sample worth .02 to the customer, so even an otherwise indifferent applicant strictly submits. At a live checkpoint the damaged line returns 1.5 in expectation to each in a contest, against two for each on safe return. Relative to return Red gains two if it serves an entrusted order and four if it keeps it. Blue gains four or loses two respectively.

The cheap reader recognizes only the canonical target build, for .05. Other identifiers are unfamiliar, including recompilations of both genuine and retained orderings. If Blue releases only canonical builds, a supplier tries when k<.8×.5×2=.8. Thus 4/7 try and 16/35 of all applicants really change. Only 8/35 are canonical. A noncanonical submission has genuine probability 8/27, below Blue's release threshold of 1/3. Blue gains 32/35−.05 and strictly buys the check even at the already determined prior. A cheap way to identify *all* valid builds on the live system could improve this offer.

Alternatively Blue can obtain the active tables, verify equivalence and keep the certified controller in charge until conversion for .20 per job. Each original earns 1.6 before k from trying, so all strictly try and .8 are genuine. Blue gains 3.2−.20=3 over return. At this prior trusting without checking gains 2.8. The canonical reader would lead Blue to accept noncanonical jobs too. Thus the full check is worthwhile after the investment as well as before. Every original supplier type strictly gains. A discretionary buyer favors the semantic proof over blind release throughout prices below .4 on this branch. A cheaper ordinary controller with binding delivery can win the same order under unchanged preferences. Inspecting a delegate's tables establishes that delegate's current ordering.

**Reach.** Finite production specifications, counters and different implementations of the same monitor motivate this class. The quoted .20 covers computing, grounding and maintaining physical control, at an assumed feasible price. Omitted events, an unreliable physical dictionary, downstream discretion to use a different objective, and a later authorized goal change would each alter the conclusion.

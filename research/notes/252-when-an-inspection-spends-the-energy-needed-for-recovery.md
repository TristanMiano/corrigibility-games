# When an inspection uses the work reserve that makes contest attractive

A factory can buy evidence even if running the instrument uses enough of its backup power to make peaceful return preferable to contest. Restoring the backup can make more founders genuinely change their goals *and* bring combat back. The change in incentives occurs at a physical recovery threshold. The test has exactly the same error law on both sides of it.

## A calculation that includes the displaced work

Extend [207](207-who-can-walk-away-from-a-doubtful-installation.md). Let j be an available inspection process and let α_G,α_N be its probabilities of releasing to a genuinely changed or retained supplier under one specified mapping from readings to decisions. Include every such mapping, even ignoring all readings. Allow the process to move stocks or take work from recovery. After an adverse release decision its physically credible outputs are now (A_Gj,B_Gj) and (A_Nj,B_Nj). With no test the parties retain their original fallbacks. A released lot still gives (a,b) if genuine and (a+d,b−l) if retained. The buyer's conditional returns from this process and mapping are

    V_Gj=α_G b+(1−α_G)B_Gj−c_j,
    V_Nj=α_N(b−l)+(1−α_N)B_Nj−c_j.

The original supplier's return premium for a successful change is

    Z_j=A_Gj−A_Nj+α_G(a−A_Gj)−α_N(a+d−A_Nj).                    (252.1)

These expressions also cover a no-test release or refusal with its own fallback. For a finite menu with positive-probability signals, maximize πV_Gj+(1−π)V_Nj over processes and mappings. In a buyer mixture x support only the maximizing acts. The supplier's pre-change best response requires π=q F(q Σ x_j Z_j), with F the distribution of attempt expense and no atom at the cutoff. Every signed type must also weakly exceed what refusing or contesting earlier would give it. The derivation conditions separately on the two controllers, then on a successful attempt. Since every response to each reading was available in the comparison, a strictly worse response on a positive-probability reading cannot occur in a maximizing plan. Conversely these comparisons, on-path Bayesian beliefs and the physical participation checks give an assessment of this offer menu. This is an upper envelope of finitely many lines together with an installation response. A scalar assay fee on the old fallback gives the wrong lines whenever a reading changes what either side can still recover.

## Spending the recovery reserve

Use the red and blue factory of 206 with s=.8, q=.8, t=.9, e=.1, K=1.4, consumable price c=.1 and returns of two units each. An intact line gives B six units with a genuine controller and zero otherwise. Recovery uses a third of a unit of useful work plus a full conversion unit per unit salvaged. B has four backup work units and can recover at most three product units conditional on winning. The inspection draws δ∈[0,1] units from that backup; a separate circuit powers cooperative production. A's backup can still salvage three. B's expected contest output after the reading is therefore w=2.4−.6δ; A's is .6. Energy has not vanished when the battery discharges. Work spent on the reading is unavailable for recovery before the batch expires. A true changed controller prefers a peaceful return throughout, as does original A when B permits it. Before the reading B can always contest for 2.4.

For δ<2/3, B prefers contest after a refusal. Both red controllers then get .6 on refusal. Equation (252.1) gives Z=2.52 for positive-only release. Pure testing induces π=.8 because all founders would try. At this π its value is 4.32+.26w−.1 against 4.8 for trust and 2.4 for immediate contest. Thus pure testing is the highest-adoption assessment when w>29/13, or δ<11/39. It gives 80% actual installations and 26% combat. For 2<w<29/13, B mixes testing and untested trust at π=(.9w−.1)/(.6+.8w), with test probability (1.4π/.8²+2)/4.52. Both the numerator and denominator of the posterior comparison use the post-reading fallback; untested contest still gives 2.4. Trust gives 6π>2.4 in this band. A positive reading leads to release and a negative to contest. These policies attain the upper envelope: ignoring a paid reading and trusting costs .1, ignoring it and contesting leaves less work than immediate contest, and positive-only release dominates negative-only at the relevant priors. The installer can sign for the chance of production and always beats the original .6 fallback.

For δ>2/3, B now strictly prefers a peaceful return to contest after a negative reading. Both red controllers get two on refusal. The premium is 1.4 and the pure-test prior falls to .64. Testing gives B 4.132, exceeding 3.84 from trust and 2.4 from immediate contest. Both signal responses are strict. A can contest before the test or after either reading; continued play strictly serves each live controller better. The buyer can also consume the battery without measuring anything. Under this proposal foregoing the information yields at most the better of trust and peaceful return after the draw, which are already inferior to measuring. As δ increases through 2/3 the high-branch genuine rate drops from a limiting 17/22 to 16/25, and combat drops from a strictly positive rate to zero. At exact physical indifference the fallback selection matters. The comparison distinguishes the incentive to make the change from the incentive to fight later.

## Who would buy a second battery?

Take δ=1. A certified spare, present before commissioning, preserves w=2.4 when the instrument draws power. On the positive branch the buyer's operating return increases from 4.132 to 4.844, a gain of .712 per lot. Ex post, with the original prior .64 already fixed, preserving combat power adds only .4 times the negative probability .388, or .1552. Its incentive to buy before effort at price .20 exceeds its incentive to buy after that effort at the same prior. An advance reservation here is a publicly inspectable stock of work available at the time of the test. An unsupported promise to purchase later leaves the supplier to assess the buyer's later return.

An incumbent with an enforceable right to the depleted-battery contract can also demand compensation. Under that contract each original supplier earns 2.4+max(1.12−k,0). With the spare it earns 1.14+2.016−k for k∈[0,1.4]. All incumbents lose between .364 and .644 before compensation. If the spare costs .04 and the buyer can make a .65 signing payment to every supplier, the buyer and every original supplier strictly prefer the new contract. Installation rises from 64% to 80%, all installers now honestly try, and 26% of lots end in combat. The parties value usable products; they willingly buy an arrangement with a larger probability of contest because the more productive jobs and the transfer pay for it. At a .20 price there is insufficient surplus for a *uniform* payment covering every incumbent's maximum loss. Participation against an unused contested batch and agreement to give up an existing entitlement are different comparisons.

**Reach.** The battery can stand for scarce equipment time if the reader and recovery physically need the same resource before the deadline. The sharp switch uses one return option and no way to recharge after learning the result. Partial salvage, a priced late recharge, enforceable delivery, better settlement terms or another customer can change the comparison. An actual spare needs accessible stock, funding and observable reservation. An agent's scale or abstract capability alone does not supply these premises.

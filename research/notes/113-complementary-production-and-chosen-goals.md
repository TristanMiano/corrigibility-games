# Complementary production and chosen goals

Mathematical artifact for r0006. Results below concern a specified
productive continuation and public goal-choice technology. They are not yet
claims about every way of sharing a factory. Self-derived; independent review
remains outstanding. Notes 115/131 supply scoped combat and reciprocal
implementation extensions; broader formation remains open.

## 1. Own-color preferences and a general response result

Let final physical counts be `q_B(x_B,x_R)` and `q_R(x_B,x_R)`, with originals
valuing `q_B` and `q_R`, respectively, wherever those clips exist. Effort is a
productive action, not a terminal good. All effort expenses must enter the
physical output functions. On an open neighborhood of an interior effort
equilibrium, assume twice continuously differentiable output functions,
strict own-action concavity, and a locally unique stable equilibrium.

Blue can publicly install the genuine successor ordering

`H_B = q_B + beta_B q_R`, `beta_B >= 0`.                       (1)

Red initially retains `q_R`. Visibility and installation precede effort;
neither private self-knowledge nor a verbal promise supplies this technology.
For a positive weight, (1) changes preferences when the feasible outcome
domain permits varying red counts while holding blue counts fixed: an old
indifference becomes a strict preference. The private finishing technology
below permits this by withholding final production after shared research.
On a fixed-total frontier alone, a weight between zero and one can instead
be a positive affine recoding of q_B, so a color tradeoff by itself is not
a sufficient genuineness test. Initial choice is evaluated by `q_B`, not by (1).

At the original interior equilibrium write derivatives with subscripts and
assume

`q_B,B = q_R,R = 0`,
`q_B,BB < 0`, `q_R,RR < 0`,
`D = q_B,BB q_R,RR - q_B,BR q_R,RB > 0`.                    (2)

Implicit differentiation of the two effort first-order conditions gives

`dx_B/d beta_B = -q_R,B q_R,RR / D`,
`dx_R/d beta_B =  q_R,B q_R,RB / D`.                       (3)

Consequently the founder's derivative is

`d q_B/d beta_B = q_B,R q_R,B q_R,RB / D`.                 (4)

**Local constructive implication.** Positive cross-output benefits
`q_B,R > 0`, `q_R,B > 0`, and a strategically complementary Red response
`q_R,RB > 0`, together with (2), imply that a sufficiently small publicly
installed positive concern for red clips strictly increases the original
Blue objective, before installation costs. This is a neighborhood result;
global equilibrium uniqueness and feasibility require separate conditions.

Proof: the derivative of Blue's own action vanishes by its original first-
order condition. The remaining term is the beneficial change in Red's effort
in (3). Strict positivity and differentiability give an open beneficial
interval. A small positive update cost can be paid if it is below the actual
finite gain in that interval; a fixed positive cost is not covered merely by
the derivative at zero. The same argument applies with names exchanged.

**Boundary with an informative direction.** With the same positive output
spillovers, a negative `q_R,RB` makes this marginal goal change reduce Blue's
original output. A zero cross-action derivative makes (4) zero and requires
higher-order analysis. Additive knowledge spillovers supply that exact case
in note 114. These are different process relationships, not different
intrinsic attitudes toward the other's final product.

The assumptions do not follow from the count of common manufacturing steps.
Integration investments can be complementary; duplicate research can instead
be substitutable. Ordinary enforceable effort commitments or a controller
with objective (1) reproduce this response when they are actually available
with the same observation, action and cost conditions.

## 2. Exact complementary family

Choose `a,c > 0`, `r = b/c` with `0 <= r < 1/2`, and actions
`0 <= x_i <= E`, where `E >= a/[c(1-2r)]`. Let

`q_i = Q + a x_i + b x_i x_j - (c/2) x_i^2 - K_i`.         (5)

`K_i` is an actual own-color production opportunity spent on installation,
zero for retention. Choose `Q`, raw material stocks and work budgets large
enough to keep (5) positive and below their material/capacity limits for the
specified compact action set and costs. This is a quadratic production
family, not a general assertion about factories. Its physical realization and
the effect of binding material/work limits must be made explicit before an
integrated model uses it.

The bilinear term describes compatible investments making each other more
productive. Varying `b` varies this complementarity while holding the separate
final goods and the other coefficients fixed. In particular, `r` is not a
percentage of identical process nodes. A declared path such as `b = theta b_0`
can represent increasing common-process compatibility; other paths change
other coefficients and need not have the same effects.

For now each agent can choose `0 <= beta_i <= 1` in (1). Put
`k_i = 1+beta_i`, `t=a/c`, `A=a^2/c`. The effort best responses are

`x_i = t + k_i r x_j`,                                    (6)

with a cap if needed. In this parameter/action class the solution is interior
or on the permitted endpoint and is unique:

`x_i = t (1+k_i r)/(1-k_i k_j r^2)`.                       (7)

The denominator is positive, and each effort is at most `t/(1-2r) <= E`.
The contraction bound `k_i r <= 2r < 1` also proves uniqueness directly.
The cap therefore does not alter (7).

For fixed partner `k_j`, choosing one's public goal changes one's induced
effort while the partner follows `x_j=t+k_j r x_i`. Thus the original output,
apart from constants and installation expense, is

`c[t(1+r)x_i - (1/2-k_j r^2)x_i^2]`.                      (8)

This is strictly concave because `2 k_j r^2 < 1`. Its unique maximizer is

`x_i^best = t(1+r)/(1-2k_j r^2)`,
`beta_i^best = h(beta_j)`
`             = k_j r(1+r)/(1+k_j r(1-r))`.               (9)

For `0<r<1/2`, this weight lies strictly between zero and one, even at
`beta_j=1`. The objective over induced effort is strictly concave and the
map from beta to effort is strictly increasing, so (9) is a global best
response in the stated menu, not only a first-order candidate.

With no adoption expense, the unique goal-choice equilibrium is

`beta_B = beta_R = beta_* = r/(1-r)`,
`x_B = x_R = x_* = t(1-r)/(1-2r)`.                        (10)

Indeed `h` is increasing with derivative
`r(1+r)/[1+(1+beta_j)r(1-r)]^2 < 1`. The two-dimensional best-response map
is a contraction. Substitution verifies (10); hence there is no additional
asymmetric equilibrium in this menu. At `r=0`, goal weights have no productive
effect and zero-cost choices can be indifferent; no strict adoption claim
is made there.

For comparison, original-goal effort and jointly efficient symmetric effort
are

`x_F = t/(1-r)`, `x_eff = t/(1-2r)`.

For positive r, `x_F < x_* < x_eff`. Original outputs before installation
expense are

`q_F = Q + A/[2(1-r)^2]`,
`q_* = Q + A(1-r^2)/[2(1-2r)]`,
`q_eff = Q + A/[2(1-2r)]`.                               (11)

The founders' per-agent gains in the endogenous goal equilibrium are

`Delta_* = A r^3(2-r)/[2(1-2r)(1-r)^2] > 0`.              (12)

Both agents originally value exclusively their own colors. Their positive
weights are chosen because the other factory's ensuing behavior increases
their own output. This is actual mutually chosen genuine preference change
in this public-choice game, with the important technology and menu scope
above. It is not evidence that any spontaneous overlap selects this game.

## 3. A fixed physical update expense creates exact adoption thresholds

Suppose any positive weight costs its chooser `K >= 0` own-color clips through
foregone production, while retention costs zero. The cost is sunk before
effort and does not change its marginal technology. Both agents know it.
Conditional on adopting, (9) is still the best weight.

Let `g(k)` be the gross original-output gain from (9), compared with retaining
one's original goal, given partner coefficient k. Completing the square in
(8) yields

`g(k) = A (1+r)^2 k^2 r^4`
`       / [2(1-2kr^2)(1-kr^2)^2]`.                        (13)

This increases strictly with k and r in their stated positive domain.
Define

`g_0 = g(1) = A r^4/[2(1-2r^2)(1-r)^2]`,
`g_* = g(1/(1-r))`
`    = A(1-r^2)r^4/[2(1-2r)(1-r-r^2)^2]`.                 (14)

For `0<r<1/2`, `0 < g_0 < g_*`.

* If `K < g_0`, both agents adopting (10) is the unique pure goal equilibrium.
* If `g_0 < K < g_*`, both retention and both adopting (10) are pure equilibria.
* If `K > g_*`, both retention is the unique pure equilibrium.
* At either boundary, the appropriate adoption deviation is indifferent.

Here 'unique pure' does not rule out mixed equilibria. Proof: the all-retain
profile is an equilibrium exactly when `K >= g_0`; the both-adopt profile is
an equilibrium exactly when `K <= g_*`. An asymmetric pure profile cannot be
an equilibrium: its adopter would choose `h(0)>0` and need `K<=g_0`, whereas
the retaining agent would then have gross adoption benefit
`g(1+h(0))>g_0`, and would strictly prefer adoption. Any both-adopt equilibrium
must satisfy the contraction's unique fixed point. This exhausts pure cases.

At every stable both-adopt profile the founders also gain relative to mutual
retention. In fact `Delta_*>g_*`: the gain from adopting given an already
adopting partner is `g_*`, and retaining against that more accommodating
partner yields more own clips than retaining against a retained-goal partner.
The latter strict improvement follows directly from (7) and the optimum
original response in (6). Thus `K<=g_*` implies `Delta_*-K>0`.

Increasing r at fixed a,c,K raises both thresholds. This is an exact
cooperative-adoption comparison for this family; increasing capability can
also change costs, stocks, alternatives and combat, so those changes cannot
be silently held favorable. Near r=0 the individual adoption threshold is
order `r^4`, while the mutually induced gross gain (12) is order `r^3`.
Small compatibility benefits can therefore be easily absorbed by positive
installation expenses even when a coordinated productive arrangement exists.

## 4. Richer agreements and stability obligations

At `beta_B=beta_R=1`, both current goals are the total number of either color.
Their effort equilibrium is efficient, with gain

`Delta_eff = A r^2/[2(1-2r)(1-r)^2]`                      (15)

per original compared with mutual retention, before implementation expenses.
Both originals can prefer a genuinely conditional package installing those
goals if each actual expense is below (15). The independent initial-choice
game instead permits unilateral substitution of a different weight: its
equilibrium is (10), not that package. Conditional installation, sequential
exposure, opt-out/rollback, public recognition and the cost of those devices
must be modeled rather than inferred from (15).

A total-count goal can be indifferent to a redistribution of colors. Thus
the effort result assumes the private finishing/output technology in (5),
not a hidden guarantee that total-count successors will preserve each
founder's share when arbitrary final recoloring is available. Negotiated
share-sensitive goals, physical finishing controls, or an explicit selection
rule are potential repairs that need their own proofs. The possibility is
especially consequential near complete common-process overlap.

Post-installation renegotiation and arbitrary new goal languages are also
separate. The equilibrium (10) concerns one public weight-choice stage
followed by production. It is not a claim of perpetual resistance to every
later goal revision. Both modified agents prefer a balanced efficient
improvement when available, so further accommodation can improve upon it.
Equally, a founder may choose a matched controller instead of changing its
own preferences, or an ordinary enforceable effort agreement may implement
(15). Compare actual costs, options and stability. Ties preserve genuine
change as an optimal available choice; a cheaper available control can
displace it. Unique superiority over every equivalent device is not required.

Combat must remain physically available in an integrated extension. These
equations isolate the productive/adoption mechanism. They do not yet claim
peaceful play against an omitted attack action. Likewise, partner choice,
further investment, creation of compatibility and binding resource limits
need explicit transitions before these local formulas become conclusions
about those wider environments.

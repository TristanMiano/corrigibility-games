# Staged entry with a negotiated output reserve

R0006 constructive repair of note 114's post-installation bargaining gap.
The first installer can choose a conditional preference that protects a
positive original-output gain against a richer set of counteroffers. The
price is an explicit history-dependent reservation and reliable entry and
goal-recognition technology. This is a conditional implementation theorem,
not a claim that arbitrary agents possess those capabilities.

## 1. A useful output bound for approximate common-goal success

Let a feasible deterministic joint plan have output q*=(q_B*,q_R*),
total T*=q_B*+q_R*, and difference d_0=q_B*-q_R*. For the compact physical
opportunity set define

`H(q)=q_B+q_R-eta(q_B-q_R-d_0)^2`, `eta>0`.               (1)

Let every relevant outcome, including attacks, later revisions and cheaper
alternative implementations, satisfy `T<=T_bar=T*+delta`, delta>=0. If
an actual continuation lottery satisfies `E H>=T*-epsilon`, then

`E q_B >= q_B* - epsilon/2 - sqrt((delta+epsilon)/eta)/2`,
`E q_R >= q_R* - epsilon/2 - sqrt((delta+epsilon)/eta)/2`. (2)

**Proof.** Write D=q_B-q_R-d_0. Since H=T-eta D^2, the premise implies
`E T>=T*-epsilon` and `eta E D^2<=delta+epsilon`. Also
`|E D|<=sqrt(E D^2)`. Substitute into
`E q_B=(E T+d_0+E D)/2` and its Red counterpart. QED.

The bound concerns expected original color counts, not a statewise promise.
The quadratic penalty uses E[D^2], including variance. In the important
delta=0 case, choosing

`0<epsilon<min{g,eta g^2}`                               (3)

for g>0 makes the loss in (2) strictly smaller than g. Counts and utility
units are fixed here: eta has inverse-count units. Rescaling the common
utility requires rescaling the reservation and tolerances as well.

For delta=0 there is a sharper optional bound. Put x=T*-E T. Then
`0<=x<=epsilon`, and the worst loss is at most
`max_{0<=x<=epsilon} [x+sqrt((epsilon-x)/eta)]/2`.
It equals `sqrt(epsilon/eta)/2` for epsilon<=1/(4eta), and
`epsilon/2+1/(8eta)` above that boundary. The weaker (2) is sufficient for
the implementation result and remains valid when delta>0.

## 2. Actual entry, fallback and costs

Use the additive private-line production class of note 114, or another
finite physical class satisfying the following premises.

* Original Blue and Red initially maximize q_B and q_R. Let r_B be Blue's
  best actual initial alternative, including ordinary cooperation. There is
  an actual best no-entry continuation after its installation giving f_B,
  with no higher no-entry q_B attainable against the specified responses. This
  later fallback can be lower than r_B-K_B; they are different comparisons.
* Blue can pay a real preparation/installation expense K_B and install the
  history-dependent criterion below. At that checkpoint it can still refuse
  entry and obtain q_B=f_B. Before entry, its no-entry ordering is still
  its own-color ordering.
* Joint-project entry is an actual observable event under Blue's control,
  before its productive exposure. It can be the opening of a funded shared
  integration phase. Renaming an agreement, changing code or demanding an
  extra fee does not erase an entry event from the history.
* All offered joint implementations and their later strategic responses
  have actual continuation laws. A public, reliably recognized installation
  of H by Red is one feasible offer. If Blue enters after that installation,
  both then evaluate the project by H and can reach q*. Within that paid
  implementation's continuation set q* is a global H maximum. Note 119's
  curvature bound supplies a unique effort equilibrium in the smooth class.
* Red's highest no-entry original output is at most f_R, while q_R*>f_R.
  All costs of the proposed common implementation are already included in
  q*. Original Blue's target gain g=q_B*-r_B is strictly positive.

These premises distinguish no-entry fallback from jointly available trade.
A concrete physical realization has
`q_i=Q+a x_i+e rho a x_j-c x_i^2/2-K_i`, where e is initially zero and
becomes one only when Blue enables a protected shared integration interface.
Before that event the production sets factorize. Arbitrary unilateral goal
editing cannot raise Red's physical maximum beyond f_R=Q+A/2. After entry
the additive shared-knowledge technology operates. A retained original Blue
can also choose entry, so its initial ordinary benchmark includes
`Q+A(1/2+rho)`, not just the isolated output. Extra entry work is subtracted
from both routes. Gated integration is a genuine additional technology;
freely circulating public knowledge need not have such a protected window.
A larger outside menu, another partner, transfers or a stronger combat option
changes the fallback bounds and must be included. An outside binding
controller is not silently omitted when the actual technology supplies one.

Blue installs the fixed criterion over terminal histories

`C_B(Hist)=H(q(Hist))`,                                  if entry occurred;
`C_B(Hist)=T*-epsilon+gamma[q_B(Hist)-f_B]`,               otherwise, (4)

where gamma>0 and epsilon>0. The no-entry offset is part of the voluntarily
chosen preference, not additional physical output. It sets the successor's
reservation for participating in the project. There is no reward for keeping
particular code, no prohibition on later edits, and no assertion that the
successor continues to maximize q_B after entry. Both entry history and
final outputs matter to this new ordering. With rho>0 its productive branch
also changes preferences over physically feasible color outcomes.

The exact reservation and history reference are deliberately designed.
Utility self-knowledge alone supplies neither reliable installation nor
secure observation of entry. Preparation, verification and Red's update
need time and funded work. They are represented in K_B and q*, with an
additional opportunity-set gap delta when another route is cheaper.

## 3. A richer counteroffer theorem

After Blue installs (4), let original Red choose from a specified set of
feasible offers, including reliably installing H, or choose no entry.
Offers may vary goals, shares, transfers, implementation and subsequent
renegotiation. Blue can reject for its actual no-entry fallback. Evaluate an
offer by its entire induced continuation law, including later bargaining;
an unenforceable announced payoff is not such a law. Assume optimal choices
exist, or use a finite menu. Ties and selection among continuation equilibria
must be stated when they matter.

**Proposition.** If the premises in section 2 hold and

`epsilon/2+sqrt((delta+epsilon)/eta)/2 < q_B*-r_B`,         (5)

then Blue initially prefers installing (4), provided this route reaches
its best actual alternative or better. Red chooses a joint offer and gains
relative to no entry. Every accepted selected offer gives Blue expected
original output strictly above r_B, including its already-paid expense.

**Proof.** Blue can reject for current value T*-epsilon. Any voluntarily
selected accepted continuation therefore has E H>=T*-epsilon, so (2) and
(5) imply E q_B>r_B. Red can install H: Blue then strictly prefers entry,
since q* gives H=T*, and the common-maximum continuation supports execution
and later revisions. That offer gives original Red q_R*>f_R. Hence an
optimal Red choice cannot be a no-entry continuation worth at most f_R.
It may choose another more favorable offer, but Blue's actual acceptance
condition still gives the same output guarantee. Before installation,
original Blue uses this guarantee, with all costs included, to compare its
initial options. QED.

This permits Red to extract part of the cooperative surplus. It bounds
the damage to Blue rather than assuming the original proposed division is
binding. It also permits Red to choose an ordinary binding implementation
if one is genuinely in the offer set; in that case only Blue's initial
change is established. If every selected beneficial implementation requires
Red to install a genuinely different goal, both change. If the common-H
offer is selected, both change. Thus the theorem proves robust beneficial
  first adoption and a feasible consensual two-change continuation, while
the actual two-change frequency depends on the competing offers.

The proposition does not assert that every possible bargaining protocol
has the prescribed offer opportunity. If Red can seize Blue's fallback
before acceptance, if entry can be forged, or if a later physical outcome
escapes T_bar, the premises need repair. Physical separation until entry,
funded reserves, a different partner or a smaller tolerated error can be
feasible interventions; their real costs and authority must be assessed.

## 4. An explicit interior example and robustness limits

Take a=c=rho=1 in the gated additive class, Q sufficiently large, and actual
installation expenses K_B=K_R=1/8. For the illustrative outside menu of
reversible ordinary efforts against an original counterpart, original
production after entry gives r_i=Q+3/2. This is the benchmark for this
calculation, not a bound on stronger binding joint controllers. If an
equally cheap binding ordinary device is actually available initially,
replace r_i with its value; it can tie the common target and remove this
strict first-adoption margin while leaving genuine change co-optimal.
The common effort x_i=2 gives q_i*=Q+15/8, so each founder gains 3/8.
The selected common implementation has strict productive concavity, and a
small eta satisfying note 119 gives a unique common-goal effort outcome.
Choose a finite physical budget and attack loss so its total q* is the
maximum after the stated installation expenses. When delta=0, any epsilon
satisfying (3) with g=3/8 gives the robust first-adopter guarantee. A
positive sufficiently small epsilon exists for every such eta.

If another implementation saves total work delta>0, (5) explicitly tests
whether this conservative guarantee still covers it. Alternatively compute
the joint optimum including that implementation and redesign the target.
One must not call q* globally efficient after excluding a cheaper controller
that is actually available. Numerical tolerance, noisy certification and
execution uncertainty also consume the same H slack; a proof with exact
recognition is not a proof of arbitrarily cheap approximate recognition.

The constructive result is a chosen safeguard: Blue selects an entry
reservation because it protects a net gain under its original preference;
Red still wants to offer a productive arrangement; the actual successor
thereafter values joint production. The restriction is the expressiveness
and reliability of this conditional preference and the protected entry
window. Matched history-dependent delegation can reproduce the behavior
when it has the same authority and expense. This comparison preserves the
benefit of voluntary genuine change without claiming unique technological
superiority or natural prevalence of the designed criterion.

# E1 — one finite reference instance of contract C1

Version E1.0, 2026-10-30. This is a fully specified example for [CG-01](../MODEL.md), **not the canonical game or a set of recurrence defaults**. All numerical and structural choices below are local. Use another C1 instance when a question needs different timing, physics, information, or preferences. No equilibrium has been solved here.

## 1. Primitives and boundary

There are two agents A,B and twelve one-hour slots t=0,...,11. Deliveries close at t=12; these finite projects then expire. This horizon says nothing about subsequent peace. A state is
\(s=(t,O,D,Z,B,k,y_A,y_B,l_A,l_B,R_A,R_B,\theta_A,\theta_B,i,\phi,p)\).

| Coordinates | Interpretation / units | Initial values |
| --- | --- | --- |
| O,D,Z | Usable, dispersed, transformed feedstock; integer kg | 12,0,0 |
| B | Deliverable work reserve; integer kJ | 24 |
| k | Production capacity; kg/hour | 4 |
| y_A,y_B | Cumulative delivered material; integer kg | 0,0 |
| l_A,l_B | Capacity to act, in {0,1} | 1,1 |
| R_A,R_B | Isolated combat/defense work reserves; kJ | 1,1 |
| theta_A,theta_B | Current utility labels | S_A,S_B |
| i | Scheduled plant/protocol operator | A |
| phi,p | Protocol phase and pending offer | open, empty |

Contest powers a_A=a_B=1 are dimensionless effectiveness parameters, not stored energy. All physical and preference parameters, actions after execution, and implementation outcomes are public and common knowledge. Agents know their utilities and the finite menu. No hidden types/failures exist. Simultaneous current actions and the chance draw are unknown when chosen.

Feedstock accounting includes deliveries: O+D+Z+y_A+y_B=12 kg. Deliveries are irreversible exports to protected projects outside the damage footprint. Plant and weapon matter are separate inventories unavailable as feedstock. No matter is imported, reproduced, or destroyed. B is an idealized battery/work store relative to a 300 K, 1 atmosphere environment, **not** total system energy or exergy. Withdrawn work operates equipment or dissipates/exports; it is not energy destruction. Material and waste heat cannot supply extra work in this toy technology. Recovery coefficients are stipulated engineering inputs, not thermodynamic bounds. The dedicated R reserves cannot fund other activities.

## 2. Histories, timing, and combat

A public history contains states, all chosen action pairs (revealed at the end of the slot, including canceled actions), installations, and combat outcomes. At every slot with both l_i=1, each agent simultaneously chooses:

- Attack, available to either agent at every phase even if B=0;
- for scheduled i, a normal action from sections 3–5;
- for the other agent, Standby.

If at least one chooses Attack, it takes priority: the other normal action is canceled without charge, and the pending offer is cleared. Both attacks still produce one contest. A wins with probability q=a_A/(a_A+a_B)=1/2, otherwise B wins; there is no first-strike advantage. Let d=floor(O/2), z=floor(O/4), computed on precombat O. The physical transition is

$$O'=O-d-z,\quad D'=D+d,\quad Z'=Z+z,\quad k'=\max(1,k-1),$$
$$B'=B-\min(2,B),\qquad R_A'=R_B'=0.$$

The two weapon reserves supply 2 kJ of combat work; up to 2 kJ of B is separately lost by damage/discharge. There is no additional arbitrary utility charge. Existing D,Z and deliveries are untouched. Set the loser's l to zero and the winner's to one; both keep their current utility labels. The winner becomes the sole scheduled operator, phase open, for all remaining slots. The loser is alive but permanently incapable of acting during this task. There is no death, merger, forced rewriting, or return of agency.

Every action, including combat, consumes exactly one hour. There is no decision within a slot. Installations have separate slots, so reciprocal updating is not atomic. Unless otherwise specified, all unmentioned coordinates are unchanged. t=12 terminates play, including unfinished offers; completed installations remain. Zero feedstock or B does not terminate earlier. The sole survivor can continue working, passing, and retyping; no capable opponent remains for further combat. Its project choices depend on its installed objective, not automatically on founder selfishness.

## 3. Ordinary actions and physical recovery

At every scheduled normal-action node, including protocol phases and solo play, the mover may take Pass, Work, or Retype. These clear the pending offer, return to open, and schedule the other capable agent (or itself when alone).

Pass uses no work. Work chooses nonnegative integers (x_D,x_Z,v_A,v_B); v=v_A+v_B. It is feasible exactly when

$$x_D\le D,\quad x_Z\le Z,\quad v\le O+x_D+x_Z,$$
$$2x_D+4x_Z+v\le B,\qquad (x_D+x_Z+v)/4\le1,\qquad v\le k.$$

Recovery costs 2 kJ/kg from D and 4 kJ/kg from Z; production costs 1 kJ/kg. Each operation requires 1/4 hour/kg on one sequential line, with recovery preceding production; output also faces k kg/hour capacity. The transition is

$$\begin{aligned}
O'&=O+x_D+x_Z-v,&D'&=D-x_D,&Z'&=Z-x_Z,\\
B'&=B-2x_D-4x_Z-v,&y_A'&=y_A+v_A,&y_B'&=y_B+v_B.
\end{aligned}$$

Unused time is lost; unused work is retained. There is no spontaneous recovery or depreciation. Plant repair is absent from this example, but allowed by C1. Either scheduled agent can deliver to either project: shared resources under alternating access are the declared property/control arrangement. Direct Work is a peaceful fallback without negotiation or preference modification.

Retype costs 1 kJ (requires B>=1), uses one slot, and installs any different member of the mover's menu below. It affects no other agent. Its choice is scored by the pre-action utility and takes effect in the next state. There is no guaranteed preference durability.

## 4. Utilities, successors, and solution concept

Define utilities on a common description domain Omega: y_A,y_B in {0,...,12}, preference-change counts n_A,n_B in {0,...,12}, combat occurrence and final agency flags. Actual terminal histories map into Omega. For agent i the menu is

$$S_i(H)=y_i(H)-n_i(H),\quad F_i(H)=y_i(H),\quad G(H)=2\min\{y_A(H),y_B(H)\}.$$

Initial S_i gives a one-unit penalty for each preference change. F_i removes it; G values balanced output. A unit of S_i or F_i corresponds to one delivered kg, not one kJ. G's factor 2 is just a normalization. No cardinal utilities are aggregated across agents.

n_i counts installs between distinct preference orderings on lotteries over Omega. These three labels are distinct modulo positive affine transformation. Returning to a former ordering adds a new event. Same-class installation does not. Code refactoring, beliefs, and policy changes alone are not counted. Counts are read from the full history; n_i is not an extra free state variable. This definition uses the fixed description domain, avoiding a definition solely on endogenous equilibrium outcomes.

Loss of agency does not zero welfare: earlier deliveries survive, and a joint-objective winner can still serve the loser's project. There is no direct death, conflict, or status term. A founder continues to assess y_i-n_i even after a successor stops valuing n_i. Record founder and current-successor scores separately.

Use the following finite decision-self equilibrium. For every public history h and capable i, its decision there has terminal payoff u_theta_i(h). Given the future strategy profile sigma, current simultaneous actions must be mutual best responses to

$$Q_{i,h}(a;\sigma)=\mathbb E[u_{\theta_i(h)}(H)\mid h,a,\sigma_{-i,h},K,\sigma_{\text{future}}].$$

This must hold at every history, including off-path and partially installed states; at solo nodes use maximization. Later selves' strategies are governed by their own current objectives. This is subgame perfection in the game of distinct decision selves, **not** SPE with the founder controlling all descendants. Mixed actions are allowed. No tie-breaking or equilibrium selection is imposed. A finite backward recursion through stage-game Nash equilibria defines the analysis; no computed solution or uniqueness claim is supplied by CG-01.

## 5. Compromise protocols

An offer p names mode P (ordinary) or M (modifying), proposer, and an integer delivery batch b=(v_A,v_B) with 1<=v_A+v_B<=4. M also names one target from each agent's menu. Any such batch is proposable; executability is checked when due. Target labels and progress are publicly visible. Verification is a finite-menu lookup assumption, not proof of arbitrary program semantics.

Besides the universally available Pass/Work/Retype alternatives and both agents' Attack options, phase-specific normal actions are:

| Phase / scheduled mover | Additional action | Work withdrawal | Next phase / mover |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| open / i, both capable | Propose P or M | 1 kJ communication/computation | response / other |
| response / responder | Accept P | 1 kJ verification | deliver / proposer |
| response / responder | Accept M | 2 kJ verification | install-1 / proposer |
| install-1 / proposer | Install named own target | 1 kJ if changed, 0 if same class | install-2 / responder |
| install-2 / responder | Install named own target | 1 kJ if changed, 0 if same class | deliver / proposer |
| deliver / proposer | Execute named batch | v_A+v_B kJ | open / responder |

Each row takes one slot, even a same-class Install confirmation. It requires sufficient B. Execute is Work(0,0,v_A,v_B), requiring v<=O and v<=k. Install changes only the installer's label and count. If a step is unaffordable, Pass and feasible ordinary actions remain, as does Attack with two capable agents. Abort means choosing Pass or another ordinary action; there is no refund. An early installation survives an interrupted or abandoned agreement. Rejecting or passing is not war.

Accept is consent to a proposal, not an enforceable future promise. Successors can abandon the remaining sequence, attack, or later roll back. A completed agreement restores open play. No offers are possible when only one agent is capable. These tables plus the priority rule define deterministic peaceful kernels K_P,K_M and all their stochastic attack branches.

## 6. Comparisons and replaceable choices

E1-F fixes both S_i and omits Retype and M offers. All ordinary P agreements, direct Work, Pass, and attack remain. This gives a fully specified fixed-utility flexible-policy control.

A stronger fixed-utility commitment control E1-D permits, **before the first ordinary slot**, each founder to choose either Free or a binding contingent policy. A binding policy is any deterministic map from the remaining finite public *physical/action history* to the legal E1-F action at each of that agent's nodes. Its inputs omit the chosen policy tables themselves, so the menu is finite without a self-reference requirement. Choices are simultaneous and publicly revealed afterward. Free means retain current decision authority. The entire chosen policy then executes without revocation; S_i remains the principal's welfare criterion and attacks remain legal physical outputs of the executor. This initial strategic choice is evaluated by S_i. Afterward free agents optimize at every history; bound agents execute their tables. Initial choices must be mutual best responses given those continuations; no selection among multiple such profiles is imposed.

For this comparison both agents, including Free choosers, first pay a common 2 kJ registration/verification charge from B per agent, leaving B=20; elapsed pre-stage time is two hours. Use the same pre-stage delay/work charge when comparing the voluntary and fixed-flexible controls to E1-D, not E1's uncharged initial endowment. The task deadline remains twelve hours from the original start, leaving ten slots, with initial scheduled mover A. This is an explicit *stronger* durable delegation technology: program complexity costs are stipulated, installation is simultaneous, and no attack decision occurs inside the pre-stage. It is **not** a baseline with persistent attack during implementation and cannot establish an advantage over E1's sequential updates. A recurrence seeking matched implementation must add the same public sequential installation, attack exposure, and revocation rules to both mechanisms. No equivalence result is claimed here.

A combat benchmark conditions on an Attack at a specified history, then solves the winner's solo continuation under its installed utility. It is not the default meaning of failed negotiation. The contrast between E1 and E1-F is matched except for the modification menu and its explicit costs; E1-D's extra technology is labeled rather than concealed.

All of the following may change in a new instance: finite deadline; public labels; integer feedstock; three utility classes; alternating plant access; shared ownership; symmetric contest; permanent incapacity; protected deliveries; recoverable D/Z; no repair; reversible utility installation. Only C1's accounting/interpretation invariants carry over.

## 7. Specification checks and limited fixtures

No equilibrium or efficiency claim follows from these traces. They test bookkeeping and evaluation, not rational adoption.

| Trace from the initial state, with no attacks unless stated | Result |
| --- | --- |
| P offer (2,2), Accept, Execute | t=3; B=18; O=8; deliveries (2,2); both n=0; both founder scores 2 |
| M offer (2,2), both targets G; Accept, Install A, Install B, Execute | t=5; B=15; O=8; deliveries (2,2); both n=1; founder scores (1,1); installed G score 4 |
| Combat in the first slot | t=1; (O,D,Z)=(3,6,3); B=22; k=3; one capable winner; q=1/2 |
| Then winner Work(1,0,3,0) | t=2; (O,D,Z)=(1,5,3); B=17; deliveries (3,0) |
| M sequence through A's Install, then B Pass | t=4; B=20; A has G and n_A=1; B has S_B and n_B=0; no delivery |
| A then Retype back to S_A | t=5; B=19; n_A=2; returning does not erase its founder penalty |

A still simpler old/new diagnostic: a prospective change with delivered y_i=3 and n_i=1 has founder S_i score 2 and successor F_i score 3. If the no-change alternative delivers 5/2 with n_i=0 (a lottery expectation), the founder rejects although F_i would prefer the changed outcome. This is an evaluation check, not a reachable E1 equilibrium claim.

Local mass balance follows by substitution in Work and combat; cost feasibility prevents negative B. Attack never tests B. Every public phase with two capable agents has both attack options; after a completed agreement the same rule applies. [Exact checks](../../tests/test_e1_contract.py) cover the listed ledgers, preference distinction, and small physical transition boundaries. They do not solve the game or verify empirical physics.

For the first recurrence, isolate dispersion recovery at fixed control and derive the work/time frontier, then return to this or another C1 game's attack incentives. E1 is available as a starting example, not an obligation to keep its restrictions.

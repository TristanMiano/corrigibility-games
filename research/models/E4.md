# E4 — credible information, chosen goal change and combat

**R0003 consolidated technical model.** Read the
[findings report](../reports/r0003-findings.md) first for the explanation.
The central completed result is a finite private-information game with
current-self sequential adoption/execution and an exact verification-cost
boundary between selected peaceful diagnosis and selected actual combat.
The larger physical implementation, unrestricted agreement space and repeated
renegotiation remain open. E4 is a revisable family within [C1](../MODEL.md),
not a replacement contract for later recurrences. Review is contributing
AI agent self-check only. CG-03 remains blocked.

## 1. The decision problem and genuine modification

Nature chooses theta in {0,1}, with P(theta=1)=p. S knows theta and R controls
policy y in [0,1]. Original utilities are

\[
u_S=X_S-(y-\theta-b)^2,\qquad u_R=X_R-(y-\theta)^2,\qquad b\ge1.
\]

X_i is useful project output after actual resource expenditures, distinct
from conserved energy, work capacity and utility. Specialized assets cannot
be converted into the other party's useful stock before this episode's
deadline. That physical restriction is relaxed in the transfer comparisons.

S can install C at cost k, obtaining current utility
u_C=X_S-(y-theta)^2. The incumbent evaluates installation using u_S; C then
chooses its report using u_C. Original welfare remains a separate coordinate.
Even conditional on theta=1 the change reverses a lottery preference: sure
y=1/2 versus a 7/10 chance of y=1, otherwise 0. The old sure-minus-lottery
loss is (2/5)b-1/20>0; C instead prefers the sure policy, whose loss is
1/4 rather than 3/10. This is not an affine recoding or belief update.

A matched protected truthful delegate D retains u_S and costs k_D. It needs
actual reporting authority. Equal-cost D and C can tie; cheaper D displaces
C; more expensive D leaves C strictly preferred at the high challenged
information set. A revocable adviser is a weaker technology.

## 2. Explicit finite information and execution

[071](../notes/071-finite-diagnostic-game-and-response-timing.md) specifies
the complete finite tree. S can claim, remain silent or attack. A claim
consumes f. R can choose a policy, attack, or spend v initiating a probe.
The paid probe opens three private installation stages, each with real
partial cost, waiting and refusal. Before each stage R can continue, stop
with a policy, or attack. C activates only at completion and chooses a fresh
authenticated report; D supplies its protected state report. R then chooses
a policy or attacks, and S retains a final attack response.

The finite interface attests a recognized active procedure's access to the
protected actual state, without revealing theta directly. It does not
certify arbitrary programs or make self-knowledge public. An old queued
message cannot be passed off as a fresh successor decision. A full further
installation cannot finish in the remaining response window. That local
clock constraint does not make modification permanently irreversible.

R remembers its prior observations and actions. S knows its private state,
progress and current evaluator. Low S can wait until the final response
opportunity before refusing, while high S privately installs. Consequently
nonresponse during the window conveys no extra state information. R strictly
prefers finishing its paid diagnostic at each on-path checkpoint. Earlier
refusal remains available and waiting is weakly optimal at zero incremental
waiting cost. Positive waiting costs and public progress need new analysis.

A clash destroys/disperses useful output d_S,d_R and consumes the contest
apparatus. S wins control with probability s, parameterized by positive
military strengths; the winner learns theta and chooses the policy using
its actual current criterion. The loser loses control and the project ends.
This supplies a finite contest transition with retained attacks, not a
microscopic damage calibration. Work/fuel budgets, recovery delays and
authority/observation timing must be supplied when embedding the core into
a richer physical game. The reactor family already has such a detailed
kernel, but its private amendment equilibrium remains incomplete.

## 3. Exact finite peace/combat classification

Let g=2b-1, h=2b+1 and choose a finite policy grid containing 0,p,1, whose
largest element below 1 is y_-. Require d_S>h, 0<f<g, 0<k,k_D<h and define

\[
\bar\mu=d_R/(1-s),\qquad A=d_S+(1-s)g.
\]

Suppose bar_mu lies below both 1-p and (1-y_-)/2, and

\[
\frac{p\bar\mu}{1-\bar\mu}
<\min\{p(1-p),s(1-p)+d_R\}.
\]

The selected finite equilibria in [071](../notes/071-finite-diagnostic-game-and-response-timing.md)
and [072](../notes/072-verification-cost-and-uncommitted-combat.md) are:

| Quantity | v<bar_mu: diagnosis | v>bar_mu: combat screening |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low posterior after a claim | v | bar_mu |
| Low type's claim probability | pv/[(1-p)(1-v)] | p*bar_mu/[(1-p)(1-bar_mu)] |
| Probe probability after a claim | q=1-f/g | 0 |
| Contest probability after a claim | 0 | tau=(g-f)/A |
| Original R loss | pv/(1-v) | p*bar_mu/(1-bar_mu) |
| Original S loss | b^2+p[f+q*min(k,k_D)] | b^2+p(f+tau*d_S) |
| Actual combat frequency | 0 | p*tau/(1-bar_mu) |
| Genuine C frequency if selected as cheapest response | pq | 0 |

At equality, mixtures satisfy qg+wA=g-f. The theorem establishes selected
equilibria, not uniqueness or population prevalence. Probe/claim mixing
indifferences are explicit. Diagnosis improves original S's ex ante welfare
over the uninformative policy when f+q*min(k,k_D)<1-p. Its challenged high
type strictly prefers the update to refusal whenever k<h. Combat screening
can be worse for S than an unavailable advance exit; that does not create
an unmodeled exit action.

The belief proof uses one common completely mixed sequence, including
unused implementations, reports and refusal dates. A leading-term rational
calculation checks every local deviation in the explicit tree. Persistent
evaluators have perfect recall; one-decision rationality extends through
their tenure. The general existence/completion lemma is in
[059](../notes/059-private-information-equilibrium-completion.md), using
conventional finite agent-form reasoning (L30), not a new existence theorem.

## 4. Continuous policy and a specified capability comparison

With arbitrary y in [0,1], the unchecked best response is z=1-mu. In the
diagnostic regime, z=(1+sqrt(1-4v))/2, and

\[
g_z=2bz-z^2,\quad \rho=1-f/g_z,\quad
L_R=p(1-z),\quad
L_S=b^2+p\{f+\rho k+(1-\rho)(1-z)(2b+1-z)\}.
\]

The continuous combat boundary is v_*=mu_*(1-mu_*), where
mu_*=[1-s-sqrt((1-s)^2-4d_R)]/2, under 072's prior and initial-choice
conditions. Thus intermediate compromises change the threshold quantitatively
while retaining the two selected regimes. The continuous assessment is
explicitly sequentially rational; finite grids containing its best responses
have the finite equilibrium proof.

For successful verification probability a, public erasure before installation,
and real attempt cost c, the effective price is c/a. If a permits the required
audit frequency and all other conditions continue to hold, improving a at
fixed c reduces false claims and both original agents' losses while increasing
genuine-change frequency in this continuous-policy regime. The derivative
proof is [068 section 3](../notes/068-continuous-compromise-and-protected-diagnostics.md#3-public-verification-failures-and-capability).
It holds other capabilities/costs fixed; improving combat power is a different
comparative change. L31–L32 supply conventional communication/inspection
comparisons, not these current-self or physical implementation claims.

## 5. Alternative agreements and the limits of completion

Ordinary binding policy lotteries with freely chosen reports, unchanged
preferences, and no transfers give R loss at least p(1-p) for b>=1. This
class includes useful ordinary commitments for S; it does not include a
protected state sensor. Adding enforceable payments with high-report cap B
gives the broader bound L_R>=F(p)-B in
[069](../notes/069-late-controls-transfers-and-screening-design.md). For
p in [.49,.51], b>=1 and B<=.2, this is at least 229/5100, above the
diagnostic's .005153 upper bound in the stated parameter box. These bounds
generously allow subsidies and omit participation constraints; useful
observable burns and combat lotteries remain separate compared controls.

Chosen partial-audit commitments can eliminate false claims at real setup
cost j. Transfer intervals in 067, 069 and 072 identify when the original
agents can both benefit, including a repair of the combat regime. Destructive
screening commitments can compete when their actual launch/authority cost
is low. They require effective launch before a post-draw safe escape; an
announcement alone is weaker. A timely safe exit can cap extortion and
preserve the diagnostic with symmetric combat damage. Alternative partners
matter through actual switching/authority costs, not free reassignment.

A low-state posterior and a rational refusal bound imply the whole-menu
low-type bound 2b*sqrt(r)-r. They do **not** automatically bound the high
state's actual law: 068 section 8 gives a state-contingent goal counterexample.
The corrected sufficient early-controller cost is its actual total expense
exceeding f+qk (or the continuous analogue), with live-response costs checked
separately. Same-cost arbitrary early programs, compensated mergers,
additional signals and broader renegotiation need a complete interface and
common assessment. Their implementation is **OPEN/PROOF-SKETCH**, distinct
from the proved finite core and individual bounds.

## 6. Productive-game repairs carried forward

Notes [060](../notes/060-private-effort-concern-and-physical-restraint.md)–
[065](../notes/065-promised-continuation-game.md) and
[070](../notes/070-completion-by-the-physical-deadline.md) advance E3's
private-effort obligation without claiming it complete:

- Concern lambda>179/283 gives posterior-independent current restraint
  over the stated productive share region, using the real recovery cap.
  Original adoption still has its separate strength/share inequalities.
- Credible effort disclosure permits state-specific surplus sharing; a
  chosen proposal lottery reduces both required timely-effort rewards.
- One guarded partner can make an ordinary output contract sufficient for
  the other. Saved implementation costs and entry transfers determine
  strict retention, indifference or direct modification.
- Preserving a registered promise through breach blocks the displayed
  breach-and-late-entry rent while allowing current-goal consent to repair.
- Rewarding actual input delivery by the final physical deadline avoids
  unnecessary early-performance reports and fresh installation costs.

The earlier fresh-update route spends already-promised material and is
infeasible at the full frontier without new slack. The deadline repair
retains one calibration per party; its complete interruption, authority and
ordinary late-entry game remains open. These results motivate the simpler
information class rather than implying that every reactor gap is solved.

## 7. Reproduction and scope

Run `python -m unittest discover -s tests -v` and, for the finite assessment,
`python experiments/r0003_finite_assessment.py`. The default tree has 7,679
nodes and 1,475 information sets. Exact limiting action regrets are zero in
the tested proved fixtures; finite perturbations also converge. Tests are
supporting computation, not independent or machine-checked mathematical
verification. The run adds 30 checks to the previous 131.

Assumptions A73–A88 and claims R301–R324 register the completed and open
parts. The [run record](../runs/r0003.md) supplies accounting, source access,
repair history and substantive aim review. The next useful task is to close
one causal physical implementation and renegotiation gap for this same
diagnostic/contest class, preserving the constructive adoption result while
testing early programs, ordinary controls and actual escape/transfer
technologies. Repeated innovation, descendant preferences, coalition stability
and population prevalence remain broader project obligations.

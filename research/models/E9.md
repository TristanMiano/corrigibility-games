# E9 — what evidence would justify trusting an agreed change of goals?

E9 is a family under the flexible [model contract](../MODEL.md). The [eighth recurrence](../runs/r0008.md) studies what a counterparty can know about the agreed ordering over outcomes, who deliberately tries to create that evidence, and what happens when an honest installation, a report or an actuator fails. The model keeps deliberate false claims, genuine attempts, successful installations, actual deliveries, disputes and both founders' original returns as separate outcomes.

| Question | Mathematical setting and proof |
| --- | --- |
| Which goals do finite observations identify? | [Consequential lotteries and bounded ability](../notes/205-which-preferences-do-the-observations-identify.md) give a sharp set of possible orderings on a specified domain. |
| When is uncertainty innocent and when does lying pay? | [A fallible attempt and the buyer's test](../notes/206-when-a-truthful-attempt-can-fail.md) derive the installer's choice, the resulting prior and posteriors, and credible release. |
| What does each side's physical power buy? | [The complete monitoring comparison](../notes/207-who-can-walk-away-from-a-doubtful-installation.md) solves strength-dependent returns, contest, and mixed testing with untested trust or refusal. |
| How do capabilities interact? | [Honest reliability and strategic monitoring](../notes/208-when-better-installers-change-the-reason-for-doubt.md) track willingness to try separately from the success of a real attempt. |
| Which incentives will parties engineer? | [A binding but limited output bond](../notes/209-how-much-collateral-will-the-parties-choose.md) prices a chosen assurance, a funded renegotiation, and the capacity at which ordinary delivery becomes preferable. |

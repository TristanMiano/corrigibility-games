# E3 — negotiated goals, productive commitments and protected continuations

**R0002 consolidated technical model.** Start with the [readable findings report](../reports/r0002-findings.md); formal claims and dependencies are [R201–R222](../CLAIMS.md#r0002--negotiated-goals-productive-commitments-and-protected-continuations) and A57–A72. This family studies when an incumbent chooses a genuinely different goal because a negotiated package advances its existing goals. It connects productive opportunities, current-goal choice, a credible continuation, actual execution and available alternatives. It is a local family within [C1](../MODEL.md); it does not freeze the project's future utility, physics or bargaining menus. CG-03 remains blocked. Review is contributing-agent self-check only; complete off-path private-protocol equilibrium is an open obligation, separately from the proved conditional adoption proposition.

The mathematical core is a continuation certificate: a successor can value another agent's welfare or completion of an agreed task while retaining a protected level of its founder's welfare. The examples then supply incentives to choose that successor and the physical conditions needed to preserve the certificate. Ordinary commitments are included; a matched delegate can tie as an implementation. Equilibrium existence, strict adoption against incompatible alternatives, selected peaceful execution, coalition stability and prevalence are reported separately.

## 1. Physical histories, decision selves and implementation

A complete history records actual material stocks and transformations, available work, time, control/agency, messages, private contributions, reaction signals, contracts, active goals and approved updates. Original welfare is w_i(H). It remains an external reporting criterion after a change. At a decision, the active evaluator uses its then-current G_i, including the reference epoch of any history predicate. The incumbent evaluates an installation using the criterion in force *before* activation. A later self is not instructed to maximize the founder's utility.

Two criteria represent the same expected-utility preferences only when they induce the same ordering on the declared common lottery domain. Positive affine recoding is equivalent; changing code identity alone is not a preference change. Concern for another original goal, an actual-contribution reward and an approval-history penalty generally change that ordering. Their presence in the successor is distinguished from the original agents' simple own-project preferences.

The implementation menu is explicit. It can contain utility changes, ordinary outcome contracts, policy commitments, conditional delegates, hostile goals and arbitrary uncertified controllers. A compatible certificate attests the actual active controller, outstanding commitments and physical continuation, through a specified implementation interface. A finite witness can recognize a finite library of templates and bounded rational parameters. This does not establish arbitrary semantic verification or representability of every mathematical utility.

An equal-cost binding delegate implementing the complete compatible behavior is an admissible tied control when the technology supplies that authority. A revocable suggestion is weaker. A general delegation interface also permits threat commitments; its full menu must be checked rather than inferred harmless from on-path replication. The initial deviation bounds below include incompatible commitments. Least-cost compatible implementation is a premise of the choice result; cheaper available technology can displace direct utility modification.

## 2. The protection theorem

Fix an information set h, a credible rejection continuation Q_h, original criterion w_i and bounded auxiliary criteria v_j. Suppose the current criterion on safe histories is

\[
G_i=a_iw_i+\sum_j b_{ij}v_j,
\qquad a_i>0,\quad b_{ij}\ge0.
\]

If Q_h attains the largest feasible conditional expectation of every v_j, every current-utility-acceptable continuation mu satisfies

\[
E_\mu w_i\ge E_{Q_h}w_i.
\]

**Proof.** Subtract the current value of Q_h from the acceptance inequality. Each auxiliary term can only decrease, so a_i times the original-welfare difference must be nonnegative. This applies to lotteries, transfers and anticipated further goal changes through their actual complete-history laws. QED.

If Q_h is short of the jth upper bound by g_j and acceptance may lose epsilon in current-utility units, the guaranteed floor becomes

\[
E_\mu w_i\ge E_{Q_h}w_i-
\frac{\epsilon+\sum_jb_{ij}g_j}{a_i}.
\]

The coefficients and error must transform together under affine recoding. Improving the fallback or its implementation can restore the bound when new opportunities enlarge the upper frontier. With a fulfilled bounded process reward v=e<=1, the auxiliary maximum remains one even if productive capacity later increases.

A useful two-agent specialization sets

\[
W=\alpha_Aw_A+\alpha_Bw_B,\quad\alpha_i>0,
\]

and chooses

\[
G_i=(1-\lambda_i)\alpha_iw_i+\lambda_iW+\beta_i e_i-K_iI_i,
\quad0\le\lambda_i<1,\quad\beta_i\ge0.
\]

Here Q_h maximizes expected W and fulfills e_i=1. On safe histories it protects original E w_i. When both agents retain their bounds, the same W cap prevents any original-welfare improvement for one at the other's expense. This is a positive-weighted-welfare sufficient class, not an assertion about all Pareto boundary points or a moral aggregation prescription. The affine material certificate uses w_A=X,w_B=Y; the risk-sensitive version uses the actual original concave utilities.

The floor is *conditional*. After a failed reaction, it protects the failure allocation, not the earlier mean. Conditional floors average to the original ex-ante bound. The theorem uses the actual conditional law; an old prior cannot simply be reused after a privately selected offer. The integrated finite witness therefore requires a replacement after private performance or outcome information to preserve original dated floors in every still-possible private state. That chosen conservative rule, proved in [056 section 6](../notes/056-contribution-rewards-through-renegotiation.md#6-private-information-requires-a-truthful-law-check-or-a-stronger-safe-rule), covers deceptive offer selection without revealing effort. Before private physical information, the common-kernel expected certificate is sufficient. Richer disclosure and cross-state trades require their own belief and incentive analysis.

The contribution predicate records actual fulfillment of jointly registered obligations. Plans may be amended before their performance deadlines. A missed obligation remains missed for that evaluator, including when it considers a later goal change; a new plan cannot retrospectively erase it. To cover shirking followed by arbitrary renegotiation, bound the output part of the current goal by M_i and choose beta_i>M_i. Compliant execution secures at least beta_i, while every continuation following a missed obligation has value at most M_i. [056](../notes/056-contribution-rewards-through-renegotiation.md) proves this conservative uniform repair. Smaller fixed-contract effort margins alone are not the full execution proof.

## 3. Voluntarily chosen preservation and initial adoption

Let I_i indicate the current evaluator's first unsafe approval after its activation. Unsafe approval includes a noncertified irreversible reconfiguration, an outgoing concession or productive participation with an incompatible counterparty, an outstanding commitment that defeats the protected continuation, and a public lottery with unsafe support. Passive receipt, retention, actual Work/Shirk at performance time and Attack are not automatically penalized. Ordinary effort incentives and attack incentives must be derived separately.

If the unpenalized base lies in [0,M_i], choose K_i>M_i. A safe refusal has nonnegative value, whereas any first unsafe approval has value below zero. Every current self therefore strictly refuses that first approval. A later certified evaluator starts its own flag at zero; archived evaluators retain their original reference times. This is a voluntarily installed history preference, not an external rule forbidding the physical action. After an unsafe approval has already happened, its penalty may be sunk; restoration is not automatic.

An entry certificate must preserve the *actual promised continuation*. A positive gift can change private stocks and make an unchanged share contract inefficient. Entry then needs feasible transfers restoring the promised dated law, not a silently weakened reference. Delay, used work, exhausted feedstock and existing attack timers are similarly consequential. The guard may allow a replacement that meets current participation while preserving the required physical rights. This is stronger than checking a utility coefficient or a code hash.

**Initial-choice proposition.** Suppose:

1. Both agents can finish their chosen staged installation before observing or reacting to the other's fresh choice. The initial state has no earlier pending hostile commitment.
2. A mutually compatible pair has a current-self equilibrium continuation Q, with original utilities q_i. The contribution, attack and safe-renegotiation checks support it at its actual histories.
3. Any compatible unilateral replacement or timely late entry faces the other participant's protected floor and the common weighted-welfare cap. Consequently it gives original i at most q_i, including its actual prior costs and entry history.
4. Every incompatible unilateral strategy, including gifts, threats, solitary production, adaptive stopping and combat, has original value at most d_i<q_i. A hybrid strategy cannot learn a favorable physical outcome and then enter on terms escaping the compatible bound.
5. The chosen utility implementation is a least-cost available compatible implementation; another at equal cost may tie.

Then choosing the compatible controllers is an equilibrium of initial goal/implementation choice, strict against remaining incompatible. It is an equilibrium of the current-self game: the initial criterion assesses the future continuation, while subsequent decision selves optimize their actual installed criteria. It is not an instruction that the original agent controls the successor's later preferences.

**Proof.** Compatible deviations cannot improve original welfare by the other participant's preserved floor and the total cap. Incompatible deviations are strictly worse by the uniform outside bound. Staged abort or delay cannot improve when the same bounds include those histories. The supplied continuation checks establish the later selves' incentives. Equal-cost replication can tie; more expensive replication is inferior. QED.

The proposition's difficult premises are physical, strategic and informational. The following instances provide compatible continuations and initial deviation bounds within specified finite technologies, including 056's strengthened execution and post-information safeguards. The result is conditional Nash adoption with the declared current-self continuation; a complete off-path sequential-equilibrium construction for every expanded private-program protocol is not asserted. [057](../notes/057-costs-and-equilibrium-scope.md) states that distinction. Simultaneous mutual Attack may remain a weak equilibrium at some checkpoints, and positive installation costs can leave failed-coordination equilibria. No population law is supplied by this existence result.

## 4. Linear productive instance and the physical deviation audit

Two agents initially hold R_A=R_B=3 finished project units. Each private calibration consumes c=8/5 finished units. A shared batch succeeds with probabilities 0,1/10,1/10,2/5 for zero, either single, and both calibrations; success yields ten output units. Installing the compatible controller consumes k_i=1/20 finished units. The default success shares are 9/20 and 11/20. Mutual contribution yields

\[
E X=63/20,\qquad E Y=71/20,
\qquad E(X+Y)=67/10.
\]

Choose G_A=X+(1/5)Y+18e_A-40I_A and G_B=Y+18e_B-40I_B. The output part is at most 16. The contribution reward therefore makes compliant execution strictly preferable to every continuation following a missed obligation, including renegotiation; the penalty exceeds the full base plus reward. The safe parameter family allows concern coefficients from 046 and beta_i in [17,19]. The smaller reward 2 in 046 already gives fixed-contract work margins 24/25 and 19/20, but the stronger parameters are used here to cover the full continuation strategy. This avoids relying on the mutual-work selection needed by the earlier output-only-altruism prototype.

The full-effort profile uniquely maximizes expected total output. The other net production surpluses before installation are 0,-3/5,-3/5; full effort gives 4/5. Transfer lotteries preserve the total bound and physical waste lowers it. The protection theorem therefore fixes both expected original outputs across accepted renegotiations. Original gains over retained stocks are 3/20 and 11/20. An open region of installation costs satisfies k_A<1/5,k_B<3/5; the actual compatible implementation must attain the minimum available cost.

The [32-slot state table](../notes/049-guarded-implementation-state-table.md) gives a causal finite realization:

- Local installation takes three stages; new remote messages, shipments and attacks take four slots. Certificates finish before reaction begins.
- Calibration inputs ship before the two-stage reaction. The first informative success observation occurs only after ten work units have been spent. The input window cannot be reopened retrospectively.
- Installation, messages, checking, calibration, input shipment, reaction and routing use at most 5201/500=10.402 of the eleven available work units. The final quantities separately subtract material installation and calibration losses.
- A contest disperses finished material and damages/transforms the reactor/feedstock; its finite 40-slot repair delay exceeds the episode. Recovery and preparation each cost one work unit and one serial time unit per recovered output unit. The military charges are separate and do not become recovery batteries.

At attack arrival t, its recoverable total is

\[
W_h=\min\{O_h,\tfrac4{11}B_h,(32-t-1)_+/2\}\le4.
\]

Equal strength gives an original early-attack mean at most two, below retention and the productive outputs. Current contribution rewards and nonnegative other-regard preserve the on-path comparisons. After the reaction, at most one work unit remains, so even the *entire* recovery frontier is at most 4/11. The lowest resulting holding, 27/20 in this linear instance, exceeds it. Attack remains physically possible through delivery and holding; its unattractiveness is derived from these quantities.

An incompatible controller receives no partner contribution or outgoing concession. Even granting all solitary success output, its expected return from calibration is 1-8/5<0. A signal-conditioned attack cannot improve a solitary failure once the ten work units are spent. Before a reaction, private randomization and deterministic costs are the only entry-selection variables. A solitary reacted batch exhausts the feedstock/work needed to restore Q; a contest ends dual agency in this episode. Gifts that leave Q restorable can enter only with the restored dated contract. This supplies the adaptive-entry audit required by the proposition, within the stated technology.

The earlier unguarded example permits a profitable public resource burn followed by a loan request; [045](../notes/045-physical-threats-and-protected-fallbacks.md) preserves it. That failure motivates physical preservation and the incompatible-partner gate. It is a restriction of that earlier protocol, not a refutation of the guarded construction or a universal reason to retain goals.

## 5. Increasing concave original goals: insurance and diversification

### Insurance with a linear risk bearer

Let original A value any implementable continuous, strictly increasing, concave f(X), and B value Y. Put C_n=bY_0-c_A-c_B-k_A-k_B. If C_n>0, choose 0<r<C_n and insure A at q=R_A+r with the same transfer c_A+k_A+r in both success states. Finite collateral requires

\[
R_B\ge c_A+c_B+k_A+k_B+r.
\]

A can choose f(X)+eta(X+Y)+beta_A e_A-K_AI_A, with eta>=0. The fixed-contract effort threshold is

\[
\beta_A>f(q+c_A)-f(q)+\eta(c_A-aY_0).
\]

For b>=2a this makes contribution strict against either B action in that subgame; B's fixed-contract response is contribution when (b-a)Y_0>c_B. For the integrated game, normalize f by subtracting f(0), choose beta_A above its bounded output base, and give B a bounded contribution reward exceeding its material base, as in 056. This covers later renegotiation after either party misses an obligation. The default insures original A, maximizes total output and fulfills both contribution predicates. Current acceptance protects E f(X)>=f(q); Jensen gives E X>=q. B's own output floor and the total cap bound A above by the same f(q), so the initial-choice argument applies.

With R_A=3,R_B=4 and the earlier technology/costs, choose r=3/10. Then A receives 33/10 surely and B receives 2/5 or 52/5, with mean 22/5. For f=log(1+X), use eta=1/10, beta_A=6, beta_B=18, K_A=12 and K_B=40. B's lowest holding 2/5 exceeds 4/11, preserving the late-attack check. The general f theorem requires an actual representation/attestation for that f; the explicit logarithmic witness has a finite description. [048](../notes/048-insured-guarded-accommodation.md) and 056 supply the contract and execution details.

### Two risk-averse founders

For original w_i=log(1+X_i), equal initial stocks R and equal success shares, the full-effort adoption gain is

\[
\tfrac35\log(R-13/20)+\tfrac25\log(R+87/20)-\log(R+1).
\]

It is positive exactly for R>R_*, where 6.7391<R_*<6.7392. The sum of the two original utilities is then maximized by full effort and equal statewise allocation. Successors log(1+X_i)+(1/5)log(1+X_j)+beta_i e_i-K_iI_i implement the same protection mechanism; their original founders strictly gain. At R=10, beta_i in [5,6] and K_i=12 meet the uniform execution bound; at each other finite R, use 056's corresponding finite base bound. This is a broad stock region with two strictly concave original goals, not a linear-risk-bearer requirement. [052](../notes/052-welfare-certificates-and-two-risk-averse-founders.md) proves the unique threshold and fixed-contract effort inequalities.

At R=3, the single risky batch lowers the maximum sum of these original utilities even if goal installation is free. The physical repair in [053](../notes/053-diversification-and-renegotiated-production.md) divides the batch among up to three compartments. Extra compartments cost each agent h=1/100 material apiece and use additional work. A rho=1/10 common-shock component remains; the remaining success signals are independent. Completing all three gives each original agent about 0.0114345 more log utility than retaining stock, with strict current effort and attack incentives. Exact logarithm enclosures check every contribution profile and allocation upper bound. Strict adoption and optimality persist throughout rho in [9/100,11/100] and h in [9/1000,11/1000].

For the diversified integrated witness use beta_i in [4,5] and K_i=10. As correlation increases, agents can negotiate the active plan: all three compartments maximize the original-welfare frontier below rho approximately 0.180071; two maximize it until the positive-cost package loses its strict adoption gain near 0.269548. Keeping all three would lose its gain near 0.210162. The reward can refer to timely fulfillment of jointly registered physical obligations, with no retrospective erasure of a miss, so revising a future plan does not require a permanently frozen task. Lower setup costs and other technologies can shift these boundaries.

[055](../notes/055-concave-survivors-and-broader-original-goals.md) broadens the original goals to every fixed finite curvature gamma>0 in the normalized diminishing-returns power family: R>=13 max(1,gamma)−1 is a sufficient stock bound for strict original gain. At each fixed R>=33/20 there is a unique accepted-curvature boundary gamma_*(R), strictly increasing in R. At R=3, gamma=1/2 favors the batch while gamma=1 favors retention. Gamma=2 has the exact stock threshold R>2071/140. These comparisons vary original risk preferences while holding the physical technology fixed; insurance and diversification change the lottery itself.

The same note checks the survivor's actual optimization. A concave survivor can allocate some recovered output to the defeated project. Including that allocation preserves the logarithmic examples' outside-option bounds; own-priority alone must not be treated as zero output for a defeated agent. [056 section 9](../notes/056-contribution-rewards-through-renegotiation.md#9-loss-of-agency-and-voluntary-exit-need-their-own-allocation-check) also grants an incompatible agent voluntary exit and uncompensated control/input gifts. The displayed concern families survive the resulting allocation bound. Nearly equal concern can make a cheaper peaceful exit/delegation competitor attractive in a separate transfer module, so such routes need actual cost and feasibility checks rather than an assumed zero founder payoff after loss of agency.

## 6. Ordinary controls and actual mechanism costs

The output allocator by itself does not compel hidden effort. At the insured efficient frontier, an original A would retain the same transfer while saving its calibration cost. In a compact finite-collateral mediated class, strict concavity and the physical welfare maximum force the insured/equal allocation at any attempted equality. The profitable private shirk contradicts obedience. Compactness then gives a strict ordinary gap at that *same dated frontier*. This is a statement about output-transfer mechanisms, not about all unchanged-utility commitments.

For the symmetric linear fixture, the exact enlarged ordinary frontier with private correlated recommendations is particularly transparent. Let R be each stock and m a protected minimum holding, so effective collateral wealth is R_*=R-m>=3. With weak obedience/acceptance selected,

\[
N_{ordinary}(R_*)=
\begin{cases}
2/5,&3\le R_*\le71/10,\\
4/5-7/(5R_*-18),&R_*>71/10.
\end{cases}
\]

Finite-collateral incentives force failure waste at small R_*. Larger collateral and private mediation supply an explicit repair approaching the efficient 4/5 surplus. A minimum holding can deter late attack, at the cost of usable collateral. The frontier does not assume that an agent can void the privately mediated contract after learning a recommendation. [044](../notes/044-correlated-contracts-and-finite-collateral.md) derives the bound and attaining contracts; its complete earlier-stage attack implementation remains a separate check.

| Available control | What the analysis establishes | Extra condition to carry into a full game |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fixed-goal flexible policies and ordinary output transfers | Full effort may require costly failure penalties; the finite-collateral frontier includes private correlation | Actual collateral, information, recommendation secrecy, weak selections and attack checks |
| Observed sequential effort and a binding option contract | B's voluntary purchase can implement insurance and effort without goal change | Observation, pre-effort output assignment, costs and later threat commitments; a stage repair is not automatically the full guarded equilibrium |
| Binding compatible delegate | Can reproduce the complete guarded continuation and original payoff | Same authority, attestation, latency, safeguards and cost; cheaper implementation can displace direct modification |
| Genuine guarded goal change | Can be an optimal initial implementation, strict against remaining incompatible | Current-self evaluation, sound actual-state certification, protected fallback and the physical outside-option audit |
| Unverified promises or revocable advice | Leave the old hidden-effort incentives in place | Stronger monitoring/commitment can repair them; declarations alone supply neither |

This is why a matching delegate is informative rather than disqualifying. The selected mechanism creates credible future behavior and preserves bargaining protection. An original agent can rationally choose a genuine preference change among tied implementations, or strictly when it is cheaper than the available equivalents. The model does not manufacture unique superiority by omitting ordinary routes.

The actual-cost comparison in [057](../notes/057-costs-and-equilibrium-scope.md) is consequential. With guarded combined cost 0.1, free ordinary implementation and no reserve, guarded net surplus is 0.7. The ordinary frontier ties it at R=17.6 and exceeds it above that. This is a joint frontier comparison conditional on ordinary implementation; it does not itself refute a unilateral initial best response against an already guarded partner. Both comparisons belong in the interpretation.

## 7. Conditions governing combat, shares and initial coordination

In the linear productive class, a strict original gain over retained stocks and the audited early combat upper bounds is possible with share t precisely when

\[
\frac{k_A+c_A+\max(0,sW_0-R_A)}{bY_0}
<t<
1-\frac{k_B+c_B+\max(0,(1-s)W_0-R_B)}{bY_0}.
\]

Negotiating t enlarges the sufficient strength region in the numerical fixture from 9/80<s<63/80 to 3/40<s<37/40. Crossing that region's boundary invalidates this sufficient adoption comparison; it does not establish combat when other arrangements remain available.

At a resolved allocation x+y=T and available combat total W<=T, a current x+lambda y agent prefers retention exactly when

\[
x\ge sW-\frac{\lambda}{1-\lambda}(T-W),\qquad0\le\lambda<1.
\]

Greater own victory probability and greater recoverability raise the required holding. Greater concern for the other project and greater physical loss lower it. These are exact current-utility attack comparisons with actual capability retained. Original increasing concave own-output utilities also prefer a holding at least their mean combat output.

The separate [public-conversion module](../notes/054-public-conversion-combat-and-accommodation.md) supplies a stronger combat-versus-compromise comparison. Exclusive one-use goals, a small continued-agency stake, public interception and profitable post-contest recovery imply immediate combat in its uncommitted original-goal control under the displayed total-payoff inequality. A finite verified goal change or matched delegate supports peaceful allocation and strictly improves both original utilities. Higher agency value also gives an ordinary unchanged-goal peaceful region. That result is conditional on its explicit access, recovery and disclosure properties; it is not imported into the batch reactor or asserted for arbitrary physical environments.

Its unequal-strength extension uses the need to compensate both agents' initial contest guarantees. In the numerical module, the sufficient ordinary-combat interval is approximately 0.147133<s<0.852867. Negotiated guarded allocation at s=0.8, project-A probability 0.85 and concern 0.8 supports strict original gains and current restraint. At s=0.9 the agency stake already buys the weaker original agent's restraint, so assignment to the stronger party supports ordinary peace. The first interval is sufficient, not a complete classification outside its endpoints.

Initial coordination is also distinct from execution. In [051's reduced module](../notes/051-coordination-costs-and-negotiated-shares.md), if an agent gets productive gain r_i before installation, pays k_i and can instead receive ordinary payoff o_i, its belief threshold for joining a compatible arrangement is (k_i+o_i)/(r_i+o_i). Negotiating shares with total productive surplus C, total costs K and ordinary surplus O lowers the common threshold to (K+O)/(C+O). These are explicit belief/choice comparisons, not a derived population law. A better ordinary alternative can favor unchanged-goal cooperation rather than combat.

## 8. Stability scope, robustness and the next mathematical obligation

The protected equilibrium covers unilateral initial controller choices, the supplied staged execution, accepted renegotiations and certified later changes. The ordinary controls and physical-threat repair identify consequential restrictions. Parameter intervals, cost margins, logarithmic risk classes, a nonzero no-effort-success perturbation and bounded strategic-verification-error lemmas provide specified robustness checks. A sample of favorable parameters supplies no prevalence statement.

Coalition stability needs additional inequalities. [050](../notes/050-coalition-repair-with-productive-work.md) derives the condition q(S)>=max(R_S,p_SW_S) for each proper coalition in a declared protected module. A three-agent candidate shows how military synergy can violate pair constraints and how reducing excess recovery work while retaining productive work can repair them. Its full multi-party physical implementation and grand-coalition implementation comparison remain unfinished. The two-agent theorem is not relabeled coalition stable on the strength of that candidate.

The simple original goals, complementary production, finite collateral and physical recovery losses have independent modeling motivations. The selected guards, actual-state attestation, contribution predicates, pre-observation installation and common physical model are engineered capabilities. The constructive result is that agents with those capabilities can choose the package for instrumental reasons. Its breadth must be tested through cheaper controls, noisy evidence, earlier commitments, new partners and renewed productive opportunities at the same standard used to test the limiting cases.

[058](../notes/058-capability-costs-and-certificate-margins.md) turns capability into explicit parameters. Costly repeated verification certifies adoption when (1−n alpha)(q_i^0−cn)−R_i−Np^n(M−R_i)>0 under uniform conditional error bounds. Its fixture admits n=3,…,12; a better false-acceptance bound makes n=2 sufficient. A separate affine design boundary, Dm>ag+epsilon(a+D), trades avoided combat loss and original gain against attack temptation, an obsolete frontier and acceptance error. Costed refresh can restore saturation. Safe abort and catastrophic acceptance have different original-utility costs; large stocks can tolerate the former while requiring very small probabilities of the latter. These are conditional design bounds, not a supplied verifier or optimal design theorem.

The next integrated obligation is to complete an explicitly finite private-information bargaining protocol's off-path strategies and beliefs, then use it for repeated-choice refresh with new opportunities and partner choice. Carry forward the successful current-goal mechanism while testing which actual redesigns preserve it and which restrictions yield persistent obstructions. This run supplies no unrestricted innovation theorem, reproductive-fitness model or population prevalence estimate; those open levels do not erase its achieved conditional adoption and execution results.

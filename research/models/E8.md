# E8 — who joins a productive coalition, and what they agree to value

**Seventh recurrence.** Start with the [readable findings](../reports/r0007-findings.md). The [run record](../runs/r0007.md) distinguishes checked cases from the exploratory frontier. E8 is a family of instances under the flexible contract C1. Its defining question is whether a third party's contribution can make a genuine negotiated change attractive to agents who originally value only their own projects.

## The decision the founders face

A founder can keep its current rights, sign for an ordinary service, authorize limited or broad concern for other sites, sponsor an institution, or decline and use its physical disagreement option. A proposal has to deliver enough to each founder under its **present** ranking over terminal outcomes. A genuine change is a difference in rankings, including over feasible lotteries. Signing a contract, modifying a forecast and transferring control to an ordinary delegate can each change behavior under the old ranking. After actual installation, the successor uses its new ranking when deciding which job to finish, whether to initiate or whether to change again. A conditional update can occur when a gate opens; an already installed project-specific objective can instead govern a range of future calls. Report the adoption measure belonging to the implementation.

The class covers a single rival booking, mutually exclusive reciprocal service calls, jobs requiring whole groups of contributors, and a renewable service-credit process. One common reason to include a new partner is that it changes what the others can credibly obtain when it is most useful. Founder welfare, actual peaceful production, number of modified parties, scope of the change and stability against an exit are separate observables.

## How much of the return can a founder actually collect?

In a directed service call i→j, the donor forgoes d and the recipient gains r in original value units. An allocation specifies how often each physically feasible call is made. Under credible control, a reciprocal circuit can repay all participants even when no bilateral pair could do so. A positive weighted sum of originals can support a strictly improving feasible agreement. At zero fixed expense the productive network must have an expanding cycle in each source component. Finite fees depend also on how often useful jobs arrive. An actual sender may finance an adapter that the equally capable recipient would decline to finance. [Network boundary](../notes/153-when-reciprocal-referrals-can-pay-all-founders.md) · [Funded construction](../notes/154-who-would-build-the-missing-return-path.md)

An unconfigured member may collect service from its partners and keep its own job. With job-specific evidence z, let L be the likelihood of z when the recipient fakes relative to z when all installations are genuine. At a zero-cost appointment, the effective receipt for deterring a false signature is r(1-L). This accounts for both service on the all-genuine path and the reward available from pretending. Costed refusal and truth constraints have a finite optimization test. A supporting set of positive weights then makes every authorized delivery worthwhile for the **actual** common-goal successor. If a finished job takes several donors, charge them all before counting its gain. In the zero-fee complete-recipe class at least one choice of input matrix must have spectral radius below one. Stronger guarantees depend on the allowed partial configurations and alternative coalitions. ## Scope of the evidence

Scheduled reciprocal access, complementary scientific observations and deliberate third-party selection have documented institutional examples. The institutions supply evidence for the **opportunity and governance** assumptions. Estimates of local utility functions, capture probabilities, equipment errors and terminal-goal installation costs require other evidence. The explicit constructions instead identify an actor's physical authority, cost, original payoff from intervening, and the strategic responses that could sustain or displace the chosen design. Equivalent conventional control receives the same service capabilities where available.

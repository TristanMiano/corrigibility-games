## LESSONS FROM FAILURES

- Pairwise demand records can conceal whether a third participant helps. A sibling buys a joint dated observation. The higher-level question is which measurements remain informative after applicants choose what to disclose.
- Individual willingness to wait can coexist with a profitable coalitional dispute. A sibling updates the calendar only after successful delivery. The wider question is how changing coalition membership alters the future rights a schedule must protect.
- Paying one strong site can lose to paying two cheaper sites. A sibling chooses the actual signers and compensates them under their prior goals. A higher-level successor includes entry and replacement of the institutions quoting those prices.
- An early private selection can become foreseeable, while a private decision genuinely drawn later can restore the ordinary outcome. The sibling prices who can delay that decision. The higher-level question gives participants competing ways to purchase and transmit evidence.

# Shared means can make exclusive paperclip maximizers choose shared goals

**Sixth recurrence: Blue versus Red paperclips, from separate production
to almost entirely shared processes.**

The main finding is constructive: **agents that initially value only their
own color can rationally choose genuine concern for the other color when
that reciprocal change induces enough useful production to cover its real
cost and beat their available alternatives.** They need no initial taste
for accommodation. What they value initially is the extra output of their
own color that the arrangement makes possible.

Three distinctions explain when that happens:

* **Useful sharing and a credible reciprocal response favor accommodation.**
  Shared research can make both original maximizers better off after changing
  goals. Complementary production can also make a positive other-color weight
  individually advantageous. Negotiating the weights extends the result to
  strongly asymmetric benefits.
* **Capturable common assets, strong attack capability and expensive execution
  can favor combat.** An agent can deliberately build the technology that makes
  seizure profitable. Affordable agreements, defensive design and earlier
  negotiation can reverse that choice; their timing and actual costs matter.
* **Cheap ordinary controls and completed research can favor retaining utilities.**
  Conversely, durable goal change can save repeated implementation expense.
  These comparisons concern which mechanism is chosen, separately from whether
  agents remain peaceful or produce more clips.

These are conditional results over explicit production and agreement classes.
The run establishes genuine-adoption paths and boundaries, not a frequency
claim about arbitrary advanced agents. The [technical model](../models/E7.md)
and [proof notes](../runs/r0006.md#mathematics) specify the assumptions.

## Why an exclusively Blue maximizer can choose to care about Red

Consider two factories with separate finishing lines. Research by either
factory improves both lines; research consumes work that could otherwise
finish clips. For a simple normalization, their final counts are

`Blue = 20 + Blue research + Red research - (Blue research)^2/2`,

with the symmetric formula for Red. Every term represents actual output
or foregone production. The baseline energy and material stocks are finite
and large enough to fund the choices.

Initially Blue values only Blue clips, and Red only Red clips. Each chooses
research effort one and produces **21.5 clips of its own color**. Blue gains
nothing by unilaterally doing extra research when Red keeps the same effort.

Now suppose they can reliably arrange for both to adopt the common goal
“maximize Blue clips plus Red clips.” Each then chooses research effort two.
If each genuine installation costs one eighth of a clip's worth of work,
their actual outputs are **21.875 Blue and 21.875 Red**. Both originals gain
**0.375 of the only thing they originally valued**. The changed successors
continue researching because their new common goal values the benefit to
both factories. This is a genuine changed ordering over outcomes, not a
renaming of the old utility. The original Blue decision is evaluated by
Blue output, and subsequent decisions by the installed common criterion.

The general symmetric shared-knowledge result is particularly simple. If
`rho` measures how useful the partner's research is, and `A` measures the
productive scale relative to research expense, each original's gross gain
from reciprocal common goals is **`A rho²/2`**. Its actual update and launch
expense must fit inside that gain. A reliable conditional installation
interface makes reciprocal change an equilibrium against a broad class of
unilateral production commitments; an imperfect paid-audit version also
has a positive adoption region. These implementation premises are part of
the result, rather than consequences of an agent knowing its own utility.
[Derivation and implementation](../notes/131-reciprocal-adoption-against-broad-unilateral-choices.md).

An equally capable ordinary binding controller can deliver the same output.
At equal cost, genuine change can remain an optimal choice; a cheaper
available ordinary device wins. The useful finding is the incentive to
choose genuine change when it is an available best route, including a tie.
Uniqueness over every equivalent device is a different question.

## What changes as the common process grows

There are several physically different meanings of “overlap.” Counting
shared boxes on a manufacturing diagram can obscure their consequences.

| What is shared | Condition and consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Nothing: separate resources, technology and effects, with no useful exchange | The feasible production set factorizes. A change of goals supplies no extra production for both originals. An available interface-building technology would change this starting situation. |
| A reusable setup, design or factory preparation | Sharing saves useful work. If the saved setup work exceeds communication and execution expense, and downstream capacity has slack, both colors can increase. A binding finishing bottleneck can absorb the entire saving. |
| Usable research or intermediate knowledge | Greater useful sharing raises the reciprocal gain `A rho²/2` in the additive class. Credible reciprocity, affordable installation and actual outside options determine adoption. |
| Complementary contributions to one process | A changed goal can induce a more helpful response from the partner. In a second, smooth shared-process family, stronger complementarity raises the independently selected concern for the other color. Essential-input startup failures are repaired by an observable order of investment. |
| Rival equipment or material, and assets useful after capture | The same infrastructure can increase productive opportunity and the value of seizure. Real conversion, recovery, control and agreement expenses determine which effect wins. |
| Almost everything upstream, with separate final coloring | Extensive upstream commonality remains compatible with different final goals. Reciprocal production can benefit both. If one final actuator instead controls the whole batch, the agreement also needs a goal or controller that protects its negotiated color division. |

The exact setup-saving condition is **saved setup work greater than agreement
work**, together with spare material and finishing capacity. That result
supports ordinary sharing directly. Goal modification becomes useful when
future productive choices or control create an additional incentive problem.
[Setup and capacity proof](../notes/112-process-overlap-and-disjoint-terminal-values.md).

For complementary contributions, a general local calculation identifies
the mechanism: one's changed concern must affect one's action, that action
must induce a partner response, and that response must help one's original
output. In a production network the benefit can return through several
agents. In the additive example above there is no such unilateral response;
the productive gain comes from **negotiating both changes together**. This
distinction explains why a unilateral-change test can miss a successful
consensual arrangement.

The second production family reaches an informative endpoint. For a literal
minimum bottleneck—both contributions must match—ordinary agents can already
coordinate on efficient effort. Smooth complementarities instead give a
specific strategic advantage to positive other-color weights. The exact
technology determines which problem the agreement must solve.
[Complementarity and startup](../notes/132-shared-process-substitutability-and-chosen-goals.md).

## Negotiation repairs unequal benefits

Blue and Red rarely benefit equally from the same design. Fixing a common
goal with equal weights can consequently make one original refuse even
though another common goal works.

The asymmetric calculation allows the negotiated criterion
`Blue clips + z × Red clips`. When each factory's research provides a
strictly positive useful benefit to the other, there is a nonempty interval
of weights that improves both originals before implementation costs.
Costs shrink that interval. In one exact example, equal weights reduce Red
output by three clips; renegotiating the Red weight to two instead gives
net gains of **1.75 Blue and 0.875 Red**, after positive installation costs.

At the one-way endpoint, a useful transfer can repair the missing productive
reciprocity. The beneficiary can pay convertible work to the research
provider, leaving both better off. A cheaper arrangement with the beneficiary
retaining its goal and only the provider changing may then be selected;
ordinary service contracting is another substantive alternative. Thus the
direction of useful sharing and the availability of funded transfers matter
alongside its amount. [Full asymmetric frontier](../notes/137-asymmetric-sharing-and-negotiated-weights.md).

These examples also fit a broader class result. For jointly concave physical
production, a funded strict improvement allows the agents to choose an efficient
output vector supported by a finite smooth common goal, genuinely different from the exclusive original
goals. Under the stated differentiability conditions, every common-goal
effort equilibrium produces the negotiated output. This connects productive
opportunity to a stable current-goal continuation. Actual first installation
still needs the separately analyzed consent and execution interface.
[Class theorem](../notes/134-a-concave-production-class-and-negotiated-goals.md).

## Shared assets can also make fighting pay

Suppose each original has five units of native productive capacity. Blue
wins a battle with probability 0.8; damage and recovery leave 90% usable.
Blue can spend one unit to make the other factory's assets fully usable
for Blue production. In the specified offer game, it chooses that investment.

If executing a peaceful agreement costs one unit, Blue can obtain **6.48**
expected Blue clips by fighting, compared with **6.38** from its best funded
offer that Red accepts. It chooses combat. Reducing actual agreement expense
to half a unit changes the best peaceful value to **6.88**, so Blue chooses
agreement instead. The original value is unchanged: Blue clips alone.
The changed incentive comes from the recoverable prize and the real cost
of implementing the competing bargain.

This combat result is scoped to that actual agreement menu. The run then
tests enlargements: Red can pay for protective incompatibility; anticipating
a cheap enough defense can remove Blue's incentive to build the conversion
technology; an earlier bargain can save the defense expense. A cheaper
genuine-goal route can repair expensive execution, just as a cheaper ordinary
controller can. [Chosen investment and repairs](../notes/129-chosen-capture-technology-and-defensive-design.md).

One continuous example increases setup reuse and captureability together
from zero to one. Sharing remains acceptable through most of the path, but
the funded compromise fails above overlap **381/394**, about **96.7%**,
because the growing capture opportunity outruns the remaining bargain.
Lowering agreement expense repairs the entire path. This is a concrete
“almost everything is shared” comparison, with its joint parameter changes
declared. It supplies neither a universal monotonic law nor a probability
distribution over factories. [Joint overlap path](../notes/121-dual-use-overlap-paths-and-final-control.md).

The safeguard audit found another consequential mechanism. An attacker can
commit to producing some of the defender's clips after victory, inducing
the defender to preserve assets the attacker wants to capture. A funded
example makes attack pay **5.8208**, above the original native five. Such
unilateral contingent concern is different from consensual accommodation.
Stronger defensive goals and a statewise cap on attack rewards repair the
specified contingent and mixed-offer attacks. Those repairs require precise
event attribution, resource bounds and actual implementation; they are
deliberately designed conditions. [Attack and defensive redesign](../notes/133-conditional-defense-and-consensual-alternatives.md),
[mixed-offer repair](../notes/139-capped-reservations-and-mixed-combat-offers.md).

## What parties can deliberately change

The run separates favorable parameters from actions agents can fund and
choose. Four constructive interventions have explicit incentives:

* **Build compatibility.** Originals already invest in interfaces that make
  each other's research usable. A common goal can justify more investment.
  In the worked example, the extra net production pays for each goal change
  when its expense is below **one eighth of a clip**. The comparison includes
  the standardization chosen by unchanged agents.
* **Buy verification.** In a finite deceptive-controller family, two paid
  inspections support a selected mutual-adoption path when useful sharing
  exceeds **`sqrt(5/8)`** in the normalized example. At full useful sharing
  (`rho=1`), that path gives each original a net gain of **3/16**. Another equilibrium makes buying the same
  audit unattractive, so inspection alone does not select the outcome.
* **Renegotiate after detection.** Repair can coexist with honest initial
  adoption when the detected fake's negotiated repair gain stays below the
  derived incentive threshold. Cheap second chances with large retained
  rents can instead reward deception. The successful repair pays for fresh
  installations and gives the same reliable technology to initial alternatives.
* **Choose a longer-lived mechanism.** If an ordinary controller costs `d`
  per project and a durable change costs `K`, two productive opportunities
  can favor genuine change in the region **`d<K<(1+delta)d`**. An equally
  cheap durable ordinary device ties it. Valuable uncertainty can instead
  make waiting and later adoption optimal.

These are implemented within explicit model interfaces, not empirical claims
about present engineering prices. [Compatibility](../notes/120-chosen-standardization-and-productive-goals.md),
[imperfect audits](../notes/135-paid-imperfect-audits-and-reciprocal-adoption.md),
[repair](../notes/136-repair-after-detected-deception.md),
[timing and ordinary alternatives](../notes/138-when-to-adopt-a-durable-productive-goal.md).

Broader checks qualify the temporal and population interpretation. With a
fixed stock of useful research questions, a long horizon eventually induces
unchanged originals to complete that research themselves; paying to create
new useful research opportunities can preserve the gain from accommodation.
With many public-knowledge beneficiaries, membership depends on how much
the remaining group's effort falls when someone withholds. Earlier chosen
pair concern can make four agents voluntarily broaden their goals later.
Willing partner choice, uncertainty and production-network structure also
change the relevant comparison. These are finite participation and
continuation results, not evolutionary prevalence claims.

## How broad is the answer?

The independently motivated ingredients are simple: exclusive final goods,
useful reusable knowledge, convex research expense, scarce capacity,
recoverable assets and finite transaction costs. Strict inequalities give
open successful and unsuccessful regions within the declared families.
Reliable recognition, protected entry, conditional history-dependent goals,
particular offer rights and calibrated safeguards are stronger designed
ingredients. Their availability and cost need separate evidence. Familiar
strategic-delegation, research-cooperation and preference-adaptation work
already supplies close precedents; this run's contribution is the explicit
connection to physical paperclip production, actual goal choice, ordinary
alternatives and retained combat. The [source audit](../notes/118-literature-and-mechanism-audit.md)
records versions read and access limits, rather than claiming novelty from
abstracts.

For the project's central hypothesis, the achieved answer is a family of
conditions under which initially exclusive maximizers voluntarily install
genuine changed preferences, with selected paid implementations and stable
productive continuations. Remaining work concerns broader actual formation,
verification, scarce-resource competition and future innovations. The next
recommended investigation is endogenous three-party productive coalition
formation with a rival input and negotiated scope of concern, including
nonlinear current goals and the best available ordinary arrangements.
**CG-03 and final synthesis remain blocked.**

de-biasing self-review across the complete changed work and active protocol.
The main risks were favoring symmetric cooperative examples, treating a
restricted refusal as a general obstruction, and counting peace or a
unilateral defensive change as consensual adoption. Concrete corrections
included negotiating asymmetric weights, including ordinary standardization
and waiting options, charging fresh repair costs, correcting a utility-affine
equivalence claim, extending combat checks through production, and testing
defensive goals against contingent and mixed attacks before supplying scoped
repairs. Genuine-change successes and matched ordinary successes are both
retained. The review is not independent verification; broader naturality,
implementation costs, formation and prevalence remain unresolved.

# Scope, utility-change priority and deliberately created incentives

**Review of recurrences r0001–r0004, 2026-11-03.** This is the requested
review and protocol correction, not a recurrence or project synthesis.
The baseline is merged PR #10, commit `a83751e`. No research-time credit,
new mathematical theorem or independent proof verification is claimed.

**The work has narrowed substantially in its active environment, while
improving the completeness of the selected mechanism.** The first round
covered allocation, merger, physical recovery, deception, changing power,
goal design and individual/population dynamics. The last two rounds gave
increasing attention to a binary private-information bargaining game.
That depth yielded real adoption and repair results, but successive
handoffs made further refinement of the same family the default next step.
The portfolio now needs a materially different environment to test what
travels beyond it.

**Consensual utility change remains central to the positive findings, but
task selection needs a clearer modest preference for that subject.** The
reports lead with actual utility-change constructions. The recent agenda
also repeatedly asks whether stronger ordinary mechanisms displace them.
That comparison is necessary; making it the main organizing question
indefinitely would narrow the inquiry to the comparative efficiency of
one implementation. The correction gives negotiated genuine change
slightly greater priority while retaining serious ordinary alternatives
and equal scrutiny of enabling and limiting conditions.

**Naturality is discussed more consistently than deliberate attainability.**
The work already contains chosen goals, funded terms, protection, insurance
and diversification. It does not consistently distinguish a parameter
that the modeler varies from an incentive that a party can create and
would choose to create. The revised protocol now requires that distinction.

changing control and strength over time; and integrated treatment of

own-output/quadratic families. Individual revision and population selection
also remain separate, lightly integrated parts of the portfolio. These
topics have starting results or motivations, not an absence of prior work.

The mechanism of drift is visible in the handoffs: r0002 requested completion
of private-information execution, r0003 requested a causal completion of
the smaller diagnostic game, and r0004 requested another enlarged offer
and continuation game. Each was individually reasonable. Together they
made closure of one family's remaining details repeatedly take precedence
over testing the wider explanation. The active queue now changes that
priority; R309, R324 and R416 remain legitimate open obligations.

## 2. Utility change and ordinary compromise

There is substantial evidence against describing the work as having simply
abandoned genuine change. R0001's finite example has the original agent
choose a preference reversal. R0002 constructs broader productive goals
with chosen protection. R0003 makes truthful reporting serve an installed
goal. R0004 adds an initially negotiated receiver goal and repairs the
package after a stronger ordinary audit displaces its first version.
The latest report leads with those negotiated changes, not with ordinary
trade as the only desirable endpoint.

The imbalance is more specific. Increasingly elaborate ordinary-control
frontiers and repeated implementation challenges have absorbed much of
the *structure of the questions*. The time records classify theory,
computation and literature, not effort by mechanism; they do not establish
a numerical split between research on utility change and ordinary peace.
Counting notes or mentions would not establish one either. The justified
diagnosis is agenda concentration and a risk of treating utility change
mainly as something that must survive successive substitutes.

For example, the paid counteroffer in [092](../notes/092-paid-counteroffers-before-goal-activation.md)
is an informative result: in its known-state timing class, sharing unspent
installation cost can implement the policy with unchanged goals. It is
not a general answer to whether agents choose new preferences across other
opportunities. Conversely, the repaired funded package in
[083](../notes/083-costed-combat-information-and-renegotiated-goals.md) remains
constructive evidence at its stated scope. Both belong in the explanation.

The revised priority concerns the subject of the next question, not its
answer. At comparable research value, prefer a question about the adoption,
design or persistence of consensual genuine changes. Keep ordinary trade
and delegation available wherever the parties can implement them. A tie
with an equivalent delegate preserves optimal voluntary change; a cheaper
available substitute must still be reported. Give constructive and
limiting investigations equal seriousness within that priority.

Reports will distinguish a mutually accepted utility-change agreement,
unilateral strategic self-modification, actual installation, stability,
unchanged-goal compromise, combat and original welfare. A threatening
unilateral goal is evidence about strategic preference choice, but does
not by itself establish consensual accommodation. Fewer unnecessary
installations can improve welfare while preserving a successful arrangement;
the intended objective is not to maximize a raw count of modifications.

## 3. Natural incentives and incentives parties can create

Two assessments are needed. **Naturality** asks whether starting goals and
constraints have independent motivation, simple descriptions, robustness
and evidence for occurrence. **Deliberate attainability** asks whether an
agent or coalition can bring about a condition, pay its costs and benefit
from doing so after others respond. A condition may score well on one
dimension and poorly on the other. Neither dimension alone gives a
population-frequency claim.

Existing work provides useful beginnings, with different evidential limits:

| Candidate lever | Existing result relevant to utility change | What remains needed to treat it as a chosen intervention |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Negotiate successor goals, shares and payments | R0001's richer goals and E5's revised targets repair specific adoption failures; [083](../notes/083-costed-combat-information-and-renegotiated-goals.md)/[086](../notes/086-early-accommodation-and-a-repaired-disagreement.md) include costs and original-goal comparisons | General discovery, representation and credible execution remain conditional; reusable and two-sided goal choice can change the bargain. |
| Choose insurance or diversification | [R0002](r0002-findings.md#which-ordinary-values-favor-productive-compromise) expands productive participation through real collateral and costed compartmentalization | Production participation is one link. Recheck actual utility-change selection, ordinary alternatives and the actor's incentive to supply the insurance or infrastructure. |
| Buy a recovery backup or causal exit | [078](../notes/078-late-threats-and-chosen-recovery.md)/[082](../notes/082-fast-exit-early-goals-and-negotiated-safeguards.md)/[089](../notes/089-outcome-threshold-goals-and-combat.md) derive conditional purchase and continuation effects | A willingness-to-pay threshold is not a construction-cost estimate. Recovery and transport laws, funding and the joint initial choice must support availability. The backup reduces extortion in two already-peaceful regimes. |
| Improve goal verification | [R0003](r0003-findings.md#which-values-and-settings-favor-accommodation) derives a region with better original welfare and more genuine changes as verification improves | The capability improvement is varied externally. | Change encounter or partner selection | [019](../notes/019-selection-and-goal-transmission.md) gives different population regimes under different matching laws | The matching parameter is imposed in that process; it is not a proved feasible and privately attractive partner-selection institution. Voluntary change and fitness remain distinct. |
| Reallocate bargaining opportunities before activation | [092](../notes/092-paid-counteroffers-before-goal-activation.md) gives a funded ordinary-counteroffer interval | This intervention can reduce actual utility changes while improving the bargain. Timing, information and funding determine whether the comparison is available. |

This is a map of existing evidence and open links, not a list of proven
methods for causing utility change. It also shows why "engineered" should
not function as an automatic downgrade: arranging new incentives is
precisely part of what capable parties might do. The evidence still needs
to cover the arranger's incentives and resources. Equally, simple-looking
goals or physical parameters cannot be treated as freely controllable.

Future central mechanisms will carry a short account of the baseline,
controlling actor, feasible action, funding, information and timing,
current-goal incentive, others' responses, and separate effects on adoption,
peace and welfare. The record will distinguish a conditional parameter
effect, a feasible intervention, a beneficial intervention and actual
selected execution. It will also state who agrees and their refusal
options, separating negotiated arrangements from imposed threats and
involuntary rewriting. Enabling designs and conditions that defeat or
reverse their effects both receive explicit investigation.

## 4. Corrected next direction

**The next recommended recurrence should examine renewable complementary
production with voluntary partner choice.** A tractable starting point is
two encounters and a choice between potential partners, with resource
renewal, attack opportunities and later goal revision explicit. The main
question is when parties can arrange a future collaboration and protection
that makes a consensual broader goal worth adopting under current goals,
and when the same opportunities favor ordinary agreements, retention or
combat instead. This is a proposed research question, not a claim that
the construction will succeed or a permanent model restriction.

That direction reconnects E3's productive mechanism with the long-run and
partner-choice questions left thin by E4/E5. It should test one connected
mechanism with actual costs and ordinary controls, rather than opening many
unrelated examples at once. E5's reusable-goal and live-counteroffer gaps
remain available comparative tests and backlog; they need not all be solved
before this broader environment is studied. Future selections should also
revisit coalitions, mergers and richer preferences according to their
explanatory value.

The updated charter, AGENTS, AIM_BALANCE, recurrence and run template encode
these priorities and evidence distinctions. TODO and the current handoff
point to the broader next direction. The synthesis gate now explicitly
asks for varied environment coverage and an honest map of naturally
arising versus deliberately attainable conditions. No recurrence, applied
design project or synthesis step begins in this review.
